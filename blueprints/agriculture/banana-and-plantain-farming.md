            - name: Choosing banana, plantain or both for the first block
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Plantain is cooked green or ripe and sells by the finger or the bunch to vendors, households and chip makers, while dessert bananas, called fig in parts of the Caribbean, are sold ripe and bruise easily on the way to market. The two need the same ground and care but very different handling after harvest, so the choice decides your buyers, your transport and how fast you must sell. Starting with one crop and adding the other once the first block is bearing keeps the learning manageable.

                ## Milestones
                1. Local prices and usual buyers for green plantain, ripe plantain and ripe fig written on one page.
                2. Three growers or vendors asked which sells more steadily in your area and why.
                3. One crop, or a split between the two, chosen for the first block with the reason written down.
                4. The number of mats to plant in the first block set.

                ## Notes
                Plantain travels and keeps better green, which forgives a slow week at market. Ripe bananas give you two or three days at most.

            - name: Site selection for wind shelter and drainage
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Bananas and plantains are tall, shallow rooted and top heavy once a bunch hangs, so they snap or topple in winds far below hurricane strength, and they rot in ground that stays waterlogged after rain. Walking candidate plots after a heavy shower and on a windy afternoon shows you which ground keeps a block standing. A sheltered, deep, well drained spot near water is worth more than a larger exposed one.

                ## Milestones
                1. Two or three possible plots walked, with the direction of the prevailing wind noted for each.
                2. Each plot checked within a day of heavy rain for standing water.
                3. A spade hole dug in each to see soil depth and colour down to at least half a metre.
                4. One site chosen, with the main wind and drainage risks written beside it.

                ## Notes
                Low spots along drains look lush and are often where disease and waterlogging start. Ask neighbours where their bananas blew down in the last storm.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:

                output_kind: decision

                Most banana pests and diseases, from weevils and nematodes to Moko and Panama wilt, arrive on planting material taken from someone else's field.             - name: De-suckering rounds and follower selection
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Left alone, a banana mat throws suckers constantly, and they compete with the mother plant and give a crowded clump of small bunches. Keeping one strong follower, and sometimes a second younger one, and removing the rest on a regular round keeps bunches large and spaces harvests out through the year. Choosing followers that line up with the row also keeps the block in order as it ages.

                ## Milestones
                1. A rule agreed for how many followers each mat keeps.

                3. Followers chosen on the side that keeps the row straight.
                4. A monthly round set and kept for three months.

                ## Notes
                Cutting a sucker off at ground level often lets it regrow. Gouge out the growing point or cut it low and twist, the way your extension officer shows you.

            - name: Monthly de-leafing and field sanitation
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Dead and badly spotted leaves hanging on a plant are where leaf spot fungi produce the spores that infect the young leaves above. Cutting off hanging and heavily diseased leaves each month and laying them flat on the ground to rot reduces the disease load and lets light and air into the block. Leaves rubbing against a bunch also scar the fruit, so de-leafing protects price as well as plant health.

                ## Milestones
                1. The rule for which leaves to remove agreed with the extension officer.
                2. Every mat de-leafed with a clean pole knife.
                3. Cut leaves laid flat between rows, away from young plants.
                4. A monthly round kept for three months, with the number of mats done recorded.

                ## Notes
                Never strip healthy green leaves to tidy a plant. A plant needs a good number of working leaves at shooting to fill its bunch.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: operating
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "The field book records three consecutive monthly de-leafing rounds covering every mat in the block."
                cadence: rolling
              tasks:

                - "Sharpen the pole knife and fill the blade dip"

# Banana and Plantain Farming

This area is for anyone growing bananas or plantains to sell, from a grower with fifty mats along a drain to a farmer putting in plantain by the acre for a chip maker. It starts with foundations (banana, plantain or both, a sheltered site that drains, clean planting material, spacing, land preparation, costs and buyers lined up before planting), then the weekly and monthly machinery of mat walks, de-suckering, de-leafing, bagging and propping, leaf disease scoring and price watching, the crop knowledge that keeps a block productive for years, decisions on varieties, ratoons and markets, dated events such as the first bunch harvest and the pastelle leaf season, situations from a backyard patch to an old neglected field, and finally specialist work on ripening rooms, sucker multiplication and Fusarium wilt biosecurity.

What repeats is a Saturday mat walk, a Tuesday bagging and ribbon round, a Thursday propping check, a Wednesday leaf disease score, a Friday price check, a daily rain reading, monthly de-suckering, de-leafing, weeding, weevil trap counts, records and sales tallies, and quarterly and yearly reviews. The Purchase decision, Metrics log, Operational checklist and Risk register templates pair with the projects that keep those records.
