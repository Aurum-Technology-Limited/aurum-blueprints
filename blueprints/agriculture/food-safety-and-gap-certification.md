            - name: Field risk assessment and land history check
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Fields that were pig pens, dumps or flood plains five years ago can still carry pathogens or chemical residues, and GAP standards ask for a written assessment of each production site. Recording each field's past use, flooding history and neighbouring hazards, with the controls you will apply, is the document auditors ask for first.

                ## Milestones
                1. Previous land use for each field recorded from memory, neighbours or lease papers.
                2. Flooding, runoff and neighbouring livestock or septic risks noted per field.
                3. Each hazard rated by likelihood and severity.
                4. A control written for every high hazard, such as a buffer, a test or not growing ready-to-eat crops there.

                ## Notes

            - name: Agricultural water source risk assessment
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Irrigation and spray water that touches the edible part of a crop is one of the most common routes for E. coli and Salmonella onto produce. Assessing each source, whether a covered well, a river intake, a pond or a rainwater tank, for what could enter it and how the water reaches the crop shows which sources need treatment, testing or a change of irrigation method.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every water source listed with its use: irrigation, spraying, washing, cooling or drinking.
                2. Each source inspected for animal access, runoff, septic proximity and the condition of covers and pipes.
                3. Application method noted for each crop, overhead, furrow or drip, and whether water touches the harvested part.
                4. Each source rated and given a control, such as fencing, a cover, testing or switching to drip.

                ## Notes
                Water used after harvest, for washing, cooling or ice, is usually held to a drinking water standard. Check the exact requirement in your chosen scheme.
              priority: high

# Food Safety and GAP Certification

Food Safety and GAP Certification is for growers and small processors whose buyers have started asking for proof: a hotel chef wanting a food safety plan, a supermarket wanting a GAP certificate, an exporter wanting water test results. The projects start with foundations such as buyer requirements, choosing a standard, site and water risk assessments, toilets and handwashing and a gap analysis, then the routines that keep a farm audit ready, the skills behind HACCP and sanitation, decisions about water, crates, sheds and certification bodies, the audit itself, situations from a small hotel supplier to a farm recovering after a flood, and specialist work such as HACCP plans for processed products and running a farmer group's internal control system.

What repeats is the daily hygiene check and pre-shift clean on harvest and packing days, weekly container cleaning and animal intrusion walks, monthly pest, manure, foreign body and corrective action reviews, quarterly water tests and self-audits, and the yearly recertification cycle. The Compliance control, Operational checklist, Training program, Vendor, Purchase decision and Remediation plan templates pair with the projects that name them. Limits, intervals and sanitiser strengths come from your chosen standard, the product label, the laboratory and your extension officer or auditor, never from this list.
