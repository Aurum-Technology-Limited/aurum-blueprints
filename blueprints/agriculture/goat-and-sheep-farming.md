                ## Purpose
                Before buying a single animal, decide what the flock is for, because a meat herd, a dairy herd and a breeding stock business need different breeds, housing, labour and buyers. Settling it on paper with rough numbers for each option stops the common pattern of buying whatever goats were for sale and finding none of them suit the market you have.

                1. The three options, meat, milk and breeding stock, each written up with likely buyers within an hour's drive.

                3. A rough yearly income per breeding female estimated for each option, using local prices you have checked.
                4. One main enterprise chosen and written in a sentence, with any side income named.

                ## Notes
                Most Caribbean smallholders start with meat goats, because live sales for curry goat and festival demand are steady, while dairy needs daily milking and usually a licence to sell milk. Breeding stock sales come later, once your records can prove what your animals do.

                ## Purpose
                Goats browse shrubs and test every fence, while hair sheep such as Barbados Blackbelly graze grass, cope well with heat and are calmer to contain. Matching the species to your land, fencing budget and buyers saves years of fighting the animals you chose.

                4. A species decision recorded, with the starting breed or breeds named.

                ## Notes
                Mixed flocks suit land with both grass and bush, but goats and sheep share many of the same stomach worms, so mixing them does not halve the parasite problem.

            - name: Night house with a raised slatted floor
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Wet ground is where most goat and sheep troubles start: worms, foot rot, pneumonia and kids lost to cold and damp. A well-ventilated night house with a raised slatted floor, a roof with a good overhang and separate pens for kidding and young stock keeps animals dry at night and makes cleaning a quick job.

            - name: FAMACHA scoring and targeted worm control
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Barber's pole worm is the main killer of goats and sheep in the wet tropics, and dosing the whole flock on a calendar has bred worms that survive most products. Checking eyelid colour with a FAMACHA card and treating only the animals that need it, under rules agreed with your vet, keeps animals alive and the dewormers working.

                ## Milestones
                1. At least one person on the farm trained to score eyelids with a FAMACHA card through a vet, extension officer or course.
                2. Treatment rules agreed with the vet: which scores are treated, with which product, and how each dose is set by weight.
                3. Every animal scored on a set day, with scores and treatments recorded by tag number.
                4. Animals that need treatment again and again flagged for the cull list.

                ## Notes
                Many keepers score every two weeks in the wet season and monthly in the dry season; agree your intervals with the vet. FAMACHA only detects barber's pole worm, so the vet may add other checks for other parasites.
              priority: high
              frontmatter:
                mode: operating
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "Every animal has a recorded eyelid score each scoring round, and only those meeting the vet's treatment rule are dewormed."
                cadence: rolling
              tasks:
                - "Ask the vet or extension office where to get FAMACHA training and a card"
                - "Agree the treatment rules and products with your vet in writing"
                - "Score every animal's eyelids and record the results by tag @recurring(monthly:8)"
                - "Flag animals treated more often than the vet's limit for the cull list"

            - name: Rotational grazing and paddock rest
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Worm larvae live in the bottom few centimetres of grass, and a paddock grazed short and often becomes a worm farm. Moving the flock on before the grass is grazed low and resting each paddock long enough for larvae to die off cuts worm burdens and grows more feed.

                ## Milestones
                1. A grazing order for all paddocks, with move-in and move-out dates.
                2. A rest period agreed with the extension officer or vet for your climate and each season.
                3. A rule for moving the flock based on grass height, not the calendar.
                4. A full season of moves recorded, with any paddock that failed to recover noted.

                ## Notes
                Rest periods that break the worm cycle are usually longer than people expect, so ask the extension officer. Cutting a paddock for hay or grazing cattle behind the flock also cleans it.
              priority: medium

                Lactating does and ewes in tropical heat drink far more than most keepers expect, and dirty or sun-warmed water means they drink less. # Goat and Sheep Farming

Goat and Sheep Farming is for anyone raising goats or hair sheep as a business in the Caribbean and other warm, wet places, from a few backyard does to a commercial flock supplying abattoirs, festival buyers or milk customers. The projects begin with the foundations: choosing the enterprise and species, tags, a night house on a raised floor, fencing, sound foundation stock, quarantine and a health plan written with a vet. Then come the daily and monthly routines, the handling and health skills, breeding and feed decisions, the kidding, show and festival milestones, situations such as working off the farm or taking over a flock, and specialist work on performance recording, AI and disease screening.

What repeats is the morning and evening head count, daily browse and milking, weekly fence, grazing, water and pen jobs, monthly FAMACHA scoring, hoof trimming, weighing and breeding records, quarterly egg counts and a yearly cycle of mating dates, culling, festival orders, disease screening and the vet review. The Operational checklist, Metrics log, Purchase decision, Vendor and Training program templates pair with the projects that point to them. Dewormer, vaccine and other veterinary product choices and doses always come from your vet or the label, never from this list.
