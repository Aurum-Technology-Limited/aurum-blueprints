              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most farmers know there is an agriculture office somewhere in the district, but few know which county or parish office covers their land, when it opens or which officer handles their crop. Writing down the offices, phone numbers and opening hours that actually serve your address saves wasted trips and gets requests to the right desk first time.

                ## Milestones
                1. The district or county extension office for the farm's address identified, with phone, email and opening hours.
                2. The nearest propagation station, plant protection unit and veterinary office listed with contacts.
                3. Any CARDI, university or commodity board unit working on your main crop noted.
                4. All contacts saved in one phone group and on a printed sheet kept in the farm shed.

                ## Notes
                Ask at the office which officer covers your exact road or village. Boundaries between districts are often not where farmers expect them.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14

            - name: Recommendation follow-through tracker
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The commonest reason extension fails is that advice is given, half followed and never checked.                 ## Milestones

                2. Each recommendation marked as done, partly done, not tried or dropped, with a reason.
                3. A result recorded for every completed recommendation, with a photo or yield figure where possible.
                4. Results reported back to the officer at the next contact.

                ## Notes

# Using Government Extension Services

This area is for farmers who know the Ministry of Agriculture, CARDI, IICA and the university field stations exist but have never had much out of them. It starts with foundations (finding your office and officer, a farm profile sheet, a first visit and a notebook for every visit), then the routines that turn advice into results, the skills of field schools, short courses and good diagnostic samples, decisions about varieties, demonstration plots and government services, dated events such as field days, shows and damage assessments, situations from the new entrant to the farmer handing over, and finally specialist work as a lead farmer, trial host and committee member.

What repeats is a weekly look at advisories and the field school or trial plot, a monthly round of recommendation follow-ups, training checks and group meetings on different days, a quarterly call to your officer, and a yearly planting material order, soil test submission and review of what extension did for the farm. The Person, Meeting notes, Course and Metrics log templates pair with your officer, visit notes, training courses and trial records. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
