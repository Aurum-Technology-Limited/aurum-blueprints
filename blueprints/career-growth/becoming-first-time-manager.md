              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Plenty of strong individual contributors say yes to a team because it looks like the only way up, then discover they miss the craft and dislike the job. Before accepting, spend a week testing the offer against what the role really involves day to day, what it pays, and whether your organisation has a senior individual track you would be giving up.

                ## Milestones
                1. A written description of a typical manager's week at your organisation, gathered from two people who do the job.
                2. The pay, title and scope of the manager role compared with the next individual contributor level.
                3. A list of the parts of your current work you would stop doing, and how you feel about each.
                4. A decision recorded with your reasons, and the conditions you will ask for if you say yes.

                ## Notes
                It is reasonable to ask for a trial period or an agreed route back to individual work if the role does not suit you after a year. Ask before you accept, when you have the most room to negotiate.

# Becoming a First-Time Manager

This area is for the knowledge worker who has just been handed a team, or is deciding whether to accept one, and has quickly found that being good at the work is not the same as being good at running it. It starts with the foundations (the decision, a new definition of success, expectations with your own manager, a first round of conversations with each report and letting go of your old work), then the weekly machinery of one-to-ones, team meetings and notes, the core skills of feedback, delegation and coaching, the improvements that make a team run well, your first set pieces such as review season and a first hire, versions for particular situations like managing former peers or a remote team, and finally the questions an experienced manager works on.

What repeats is the rhythm a team relies on: a weekly one-to-one with each report, a weekly team meeting and stakeholder update, Friday notes on each person, monthly checks on your own time, workload, numbers, recognition and a talk with a peer manager, and quarterly goals, career conversations and upward feedback. The Person, 1:1, Feedback themes, Development plan, Skills matrix and Onboarding plan templates pair with the projects that use them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
