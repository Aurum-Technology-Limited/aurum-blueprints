            - name: Confidentiality rules for what your log may hold
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Wins logs often end up holding client names, unreleased figures and screenshots of internal chats, which can breach your contract or a client agreement the moment the file leaves a work account. Reading your employer's acceptable use and confidentiality policies once, and writing your own three-line rule, lets you log freely without creating a problem for your future self.

                ## Milestones
                1. The relevant sections of your employment contract and data or acceptable use policy read.
                2. A short written rule for what goes in the log: outcomes, roles and rounded or relative numbers, not confidential data.
                3. A list of the kinds of material that stay inside work systems only, such as client names or unreleased financials.
                4. A decision recorded on whether a personal copy is allowed, and in what form.

                ## Notes
                When in doubt, describe the result in relative terms (cut processing time by about a third) and keep the raw figures where they belong. If the policy is unclear, ask HR or your manager in writing rather than guessing.

# Brag Document & Wins Log

This area is for anyone who has stared at a blank self-review box and could not remember what they shipped in spring. It begins with foundations (where the log lives, what it may safely hold, its headings, an entry format, tags and a twelve-month backfill), then the weekly and monthly routines that keep it filling itself, the writing skills that make entries useful to a reader, improvements for logs that have grown messy or gone quiet, the moments when the log pays out (reviews, CVs, interviews, pay talks, leaving a job), variants for engineers, new managers, contractors and part-timers, and finally the long-view work of senior people and managers who help others keep logs.

What repeats is a Friday wins entry, an optional one-line daily note, a monthly roll-up, a calendar sweep and a link check, a quarterly highlights summary and tag clean-up, and a yearly archive. The Metrics log, 1:1, Feedback themes and Resume templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
