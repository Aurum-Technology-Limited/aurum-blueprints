            - name: Push and pull reasons for changing field
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most people who want out of their field can list what they are running from far more easily than what they are running towards, and a change built only on push factors often lands in the same problems with a new job title. Writing both lists down, then separating what is about the field from what is about this employer or this manager, tells you whether you need a new career or just a new job.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of at least eight things you want to leave behind, each tagged as field, employer, manager or role.
                2. A list of at least five things you want more of, written as conditions rather than job titles.
                3. A one-paragraph conclusion on whether a change of field, not just of employer, is needed.
                4. The conclusion read aloud to one person who knows your work and their reaction noted.

                ## Notes
                If most of the push factors are tagged employer or manager, test a move within your current field first. It is cheaper and faster, and nothing stops you changing field later.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A written page of push and pull reasons, each tagged, ending in a recorded decision on whether a field change is the right response."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "Write every frustration with your current work on one page, without editing"
                - "Tag each frustration as field, employer, manager or role"
                - "List five conditions you want in your next work, not job titles"
                - "Write a one-paragraph conclusion and share it with someone who knows your work"

            - name: Financial runway for the switch
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A career change usually costs money twice: once in retraining fees or reduced hours, and again in a lower starting salary while you build credibility. Knowing exactly how many months your savings cover at your essential spending level turns a vague worry into a number you can plan around, and it decides how fast or slow the change has to be.

                ## Milestones
                1. Essential monthly spending calculated from three months of real bank statements.
                2. Savings that can be used for the change separated from emergency savings.
                3. Runway in months written down for three cases: no income, part-time income and an entry-level salary.
                4. A target runway agreed, with a monthly savings amount to reach it.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Savings goal** template. Many changers aim for six to twelve months of essentials before any drop in income, but the right figure depends on your household and is worth checking with a financial adviser if debts are involved.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A runway sheet showing essential spending, usable savings and months of cover under three income cases, with a target and monthly savings figure."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:
                - "Download the last three months of bank statements"
                - "Total the essential spending and divide by three for a monthly figure"
                - "Work out months of runway for no income, part-time and entry-level pay"
                - "Set a target runway and a monthly amount to save towards it"

# Career Change Transition

Changing field is one of the bigger bets most people make at work, and it usually goes wrong for dull reasons: the money ran out, the new field was imagined rather than tested, or the retraining was chosen before anyone in the field was asked. These projects run in that order: foundations such as your reasons, runway and transferable experience, then the weekly machinery of exploration and conversations, the skills and routes into a new field, the tests and decisions, the dated moves, the variants for parents, licensed professionals and people changing after redundancy or illness, and finally the work of settling into and building on the new career.

The rhythms that repeat are a weekly exploration session, informational interview requests and follow-ups, study blocks while you retrain, a monthly runway check and a monthly continue or stop checkpoint, with a quarterly review of the whole plan. The Savings goal, Skills matrix, Meeting notes and Course templates pair with the runway, the transferable experience inventory, informational interviews and the retraining itself. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
