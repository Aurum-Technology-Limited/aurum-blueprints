            - name: Choosing the target role for your gap plan
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A gap can only be measured against something, and plans aimed at "getting better generally" stall within a month because nothing tells you what to practise. Naming one target role, with a job title, a seniority level and a rough time horizon of 12 to 24 months, gives every later project in this area a fixed point to measure from.

                ## Milestones
                1. One target role written down with its title, level and the kind of organisation that hires for it.
                2. A time horizon for being ready, between six and twenty-four months, recorded beside it.
                3. Up to two alternative roles parked in a short list so you can switch later without starting again.
                4. One sentence explaining why this role, in your words, kept with the plan.

                ## Notes
                If you cannot choose between two very different roles, run the plan on the one with more open adverts in your region; the gap evidence often settles the question. Deciding whether to change field entirely belongs in a career change plan, not here.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "One target role with title, level and a readiness date is written at the top of the gap plan, with a one-sentence reason."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "Write the job title you would like to hold in two years"
                - "Note the level and type of organisation that hires for that title"
                - "Park up to two alternative roles in a short list below it"
                - "Confirm the target role still fits your plans @recurring(yearly)"
            - name: Five live job adverts for the target role
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Your idea of a role and the market's description of it are rarely the same, and the adverts are where employers write down what they will actually test you on. Collecting five current adverts from different employers gives you the raw evidence for the gap analysis instead of guesses about what matters.

                ## Milestones
                1. Five adverts for the target role saved in full, from at least three different employers.
                2. Each advert labelled with its date, employer type and location or remote status.
                3. The essential and desirable requirements of each advert copied into one document.
                4. Any advert that turned out to be a different level removed and replaced.

                ## Notes
                Save the full text, not the link: adverts are taken down within weeks and you will want to reread them when you re-rate your skills. Senior or niche roles may have few adverts; add role profiles from professional bodies or a friend's internal job description.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "Five saved adverts from at least three employers, with their essential and desirable requirements copied into a single document."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "Search two job boards for the exact target title and save three adverts"
                - "Find two more from employers of a different size or sector"
                - "Copy every essential and desirable requirement into one document"
                - "Label each advert with its date, employer type and location"
            - name: Requirements map from the target role adverts
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Five adverts produce forty or fifty requirements, many of them the same skill worded differently. Grouping them and counting how often each appears shows which abilities are table stakes, mentioned in four or five adverts, and which are one employer's wish list you can safely ignore.

                ## Milestones
                1. Duplicate requirements merged into a list of 12 to 20 distinct skills or experiences.
                2. Each skill tagged as technical, people, domain knowledge or credential.
                3. A count beside each skill showing how many of the five adverts mention it.
                4. The skills mentioned in three or more adverts marked as core.

                ## Notes
                Ask the agent to group the raw requirements into distinct skills, then check its grouping yourself: it will sometimes merge two things an employer would treat separately.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A list of 12 to 20 distinct skills, each with a type tag and a count of adverts mentioning it, with core skills marked."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Ask the agent to group the copied requirements into distinct skills"
                - "Merge or split groups until each line is one testable skill"
                - "Count how many adverts mention each skill"
                - "Mark every skill named in three or more adverts as core"
            - name: Honest self-rating against the requirements map
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most people rate themselves on how much they know about a skill rather than how well they can do it under pressure, so their scores drift up. Rating each mapped skill on a simple four-level scale, from aware to able to teach it, with a one-line reason for each score, gives a starting point you can defend.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every skill on the requirements map rated on a four-level scale with written level descriptions.
                2. A one-line reason beside each rating, naming the last time you used the skill.
                3. The target level for each skill in the target role recorded beside your rating.
                4. The difference between current and target level calculated for every skill.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Skills matrix** template. A useful scale: 1 aware of it, 2 can do it with help, 3 can do it alone to a good standard, 4 could teach it. Rate what you could do next Monday, not what you once did.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A skills matrix covering every mapped skill, with current level, target level, gap size and a dated reason for each rating."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Write a one-line description for each of the four rating levels"
                - "Rate every skill on the requirements map against that scale"
                - "Add the date you last used each skill beside its rating"
                - "Record the target level the role needs and the gap for each skill"
            - name: Evidence behind every rating of three or four
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A high rating without proof is the rating an interviewer or promotion panel will take apart first. Attaching one concrete piece of evidence, a document, a result, a named project, to each skill you rated at three or four separates real strengths from comfortable assumptions before anyone else does.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every rating of three or four linked to a specific piece of work, document or result.
                2. Any rating you could not evidence lowered by one level.
                3. A short list of strengths you can prove, ready for applications.
                4. A list of skills where you have ability but no evidence, flagged as a separate kind of gap.

                ## Notes
                Evidence gaps are cheaper to close than ability gaps: often one write-up or one visible piece of work fixes them, so keep them in their own column.
              priority: medium
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "Each skill rated three or four has a named piece of evidence beside it, and unevidenced ratings have been lowered or flagged."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "List every skill you rated three or four"
                - "Find one document, result or project that proves each of them"
                - "Lower by one level any rating you cannot evidence"
                - "Flag skills you can do but cannot yet show as evidence gaps"
            - name: Outside ratings from three colleagues
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Self-ratings miss the gaps other people see every week, especially in communication, judgement and how you handle conflict. Asking three people who have watched you work, ideally a manager, a peer and someone you support, to rate five chosen skills gives you a second view and usually one surprise worth acting on.

                ## Milestones
                1. Five skills from your matrix chosen for outside rating, including at least one people skill.
                2. Three people who have seen you work asked, with a short form or three questions.
                3. Their ratings set beside yours in the matrix.
                4. Every skill where an outside rating differs from yours by a full level noted with their reason.

                ## Notes
                Ask for an example with each rating, not just a number. People find it easier to be honest when you ask "what would make this a four" than "how good am I".
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "Ratings from three colleagues on five skills sit beside your own in the matrix, with every full-level disagreement explained."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "Pick five skills from the matrix, including one people skill"
                - "Choose a manager, a peer and someone you support to ask"
                - "Send each a three-question request with a two-week reply window"
                - "Add their ratings and examples beside your own"
            - name: Ranked gap list by impact and effort
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Twelve gaps in a matrix make a list of reasons to feel behind, not a plan. Scoring each gap on how much it matters for the target role and how long it would take to close produces a ranked list, with the quick, high-impact gaps at the top where they belong.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every gap scored one to five for impact, using the advert counts and outside ratings.
                2. Every gap scored one to five for effort, with a rough hours estimate.
                3. The gaps sorted into four groups: quick wins, big bets, fill-ins and ignore for now.
                4. The ranked list saved as the second page of the gap plan.

                ## Notes
                Credentials often score high on effort and lower on impact than people expect; check how many adverts made them essential rather than desirable before ranking them high.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "Every gap in the matrix has an impact and effort score and sits in one of four named groups on a saved ranked list."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Score each gap one to five for impact on the target role"
                - "Estimate the hours to close each gap and score its effort"
                - "Sort the gaps into quick wins, big bets, fill-ins and ignore"
                - "Save the ranked list as page two of the gap plan"
            - name: The three gaps to close first
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Working on eight skills at once means none of them moves far enough to show. Choosing three, usually two quick wins and one big bet, and writing what closed looks like for each, gives you a focus you can explain in one sentence to a manager or interviewer.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three gaps chosen from the ranked list, with the reason for each.
                2. A definition of closed written for each, as something another person could observe.
                3. The remaining gaps parked on a later list with a date to revisit them.
                4. The three gaps shared with one person who will ask about them.

                ## Notes
                A good definition of closed: "ran a budget forecast for the team alone and it was accepted without rework", not "better at budgeting".
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 35
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "Three named gaps, each with an observable definition of closed, are recorded in the plan and shared with one other person."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "1"
              tasks:
                - "Pick two quick wins and one big bet from the ranked list"
                - "Write an observable definition of closed for each gap"
                - "Move the other gaps to a later list with a revisit date"
                - "Tell one colleague or friend which three gaps you are working on"
            - name: Weekly hours and learning budget for the plan
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Plans fail on time and money far more often than on motivation, and most people overestimate both by half. Setting a realistic weekly hours figure, a yearly spending limit and the employer funds you can draw on before choosing any course stops you buying a programme you will not have time to finish.

                ## Milestones
                1. A weekly hours figure based on a real week of your diary, not an ideal one.
                2. A yearly spending limit for courses, books, tools and exams.
                3. Employer training budget, study leave and subscriptions checked and written down.
                4. The total hours available before the readiness date calculated.

                ## Notes
                Three focused hours a week for a year is roughly 150 hours, enough to move two or three skills by a full level. If your real figure is under two hours, read the project for parents with two hours a week.
              priority: medium
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "The plan records a weekly hours figure, a yearly spending limit and the employer funds available, with total hours to the readiness date."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Look at last week's diary and find the hours you could have protected"
                - "Set a yearly spending limit for learning"
                - "Ask HR what training budget, study leave and subscriptions you can use"
                - "Recheck the learning budget when the new financial year starts @recurring(yearly)"
            - name: One-page twelve-month skills gap plan
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Everything gathered so far, the target role, the three gaps, the hours and the budget, is only useful if it fits on a page you will actually reopen. A one-page plan with a quarter-by-quarter sequence and a named way of closing each gap becomes the document every monthly check-in and manager conversation works from.

                ## Milestones
                1. The target role, readiness date and three priority gaps at the top of one page.
                2. For each gap, the method of closing it: course, stretch work, practice project or coaching.
                3. A quarter-by-quarter sequence so no more than two gaps are active at once.
                4. The plan saved where you will see it weekly and shared with your manager or a trusted person.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Development plan** template. Put the hardest gap in the quarter with the fewest known work peaks.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:

                cadence: one-shot

            - name: Protected weekly learning block
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Learning that waits for a free evening does not happen; learning in a recurring diary slot usually does. A fixed block of 90 minutes to three hours each week, defended like a meeting, is the single habit that decides whether the gap plan moves.

                ## Milestones
                1. A recurring block in your diary at a time you have kept free for at least three of the last four weeks.
                2. A clear rule for what happens if the block is lost, such as moving it within the same week.
                3. Eight consecutive weeks with the block used or moved, not skipped.
                4. A one-line note at the end of each block saying what it produced.

                ## Notes
                Early mornings and lunch breaks survive better than evenings for most people. Tell colleagues what the block is for; it is easier to protect when people know.
              priority: high
              frontmatter:
                mode: operating
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "The weekly learning block has been used or moved, not skipped, in eight consecutive weeks, each with a note of what it produced."
                cadence: rolling
              tasks:
                - "Book a recurring 90-minute block in your diary"
                - "Decide where the block moves to if a meeting takes it"
                - "Use the weekly learning block on the current priority gap @recurring(weekly:tue)"
                - "Write one line on what this week's block produced @recurring(weekly:fri)"
            - name: Practice log for the skills you are building
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Hours spent reading about a skill feel like progress but rarely show up at work; repetitions with feedback do. Logging each practice session, what you did, how it went and what to try next, turns vague effort into a record of real attempts and shows when a method is not working.

                ## Milestones
                1. A simple log with columns for date, skill, what you practised, result and next attempt.
                2. At least one logged practice session a week for the active gaps.
                3. A monthly count of practice sessions per skill.
                4. One method dropped or changed because the log showed it was not producing results.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Metrics log** template. Count real attempts, a drafted report, a solved problem, a mock conversation, not minutes of video watched.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: operating
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "A practice log with at least one entry a week for the active gaps over three months, and one method changed because of what it showed."
                cadence: rolling
              tasks:
                - "Set up a log with date, skill, attempt, result and next step columns"
                - "Log every practice attempt for the active gaps @recurring(weekly:thu)"
                - "Count sessions per skill at the end of each month"
                - "Change any method that has produced no visible result in a month"

            - name: Quarterly re-rating of the skills matrix
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Skills move slowly enough that monthly ratings are noise, but a quarter is long enough to see a real shift or the lack of one. Re-rating the whole matrix every three months, with fresh evidence for any rise, tells you whether the plan is working and when a gap can be marked closed.

                ## Milestones
                1. The full matrix re-rated each quarter using the same four-level scale.
                2. Every rise in rating backed by a piece of evidence from that quarter.
                3. Closed gaps moved off the active list and replaced from the ranked list.
                4. A one-line summary of the quarter's movement kept at the top of the matrix.

                ## Notes
                Keep old ratings in their own columns rather than overwriting them; a year of columns is useful evidence for a promotion case or interview.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: operating
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "Four quarterly re-ratings recorded in separate columns, each rise backed by evidence and closed gaps replaced from the ranked list."
                cadence: cyclic
              tasks:
                - "Add a new dated column to the skills matrix"
                - "Re-rate every skill in the matrix with fresh evidence @recurring(quarterly)"
                - "Move closed gaps off the active list and pull in the next"
                - "Write a one-line summary of the quarter's movement"
            - name: Monthly feedback request on one skill
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                General requests for feedback get general answers; asking one person about one skill after a specific piece of work gets something you can use. A monthly request, rotated across the active gaps and across different colleagues, gives you outside evidence of movement without making anyone feel surveyed.

                ## Milestones
                1. One specific feedback request sent each month about one skill and one piece of work.
                2. Requests rotated across at least three different people over a quarter.
                3. Each answer logged with the date, person and one action taken from it.
                4. A thank-you or follow-up sent to everyone who replied.

                ## Notes
                Two questions work well: what worked in this piece, and what one thing would have made it better for you.
              priority: medium

                - "Log the answer with one action you will take"

            - name: Reading and listening queue for the target role
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                People already in the target role read different things from you: trade publications, standards, a few respected books, the newsletters their peers pass round.                 ## Milestones

                3. One item from the queue finished each month and three takeaways noted.
                4. Items that turned out not to help removed without guilt.

            - name: Deliberate practice set-up for any new skill
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Experts improve through short, hard repetitions with fast feedback, while most adults learn by consuming material and hoping it sticks. Learning the basic structure of deliberate practice, a narrow sub-skill, a clear standard, immediate feedback and repetition, makes every hour on the gap plan count for more.

                ## Milestones
                1. One priority gap broken into three to five narrow sub-skills.
                2. A standard for each sub-skill taken from a strong example of real work.
                3. A feedback source for each, such as a checklist, an answer key or a reviewer.
                4. Four weeks of short practice sessions on one sub-skill, with results logged.

                ## Notes
                Twenty minutes on the part you are worst at beats two hours on the whole skill. If a session feels comfortable, it is probably not practice.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: learning
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "One gap is broken into sub-skills with standards and feedback sources, and four weeks of logged practice on one sub-skill are complete."
                cadence: phased
                effort_hours_estimate: "8"
              tasks:
                - "Break your top gap into three to five narrow sub-skills"
                - "Find one strong real example to set the standard for each"
                - "Choose a feedback source for each sub-skill"
                - "Run four weeks of twenty-minute sessions on the weakest sub-skill"
            - name: Clear writing for work documents
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Almost every step up asks for more writing that other people act on: proposals, decision papers, status updates and emails to senior people. A six-week sprint on putting the conclusion first, cutting length by a third and structuring for a skim reader is one of the highest-return gaps to close, because the evidence is visible in everything you send.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three recent documents of yours reviewed for where the main point appears.
                2. A short checklist of five writing rules you will apply.
                3. Six documents rewritten or written fresh with the checklist, before and after versions kept.
                4. Feedback from one reader on whether the new versions were faster to act on.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: learning
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "Six work documents written with a five-rule checklist, with before and after versions kept and one reader's feedback recorded."
                cadence: phased
                effort_hours_estimate: "10"
              tasks:
                - "Mark where the main point appears in three recent documents you wrote"
                - "Write a checklist of five rules, starting with conclusion first"

# Career Skills Gap Plan

This area is for anyone who can name the job they want next and suspects they are not quite ready for it, whether that is one step up, a sideways move or a role that does not exist at your employer yet. It starts with the foundations (a target role, real adverts, an honest rating of yourself checked by other people, and a ranked gap list turned into a one-page plan), then the weekly and monthly machinery that keeps practice happening, then specific skills most target roles ask for, the decisions about courses, credentials and stretch work, the dated checkpoints, versions for particular lives and, last, the work of people who run competency models and gap analyses for others.

What repeats is kept small on purpose: a weekly learning block and practice log, a monthly plan check-in, a monthly feedback request and development slot with your manager, a quarterly re-rating of your skills and a quarterly look at fresh adverts, and a yearly check that the target role is still the right one. The Skills matrix, Development plan, Metrics log, Reading queue and Course templates pair with the projects that use them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
