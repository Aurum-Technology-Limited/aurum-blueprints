                ## Purpose
                Asking someone to be your mentor without saying what for puts all the work on them, and most busy people quietly decline. Writing down the two or three career questions you are actually stuck on, such as whether to specialise, how to get your first management role or which master's to take, tells you what kind of mentor to look for and gives them a reason to say yes.

                3. A time horizon noted for each question, such as this term, this year or the next three years.
                4. One question chosen as the focus for your first mentor.

                ## Notes

            - name: First approach message to a prospective mentor
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                "Will you be my mentor?" asks a stranger to commit to an open-ended relationship before they know you, and most will say no. A short message that explains the connection, asks for a single 20 to 30 minute conversation about one specific question and makes it easy to decline gets far more replies, and good mentoring usually grows out of that first conversation.

                ## Milestones

                3. A polite follow-up written for anyone who has not replied after ten working days.
                4. At least one first conversation booked.

                ## Notes
                Avoid the word "mentor" in the first message. Ask for advice on one thing, offer to fit around their diary and thank them whatever they answer.

# Finding a Career Mentor

Finding a career mentor covers the whole route from deciding what you want advice on to keeping a handful of people in your corner for years, whether you are a student looking for a first mentor or an experienced professional who has never had one. The projects start with foundations (your goals, the people you already know, a shortlist and a first approach), then the routines that make mentoring work, the skills of asking, listening and weighing advice, and the decisions about agreements, trials and endings. Later projects cover dated sessions and programmes, situations such as alumni schemes, career changers, first-generation professionals and the self-employed, and finish with a personal board of advisers and taking on a mentee of your own.

The rhythms are a weekly outreach slot while you are searching, a weekly look at open actions from your mentors' advice, a monthly progress note, a quarterly relationship check and a yearly review of who is in your corner. The **Development plan** template suits your mentoring goals, the **Person** template suits each mentor's record, **Meeting notes** suits every session and **Purchase decision** suits weighing a paid mentoring platform. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
