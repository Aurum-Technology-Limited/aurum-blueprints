            - name: Expectations conversation with your new manager
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most failed starts trace back to a gap between what the manager expected and what the new hire thought they were hired to do. Asking directly in the first fortnight what success looks like at 90 days and at one year, how they like to be updated and what they would worry about if they were you closes that gap while it is still cheap to close.

                ## Milestones
                1. A meeting of at least 45 minutes held in the first two weeks with expectations as the only agenda.
                2. Your manager's view of success at 90 days and at one year written in their words.
                3. Preferred update style, frequency and channel agreed.
                4. The two or three things your manager would not want you to change yet noted.
                5. A short summary emailed back to your manager and confirmed.

                Start from the **1:1** template. Sending the summary back is the step people skip, and it is the one that protects you if expectations shift later.

# First 90 Days in New Role

The first 90 days in a new role set the story people tell about you for the next two years, and most of that story is written by how well you learn, who you meet and what you choose to fix first. The projects start with foundations (a pre-start brief, day-one logistics, the expectations conversation with your manager, a written 30-60-90 day plan and a stakeholder map), then the weekly routines that carry the ramp-up, the skills of learning a business fast, the decisions about early wins and what to change, and the 30, 60 and 90-day checkpoints. Later projects cover situations such as inheriting a team, starting remotely, following a popular predecessor or starting around caring duties, and finish with the work of senior leaders taking on a function or an executive post.

The rhythms are a five-minute daily learning note, weekly reviews of the plan, the question list and your workload, two introduction conversations a week, and a monthly progress note to your manager. The **Onboarding 30/60/90** template carries the plan, the **1:1** template suits manager and team conversations, **Person** suits key stakeholder profiles, **Meeting notes** suits the checkpoint reviews and **Feedback themes** suits the week-six feedback round. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
