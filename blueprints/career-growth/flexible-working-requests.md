                Most employers publish a flexible working policy that sets out who can apply, the form to use, who decides and how long they have to answer, and many people make an informal request without ever reading it.             - name: Your legal right to request flexible working
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Many countries give employees a statutory right to ask for a change to hours, times or place of work, and the rules differ on who qualifies, how often you can ask and what the employer must do before refusing. In the UK, for example, the right applies from the first day of employment and employers must consult before saying no. Knowing your own country's version means you can make a request that carries legal weight rather than a favour.

                ## Milestones
                1. The official government or labour ministry guidance for your country found and saved.
                2. Whether you qualify, and under what conditions, written in a sentence.
                3. The number of requests allowed in a period, the employer's response deadline and any appeal step noted.
                4. The business reasons an employer may lawfully give for refusal listed, if your country defines them.
                5. A free advice service, union contact or employment adviser identified for questions.

                ## Notes
                This project records the rules; it does not decide whether a refusal was lawful. For that, speak to an employment adviser, your union or a lawyer qualified in your country.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A saved note states whether you qualify for a statutory request in your country, the response deadline, the request limit and one named source of advice."
                cadence: one-shot

              tasks:

# Flexible Working Requests

This area is for anyone who needs their working week to bend: a parent fitting work around school hours, a carer whose week is shaped by appointments, or a knowledge worker who wants a compressed week or a day kept clear for study. It starts with the groundwork (your policy and legal right to ask, a time audit, the pattern you need and what it does to pay and pension), moves through writing the request, the business case, the trial proposal and the meetings and decisions that follow, then the routines that keep an arrangement working, the skills of doing a full role in fewer hours, the situations parents, carers, managers and new joiners face, and finishes with job shares, annualised hours, four-day week pilots and keeping progression on track.

What repeats is the machinery of a pattern that works: a weekly plan on your last working day, a weekly team availability note, a monthly delivery log and manager check-in, a monthly look at actual hours against contracted ones, quarterly reviews of the arrangement and the school holiday calendar, and a yearly decision on whether the pattern still fits. The Meeting notes, 1:1, Metrics log and Operational checklist templates pair with the projects that point at them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
