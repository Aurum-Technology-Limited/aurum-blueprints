              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Somewhere around the senior level most people are nudged toward management, often because it looks like the only way up. Making the choice on purpose, by comparing what you enjoy on a good week with what managers and staff engineers actually spend their days on, stops you drifting into a role you did not want or refusing one out of habit.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of the ten tasks from the last two months that gave you the most energy, and the five that drained it.
                2. Thirty minutes each with one manager and one staff or principal person, asking what fills their week.
                3. A written comparison of both paths against your own list, with the trade-offs named.
                4. A decision recorded, with the conditions under which you would revisit it.

                ## Notes
                Choosing the IC track is not permanent. Many strong staff engineers have managed for a few years; the point is to choose the next two or three years, not the rest of your life.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A one-page written decision for the next two to three years exists, citing the energy list and both conversations, with a revisit condition."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "5"
              tasks:
                - "List the tasks from the last two months that energised and drained you"
                - "Ask one manager and one staff engineer what fills a typical week"
                - "Write a one-page comparison of both paths against your list"
                - "Record the decision and the condition that would make you revisit it"

# Individual Contributor Career Path

This area is for senior engineers and other senior specialists, analysts, designers, researchers and architects, who want to keep growing on the technical track toward staff and principal roles instead of switching to people management. It starts with the foundations (choosing the track, reading the ladder, picking an archetype and agreeing direction with your manager), then the weekly and monthly machinery of writing, reviewing, mentoring and relationship building, the skills of design documents, strategy and influence without authority, the decisions about scope and commitments, the dated moments such as a staff project kickoff or a strategy presentation, versions for particular situations and, last, the work principal-level people do for the whole organisation.

What repeats is a weekly writing block, a weekly design review slot and calendar plan, monthly time audits, mentoring, peer conversations and incident reading, and quarterly checks on scope, technical bets and peer feedback, with a yearly refresh of the ladder, your vision and your long-range plan. The Skills matrix, Development plan, 1:1, Reading queue and Feedback themes templates pair with the projects that use them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
