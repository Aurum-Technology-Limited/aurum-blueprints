            - name: Reading the internal mobility policy properly
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most employers set rules for internal moves that nobody reads until an application is rejected: a minimum time in the current role, a recent performance rating, whether your manager must be told before you apply, and how long the old team can hold you after an offer. Reading the policy in one sitting and writing down the five rules that apply to you prevents the most common and most avoidable internal transfer failures.

                ## Milestones
                1. The current internal mobility or transfer policy found on the intranet or obtained from HR.
                2. Your eligibility on tenure and performance rating confirmed in writing.
                3. The rule on when your current manager must be told noted, with the exact wording.
                4. The standard notice or release period between teams recorded.
                5. Any open questions sent to your HR business partner and answered.

                ## Notes
                Policies often differ by country, level or business unit. Check you are reading the version that covers your contract, not a global summary.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A one-page note of the mobility rules that apply to you, including eligibility, manager notification and release period, with HR's answers to any open questions."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Search the intranet for the internal mobility or transfer policy"
                - "Check the tenure and performance rating needed to apply"
                - "Note the exact rule on telling your current manager"
                - "Email your HR business partner any question the policy leaves open"
            - name: Push and pull reasons for wanting to move
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                People who move to escape a team often land in a similar problem one floor up, while people who move toward something specific tend to stay and grow. Writing down what is pushing you out and what is pulling you toward a new team, and testing each reason against whether a move would really fix it, keeps the search honest and gives you a clean answer when a hiring manager asks why.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of push reasons, such as workload, manager fit or a stalled domain, each in one line.
                2. A list of pull reasons, such as a skill, a product area or a level, each in one line.
                3. Each reason marked as fixable in place or needing a move.
                4. A two-sentence answer to "why do you want to move" that is true and forward-looking.

                ## Notes
                If most reasons are fixable where you are, look at the job crafting area before applying anywhere. A move made only to escape uses up goodwill you may need later.
              priority: high

                mode: research
                output_kind: decision

            - name: Telling your manager you are exploring a move
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                In most companies your manager finds out about an internal application within days, and hearing it from someone else damages trust far more than hearing it from you. Planning a calm conversation that frames the move around growth, not complaint, and offering to protect current work, usually turns a manager from a blocker into a reference, and sometimes into an advocate.

                ## Milestones
                1. The policy rule on manager notification checked before the conversation.
                2. Three talking points written: what you want to grow into, why it cannot happen here, and how you will protect current work.
                3. The conversation held in a private one-to-one, not on chat or in a corridor.
                4. Agreed next steps and anything your manager offered written down the same day.

                ## Notes
                Expect one of three reactions: support, a counter-proposal to keep you, or quiet resistance. Decide in advance what you would say to a counter-proposal so you are not negotiating on the spot.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: event-completion
                success_criteria: "A private conversation with your manager about the move has taken place, with agreed next steps written down within a day."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Write three talking points framing the move around growth"
                - "Decide how you would answer a counter-proposal to stay"
                - "Book a private one-to-one with your manager for the conversation"
                - "Write up what was agreed within a day of the meeting"

# Internal Transfer & Role Change

Moving inside the same employer looks easier than changing jobs, but it has its own rules: a policy on eligibility, a current manager who usually hears first, hiring managers who already know your reputation, and two teams that both want the same weeks of your time. The projects start with foundations (the mobility policy, your reasons for moving, a map of target teams, your current standing and the conversation with your manager), then the routines that run an internal search, the skills of learning a new team's domain and interviewing as an insider, the decisions about level, pay band, timing and secondments, and the dated events of interviews, announcement, handover and the first months on the new team. Later projects cover situations for engineers and knowledge workers switching team, function or office, moves after a reorganisation, and finish with proposing a role that does not exist yet, overseas entity transfers and a multi-move career plan.

The rhythms are a weekly scan of internal openings, a weekly guard on current-role delivery, a weekly search block, monthly coffees across the organisation, monthly contributions to the target team's work, quarterly check-ins with HR and your skip-level manager, and a yearly look at the talent review calendar. The **Resume** template suits the internal CV, **1:1** suits the HR and skip-level conversations, **Skills matrix** suits a function switch, **Operational checklist** carries the handover and **Onboarding 30/60/90** carries the first months on the new team. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
