            - name: Reasons and shape of a late-career pivot
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most pivots after 50 start from a push (a restructure, a new boss, fatigue) rather than a pull, and a pivot built only on escape tends to land in a similar job with less pay. Writing down what you want more of, what you want less of and roughly how many more working years you expect gives every later decision in this area something to be checked against.

                ## Milestones
                1. A short list of what you want more of and less of in work, ranked.
                2. The push and pull reasons separated, with the strongest pull named.
                3. A rough working horizon stated, such as five years full time then part time.
                4. A one-paragraph pivot statement saved where you will reread it each quarter.

                ## Notes
                Keep the statement honest rather than impressive. It is for you, not for a cover letter.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A one-paragraph pivot statement exists naming the main pull reason, what must change and an expected working horizon in years."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Spend twenty minutes listing what you want more of and less of in work"
                - "Mark each reason as a push away or a pull towards"
                - "Write how many more years you expect to work and in what shape"
                - "Draft a one-paragraph pivot statement and save it"

# Late-Career Pivot (50+)

Late-career pivot covers reshaping working life after 50, whether that means a new employed role, a phased step down, consulting on what you already know, a part-time bridge job or an encore career that matters more than the pay. The projects start with foundations (your reasons, the pension and benefits position, a minimum earnings figure, the household conversation and an inventory of thirty years of experience), then the weekly and monthly routines that keep a pivot moving, the skills of an age-aware search, and the decisions about route, money, health cover and age bias. Later projects cover the dated moments, situations such as caring for a parent, a redundancy offer or unretiring, and finish with fractional executive work, expert advisory roles and passing your knowledge on.

The rhythms are a Tuesday pivot session, a Friday look at the lead pipeline, a Thursday hour on a current tool, a monthly reconnection with former colleagues, a monthly income and pension check, a quarterly progress review and a yearly birthday check. The **Resume** template suits the rebuilt CV, **Course** suits a refresh credential, **Monthly budget and bills** suits the lower-earning budget and **Metrics log** suits the energy log. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
