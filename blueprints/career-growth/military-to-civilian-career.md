            - name: Transition timeline counted back from your exit date
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most service leavers say afterwards that they started too late: resettlement entitlements, courses and job applications all have lead times, and the final months fill up with out-processing. Fixing your exit date and counting back from it, with the last date for each entitlement, course booking and paperwork step, turns a vague sense of time left into a plan you can work to. Start 12 to 24 months out if you can, and today if you cannot.

                ## Milestones
                1. Your confirmed or expected exit date written at the top of a single timeline, with a note on how firm it is.
                2. Every booking deadline for resettlement entitlements, courses and paperwork placed on the timeline in date order.
                3. Three phases marked with start dates: preparation, active job search and final out-processing.
                4. The timeline shared with your partner or a trusted friend so someone else knows the key dates.

                ## Notes
                If your exit date might move, plan to the earliest likely date. Leaving with time to spare costs nothing; a course you could not book in time is simply gone.
              priority: high

# Military to Civilian Career

Leaving the armed forces means changing job, employer, home, routine and often identity at the same moment, and most of what goes wrong is predictable: paperwork left behind, entitlements unused, experience nobody outside understands. These projects follow the order a good transition runs in: foundations such as the timeline, records, money, housing, healthcare and a translated record of what you did, then the weekly and monthly machinery of the search, the skills of telling your story to civilians, the big decisions on route, place and pension, the dated events from placements to discharge week, variants for short-service leavers, senior officers, senior NCOs, medical discharges, partners and late restarters, and finally the specialist work of contracting, business and helping the next cohort.

The rhythms that repeat are a weekly search session with Friday follow-ups, weekly veteran outreach and a Sunday wellbeing check, monthly checkpoints on the plan, the savings runway and resettlement entitlements, a monthly mentoring session, and quarterly and yearly credential checks. The Savings goal, Skills matrix, Resume, Meeting notes and Onboarding 30/60/90 templates pair with the runway, the role inventory, the civilian CV, veteran conversations and the first months in a civilian job. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
