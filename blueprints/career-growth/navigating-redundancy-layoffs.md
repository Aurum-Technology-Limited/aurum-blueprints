            - name: First 72 hours after a redundancy notice
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The day the notice lands is the day people sign things, post angry messages or tell nobody at all, and each of those can cost money or options later. Spending the first three days on a short, fixed list (write down what was said, ask for everything in writing, check the deadlines, sleep on any signature) keeps every choice open while the shock settles.

                ## Milestones
                1. Notes of the notice meeting written the same day, with who was present and what dates were given.
                2. A written request sent to HR for the redundancy letter, the proposed terms and any consultation timetable.
                3. Every deadline in the letter listed with its date, including any date to accept or sign.
                4. Nothing signed and nothing posted publicly until the terms have been read in full.

                ## Notes
                You can usually ask for time to consider terms. A polite email saying you will respond by a stated date is enough, and it is rarely refused.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 7
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "Within three days of the notice there is a dated meeting note, a sent request for written terms and a list of every deadline."

                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "Write down everything said in the notice meeting while you still remember it"
                - "Email HR asking for the redundancy letter and proposed terms in writing"
                - "List every date and deadline mentioned in the letter"
                - "Tell one trusted person outside work what has happened today"

# Navigating Redundancy & Layoffs

Navigating redundancy and layoffs covers everything from the moment a role is put at risk to the first months of the next job, written for anyone who has just had the meeting, or suspects it is coming. The projects start with foundations (the first 72 hours, your contract terms, a paper trail, cash runway and benefits), then the routines that hold a search week together, the knowledge that makes consultation and severance talks fairer, the decisions about alternative roles, voluntary terms and severance money, and the dated events from the consultation meeting to the final payslip. Later projects cover particular situations, such as parental leave, a work visa, being close to retirement or staying on after a cut, and finish with managers and employee representatives on the other side of the table.

The rhythms are a weekly search plan and review, a weekly wellbeing note and evidence log, a monthly runway and spending check, and a quarterly look at your readiness kit and your employer's warning signs. The **Monthly budget and bills** template suits the essentials budget, the **Metrics log** template suits the runway review, the **Habit tracker** template suits the weekday structure and **Meeting notes** suits every consultation meeting. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
