            - name: Board ambition statement and fit test
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                People who go after a board seat because it sounds senior tend to end up on the wrong board, or on the right board for the wrong reasons, and nominations committees notice within minutes. Writing down why you want a seat, what you would bring, what you hope to learn and which causes or sectors you care about gives every later application a spine and stops you saying yes to the first offer.

                ## Milestones
                1. A half-page statement of why you want a board role, written in your own words.
                2. Three things you would bring to a board and two things you want to learn, each with an example.
                3. A list of causes, sectors or organisation types you would and would not serve.
                4. A two-sentence board pitch you can say aloud without notes.

                ## Notes
                Test the statement on someone who already sits on a board. If they cannot tell what kind of board you are suited to, the statement is still too general.
              priority: medium

            - name: Due diligence before accepting a board seat

# Non-Executive Board Roles

Non-executive board roles covers the whole route from wanting a seat to holding one well: trustee boards, school and college governing bodies, public bodies, advisory boards and company boards. The projects start with foundations (your motive, employer permission, time budget, board CV, duties and reading accounts), move through finding and accepting a seat with proper due diligence, then the routines and committee skills of a sitting director, and finish with situations for the working executive and the retiree, dated board events and the specialist work of chairing, mergers and listed boards.

The rhythms are a weekly scan of board vacancies, a monthly network conversation and board pack reading block, quarterly catch-ups with the chair and chief executive and a risk register review, and a yearly round of interest declarations, board evaluation, accounts sign-off and the AGM. The **Board meeting** template suits each meeting cycle, **Risk register** suits risk oversight, **Resume** suits the board CV, **Skills matrix** suits your experience inventory, **Onboarding 30/60/90** suits induction and **Development plan** suits your director development. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
