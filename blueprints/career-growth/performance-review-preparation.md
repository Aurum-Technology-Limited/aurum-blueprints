                Most review surprises come from not knowing the process: the self-assessment closes earlier than expected, peer nominations shut a week before that, and calibration happens before your manager has read anything.             - name: Writing a self-assessment that leads with impact
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most self-assessments list activities: ran the migration, attended the steering group, wrote the docs. Calibrators reward results: what changed for the customer, the team or the business because of the work. Learning to write each point as result first, then how you did it, is the single biggest improvement in most reviews.

                ## Milestones
                1. Five strong and five weak example statements from real reviews compared.
                2. A personal formula written: result, how you achieved it, who benefited.
                3. Ten of your own activity statements rewritten as result statements.
                4. One rewritten paragraph reviewed by a trusted colleague.

                ## Notes
                Lead each paragraph with the outcome in plain words. Put the method and the effort in the second sentence, not the first.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: learning
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "Ten of your own statements rewritten in result-first form, with at least one paragraph reviewed by a colleague."
                cadence: phased
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:
                - "Write down five statements from your last review that describe activity"
                - "Rewrite each one starting with the result it produced"
                - "Ask a trusted colleague to read one rewritten paragraph cold"
                - "Keep the before and after pairs as a reference for future reviews"
            - name: Quantifying results without perfect data
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Many good results have no tidy metric: a problem that stopped recurring, a decision made faster, a team that stopped escalating. Learning a few honest ways to put scale on them, such as hours saved, people affected, incidents avoided or time to decision, makes them count in calibration without inventing numbers.

                ## Milestones
                1. Five results from this period with no obvious metric identified.
                2. A reasonable, honest measure found for each, with its source or estimate method noted.
                3. Any estimate labelled as an estimate with its assumptions.
                4. The quantified versions ready to drop into the self-assessment.

                ## Notes
                Never round up or claim the whole team's result as yours. A modest number you can defend beats a large one you cannot.
              priority: medium

            - name: Taking critical feedback in the review meeting
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                When a manager raises something you did not expect, people argue, go quiet or say something they regret. Practising a simple response, asking for an example, summarising what you heard and asking for time to reflect, keeps the meeting useful and the relationship intact.

                3. A plan to write feedback down during the meeting rather than debate it.
                4. A follow-up step decided for any feedback you disagree with.

                ## Notes

# Performance Review Preparation

This area is for anyone who has sat down the night before a self-assessment deadline and tried to remember what they did in March. It starts with foundations (the cycle's dates and form, last year's goals, an evidence sweep and an expectations conversation), then the monthly and quarterly routines that keep a review from being a scramble, the writing and feedback skills reviews reward, the decisions about ratings and next-year goals, the review meetings themselves, the situations that complicate a cycle such as leave, a new manager or a performance plan, and finally the specialist work of senior engineers and long-serving professionals.

What repeats is a monthly goal check and metrics snapshot, a review item on your one-to-one agenda, a feedback request to one colleague a month, a quarterly mini self-review and the yearly run-up to review season. The Feedback themes, 1:1, Metrics log, Development plan and Meeting notes templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
