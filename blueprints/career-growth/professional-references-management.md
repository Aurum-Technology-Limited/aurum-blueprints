            - name: Referee inventory from the last ten years
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before choosing anyone, list everyone who could speak about your work: line managers, skip-level leaders, project sponsors, peers, direct reports and clients from roughly the last ten years. Most people can name two referees from memory and twelve once they work through each role in turn, and the longer list is what saves you when a favourite referee is on leave the week an offer lands.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every role from the last ten years listed with dates, employer and title, matching your current CV.
                2. For each role, at least two people named who saw your work closely, with their relationship to you.
                3. Each name marked as still in touch, lapsed or unknown.
                4. A short list of the eight to twelve strongest candidates picked out from the full inventory.

                ## Notes
                Include people you managed and clients. Many hiring processes now ask for a peer or a direct report as well as a manager, and they are the hardest to find in a hurry.
              priority: high

            - name: Choosing your core three referees
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Three referees is the number most employers ask for, and the usual mix is your current or most recent manager, a previous manager and one person who saw a different side of you, such as a client or a peer. Choosing them deliberately, rather than whoever answered last time, means the people speaking for you are recent, senior enough to be credible and genuinely positive.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each shortlisted candidate scored on recency, closeness to your work, seniority and how warmly they would speak.
                2. Three core referees chosen, with at least one line manager among them.
                3. Two reserves named for when a core referee is unavailable or unsuitable for a particular role.
                4. The reason for each choice written in one sentence.

                ## Notes
                A senior name who barely knew you is weaker than a middle manager who can describe three things you did. Checkers ask for specifics.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "Three core referees and two reserves are recorded, each with a one-sentence reason and at least one line manager in the core three."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Score your shortlist on recency, closeness, seniority and warmth"
                - "Pick three core referees including at least one line manager"
                - "Name two reserves and note which kinds of role each suits"
                - "Write one sentence on why each person made the list"
            - name: Asking permission from each referee
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Naming someone who has not agreed is the fastest way to get a cautious or confused reference, and a few people will say yes out of politeness when they would rather not. Asking each person directly, with an easy way to decline, tells you who is truly willing and how they prefer to be contacted.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each core referee and reserve asked personally by call, video or a short individual message.
                2. A clear yes or no recorded from each, with any conditions they set.
                3. Each referee's preferred contact method and best times to be called noted.
                4. Anyone who hesitated thanked and quietly moved off the list.

                ## Notes
                Give an exit in the ask itself, for example: if you would not feel able to speak in detail, that is completely fine. A warm no is far better than a lukewarm yes.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21

            - name: Referee privacy and consent ground rules
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Referees are trusting you with their contact details and their name, and data protection rules in many countries expect you to handle both with care. A few written rules about when you share their details, with whom and how you warn them stop the most common annoyance referees report, which is a call from a stranger they were never told about.

                ## Milestones
                1. A rule that referee details are shared only after an offer or a final stage, unless a referee agrees otherwise.
                2. A rule that each referee is told the employer, role and likely timing before any contact.
                3. Agreed handling for referees who want to give a written reference only, or calls only.
                4. Referee details removed from old applications, shared folders and job sites where they are no longer needed.

                ## Notes
                Many application forms ask for referees at the very first stage. It is usually acceptable to write that details will be provided at offer stage, and most employers accept it.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A written set of at least four rules on sharing referee details exists, and referee details are removed from every old online profile or form you can reach."
                cadence: one-shot

              tasks:

                - "Search old job site profiles for referee details and delete them"

            - name: One-page reminder of your work for each referee
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Even a referee who rates you highly will struggle to recall the details of a project from four years ago when a checker asks for an example. A one-page reminder for each person, listing what you worked on together, the results and two or three stories they witnessed, turns a vague reference into a specific one.

                ## Milestones
                1. A page per core referee with dates, your role, the projects you shared and measurable results.
                2. Two or three short stories each referee saw first hand, written in plain language.
                3. The strengths you would like each referee to speak to, matched to what they actually saw.
                4. Each page sent to its referee with an offer to correct anything they remember differently.

                ## Notes
                Keep it factual and modest. Referees are uncomfortable repeating claims they did not witness, and checkers can tell when a referee is reading your script.
              priority: high

# Professional References Management

This area is for the people who will vouch for you when an offer depends on it, and for the quiet work that keeps them willing and ready. It starts with the foundations (an inventory of past managers, choosing a core three, asking properly, contact details checked and a one-page reminder of your work together for each), moves into the machinery of briefing before every application, logging who has been called and keeping in touch, then the skills of matching referees to roles and preparing them for awkward questions, decisions about bad or missing references, dated events such as screening and vetting, situations from a first job to a closed employer or an exit that went badly, and ends with executive backchannels, writing references for your own team and a long-term referee succession plan.

What repeats is light: a weekly briefing check while you are actively applying, a monthly usage log and job-move check, a quarterly keep-in-touch note and summary refresh, and a yearly review of the whole list. The Person template suits each referee's record, Meeting notes suits the briefing call and Operational checklist suits the week your references are actually taken. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
