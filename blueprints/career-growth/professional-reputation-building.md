            - name: Three-word reputation baseline from colleagues
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before deciding what you want to be known for, find out what you are known for already. Asking six to eight people who have worked with you for the three words they would use to describe you to a stranger gives an honest starting point, and the gap between their words and your hopes is the work of this whole area.

                ## Milestones
                1. Six to eight people chosen from different directions: a manager, peers, a client or customer, and someone more junior.
                2. At least five sets of three words received in writing.
                3. The words grouped into recurring themes, with any surprises noted.
                4. A one-paragraph summary of how you are seen now, dated and kept for comparison next year.

                ## Notes
                Ask by private message rather than in a meeting, and make it easy to be honest: say you want plain words, not compliments. Thank everyone, whatever they say.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A dated summary of how you are currently seen, built from at least five written three-word answers from people who know your work."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "List eight people who have seen your work from different angles"
                - "Send each a short private message asking for three words they would use about you"
                - "Group the answers into themes in a single note"
                - "Write a dated paragraph on how you are seen now and how it differs from what you want"

            - name: Choosing the one thing to be known for
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                People remember a person for one thing, rarely three, so a vague reputation as good at lots of things is usually no reputation at all. Picking a specialism that sits where your experience, your interest and real demand in your industry overlap, and writing it as one sentence, gives every later piece of work something to point at.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three candidate specialisms written down, each with the evidence you already have for it.
                2. Each candidate checked for demand: who asks for this, and who is already known for it.
                3. One specialism chosen and written as a single sentence a colleague could repeat.
                4. The sentence tested on two trusted people and adjusted after their reaction.

                ## Notes
                Narrow is fine. Known for pricing strategy in subscription software travels further than known for strategy, and you can widen later once the narrow version sticks.

# Professional Reputation Building

Professional reputation is what people in your field say about you when you are not in the room, and this area is about making that sentence specific and true. The projects start with foundations (an honest baseline, a search of your own name, the one thing you want to be known for, the audience whose opinion matters and the policy limits on what you can publish), then the routines that keep you reliable and visible, the skills of writing and explaining your expertise, decisions about channels and invitations, and dated pieces such as an award entry or a flagship guide. Later projects cover particular situations, from confidential work and limited time to a new country or a public mistake, and finish with original research, a book proposal and committee roles in your field.

The rhythms are a weekly commitments check and publishing slot, monthly routines for ideas, helping others in public, mentions, relationship upkeep and feedback, a quarterly reputation review and a yearly plan and name search. The **Content pipeline** template suits the publishing slot, **Metrics log** suits the quarterly review, **Feedback themes** suits what people say after visible work, and **Portfolio piece** suits presenting creative work with its process. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
