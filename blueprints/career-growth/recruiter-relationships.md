            - name: How agency, in-house and retained recruiters differ
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most frustration with recruiters comes from not knowing how they are paid. A contingency agency recruiter earns a fee, often a percentage of first-year salary, only if you are hired and stay past a rebate period, an in-house recruiter is salaried by the employer, and a retained search firm is paid up front to fill one role. Knowing which kind is calling tells you how much time they will invest in you and whose interests they serve.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-paragraph note in your own words on contingency, retained, in-house and outsourced recruitment.
                2. Each recruiter you already know labelled by type.
                3. A list of the three questions you will ask any new recruiter about how they are engaged on a role.
                4. One conversation held with a recruiter you trust about how their fees and rebate periods work in your sector.

                ## Notes
                Fees are paid by the employer, never by you. A recruiter who asks a permanent-role candidate for money is a warning sign.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: learning
                output_kind: knowledge

# Recruiter Relationships

Recruiter Relationships is about turning the stream of agency calls and inbox approaches into three to five specialist recruiters who understand what you want, put you forward only with your consent and tell you the truth about the market. The projects start with foundations (how the industry is paid, a one-page briefing sheet, a contact register, a mapped and shortlisted set of specialists, a consent rule and scam checks), then the routines that keep those relationships warm, the skills for pay questions, job briefs and feedback, the decisions about exclusivity, going direct and contract work, the moments around a search, situations for engineers, confidential movers, early-career people and hiring managers, and finally specialist work such as talent pools, an advisory circle and learning how recruiters search.

What repeats is a Friday triage of inbound messages, monthly check-ins with your core recruiters and a monthly update of the submission register, a quarterly prune of the list and a quarterly read of the market feedback, and yearly pay benchmarks, status notes and a review of who holds your data. The **Person** template pairs with each core recruiter's record, **Metrics log** with the market feedback log and **Meeting notes** with interview debrief calls. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
