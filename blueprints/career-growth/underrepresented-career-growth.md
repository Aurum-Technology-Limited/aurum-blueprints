            - name: Career position audit against your peer cohort
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Underrepresented professionals are often promoted later and paid less than peers who joined at the same time, but the gap stays invisible until someone writes it down. Comparing your level, tenure in level, scope and pay band with three to five people who started alongside you turns a vague sense of being behind into numbers you can act on or rule out.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three to five comparison peers named, chosen by start date and starting level, not by friendship.
                2. Your current level, years in level and scope set beside theirs in one table.
                3. Any gap of a year or more in time to promotion marked and described in a sentence.
                4. A short conclusion written: on track, behind with a known reason, or behind without one.

                ## Notes
                Use public information first: job titles, org charts and what colleagues have chosen to tell you. Do not ask peers for their pay unless that is normal in your workplace.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A table compares your level, years in level and scope with at least three cohort peers and ends with a written one-line conclusion."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "List five colleagues who joined at your level within a year of you"
                - "Look up their current titles on the org chart or professional profiles"
                - "Build a table of level, years in level and scope for you and each peer"
                - "Write a one-line conclusion about where you stand"
            - name: Identity disclosure decisions at work
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Whether to mention a disability, a sexuality, a faith practice, a health condition or a working-class background at work is a personal decision with career consequences either way. Deciding it once, deliberately, for each audience (manager, team, HR, clients) is calmer than deciding it under pressure in a meeting or an interview.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each part of your identity you are weighing listed, with what you currently share and with whom.
                2. For each audience, the likely benefit and the likely risk of sharing written in a line.
                3. A decision recorded for each audience: share, share if asked, or keep private for now.
                4. A short form of words prepared for anything you have decided to share.

                ## Notes
                You can change these decisions later, and choosing privacy is a legitimate answer. Where a disclosure would trigger a legal protection or an accommodation, check your employer's policy and the official guidance in your country before you rely on it.
              priority: medium

            - name: Private record of incidents and feedback
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Comments in meetings, assignments handed elsewhere and feedback that sounds coded are easy to doubt a month later and almost impossible to raise without dates. A private, factual record kept outside work systems gives you a clear memory, lets you spot a pattern before you name it, and is ready if you ever decide to escalate.

                ## Milestones
                1. A private document or notebook set up on a personal device, not a work laptop or work account.
                2. A fixed format chosen for each entry: date, who was present, what was said or done, and its effect.
                3. Any incidents from the last six months you still remember clearly entered in that format.
                4. A monthly review in the calendar to add entries and look for repeats.

                ## Notes
                Write facts and direct quotes, not interpretations, and record positive feedback too. Keep confidential work documents out of the record; describe them instead. If you later raise a formal complaint, the Workplace Rights and Grievances area covers that process.
              priority: high
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A private record exists on a personal device with dated factual entries in a fixed format and a monthly review scheduled."
                cadence: rolling
              tasks:
                - "Create a private document on a personal device for the record"

                ## Purpose
                Pay gaps for underrepresented groups often start at the first offer and widen with every percentage rise applied to a lower base.                 ## Notes

# Career Growth for Underrepresented Groups

This area is for professionals who are underrepresented where they work, by race, ethnicity, disability, sexuality, gender identity, class background, migration or neurodivergence, and who want to progress without pretending the system is neutral. It starts with foundations (where you stand against your peer cohort, a private record, the unwritten rules, the unpaid diversity work you carry and the decisions about what to share), moves through the habits that protect credit, feedback and energy, then the skills of self-advocacy and reading coded feedback, decisions about employers and escalation, dated moments such as talent reviews and panels, versions for first-generation, migrant, disabled, LGBTQ+ and neurodivergent professionals and, last, the senior work of sponsoring others and changing how your own team hires and promotes.

What repeats is deliberately light: a weekly credit note and recovery block, monthly time in affinity networks, with a peer circle and across group lines, monthly upkeep of your private record, quarterly checks on assignments, feedback, market value and allies, and yearly reviews of your progression rate, your employer's data and your pay. The Person, Feedback themes, Development plan and 1:1 templates pair with the projects that use them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
