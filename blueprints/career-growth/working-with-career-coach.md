            - name: Coaching brief for what you want to change
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Even the best coach is only as useful as the question you bring, and people who start with a vague wish to feel clearer about their career often spend three paid sessions working out what they came for. Writing a one-page brief with the situation, the decision or change you want and how you would know it had worked gives every coach you speak to something concrete and makes the first session productive.

                ## Milestones
                1. Your current situation summarised in five sentences, without blame or polish.
                2. Two or three outcomes written so a stranger could tell whether you reached them.
                3. A time horizon set for each outcome, such as three months or a year.
                4. One outcome marked as the main focus for the first block of sessions.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Development plan** template. Outcomes such as be happier at work are too broad; narrow them to a decision made, a conversation held or a role applied for.

                Anyone can call themselves a career coach, so credentials are the quickest filter between someone trained and supervised and someone with a website. Checking accreditation with a recognised coaching body such as the ICF, EMCC or AC, whether the coach has regular supervision and which code of ethics they follow takes ten minutes per name and removes most of the risk.

                ## Milestones

                2. Each shortlisted coach's accreditation verified on the body's own directory.
                3. Supervision arrangements and the code of ethics confirmed for each coach.
                4. Professional indemnity insurance confirmed where the coach works privately.
                5. Any coach who cannot answer these points removed from the list.

                ## Notes
                Accreditation is not everything; a highly experienced unaccredited coach can be excellent. But you should still expect clear answers on training, supervision and confidentiality.

# Working With a Career Coach

Working with a career coach covers everything from deciding whether coaching is the help you need to getting full value from the last session and the months after it, for anyone paying privately and for professionals whose employer funds a programme. The projects start with foundations (your coaching brief, budget, credentials, chemistry calls, the contract and three-way agreements), then the routines that make sessions count, the skills of being coached well, decisions about format, employer or private coaching and switching coach, dated sessions and milestones, situations such as outplacement, new managers, returners and tight budgets, and finish with measuring the return and coaching others yourself.

The rhythms are a weekly session preparation slot, a Friday look at open commitments, a short weekly reflection, a monthly progress note and spend check, a quarterly goal refresh and a yearly tune-up once the programme ends. The **Development plan** template suits your coaching goals, **Purchase decision** suits choosing a coach, **Meeting notes** suits every session and **Feedback themes** suits a 360 debrief. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
