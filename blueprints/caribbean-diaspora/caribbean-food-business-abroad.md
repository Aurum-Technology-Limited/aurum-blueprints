                ## Purpose
                Most Caribbean food businesses abroad struggle by trying to sell the whole island on day one: doubles, roti, jerk, oxtail, patties and fish broth from one small kitchen.             - name: Registering the food business with the local authority
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Selling food to the public, even from a home kitchen or a weekend stall, needs the business registered or permitted before the first sale. In the UK that means registering with the local council at least 28 days before trading; in the US and Canada it usually means a health permit from the city or county, and home kitchen rules often allow only low-risk foods that exclude meat and curries. Getting this right first avoids being closed down at your busiest event.

                ## Milestones
                1. The authority covering your kitchen identified, with its food business rules saved.
                2. Whether your menu is allowed from a home kitchen confirmed in writing.
                3. The registration or permit application submitted with the kitchen address and menu.
                4. The confirmation or permit number filed where an inspector or event organiser can see it.

                ## Notes
                Each kitchen address and each food vehicle may need its own registration, and a stall at another council's event can need a separate event permit.
              priority: high

            - name: Written food safety plan for the menu
              description: |-

                Every food business needs a written system showing how it controls hazards, built on HACCP principles; in England many small kitchens use the free Safer Food, Better Business pack, and US and Canadian inspectors ask for similar written procedures. Caribbean menus have their own danger points: curry and stew cooled in deep pots, channa held warm for hours at a stall, rice reheated, raw meat marinated overnight. A plan written around your real dishes is what an inspector reads first.

                4. The plan printed, kept in the kitchen and signed by the owner.

                ## Notes
