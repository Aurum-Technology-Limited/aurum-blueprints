            - name: Dry season watering plan
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                In many islands the dry season runs roughly from January to May, and water restrictions and hot winds can wipe out a herb bed in a week; abroad, a July heatwave does the same to pots. A plan that groups plants by thirst, uses mulch and drip lines, and waters in the early morning keeps plants alive on far less water.

                ## Milestones
                1. Plants grouped by water need, with thirsty callaloo and shado beni together and dry-loving thymes apart.

                4. Watering moved to early morning, with any local water restrictions noted.

                ## Notes
                Seasons differ across the region, and Guyana and Suriname have two rainy seasons. Use your own place's pattern, not a general one.

# Caribbean Herbs and Kitchen Garden

Caribbean Herbs and Kitchen Garden is for anyone who wants shado beni, chive, thyme and a good hot pepper within reach of the stove, whether that means a yard in Arima or Ocho Rios, a balcony in Toronto or a windowsill in Leeds. It starts with the foundations: your climate, the first six plants, seed sources and the border rules on carrying plants. Then come the routines that keep a kitchen garden producing, plant-by-plant skills from shado beni to sorrel, improvements and preserving the glut, the big dates such as Christmas and hurricane season, situations from a renter's balcony to a parent's yard back home, and specialist work such as keeping heritage peppers true.

The rhythm is a Saturday garden round and a Sunday standing-water check, monthly sowing, feeding, compost turns and harvest tallies, a quarterly round of thyme cuttings, and yearly turns for seed starting, overwintering, hurricane preparation, seed testing and the annual review.
