                ## Purpose
                Scotch bonnet, wiri wiri, bird pepper and Trinidad scorpion vary enormously in heat, and getting pepper oil in your eyes or on a child's hands is a mistake people make once. Learning how to cut, store and dose pepper, including how to get the flavour of a whole pepper without the burn, makes the food taste right for everyone at the table.

                ## Milestones

            - name: Cleaning, seasoning and marinating meat safely
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most Caribbean cooks clean meat with lime or vinegar and season it overnight, and many people abroad are surprised to read food safety advice saying raw chicken should not be rinsed under the tap. Settling how your household cleans, seasons and stores raw meat keeps the flavour of home while avoiding raw juices splashed round a small kitchen.

                ## Milestones
                1. Your national food safety agency's guidance on raw poultry and marinating read and noted.
                2. A household method agreed, such as cleaning with lime in a bowl, trimming fat and skin, and no rinsing under a running tap.
                3. Meat always marinated covered in the fridge, never on the counter overnight.
                4. A separate board and knife used for raw meat and washed in hot soapy water after.

                ## Notes
                Cleaning in a bowl with lime juice and water, then pouring the liquid away carefully, keeps the tradition with less splashing. Most dishes are seasoned enough after four hours in the fridge and better after a night.

# Caribbean Home Cooking

This area is for anyone cooking Caribbean food away from the islands, whether you grew up eating it in Port of Spain, Kingston, Georgetown or Port-au-Prince or only ever watched an aunt cook it in Brixton, Brooklyn or Toronto. It starts with the foundations (a first list of dishes, a stocked cupboard, a map of where to buy ingredients, green seasoning, the right pots and the family recipe collection), moves through the weekly routines that keep the food on the table, then the core techniques from browning sugar and burning curry to roti, saltfish, provisions and jerk, the improvements and decisions, the big seasonal cooks of Christmas, Easter and Divali, the situations that change the cooking (children, a partner, a parent living with you, a student kitchen) and finally the specialist projects such as pepperpot, a supper club and a printed family cookbook.

What repeats is a Friday meal plan around one big pot, Sunday lunch, a monthly grocer trip, monthly checks on seasoning and pepper sauce, a freezer rotation, a video cooking call with family back home, a dish from a different island each month, a quarterly cupboard clear-out and the yearly Christmas, Easter and Divali cooks. The Purchase decision, Weekly meal plan and Metrics log templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
