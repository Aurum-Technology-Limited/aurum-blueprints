            - name: Medicine list and repeat prescription set-up
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Older parents often take five or more tablets a day from two or three doctors, and in an emergency nobody abroad can say exactly what they are on. A current medicine list, with doses copied from the labels, the prescriber and where each is collected, is the most useful paper at any hospital visit. It is also the moment to check registration for a chronic disease programme where one applies, such as the NHF card in Jamaica, CDAP in Trinidad and Tobago or the Barbados Drug Service.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every medicine, including eye drops, insulin and anything bought over the counter, listed with the dose written on its label.
                2. The prescribing doctor and the pharmacy for each item recorded.
                3. Registration for any government chronic disease medicine scheme checked and, if eligible, applied for.
                4. Copies of the list kept by the bed, in the handbag and in the family folder.

                ## Notes
                Ask about bush teas and herbal remedies too, such as cerasee or soursop leaf, and show the list to the doctor; some can interact with pressure and sugar medicines. Never change a dose without the prescriber.

# Caring for Parents Back Home

Looking after a mother or father in Kingston, San Fernando or Georgetown while you live in London, Brooklyn or Toronto is a job done mostly by phone, and it works best when the people, papers and plans are in place before a crisis. This area starts with the foundations, a care snapshot, the contact list, the first family conversations and permission to speak to doctors, then builds the weekly and monthly routines, the knowledge of the health system and of scams, the bigger decisions about carers, care homes and money, the visits and hospital stays, situations from the only child to the parent who refuses help, and finally dementia, end-of-life wishes and long-term funding.

The rhythm is a weekly call and neighbour check-in, monthly bills, refills, appointments, carer pay and a change log, and a quarterly review with your siblings, with yearly checks on documents, health cover and pension paperwork. The Person, Meeting notes, Monthly budget and bills, Metrics log and Purchase decision templates pair with the snapshot, sibling meeting, budget, change log and alarm projects.
