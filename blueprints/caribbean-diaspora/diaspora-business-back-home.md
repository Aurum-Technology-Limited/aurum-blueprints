              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Ventures run from abroad usually cost more and take longer than planned, because every delay means another flight, another transfer and another month of rent on an empty shop. Setting a hard ceiling on what you could lose without touching your home, pension or the children's savings, and spreading it across two years of running costs, protects your household abroad if the business back home struggles.

# Diaspora Business Back Home

This area is for anyone abroad who wants to start, buy or back a business in the Caribbean, whether that is a hardware store in St Elizabeth, a pepper farm in Grenada or a cousin's minibus route in Georgetown. It opens with the foundations (your case, the money you can risk, the rules, your own attorney and accountant, a vetted partner and a written agreement), then registration, banking, premises and staff, the routines of oversight from a distance, what to learn about accounts, customs, the single market and tax, imports and exports, launch and the annual visit, situations for families and founders, and finally farming ventures, outside finance, governance and exit.

What repeats is the oversight that keeps a business healthy from overseas: a weekly call with the manager, a Friday look at money owed, monthly accounts, reconciliations, stock counts, payroll and filings on different days of the month, quarterly backer updates and risk reviews, and a yearly visit, partners' meeting and licence renewal.
