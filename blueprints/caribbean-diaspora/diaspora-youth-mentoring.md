            - name: Safeguarding training and background check
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Anyone mentoring under-18s will be asked for a criminal record check and basic safeguarding training, and the type depends on where you and the young person live: a DBS check in England and Wales, a vulnerable sector check in Canada, a state or fingerprint check in the US, or a police certificate of character in much of the Caribbean. Getting both done before you are matched means a programme can introduce you to a mentee in weeks rather than months, and teaches you what to do if a young person tells you something worrying.

                ## Milestones
                1. The check your programme or the mentee's country requires identified.
                2. The background check applied for and the certificate received.
                3. A recognised introductory safeguarding course completed, with the certificate saved.
                4. Expiry or renewal dates for both recorded in your calendar.

                ## Notes
                If you live in one country and mentor in another, ask the programme which checks it needs; some want one from each. Never begin unsupervised contact with a minor until the programme confirms your clearance.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45

            - name: Personal code of conduct and boundaries as a mentor
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Diaspora mentoring brings boundary questions most guidance never mentions: a mentee's family asking you to send money or a barrel, an invitation to stay at the house when you are home for Christmas, a teenager adding you on social media, or a request to help them lie about their age for a job. Writing your own rules down in advance, inside your programme's policy, means you answer kindly and consistently instead of improvising under pressure.

                ## Milestones
                1. Your programme's code of conduct read and its key rules summarised in your own words.

                4. The coordinator's view on any grey areas recorded.

                ## Notes
                A common default: no money or gifts beyond small items the programme approves, no private social media contact, and no one-to-one meetings in private places. Saying no to money protects the mentee as much as you.

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Regular contact matters more than long sessions: a 45-minute call every two weeks, kept even in exam season, builds the trust that makes a mentee tell you what is really going on.                 ## Milestones

                2. A simple agenda used each time: catch-up, the goal, one next step.

                4. A note to the coordinator whenever two sessions in a row are missed.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **1:1** template to keep each session on one page. If the mentee goes quiet, keep the slot and message the guardian or coordinator rather than letting it lapse.

# Diaspora Youth Mentoring

This area is for anyone who wants to mentor young Caribbean people, whether a teenager in Port of Spain over video from London or a newly arrived student down the road in Toronto, and for associations, churches and staff networks that want to run a mentoring programme properly. It starts with the mentor's foundations (what you can give, checks and safeguarding training, boundaries, the first agreement) and the programme's (a design brief, a safeguarding policy, matching and induction), then the routines that keep matches alive, the skills of listening, goal setting, careers and application coaching, decisions about tools, budgets and struggling matches, events from a virtual careers week to results day, situations from new arrivals to retired mentors, and finally sponsorship, alumni pathways, impact and succession.

What repeats is two sessions a month, a monthly look at goals, supervision calls and match check-ins, the opportunities bulletin, quarterly mentor circles, guardian updates and safeguarding checks, and the yearly cycle of recruitment, survey and impact report.
