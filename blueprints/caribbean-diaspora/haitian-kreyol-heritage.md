            - name: Kreyol alphabet and the official spelling
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Kreyol has had an official spelling since 1979, and since the 1987 constitution it has been an official language of Haiti alongside French, yet many fluent speakers abroad still write it as if it were French, with silent letters and guesses. Learning the official system, in which every letter is pronounced and spelling follows sound, takes a few evenings and makes texts, dictionaries and children's books suddenly readable.

                ## Milestones
                1. The 32 sounds and their letters, including ch, j, ou, ui and the nasal an, en, on, listed on one sheet.
                2. The difference between e and è, o and ò explained in your own words with two example words each.
                3. Twenty familiar words rewritten from French-style spelling into the official spelling.
                4. A short message to a relative written entirely in the official spelling and checked against a dictionary.

                ## Notes
                Older relatives may spell differently and that is fine in family messages. Use the official spelling for anything you teach, publish or give to children.
              priority: medium

            - name: Kreyol grammar core of markers, pronouns and la
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Kreyol grammar is regular and learnable in weeks: verbs do not change, and meaning comes from short markers placed before them, te for the past, ap for ongoing action, pral or va for the future, and ta for would. Add the pronouns, the plural yo and the way the word for the changes shape after a noun (la, a, an, nan, lan), and you can build most everyday sentences correctly.

                ## Milestones
                1. The tense and aspect markers te, ap, pral, va and ta used in ten sentences each.
                2. The determiner rules for la, a, an, nan and lan written out with examples.
                3. Questions with ki, kote, kilè, poukisa and konbyen formed correctly.
                4. A one-page grammar summary written in your own words.

                ## Notes
                Expect variation between regions and generations. If a grandparent says it differently from the book, note both.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: learning

# Haitian Kreyol and Heritage

This area is for anyone keeping Haitian Kreyol and Haitian heritage alive outside Haiti, in Miami, Brooklyn, Boston, Montreal, Paris or anywhere the dyaspora has settled, and for parents who want their children to answer a grandmother in her own language. It starts with foundations (an honest language baseline, the official spelling, a family language plan, a contact sheet for relatives in Haiti and the heritage calendar), moves through the routines that keep Kreyol spoken and family supported, then the sounds, grammar, proverbs, history, music, writing, art and faith worth understanding. After that come decisions about classes, schools, interpreters and family projects in Haiti, the dated occasions from 1 January soup joumou to Flag Day and International Creole Day, situations such as a toddler, a teenager or a newly arrived relative, and specialist work in interpreting, teaching and publishing in Kreyol.

What repeats is a short daily practice, a weekly call in Kreyol with family in Haiti, a Kreyol dinner each week, twice-weekly radio, the monthly support transfer and Sunday dinner, a quarterly Krik Krak evening and the yearly round of Independence Day, Flag Day, Kanaval and Creole Day. The Habit tracker, Monthly budget and bills, Reading queue, Trip and Course templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
