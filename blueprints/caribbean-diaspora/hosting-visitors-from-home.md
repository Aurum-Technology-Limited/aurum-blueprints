                ## Purpose
                Before anyone applies for a visa or watches fares, the household abroad and the visitors back home need to agree when the visit happens and how long it lasts.             - name: Entry rules checked for every visitor
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Entry rules depend on the passport, not the family: a Barbadian and a Jamaican passport can face very different requirements for the same trip to London, New York or Toronto. Some visitors need a full visitor visa with an appointment and biometrics, others an electronic travel authorisation, and everyone needs a passport valid for the whole stay. Checking each person on the official government website first prevents the classic mistake of booking flights before knowing a visa is needed.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each visitor's passport country, number and expiry date written in one place.
                2. The current requirement for each person confirmed on the official government website: visa, electronic authorisation or nothing extra.
                3. Any passport expiring within a year of the trip flagged for renewal first.
                4. Typical processing times for each requirement noted against the chosen dates.

                ## Notes
                Use only the official government site of the country being visited. Agent sites dressed up to look official charge extra and sometimes give out-of-date rules.

# Hosting Visitors from Back Home

This area is for anyone in London, Birmingham, New York, Miami or Toronto expecting a mother, grandfather, aunt or cousin from Jamaica, Trinidad, Guyana, St Lucia, Haiti or anywhere else in the region, and wanting the visit to be a pleasure rather than a scramble. It starts with the foundations (dates, entry rules, the invitation letter, the visa, insurance, money, flights, medicines and the guest room), then the routines that run before and during a stay, the practical knowledge of visitor rules, customs, transport, cold weather and emergencies, the decisions about outings, shopping, driving and where everyone sleeps, the big days from arrival to departure, the situations that change the plan (a new baby, an older parent, a child flying alone, a first-time flyer, a small flat) and the harder cases of refusals, long-term visas and visitors who want to stay on.

What repeats is a monthly transfer into the hosting fund, a monthly look at the request list and the shared hosting calendar, weekly calls in the run-up, a weekly meal plan, shop, spending tally and house check-in while visitors stay, a quarterly check of the guest kit and a yearly review of documents, customs rules and how the visits went.
