                ## Purpose
                Caribbean dance is not one thing: soca, dancehall, chutney, kompa, bele, kumina and quadrille each have their own music, posture and places to learn. # Learning Caribbean Dance

Learning Caribbean Dance covers the whole floor, from a first soca class and dancehall steps to folk dances such as bele, kumina and Big Drum, chutney and kompa, carnival conditioning and the stage, for complete beginners, athletes using dance as training and creatives who want to make their own work. It starts with choosing a style, a class, shoes and a warm-up, moves through the weekly routines that make steps stick, the techniques and traditions of each island, decisions about groups and online classes, the showcases, auditions and carnival days you prepare for, situations such as children's classes, starting after fifty or coming back after a baby, and ends with choreographing, staging folk work, moko jumbie stilts and teaching.

The rhythm is mostly weekly: two short home practice sessions, a mobility routine, a fitness session and strength work, with a monthly video check, niggle review and new-steps update, a quarterly talk with your teacher and a yearly reset of goals and the carnival conditioning block. The Purchase decision, Metrics log, Habit tracker, Development plan, Training program and Portfolio piece templates pair with it.
