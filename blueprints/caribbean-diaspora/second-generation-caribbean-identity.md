                ## Purpose
                Plenty of second-generation people know the year a parent landed but not why they left, who met them at the airport, where they slept the first night or what the first winter felt like. # Second-Generation Caribbean Identity

This area is for the children and grandchildren of Caribbean parents who grew up abroad and want a firmer hold on where they come from, whether you are a student meeting other second-generation people for the first time or an adult with a family of your own. It starts with foundations such as your parents' arrival story and the questions you never asked, then the habits that keep family and community close, learning about migration, history, colour and faith, the harder conversations and decisions, trips and family events, situations such as mixed heritage, barrel children and losing a parent, and finally essays, oral history and networks of your own.

The rhythms are a weekly story call with a parent, monthly family dishes, cousin calls, elder calls, community outings and journal entries, a quarterly heritage review and a handful of yearly family dates. The Reading queue, Habit tracker and Trip templates pair with the reading lists, the identity journal and a first trip home on your own. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
