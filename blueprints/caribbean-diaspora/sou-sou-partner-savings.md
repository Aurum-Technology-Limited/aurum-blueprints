              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A sou-sou (Trinidad and Tobago and the Eastern Caribbean), partner (Jamaica), box (Guyana) or asue (the Bahamas) is a group where everyone pays the same sum each week or month and one member takes the whole pot, called the hand or the draw, each turn. Before you put money in, it helps to know the words people will use, who the banker is and what you are actually agreeing to. Fifteen minutes with the basics saves a lot of awkward questions later.

                ## Milestones
                1. The words hand, draw, turn, banker, round and throwing a hand (paying your contribution) explained in your own notes.
                2. The local name for the system where you live and where your family is from written down.
                3. A worked example of ten members paying 100 a week, showing what each person receives and when.
                4. A list of three questions you still want answered before joining.

                ## Notes
                The banker in a sou-sou is a trusted member who collects and pays out, not a bank. In most groups the banker earns little or nothing and carries the most risk.

                Before you start: this plan is general information to help you organise your own money decisions. It is not financial, investment, tax or legal advice. Check decisions that matter with a qualified, licensed adviser in your country, and remember that investing and trading can lose money.

# Sou-Sou and Partner Savings

Sou-sou and Partner Savings is for anyone saving through a rotating group, whether people call it a sou-sou, a partner, a box, an asue or a sòl, and for small business owners who use a hand to buy stock or equipment. It starts with joining well, choosing a hand size and a draw position, moves through the routines members and bankers keep, then covers the decisions that keep a group fair, the dates in the year people save for, situations such as workplace and cross-border rounds, and what experienced bankers handle.

The rhythm follows collection day: weekly reminders, payments and tallies, a monthly ledger check, a quarterly review and a yearly round renewal, with Christmas and back-to-school draws planned each year. The Savings goal, Metrics log, Operational checklist, Risk register and Meeting notes templates pair with it. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
