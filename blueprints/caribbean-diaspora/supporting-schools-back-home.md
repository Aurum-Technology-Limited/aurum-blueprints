              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most people start with the school they went to, the primary school in their village or the one a cousin now teaches at, and that pull is a good reason. Before committing, compare two or three candidates on need, on whether there is a principal or community leader you can actually reach, and on whether anyone else abroad already supports them, so your effort fills a gap rather than doubling someone else's.

                ## Milestones
                1. A shortlist of two or three schools or community groups, with location, roll size and type: government, denominational or private.
                2. A named contact person identified at each one.
                3. Existing outside support for each noted: old scholars groups, churches, companies or embassies.
                4. One school chosen, with the reason written in two sentences.

                ## Notes
                Schools in the same district often share needs and suppliers. If a well-supported school sits next to a struggling one, ask the principal which would benefit more.

                ## Purpose
                Principals back home are used to well-meaning calls from abroad that end in a box of things nobody asked for.             - name: School needs assessment with staff and parents
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A principal's top three needs are a start, but teachers and the PTA (parent teacher association) often see gaps the office does not: a Standard 5 or Grade 6 class with no readers, a science lab with no working gas, children missing school for want of bus fare. A short structured assessment, filled in on the school's side, gives you a ranked list with rough costs that you can show donors and return to every year.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page needs form agreed with the principal, covering learning materials, equipment, buildings, food and student welfare.
                2. Responses gathered from the principal, at least three teachers and the PTA.
                3. Needs ranked by urgency, with a rough cost in local currency for each.
                4. A one-page summary shared back with the school and corrected.

                ## Notes
                Keep the form short enough to fill in during a staff meeting. Long surveys sent from abroad come back half done.
              priority: high

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Government and government-assisted schools in most Caribbean countries cannot accept every gift freely: building works often need ministry or school board approval, some equipment must meet ministry standards, and denominational schools also answer to their church or religious board.                 ## Milestones

                3. Any ministry standard for computers, furniture or books noted.
                4. The principal's view on how long approvals usually take recorded.

                ## Notes
                Ask the principal first; they know the real process. Check anything that sounds unusual with the district education office or the school board.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:

# Supporting Schools Back Home

This area is for anyone abroad who wants to help a school or community back home with more than the odd box of pencils: the primary school in your village, the secondary school you left decades ago, or a struggling school a cousin teaches at, anywhere from Jamaica and Haiti to Guyana, Trinidad and Tobago, Belize and the OECS islands. It runs from the foundations (choosing the school, the first call with the principal, a needs assessment, ministry rules and a clean bank account), through the routines that keep support going, the skills of appeals, duty relief and choosing books, decisions on what to ship and what to buy locally, the drives, fundraisers and visits, plans for busy individuals, faith and community groups, rural schools and storm recovery, and finally bursaries, scholarship panels and tax-effective giving.

What repeats is the monthly call with the school, monthly bookkeeping, receipts and storage checks, standing orders and meal invoices, a quarterly donor update and scholarship check, and the yearly support plan, back-to-school cycle, book drive, prize and annual report. The Meeting notes, Vendor, Purchase decision, Operational checklist, Marketing campaign and Risk register templates pair with the principal calls, the shipper choice, the computers decision, the first shipment, the crowdfunding appeal and the risk register.
