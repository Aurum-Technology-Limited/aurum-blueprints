                ## Purpose
                First-time masqueraders lose time and patience simply because they do not know what people around them mean by section, frontline, last lap or chipping. Knowing the words and the unwritten rules of the road, such as staying inside your band's ropes and not stopping dead in front of a music truck, makes you a good bandmate from the first hour.                 ## Notes
                Useful starters: a section is a group within a band wearing one costume design; frontline costumes are the most elaborate and backline the simpler ones; chipping is the shuffling walk to the music; wining is the rolling hip movement; a fete is a party; liming is hanging out; last lap is the final stretch on Tuesday evening; bacchanal means chaos or scandal.

# Trinidad Carnival and Playing Mas

Trinidad Carnival and Playing Mas is for anyone planning to cross the stage in Port of Spain, whether you live in Trinidad, are flying in from London, New York or Toronto, or are coming back after twenty years away. It starts with the season map, the budget and choosing a band and section, moves through the routines that carry you from band launch to Ash Wednesday, the road skills and history worth knowing, the decisions about J'ouvert, fetes, drinks and transport, the big dates themselves, situations such as playing with a crew, with children or over fifty, and what seasoned masqueraders take on, from traditional characters to the Kings and Queens stage.

The rhythm follows the Carnival year: a monthly saving and costume instalment, weekly training and long walks in your road shoes, a Wednesday check on fete tickets and a Sunday check-in once the season starts, then a yearly review the week after Ash Wednesday.
