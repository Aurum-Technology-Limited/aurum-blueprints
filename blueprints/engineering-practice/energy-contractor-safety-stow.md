            - name: OSH Act duties map for a contracting firm
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The Occupational Safety and Health Act, Chap. 88:08, places duties on you as an employer, on your workers, and on the occupier of the site you work on, and a client's STOW requirement sits on top of the Act rather than replacing it. Mapping which duties fall on your firm, who in the firm carries each one and what evidence would show you meet it turns a legal text into a working list you can hand to a supervisor.

                ## Milestones
                1. The current consolidated OSH Act and any regulations relevant to your work obtained from an official source.
                2. Employer duties that apply to your firm listed in plain language with the person responsible for each.
                3. Duties on the occupier and on workers noted, so you know where the client's duties end and yours begin.
                4. Notification duties for accidents, dangerous occurrences and occupational disease written into a one-page procedure.
                5. Points of uncertainty listed for review with an OSH practitioner or attorney.

                ## Notes
                This is a working map, not a legal opinion. Where a duty is unclear, ask an OSH professional or an attorney and record their answer.
              priority: high
              frontmatter:
                mode: learning
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A duties map listing each employer duty under the OSH Act with an owner and evidence type, plus a notification procedure, is filed and dated."

# Energy Sector Contractor Safety (STOW)

Any firm that wants to work for an operator, a Point Lisas plant or an upstream field in Trinidad and Tobago will be asked two questions before the scope is even discussed: is your STOW certificate current, and can you show how you meet the OSH Act? These projects start with the foundations (what STOW requires, your duties under the Act, a gap assessment, a policy, a management system, training and passport records, a requirements register and a risk register), then build the operating machinery of committees, toolbox talks, inspections, incident reporting, statistics and corrective actions, the skills of writing job safety analyses and HSE plans, the decisions a small firm has to make, the audit and prequalification events, the situations of a one-person consultancy, a growing crew or an engineer overseeing contractors, and the advanced work of culture and ISO 45001.

The rhythm is weekly toolbox talks and site inspections, monthly committee meetings, incident reviews, client HSE reports, training expiry checks and action follow-up, quarterly equipment colour coding, internal audits, risk reviews and emergency drills, and a yearly cycle of STOW renewal, policy review and a culture survey. The Compliance control, Skills matrix, Risk register, Metrics log, Remediation plan and Training program templates pair with the projects that keep a standing record. The current STOW standard, the OSH Act and your client's contract always govern; these projects help you meet them and prove it.
