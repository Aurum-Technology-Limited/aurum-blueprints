              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                New graduates are usually handed a desk, a login and a pile of procedures, and three months later still cannot say what they are accountable for. A written 30, 60 and 90 day plan agreed with your line manager turns the induction period into named outcomes: who you have met, which systems you can use, and the first piece of work you own end to end.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page plan with three to five outcomes for each of days 30, 60 and 90.
                2. The plan agreed with your line manager in your first fortnight.
                3. A first owned task, small but real, identified and delivered before day 60.
                4. A day 90 review held, with what went well and what changes written down.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Onboarding 30/60/90** template. Ask for one owned deliverable early; graduates who only shadow for three months struggle to show competence later.

            - name: Choosing the professional institution to join
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Registration as a chartered or professional engineer usually runs through a licensed institution or a national engineering board, and picking the wrong one can mean a competence standard that does not fit your work. Comparing the two or three bodies that cover your discipline, your country and your employer's existing links lets you join once, at the graduate rate, and start logging evidence in the right format.

                ## Milestones
                1. Two or three candidate bodies listed, for example a discipline institution licensed by the Engineering Council, a national board such as the Board of Engineering of Trinidad and Tobago, or a state licensing board.
                2. Each compared on discipline fit, membership grades, graduate fees, mentoring schemes and the registration route it supports.
                3. Your employer asked which bodies it already supports or pays for.
                4. Graduate or student membership taken out with the chosen body.

                ## Notes
                Check current fees and grades on each body's own website; they change. Many employers pay one membership a year, so ask before paying yourself.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "Graduate membership of one named institution or board is active, with the reasons for the choice written in a short note."

            - name: Mapping the registration competence standard
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every registration route is judged against a written competence standard, such as UK-SPEC in the United Kingdom, and most graduates first read it two years too late. Rewriting each competence in your own words, with an example of what it would look like in your actual job, shows you which experience to ask for from week one.

                ## Milestones
                1. The current edition of your body's competence standard downloaded and read in full.
                2. Each competence rewritten in one plain sentence with an example from your own role.
                3. Competences your current job is unlikely to cover marked as gaps to plan for.
                4. The map reviewed with your mentor or a registered engineer at your firm.

                ## Notes
                Standards typically group competences into knowledge, design or application, management, communication and professional commitment. Check whether your route is for chartered, incorporated or technician registration, as the bar differs.
              priority: high

            - name: Finding a mentor outside your reporting line
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Your line manager judges your performance, so some questions are hard to ask them: whether a rotation is a dead end, whether you are being given enough design work, how to handle a difficult senior. A registered engineer outside your reporting line, ideally one your institution recognises as a mentor, gives you honest advice and a sponsor for the professional review.

                ## Milestones
                1. Two or three possible mentors named, at least one already registered.

            - name: Competence evidence log set-up
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Professional review reports fall apart when the candidate tries to remember, four years on, what they did on a project in their first year. A simple log with one row per piece of work, the competence it shows, your personal contribution and where the evidence lives costs ten minutes a week and makes the final report a matter of selection rather than recall.

                ## Milestones
                1. A log with columns for date, project, task, your own contribution, competence code, evidence location and who can verify it.
                2. Entries back-filled for everything done since you started.
                3. Evidence filed where you can still reach it if you change employer, within confidentiality rules.
                4. The format checked against what your institution's review asks for.

                ## Notes

              priority: high

            - name: Site safety readiness before your first visit
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                On a first site visit, a graduate is one of the people most likely to walk into a hazard, and one of the least likely to speak up when something looks wrong. Completing the site induction, getting the right protective equipment and understanding the permit to work system and your stop-work authority before you go means your first visit teaches you engineering rather than luck.

                ## Milestones
                1. The site or client induction and any required safety passport completed.
                2. Correct protective equipment issued and fitted, including any site-specific items.
                3. The permit to work, isolation and gas testing arrangements explained to you by a competent person.
                4. Your right and duty to stop work you believe is unsafe confirmed with your supervisor.

                ## Notes
                Stop-work authority applies to graduates too. If something looks wrong, stop, step back and ask; nobody competent will hold it against you.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building

# Graduate Engineer Development

The first four or five years decide what kind of engineer you become, and most graduates drift through them collecting tasks rather than competence. These projects start with the foundations (a 90-day plan, an institution, the competence standard, a mentor, an evidence log, a development plan and site safety), then the rhythms that turn ordinary weeks into evidence, the core skills of hand checks, drawings, standards and operators' knowledge, the choices about specialism, site or office and further study, the dated reviews and the professional review itself, variants for students, small-firm graduates, career changers and international graduates, and finally the work of leading a package and mentoring the next intake.

The repeating rhythm is a weekly diary entry and questions session, monthly mentor and manager conversations, a monthly site visit, reading and CPD record, a quarterly evidence review and a yearly development review. The Onboarding 30/60/90, Development plan, 1:1, Skills matrix, Reading queue and Job application templates pair with the projects that need them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
