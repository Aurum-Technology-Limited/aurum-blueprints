            - name: Aircraft ownership structure with counsel
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Holding an aircraft directly in a family member's name can expose them to liability and makes registration, operation, tax and a future sale harder to manage. Agreeing the owning entity, the country of registration and how the family will use the aircraft with aviation counsel and tax advisers puts the legal and tax position on record before money moves.

# Aircraft and Yacht Ownership

This area is for a family that flies privately or spends time on a yacht, or is thinking about it, and wants the assets to serve the family rather than quietly consume it. It starts with the foundations: a needs assessment, the own, share or charter decisions, ownership structures and flags, cost models and a family usage policy. Then come the management companies and the operating routine, crew, the knowledge family members need, the purchase, berth and sale decisions, dated events such as a season start or a yard period, the situations of children, older travellers and siblings who inherit, and finally specialist work on new builds, leases to operators and the yearly keep or exit review.

What repeats is a weekly look at trip requests, monthly invoice approvals, recharges, maintenance and certificate checks, crew payroll checks and personal use records, a quarterly owner's review with the managers, and a yearly round of insurance renewals, safety drills, season planning, the yard period, hurricane preparation and the usage policy review. The Purchase decision, Vendor, Compliance control, Meeting notes, Reading queue and Trip templates pair with the access decision, the adviser and manager choices, the certificates calendar, the owner's review, the family reading list and the first charter. Nothing here is legal, tax or aviation regulatory advice: structures, flags, tax treatment and operating rules should be agreed with aviation and maritime counsel and tax advisers in every country involved.
