            - name: Deciding whether close protection is needed
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before hiring anyone, the family needs a written answer to whether the risk it faces calls for people on the ground or whether better routines, alarms and privacy would do. Taking that decision from the findings of a professional security assessment, rather than from a single frightening incident or a provider's sales pitch, keeps the response proportionate and the cost defensible.

                ## Milestones
                1. The latest security assessment's findings on threats to people, as opposed to property, summarised on one page.
                2. Options compared: no protection, protection for specific occasions, part-time cover and full-time cover.
                3. A decision recorded with the reasons, signed off by the family member who holds the security brief.
                4. A date set to revisit the decision, tied to the next assessment.

                ## Notes
                If no recent assessment exists, commission one first. A provider who is hoping to win the contract should not be the one who decides that you need it.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30

            - name: Protection scope and coverage levels by person
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Close protection is rarely all or nothing: the founder may need a detail on every working day, a spouse only when travelling, and the children only on the school run and at public events. A coverage matrix that lists each person, the situations covered and the level of cover turns a vague instruction into something a provider can price and a team can staff.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every family member and any close staff who may need cover listed.
                2. For each person, the situations covered: home, commute, work, school, social, travel and events.
                3. A coverage level agreed for each cell, from none through on call to a dedicated officer.
                4. The matrix shared with the provider and used as the basis of the staffing plan.

                ## Notes
                Ask adult family members what they will actually accept. A level of cover someone resents tends to be dodged, and a dodged detail is worse than an honest lighter one.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45

            - name: Licensing and legal rules in each country
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most countries license security operatives, some license the company too, and the rules on carrying weapons, using force, recording people and detaining anyone differ sharply between them. Getting a short legal summary for every country where the family lives or regularly travels stops the family from hiring people who cannot lawfully do the job asked of them.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of countries where protection will operate, with the family's usual time spent in each.
                2. For each country, a lawyer's note on operative licensing, company licensing, firearms and use of force.
                3. A rule recorded for each country on what the team may and may not do there.
                4. The summaries filed and sent to the provider as a contract requirement.

                ## Notes
                Examples include the Security Industry Authority licence in the UK and state licensing in the US, but the details change. Treat everything here as a question for local counsel, never as a conclusion.

# Close Protection Arrangements

This area is for the parent who has been told, by a security specialist or by events, that the family now needs people whose job is to keep them safe, and who wants that arranged as carefully as any other major appointment. It starts with foundations (whether protection is needed at all, who is covered and when, the budget, licensing in each country, the provider and its contract, the team's rules and the information they hold about each person), then the operating routines that keep a detail working, the knowledge the family needs to work well with officers, decisions on vehicles, drivers, monitoring and stepping down, dated events such as a wedding, a trip abroad or a hurricane, situations such as children at school or a child studying overseas, and finally specialist work with police, protective intelligence, several countries at once and liability.

What repeats is a weekly schedule handover to the team leader, monthly provider reviews, vehicle and equipment checks, incident log reviews and certificate expiry checks, quarterly reviews of the protection plan and of who receives itineraries, and a yearly audit of the whole programme. The Vendor, Risk register, Purchase decision, Trip and Onboarding plan templates pair with choosing a provider, the incident log, the armoured vehicle decision, overseas travel and a new team's first weeks. Licensing, firearms, use of force and employment status are legal questions for a lawyer in each country, and the threat picture itself belongs with the family's security assessment.
