            - name: House manager or estate manager decision
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Families often hire the wrong role: a house manager runs one household, its staff and its supplies, while an estate manager also carries grounds, buildings, land, tenants and capital works, and a property management firm may be enough for a home that is mostly empty. Writing down what the household actually needs before advertising saves a failed hire, which can cost six months and a full year's salary in agency fees and disruption.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page picture of the household: properties, staff headcount, family members in residence and weeks of occupancy a year.
                2. The three options compared: house manager, estate manager, or an outside property management firm with a part-time coordinator.
                3. A decision recorded on which role to hire, with the two reasons that settled it.
                4. The decision shared with the spouse or other principals who will give the manager instructions.

                ## Notes
                If the honest answer is that nobody in the family will spend an hour a week with the manager, fix that first. No hire survives an absent principal with no reporting line.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A written decision naming the role to hire, signed off by every principal who will direct the manager, exists before any advert or agency brief."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:
                - "List every property, staff member and supplier the role would touch"
                - "Count the weeks each home is occupied by the family in a typical year"
                - "Compare a house manager, an estate manager and an outside firm on one page"
                - "Record the chosen role and share it with every principal"
            - name: Written role scope for the manager
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most friction between a family and its manager comes from work nobody said was theirs: the school run, the boat, the father-in-law's flat, the dog. A written scope lists what the manager owns, what they support and what is outside the job, so the first argument about it happens on paper rather than in the kitchen.

                ## Milestones
                1. A scope document with three columns: owns, supports, not in scope.
                2. Each property, staff group and major supplier placed in one of the columns.
                3. Personal errands for family members either named in scope or explicitly excluded.
                4. The scope agreed by the principals and attached to the job description.

                ## Notes
                Keep it to two pages. A scope that tries to list every task becomes a rulebook nobody reads; list areas of responsibility instead.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45

            - name: Authority matrix and spending limits
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A manager who must ask before buying light bulbs is wasted, and one who can sign a roofing contract alone is a risk. An authority matrix sets what the manager decides alone, what needs a quick message and what needs written approval, with money limits per purchase and per month.

                ## Milestones
                1. Decision types listed: purchases, supplier contracts, hiring and dismissing staff, repairs, guest arrangements and emergencies.
                2. Three levels set for each: decide alone, inform the family, get written approval first.
                3. Spending limits per purchase and per month written as figures in the household currency.
                4. Emergency spending rights defined for when no principal can be reached.
                5. The matrix signed by the principal and kept with the manager's contract.

                ## Notes
                Set emergency authority generously. A burst pipe at 2 a.m. should never wait for a reply from another time zone.
              priority: high

                ## Purpose
                Handing a manager a budget they own, rather than a card and a vague sense of what is normal, is how spending becomes predictable. # Estate Manager and House Manager

This area is for the family that has decided the house, or the estate, needs one professional running it, and wants that person to succeed: a house manager for a busy family home, or an estate manager for grounds, several buildings, land and a team. It starts with foundations (choosing the right role, a written scope, an authority matrix with spending limits, one reporting line, the delegated budget, pay, the hiring route, assessment, the offer and a first ninety days), moves into the operating routines of weekly meetings, monthly reports, expense reconciliation, staff supervision and reviews, then the skills of delegating well and reading a management report, decisions about live-in terms, bonuses and how the team is shaped, dated events such as the end of probation and a family wedding at home, situations for young families, absent owners, ageing parents and shared homes, and specialist work on working estates, household KPIs and independent spending audits.

What repeats is a weekly principal meeting, a weekly forward look of family plans and a staff rota check, a monthly management report, expense reconciliation and KPI update, quarterly budget reforecasts, access audits and preference book updates, and a yearly round of performance review, bonus assessment, leave cover and the annual household plan. The Onboarding 30/60/90, Meeting notes, Month-end close, Development plan and Metrics log templates pair with a new hire, the weekly meeting, the monthly reconciliation, the annual review and the household KPIs. Employment terms, tax and dismissal stay organisational here: agree them with an employment lawyer and the family's accountant in the country where the manager works.
