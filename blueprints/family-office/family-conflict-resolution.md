                ## Purpose
                Most family disputes that reach lawyers were visible years earlier as a cool relationship, an unanswered email or a branch that stopped coming to meetings.             - name: Early warning signs checklist for family disputes
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Disputes in wealthy families rarely begin with a lawsuit: they begin with a member asking for documents they never wanted before, a sudden new adviser, missed gatherings or questions about how distributions are calculated. A short checklist of the signals that matter in your family lets the leader and the office notice them and respond with a conversation instead of a defence.

                ## Milestones

                3. The family office head and the family leader both holding the same checklist.
                4. A quarterly review of the checklist booked.

                ## Notes
                Requests for information are a warning sign, not an attack. Answering them quickly and fully is usually the best way to stop suspicion growing.

            - name: Choosing a family mediator before you need one
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Finding a mediator in the middle of a dispute is hard, because every name one side suggests looks partisan to the other. Agreeing on one or two experienced family enterprise mediators while everyone is still on speaking terms means the first call in a crisis is already decided and trusted by all branches.

                ## Milestones
                1. Criteria agreed: experience with family wealth and businesses, neutrality, languages and locations.
                2. Three mediators interviewed by a small group drawn from different branches.
                3. One lead mediator and one alternate named and accepted by each branch head.
                4. Their contact details, fee basis and availability written into the family's dispute contacts.

                ## Notes

            - name: Panel of independent advisers for disputes
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                When a dispute starts, the family's usual lawyer and accountant often cannot advise any one member, because they act for the business, the trust or the founder. A panel of independent lawyers, accountants and valuers that any member can go to, with the family agreeing in advance who pays, keeps advice fair and stops a member feeling outgunned.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of the family's current advisers and who each one actually acts for.

                4. The panel list shared with every adult member.

                ## Notes
                Check conflicts carefully. A law firm that once advised the business on a property deal may be barred from acting for a member against it.

# Family Conflict Resolution

This area is for parents, retirees and family leaders who want disagreements in a wealthy family handled early and fairly, before they harden into estrangement or court. It starts with the foundations (a map of where tension sits, warning signs, a mediator and independent advisers chosen in calm times, ground rules and confidentiality), moves to the routines and skills that catch grievances while they are small, then the decisions on mediation, arbitration, valuation and buy-outs, the dated meetings that settle and repair, the life stages that strain families most and the specialist work of trust disputes, deadlock and separating a family enterprise.

What repeats is a quarterly relationship pulse and grievance themes report, monthly one-to-ones with each branch and follow-up on settlement terms, a quarterly equal information pack and a yearly facilitated dialogue and process review. The **Meeting notes**, **Feedback themes** and **Risk register** templates pair with mediation sessions, the anonymous pulse and the conflict map.
