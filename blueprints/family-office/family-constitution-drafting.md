            - name: Deciding whether the family is ready for a constitution
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Families usually reach for a constitution after a trigger: a founder turning seventy, a grandchild marrying, a sale of the business or a row about who gets a board seat. Before anyone writes a word, an honest look at what problem the document should solve, and whether the key people are willing, saves a year of drafting a text nobody wants to sign.

                ## Milestones
                1. The two or three events or tensions prompting the idea written down in plain words.
                2. Each senior family member asked privately whether they would take part.
                3. A short list of what the constitution must settle and what it should leave alone.
                4. A recorded decision to start now, start later with a date, or not proceed.

                ## Notes
                A constitution does not fix a live dispute. If two branches are not speaking, deal with the conflict first and come back to drafting afterwards.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30

            - name: Drafting group membership and written mandate
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                When the whole family drafts together nothing gets written, and when one parent drafts alone nobody else owns it. A small drafting group of four to seven people, with at least one voice from each branch and generation, and a one-page mandate saying what it may decide and what goes back to the family, is the usual way through.

                ## Milestones
                1. A drafting group named, with every branch and at least two generations represented.
                2. A written mandate covering scope, timetable, budget and what needs wider approval.
                3. A confidentiality rule agreed for drafts, so early wording is not circulated or quoted.
                4. A chair and a note-taker chosen for the group's meetings.

                ## Notes
                Include at least one person under forty. A constitution drafted only by the generation that already holds power tends to be rejected by the one that inherits it.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:

            - name: Choosing a facilitator and legal adviser for the drafting
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Two different people are usually needed: a family business facilitator who can run hard conversations without taking sides, and a lawyer who checks the text against trust deeds, company articles and the law of each country involved. Choosing them against the same criteria, and agreeing fees up front, avoids the common mistake of letting the founder's long-standing lawyer quietly become the author.

                ## Milestones
                1. Selection criteria written for the facilitator and the legal adviser separately.
                2. Two or three candidates for each role met by the drafting group.
                3. Engagement letters agreed with scope, fee basis and who the adviser acts for.
                4. A conflict of interest check recorded for each appointed adviser.

                ## Notes
                Ask the lawyer in writing who their client is: the family as a whole, a trust, or one individual. It changes what they can tell each branch.

# Family Constitution Drafting

This area is for a family that wants its shared purpose, ownership principles, roles and ways of settling disagreements written down in one document everyone has helped shape and signed. It starts with the foundations (whether now is the right time, who drafts, which adviser, what existing trust deeds and agreements the text must respect), moves through the skills and conversations that make drafting work, then the chapters themselves, ratification and launch, and ends with the machinery that keeps the constitution current, the life stages that test it and the specialist work of mirroring it into legal documents and handing it to the next generation.

While drafting, a monthly drafting session, a version log and a branch feedback round keep the work moving; once signed, a yearly reading at the family assembly, a quarterly check of the amendment and interpretation logs and a five-year full review keep the text honest. The **Meeting notes**, **Feedback themes** and **Risk register** templates pair with the drafting meetings, the family consultation and the stress test.
