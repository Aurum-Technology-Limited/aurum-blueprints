            - name: Family crisis scenario inventory
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Families who cope well with a crisis have usually named it in advance, so the first hour is spent acting rather than arguing about what is happening. Listing the twelve to fifteen events that could realistically hit this family, from the founder's stroke to a cousin's arrest or a class action against the family company, and scoring each for likelihood and impact, tells you which playbooks to write first.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of at least twelve plausible crisis scenarios covering health, legal, conduct, business, disaster and exposure events.
                2. Each scenario scored from one to five for likelihood and for impact on the family.
                3. The top five scenarios by combined score agreed with the family lead.
                4. The inventory saved where the crisis team can open it offline.

                ## Notes
                Ask each adult branch privately what keeps them awake. The scenario a family is most embarrassed to name is often the one most worth planning for.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A scored inventory of at least twelve crisis scenarios exists, with the top five agreed by the family lead and saved offline."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "6"
              tasks:
                - "Write down every crisis that has hit this family or a family you know in the last twenty years"
                - "Ask each adult branch head privately which events worry them most"
                - "Score every scenario for likelihood and impact on a one to five scale"
                - "Agree the top five scenarios with the family lead and record the reasons"
            - name: Family crisis team with named deputies
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A crisis team that exists only as an idea turns into twenty relatives on a group call. Naming four to six people with clear roles (lead, family liaison, communications, legal liaison, office operations) and a deputy for each means someone is in charge even if the obvious leader is the person in hospital or the one under investigation.

                ## Milestones
                1. Four to six crisis roles defined in a sentence each.
                2. A named person and a named deputy for every role, with at least one non-family member on the team.
                3. Every person has agreed to the role in writing and knows who their deputy is.
                4. A rule written for who leads when the crisis involves a team member.

                ## Notes
                Keep the team small. Wider family members are informed by the family liaison; they are not on the team unless they hold a role.
              priority: high

# Family Crisis and Reputation Plan

This area is for the parent at the centre of a wealthy family who would be the first call if something went badly wrong: a sudden illness, a family member in the news for the wrong reasons, a lawsuit or a journalist asking about the family's trusts. It starts with foundations (the scenarios that matter, a crisis team with deputies, activation rules, advisers on call, a call tree, the written plan, rules on who speaks and holding statements), moves through the routines that keep the plan alive, the skills a spokesperson and family lead need, decisions on posture, budget and outside firms, scenario playbooks and situations for parents with children abroad or online, practice runs, and ends with reputation recovery and cross-border coordination.

What repeats is a weekly monitoring digest, a monthly early warning review and channel test, quarterly contact refreshes and adviser check-ins, and a yearly plan review, media refresher and full practice run. The Risk register, Reading queue, Vendor, Operational checklist and Meeting notes templates pair with the warning register, the reading list, the crisis firm choice, the first 72 hours after a death and the tabletop exercise. Legal, medical and insurance questions belong with the family's own lawyers, doctors and brokers. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
