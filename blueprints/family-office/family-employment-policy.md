                ## Purpose
                Most families write employment rules only after a hire goes wrong: a nephew promoted over a long-serving manager, or a daughter paid twice the market rate. Naming the triggers that make the question live for your family, before anyone drafts a rule, keeps the policy focused on real problems instead of hypothetical ones.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page list of the situations that prompted the policy, past and expected.
                2. The businesses, the family office and any foundation the policy will cover named.
                3. The two or three outcomes the family most wants from the policy written down.
                4. The senior generation's agreement that a written policy will be prepared.

                ## Notes
                Keep the trigger list factual. Naming a specific relative as the reason for the policy makes it feel aimed at them and sinks it before it is drafted.

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Some families treat a job in the business as a birthright, others as an opportunity to be earned, and many have never said which. A short statement of philosophy, agreed before the detailed rules, gives every later clause something to point back to when a hard case arrives.

            - name: Outside work experience requirement
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Many family enterprise advisers suggest three to five years working elsewhere before joining, so the family member learns to be managed by someone who is not a parent and arrives with a record of their own. Deciding the length, what counts as outside and whether a promotion elsewhere is required turns a sensible idea into a rule people can plan around.

                ## Milestones
                1. The required years of outside experience set, with any difference by role level.
                2. What counts as outside written down, for example no company where the family holds over a set stake.
                3. Whether at least one promotion or strong reference from the outside employer is needed decided.
                4. Exceptions, such as a small business with no outside equivalent, described with who can approve them.

                ## Notes
                Write the rule early enough that teenagers and students can plan for it. Springing a five-year requirement on a 24-year-old who expected a job next month feels like a betrayal.

            - name: Market pay principle for family employees
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Paying a relative above market quietly turns salary into a disguised distribution, and paying below market breeds resentment and a sense of being owed. A principle that family employees earn what the role pays in the market, with ownership returns kept separate, is the clause that protects both the business and the relationships.

                ## Milestones
                1. The principle written: pay set by role and market data, not by family status.
                2. The source of market data named, such as a pay survey or an outside reward adviser.
                3. Who sets and approves family pay decided, with the family member's parent excluded.
                4. Benefits and perks brought under the same principle.

                ## Notes
                Ask the tax adviser how family salaries are treated where you operate. Some tax authorities challenge pay to relatives that looks above market, and the answer affects how records are kept.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 75
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A written market pay clause naming the data source and an approver who is not a close relative of the employee."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "5"
              tasks:
                - "Write the market pay principle in two sentences"
                - "Choose the pay survey or reward adviser the business will use"
                - "Name who approves family pay and who must step out of the decision"
                - "Ask the tax adviser how family salaries are tested locally"

            - name: Keeping salary separate from ownership returns

                Family members who own shares and also work in the business often blur the two, expecting a salary to reflect their ownership or dividends to reward their work. # Family Employment Policy

This area is for the founder whose children are asking for jobs, the parent who wants a son or daughter to earn a place rather than be handed one, and the family whose business or office already has relatives on the payroll with no rules behind it. It starts with foundations (an inventory of who works where, eligibility, entry qualifications, outside experience, market pay and the first written policy), then the operating machinery of reviews, pay benchmarks, reporting lines and the employment register, the skills of interviewing and giving feedback to a relative, decisions on internships, promotion, exits and existing staff, the dated events of launching the policy and onboarding the first family hire, situations for children, spouses and returners, and finally specialist work on cross-border roles, compensation committees and measuring how the policy performs.

What repeats is a monthly register update and mentor check-in, quarterly reviews of reporting lines, open roles and the salary and dividend split, and a yearly cycle of pay benchmarking, performance reviews, conflict declarations, promotion rounds and the family council's policy review. The Reading queue, Skills matrix, Development plan, Onboarding 30/60/90 and 1:1 templates pair with the reading list, role profiles, family employee development, first-day onboarding and mentor meetings. Employment, tax and immigration rules differ by country, so agree contracts, pay structures and anything with legal effect with the family's employment lawyer and tax adviser.
