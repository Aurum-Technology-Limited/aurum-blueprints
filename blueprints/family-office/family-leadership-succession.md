            - name: Inventory of roles the family leader holds
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most family leaders hold far more roles than anyone has written down: chair of the holding company, head of the family council, signatory on a dozen accounts, protector of a trust, chief peacemaker and the person bankers ring first. Listing every formal and informal role is the first step, because each one needs its own successor and some cannot be passed on without legal steps.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of every board seat, committee chair, trust office and signing authority the leader holds, with the entity and document behind each.
                2. A second list of informal roles, such as host of family gatherings, mediator between branches and keeper of family history.
                3. Each role marked as transferable by decision, transferable only by a legal process, or personal to the leader.
                4. The inventory reviewed and confirmed by the leader in a short meeting.

                ## Notes
                Ask the family office or company secretary for the formal list first, then interview the leader and two other family members for the informal roles. The informal list is usually longer.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A written inventory of every formal and informal role the family leader holds, each classified by how it passes on, is confirmed by the leader."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "6"
              tasks:
                - "Ask the company secretary for a list of the leader's board seats and signing authorities"
                - "Interview the leader for an hour about the informal roles they play"
                - "Ask two family members what they would miss if the leader stepped back tomorrow"
                - "Classify each role by how it legally or practically passes on"
            - name: Emergency stand-in for each key family role
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                If the family leader were taken ill tonight, someone would need to sign payments, chair the next board meeting and speak for the family within days. Naming an emergency stand-in for each critical role, separate from the long-term successor, keeps the family's affairs running while the real succession decision is made calmly.

                ## Milestones
                1. An emergency stand-in named for every role marked critical in the role inventory.
                2. Each stand-in has agreed in writing and knows where the key documents are kept.
                3. Interim signing and decision limits for each stand-in confirmed with the family's lawyer.
                4. A one-page emergency card held by the family office, the stand-ins and the leader's spouse.

                ## Notes
                Stand-ins are a bridge, not a promise. Say clearly in writing that naming someone as an emergency cover does not make them the successor, or the card will be read as an announcement.
              priority: high

            - name: Senior leader's own exit intentions
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Succession plans built around the family but not around the leader tend to stall at the last step, because the person stepping back has never said what they want. A private, unhurried conversation about timing, the roles they want to keep, money, purpose and fears gives every later step a realistic starting point.

                ## Milestones
                1. One or more private conversations held between the leader and a trusted adviser or coach.
                2. The leader's preferred timing, roles to keep and roles to give up written down in their own words.
                3. Personal concerns, such as income, identity, health or a spouse's wishes, noted for the plan.
                4. The leader's summary shared with the succession steering group with their consent.

                ## Notes
                Choose someone the leader trusts and who has no stake in the outcome. A family member who hopes to succeed is the wrong person to run this conversation.

            - name: Succession steering group with an independent adviser
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Nobody should run their own succession, and nobody who might succeed should run it either. A small steering group of three to five people, usually the leader, a respected family elder, one or two family members not in contention and an independent family business adviser, keeps the process fair and moving.

                ## Milestones

                2. A short remit stating what the group decides, what it recommends to the council or board, and what stays with the leader.
                3. A meeting rhythm and a confidentiality rule agreed by all members.
                4. The first meeting held with minutes and an action list.

            - name: Written family succession principles
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Arguments about succession are rarely about the person chosen and usually about the rules nobody agreed: whether the eldest has a claim, whether daughters and sons are treated alike, whether in-laws or non-family can lead. Writing five to ten principles before any names are discussed lets the family judge the process instead of the candidates.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of the questions the principles must answer, such as birth order, gender, in-laws, outside experience and non-family leaders.
                2. A draft of five to ten principles discussed with the steering group.
                3. Feedback gathered from each branch before the draft is final.
                4. Principles adopted by the family council or equivalent body and filed with the family constitution.

                ## Notes
                Agree principles while no one is a declared candidate. Once names are in play, every principle will be read as being for or against someone.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 60
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "Between five and ten succession principles are adopted by the family council and filed alongside the family constitution."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "10"
              tasks:
                - "List the succession questions the family has avoided so far"
                - "Draft five to ten principles in plain language"
                - "Send the draft to each branch with a three-week window for comments"
                - "Put the final principles to the family council for adoption"

              description: |-
                ## Purpose

# Family Leadership Succession

This area is for the senior generation planning to hand over the leadership of a wealthy family, and for the parents and adult children who may step into those roles. It starts with the foundations (what the leader actually does, who covers in an emergency, the principles and the timeline), moves into the machinery of development, mentoring and knowledge transfer, then the skills both sides need, the big decisions about who and how, the dated moments of announcement and handover, the hard situations such as sudden death or a founder who will not let go, and the specialist work of succession across several entities and generations.

The rhythm is monthly mentoring and knowledge transfer, quarterly development check-ins, readiness reviews and council updates, and a yearly cycle of plan review, emergency drill and feedback for the successor. The Development plan template suits each successor, 1:1 suits the mentoring sessions, Skills matrix suits the readiness dashboard, Vendor suits choosing an independent assessor, Person suits a page per candidate and Meeting notes suits the steering group.
