              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Families usually start talking about prenuptial agreements in the worst possible month: after an engagement is announced and the wedding venue is booked. Writing down now why the family wants a shared policy, and which past or expected situations prompted it, means the first couple to meet the policy meets a family decision rather than a reaction to their relationship.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page note of the reasons for a family-wide policy, written without naming any current partner.
                2. Past divorces, near misses and upcoming marriages in the family listed factually.
                3. The two or three outcomes the family most wants from the policy agreed.
                4. The senior generation's agreement that a policy will be prepared, recorded in writing.

                ## Notes
                If a specific relationship is the real trigger, keep it out of the note. A policy that looks aimed at one partner is resented by every partner after.

            - name: Whether the policy is required or encouraged
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Probably the biggest single choice is whether an agreement is a condition of receiving shares or trust distributions, or a strong family expectation with no penalty. A requirement protects more and feels harsher; an expectation is kinder and easier to ignore. Deciding this openly, with counsel's view on how each option interacts with the trusts and shareholder agreements, prevents the policy being read differently by each branch.

                ## Milestones

                3. A family decision recorded on required, encouraged, or required only above a threshold.
                4. The decision written into the draft policy in one plain sentence.

# Family Prenuptial Policy

This area is for the parent who wants family shares and trust interests to survive a child's divorce without turning every engagement into a negotiation, and for the family that has so far handled marital agreements one awkward conversation at a time. It starts with foundations (the asset map, who the policy covers, whether it is required or encouraged, policy counsel and the first written policy), then the standing machinery of the agreement register, disclosure packs, review triggers and document alignment, the skills of understanding agreements and raising the subject kindly, decisions on sunset clauses, fair provision for spouses, cohabitation and postnuptial agreements, the dated events of launching the policy and seeing the first couple through it, situations a parent actually meets, and finally specialist work across trust deeds, faiths and countries.

What repeats is a monthly register update and engagement lead-time check, quarterly reviews of upcoming relationships, vault access, document alignment and transfers to married members, and a yearly cycle of disclosure refreshes, trigger checks, trustee letters of wishes and the family council's policy review. The Vendor, Reading queue, Operational checklist and Meeting notes templates pair with the lawyer panel, the reading list, the signing meeting and the launch session. Whether an agreement holds up depends on the law where the couple lives and divorces, so every agreement and every clause with legal effect is drafted and advised on by qualified family lawyers, one for each partner.
