            - name: Employee or contractor status for each worker
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Calling a full-time nanny or driver a self-employed contractor does not make them one, and in most countries the tax authority and the courts look at control, hours and dependence rather than the label on an invoice. Getting each worker's status confirmed with an employment adviser now avoids back-dated contributions, penalties and claims later.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each worker on the register described by who sets the hours, who supplies equipment and whether they work for anyone else.
                2. The status of each worker reviewed with an employment lawyer or payroll adviser in the relevant country.
                3. A written status decision recorded for every worker, with the reasons.
                4. A plan agreed for any worker who must move onto payroll, including whether past periods need correcting.

                ## Notes
                Many tax authorities publish a status checker or guidance on domestic workers. Use it to prepare for the adviser conversation, not as the final answer.

# Household Staff Employment

This area is for the family that pays people to work in its homes, whether that is one cleaner and a weekend nanny or a household of twenty across three countries, and wants to be a fair, legal employer rather than a casual one. It starts with foundations (a register of every worker, employee or contractor status, employer registration, contracts, confidentiality agreements, payroll and injury cover), moves into the operating routines of payroll, statutory filings, hours, leave, one-to-ones and pay reviews, then the knowledge of domestic worker law, minimum pay and rest rules, decisions such as live-in or live-out and agency or direct hire, dated events like a first day, a trip abroad or an ending, situations for new parents, carers of ageing parents, nanny shares and first-time employers, and specialist work on audits, employing entities, gratuities and claims.

What repeats is a weekly time sheet sign-off, a monthly payroll run, contribution filings, leave update, one-to-one and petty cash check, quarterly document and safety checks, and a yearly round of pay reviews, contract refreshes, year-end returns, insurance renewal and bonuses. The Vendor, Compliance control, 1:1, Risk register and Onboarding 30/60/90 templates pair with the payroll provider, the filing calendar, monthly conversations, workplace safety and a new starter. Employment law differs sharply between countries and is organisational here: confirm every contract, status and termination question with an employment lawyer or payroll adviser where the staff actually work.
