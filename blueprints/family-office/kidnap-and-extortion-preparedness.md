              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Kidnap, ransom and extortion policies differ widely in who is insured, which countries are covered and what is reimbursed, and many families discover the limits only during a claim. Reading the policy line by line with the broker tells you whether children, spouses, household staff and guests are named, whether consultant fees, lost income and medical costs are included, and what the confidentiality clause requires.

                ## Milestones
                1. The current policy, or a set of quotes, obtained and read in full.
                2. Insured persons, territories, limits, excesses and exclusions summarised on one page.
                3. Gaps listed, such as uninsured staff, excluded countries or detention by authorities.
                4. Questions answered in writing by the broker and changes requested where needed.
                5. The summary filed with the crisis plan, with the policy itself in the restricted vault.

                ## Notes
                Many policies reimburse a ransom after it is paid rather than paying it, and many become void if their existence is disclosed. Confirm both points with the broker.

# Kidnap and Extortion Preparedness

This area is for parents in a family whose wealth, business or travel makes kidnap for ransom or extortion a real possibility, and who want the arrangements made calmly in advance rather than improvised on the worst day of their lives. It starts with foundations (an exposure profile, the kidnap and ransom insurance, a response consultancy, a family crisis team, a written plan, proof-of-life files, code words and profile packs), moves through the operating routines that keep it all current, the knowledge every adult should have about how cases unfold, virtual kidnap scams and the law on ransom, decisions on cover, funds and decision authority, dated events such as a tabletop exercise or a child leaving for school abroad, situations for young children, teenagers, nannies and separated parents, and specialist work on family welfare, aftercare and an annual audit.

What repeats is a quarterly look at the threat picture and a quarterly test of the call tree, weekly variation in the school run, monthly reviews of the suspicious incident and threat logs, and a yearly round of policy renewal, code word refreshes, proof-of-life updates, staff briefings and an independent audit. The Risk register, Vendor, Operational checklist and Meeting notes templates pair with the threat review, the response consultancy, the response plan and the exercise. Everything here is organisational: negotiation is always led by the professional response consultant, and every question about ransom, reporting or the law goes to the family's lawyer and the insurer.
