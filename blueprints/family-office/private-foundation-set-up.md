            - name: Founding purpose statement for the family foundation
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every later choice, from legal form to who sits on the board, depends on what the foundation is for, and a vague purpose is the most common reason family foundations drift or split within a decade. One page that states the cause, the people or places served, the time horizon and the values behind it gives the lawyer something to draft from and the family something to agree on.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page purpose statement naming the cause, the beneficiaries or places served and the intended lifespan.
                2. Each founding family member's view gathered and differences written down rather than smoothed over.
                3. A short charitable objects clause drafted in plain words for the lawyer to adapt.
                4. The statement signed off by the founders and dated.

                ## Notes
                Keep the objects broad enough to survive changes in the cause over fifty years and narrow enough that a regulator can see the charitable purpose. Your lawyer will know how the local regulator reads objects clauses.

                ## Purpose
                A private foundation brings control, permanence and a family table, but also a board, accounts, filings and running costs that can reach tens of thousands a year. Before committing, families should set the foundation beside a donor-advised fund and simple direct giving, priced over ten years, so the choice is made on facts rather than prestige.

                ## Milestones
                1. The three options compared on control, cost, privacy, tax treatment, family involvement and permanence.
                2. A ten-year cost estimate for running a foundation at the giving level the family expects.
                3. The tax adviser's written view on how each option is treated where the founders are resident.
                4. A recorded decision, with reasons, to proceed with a foundation or not.

                ## Notes
                Many families run a donor-advised fund while the foundation is being set up, or keep one alongside it for anonymous gifts. Running the full donor-advised fund is its own area.

            - name: Choosing the foundation's legal form
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Depending on the country, a family foundation can be a charitable trust, a company limited by guarantee, a non-profit corporation or a civil-law private foundation, and each carries different liability, governance and amendment rules. Choosing with the lawyer before anything is drafted avoids having to restructure once assets are inside.

                ## Milestones
                1. The legal forms available in the candidate countries listed by the lawyer.
                2. Each form compared on liability of board members, ease of amendment, privacy of filings and set-up cost.
                3. The form's fit with the purpose statement and the intended lifespan checked.
                4. A legal form chosen and the reasons recorded in the founders' file.

                ## Notes
                A trust form usually puts duties on individual trustees, while a corporate form puts them on directors of an entity with its own legal personality. Ask the lawyer to explain what that means for your family members personally.
              priority: high

            - name: Jurisdiction shortlist for registering the foundation
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Where the foundation is registered decides which charity regulator supervises it, whether donors get tax relief, which filings are public and how easily it can fund work abroad. Families giving across the Caribbean, North America and Europe often find the answer is the founders' home country plus a friends-of entity elsewhere, not an offshore centre.

                ## Milestones
                1. Where the founders are tax resident and where grants will mostly go written on one page.
                2. Two or three jurisdictions compared on donor tax relief, regulator, public disclosure, cross-border grant rules and cost.
                3. Advisers in each shortlisted country consulted on registration and reporting.
                4. A jurisdiction chosen, with any second entity needed noted for later.

                ## Notes
                Tax relief for gifts usually only applies to a charity registered where the donor pays tax. Check this before choosing a jurisdiction for reasons of privacy or cost.

                The trust deed, articles, charter or statutes are the foundation's constitution: they fix the objects, how board members are appointed and removed, who has the final say and how the foundation can be amended or closed.             - name: Foundation bank account and payment controls
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Charity money must never mix with family money, and a foundation with a single signatory who can pay anyone is one fraudulent email away from losing a grant budget. Opening a dedicated account with dual authorisation, payment limits and a verification step for new payees sets the habit from the first transaction.

                ## Milestones

                3. A written rule that new or changed payee details are confirmed by phone to a known number.
                4. Signatory list and limits approved by the board and recorded in the minutes.

                ## Notes
                Banks run their own due diligence on new charities and may ask for the founding documents, registration and board details. Start early.

# Private Foundation Set-Up

This area is for a family turning its giving into a permanent institution: parents who want the children involved, a retiree founder thinking about what outlasts them, and the nonprofit professional hired or invited to help run it. It starts with foundations (a purpose statement, the choice between a foundation and simpler vehicles, legal form, jurisdiction, advisers, founding documents, registration, the bank account, the first board and the seed gift), then the operating routines of a working foundation, the knowledge a board member needs, decisions on policies, staff and administration, dated events from the first grant round to the first audit, situations particular to founders, children and staff, and finally specialist structures such as programme-related investment, operating foundations and a planned spend-down.

What repeats is a quarterly board meeting, a monthly cycle of application review, grant payments, reconciliation and minute filing, quarterly payout and endowment reports, and a yearly round of filings, conflict declarations and policy reviews. The Vendor, Board meeting, Month-end close, Compliance control, Risk register and Onboarding 30/60/90 templates pair with choosing an administrator, board meetings, the books, the filing calendar, the risk register and a new executive's first months. None of this is legal, tax or investment advice: every structural, tax and investment decision belongs with qualified advisers in each country involved.
