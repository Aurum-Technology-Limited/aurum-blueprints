            - name: Single-family office purpose and scope statement
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Families who skip this step end up with an office that does whatever the loudest relative asks, at a cost nobody agreed. A one-page statement saying why the office exists, which services it will and will not provide, and who it serves becomes the test for every later hire, system and budget line.

                ## Milestones
                1. The three to five reasons the family wants an office written in plain words, such as consolidated oversight, privacy or continuity after the founder.
                2. An in-scope list of services and an explicitly out-of-scope list agreed by the family sponsors.
                3. The family members and entities the office will serve named, with any branches left out recorded with the reason.
                4. A signed or minuted version of the statement filed as version 1.0 with its date.

                ## Notes
                Keep investment selection separate from the office's purpose at this stage. Whether the office will manage money itself or oversee outside managers is a later, bigger decision with regulatory consequences.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact

                - "List every service you imagine the office providing in its first two years"

            - name: Choosing the legal entity for the office
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Counsel will usually present a few forms for the office itself: a company owned by the family, a subsidiary of the family holding company, a company owned by a trust, or a limited partnership. The choice decides who employs the staff, who pays the costs, how the office is taxed and how easily ownership passes to the next generation, so it deserves a deliberate decision rather than whatever shelf company is handy.

                ## Milestones
                1. Counsel and the tax adviser engaged with a written brief based on the purpose statement.
                2. Two or three entity options compared on ownership, liability, tax, employment and succession.
                3. The ownership of the office entity decided and recorded, including whether the family or a trust holds the shares.
                4. The entity incorporated, with constitutional documents and registry extract filed.

                ## Notes
                This is a legal and tax decision. The project is about briefing the advisers well and recording what was decided and why.

              description: |-

                Families who have already built an office are usually generous with lessons, and an hour with a head of office who has done it for ten years saves months. # Single-Family Office Set-Up

This area is for the family that has outgrown a patchwork of private bankers, a trusted accountant and a founder's PA, often just after a business sale, and is weighing whether a dedicated office of its own is the answer. It starts with foundations (a purpose statement, an inventory of today's affairs, a build or outsource decision, the legal entity, the location, the set-up budget and a launch roadmap), then the operating routines that carry a new office through its first year, the knowledge a sponsor needs about models, regulation and tax, decisions on the first hires, partners, premises, systems and authority, dated milestones from launch day to the first annual report, situations for founders, parents, siblings and spread-out families, and specialist work on substance, sharing an office and knowing when to wind it down.

What repeats is a weekly steering call while the office is being built, a monthly budget check, decision log and office close, a quarterly update to the family and service review, and a yearly round of scope review, filings, insurance renewal and engagement letters. The Meeting notes, Month-end close, Vendor, Purchase decision, Board meeting and Onboarding 30/60/90 templates pair with the steering call, the office books, outsourced partners, premises, the office board and the first employee. None of this is legal, tax, regulatory or investment advice: agree the entity, location and every regulated activity with qualified counsel and tax advisers in each country involved.
