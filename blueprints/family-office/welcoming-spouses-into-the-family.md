            - name: Family stance on welcoming spouses
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most wealthy families have an unspoken rule about in-laws, and new spouses usually discover it by stepping on it: a question about money at dinner, an uninvited appearance at a council meeting. Writing a short statement of how the family wants to treat spouses, and why, turns those unspoken rules into something a parent can share warmly before the wedding rather than explain awkwardly after it.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page statement of how the family sees spouses: as members, guests or something in between.
                2. Three to five principles agreed, such as equal treatment across branches and no surprises on information.
                3. The views of at least two existing in-law spouses reflected in the draft.
                4. The statement accepted by the senior generation and dated.

                ## Notes
                Write it for the spouse who will read it, not for the lawyers. If a sentence would make a new spouse feel suspected, rewrite it.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:

                success_criteria: "A dated one-page statement of the family's stance on spouses, accepted by the senior generation and including input from existing in-laws."
                cadence: one-shot

              tasks:

                - "Draft three to five principles for how spouses should be treated"

                - "Bring the final statement to the senior generation for agreement"
            - name: Where spouses sit in the family documents
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Spouses can be named beneficiaries in one trust, excluded in another, barred from holding shares by the shareholder agreement and silent in the constitution, and nobody in the family can say which. A simple table of what each document says about spouses shows where the welcome the family intends clashes with the paperwork it has signed.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of every document that mentions spouses: constitution, trust deeds, letters of wishes, shareholder agreement, office policies.
                2. A table showing, for each document, whether spouses are included, excluded or not mentioned.
                3. Contradictions between documents marked for the family's lawyers.
                4. The table reviewed by the head of office and filed in the document vault.

                ## Notes
                Ask the lawyers to confirm how each clause reads. A summary written by family members is a map, not a legal opinion.

            - name: Welcome sponsor for each new spouse
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A new spouse cannot ask their partner every question about the family, because their partner is too close to it and often does not know the answers. Naming a sponsor, usually an established in-law or a cousin from another branch, gives them one trusted person to ask what the council does, who to call at the office and why an aunt went quiet at lunch.

                ## Milestones
                1. The sponsor role written in half a page: purpose, time commitment and limits.
                2. Three or four willing sponsors identified across branches.
                3. A sponsor matched to each spouse now in their first year.
                4. Each couple told who their sponsor is and how to change if the match does not work.

                ## Notes
                Avoid making the spouse's own parent-in-law the sponsor. The role works because the sponsor is outside the couple's household.
              priority: high
              frontmatter:
                mode: service
                output_kind: deliverable
                success_criteria: "Every spouse in their first year of marriage has a named sponsor outside their own household, recorded in the spouse register."
                cadence: one-shot

# Welcoming Spouses into the Family

This area is for the parent who wants a son or daughter's new spouse to feel like family within a year, not an outsider at the edge of every meeting, and who knows that wealthy families lose more goodwill through a clumsy welcome than through any prenuptial clause. It starts with foundations (the family's stance on spouses, where spouses sit in the documents today, a sponsor, an orientation pack, forum and information rules and the policy approval), then the operating machinery of the spouse register, welcome plans, sponsor check-ins and the in-law circle, the knowledge spouses and sponsors need, decisions on seats, conflicts and fairness, the dated events from first introductions to the one-year review, situations parents actually meet, and finally specialist work on measuring inclusion and keeping a large family consistent.

What repeats is a monthly register update, sponsor check-in, calendar share and request review, a quarterly in-law circle and review of stalled welcomes, and a yearly cycle of pack refreshes, privacy and information-tier reviews, assembly feedback, the in-law gathering and an anonymous inclusion survey. The Person, Onboarding plan, Feedback themes, Reading queue and Meeting notes templates pair with the spouse record, the ninety-day plan, assembly feedback, the reading list and the first-assembly debrief. Anything that touches trust interests, marital agreements or immigration is for the family's lawyers; this area is about people, information and belonging.
