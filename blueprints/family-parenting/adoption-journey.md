            - name: Deciding together whether adoption is right for you
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Agencies expect you to have thought hard before you enquire, and the questions they ask in the first visit (why adoption, why now, what you could not manage) are easier to answer if you have already asked them of each other. A few honest conversations, written up as a short statement, turn a hope into a decision you can explain to a social worker and to yourselves in two years' time.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three conversations held about motivation, timing, health, work and what kind of parenting you can offer.
                2. A one-page statement of why you want to adopt, in your own words.
                3. A short list of the things that worry each of you, written down rather than left unspoken.
                4. A clear decision recorded: enquire now, wait until a named date, or not adopt.

                ## Notes
                Single adopters can do this with a close friend or relative acting as a sounding board. If one partner is much keener than the other, give that gap time before you call an agency; assessors notice it quickly.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 60
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A written statement of motivation and a recorded go, wait or stop decision, agreed by everyone who would be a parent."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "6"
              tasks:
                - "Book a quiet evening this week for the first adoption conversation"
                - "Write separately why you each want to adopt, then swap and read"
                - "List the ages, needs and situations you each feel unsure about"
                - "Draft a one-page motivation statement together"
                - "Record your decision and the date you will revisit it if you wait"
            - name: Comparing domestic, intercountry and early permanence routes
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Adopting a child from your own country's care system, adopting from abroad and early permanence (where a baby is placed with you as foster carers while the courts decide) are very different processes, with different waits, costs, ages of children and emotional risks. Comparing them on the same few criteria before you register stops you starting down one route and discovering halfway that another fitted your family better.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each route described in a paragraph: typical children, timescale, cost and who runs it.
                2. The same five criteria scored for each route, such as age of child, waiting time, cost, contact and risk.
                3. Eligibility rules for intercountry adoption checked for any country you are considering.
                4. One route chosen, with the reasons written down.

                ## Notes
                Routes and rules vary a great deal by country, so take the facts from your national adoption body and the agencies themselves, not from forums.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A comparison of at least three adoption routes against the same criteria ends in one chosen route with its reasons recorded."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "5"
              tasks:
                - "Find your national adoption body's overview of the available routes"
                - "Write down five criteria that matter most to your family"
                - "Score each route against the same five criteria"
                - "Note the main emotional risk of each route, not just the logistics"
                - "Agree the route you will pursue and record why"

# Adoption Journey

This area is for anyone thinking about adopting, part way through it, or already parenting an adopted child, whether you are a couple, a single adopter or a family with children already at home. It starts with the foundations (the decision to adopt, choosing a route and an agency, documents, money and the people around you), then the routines that carry you through assessment, waiting and the first years, the skills adoptive parenting asks for, the decisions about matching and names, the dated moments from approval panel to the adoption order, the situations that change the plan, and finally specialist work such as intercountry dossiers, birth family meetings and placements under strain.

What repeats is a weekly couple check-in and session preparation during assessment, monthly logs and peer group meetings, a weekly rest rota after placement, and yearly letterbox letters, support reviews and adoption day. The Reading queue, Meeting notes, Savings goal, Operational checklist and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
