            - name: Choosing where to give birth
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most areas offer some mix of a hospital labour ward, a midwife-led unit beside or away from a hospital, and birth at home, and which ones are open to you depends on your pregnancy and your history. Comparing them on distance, transfer arrangements, the pain relief on offer and what your midwife recommends turns a default into a choice you made. Deciding by around 34 weeks leaves time to tour, pre-register and plan the route.

                ## Milestones
                1. The birth settings available locally listed, with the ones your midwife says suit your pregnancy marked.
                2. Each option compared on travel time, transfer arrangements and the pain relief it offers.
                3. Your questions about each setting answered by a midwife.
                4. A first choice and a fallback recorded in your maternity notes.

                ## Notes
                Your choice is not locked in. Most units let you change your mind, and labour itself sometimes changes it for you, so plan the route and the bags for both options.

                Before you start: this plan is general information to help you organise your own health. It is not medical advice and does not replace a doctor, nurse, pharmacist or other qualified professional. Check any change to treatment, medicine or diet with them first, and get urgent care if you feel unwell.

# Birth Plan & Hospital Bag

This area is for expectant parents in the last months of pregnancy, from choosing where to give birth to the first days back at home. It starts with the foundations (the birth setting, a tour, pre-registration, birth partners, a when-to-call card, transport, the notes folder, three bags and a fitted car seat), then the knowledge behind good choices, writing and agreeing the birth plan itself, the routines of the final six weeks, a few decisions on support and kit, the events from a practice drive to discharge day, versions for caesareans, home births, twins, second babies and solo parents, and finally the specialist situations.

What repeats is a Sunday countdown check, a Wednesday bag check, a Friday fuel and charger routine, a Saturday freezer batch, a Monday grocery slot and a Thursday help rota confirmation, with a monthly reread of the birth plan and a few minutes of daily breathing practice. The Operational checklist, Purchase decision, Meeting notes, Weekly meal plan and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
