            - name: Paced introductions to your partner's children
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Children who meet a parent's new partner too early, or as a surprise at the breakfast table, often decide the newcomer is a threat before they know anything else about them. Planning a slow sequence of short, low-stakes meetings over several months, led by each child's own parent, lets the relationship start as acquaintance rather than replacement. It matters most in the weeks before any talk of moving in.

                ## Milestones
                1. Both partners agree the relationship is stable enough for the children to meet, with a rough timeline written down.
                2. A first meeting held somewhere neutral and short, such as a park or a cafe, with the child's own parent present.
                3. At least four further meetings completed over two or three months, building from outings to time at home.
                4. Each child's reaction after every meeting noted by their own parent, and the pace slowed where a child struggled.

                ## Notes
                Younger children cope with 'this is my friend' at first; older children usually see through it and resent being managed, so tell them plainly. Keep overnight stays out of the early meetings.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 60
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: event-completion
                success_criteria: "Each child has met the new partner at least five times over two or more months, with their reactions noted by their own parent."
                cadence: phased
                effort_hours_estimate: "10"
              tasks:
                - "Agree with your partner the earliest month you would introduce the children"
                - "Choose a short neutral outing for the first meeting with each child"
                - "Ask each child afterwards what they thought, without pushing for approval"
                - "Plan the next three meetings, moving gradually from outings to home"
            - name: Partner expectations talk before merging households
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most blended families stumble on assumptions nobody said out loud: that the step-parent will discipline, that weekends belong to the couple, that money for one set of children comes from a shared pot. Each partner writing down their expectations on roles, discipline, money, time and space, then comparing notes, surfaces the gaps while they are still conversations rather than rows. It is the cheapest piece of preparation for living together with children.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each partner has written private answers to the same ten questions on roles, rules, money, time and space.
                2. The two sets of answers compared in one unhurried conversation, without the children around.
                3. A short list of agreements and a separate list of open disagreements written up.
                4. A next step for each open disagreement: decide now, revisit after three months, or take to a counsellor.

                ## Notes
                Useful questions include who disciplines whom, what happens on weekends the children are away, how child costs are split, and how much time each parent spends alone with their own children.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A written list of agreed expectations and open disagreements, each disagreement with a next step, exists and both partners have read it."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:
                - "Ask the agent to draft ten expectation questions for a blended household"
                - "Write your own answers privately before seeing your partner's"
                - "Book a two-hour child-free evening to compare answers"
                - "Write up agreements and open disagreements in one shared note"
            - name: Telling the children you are moving in together
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                How children first hear that two households are merging colours how they greet the change for months. Each parent telling their own children, ideally on the same day so nobody hears it second-hand from a step-sibling, with concrete answers about bedrooms, schools and time with the other parent, takes much of the fear out of the news.

                ## Milestones
                1. A date and setting agreed for each parent to tell their own children.
                2. Answers prepared for the practical questions each child is likely to ask: where they will sleep, which school, when they see their other parent.
                3. Every child told on the same day, by their own parent.
                4. A follow-up chat held with each child within a week to catch worries that surfaced later.

                ## Notes
                Where it is safe, tell the children's other parent before the children, so they are not caught off guard and can reassure rather than react.
              priority: high

            - name: Step-parent consent and permissions check
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                In many countries a step-parent has no automatic legal say over a step-child's medical treatment, school trips or passport, even after years of living together. Finding out what the step-parent can and cannot sign where you live, and arranging written permissions for the everyday situations that matter, avoids being turned away at a hospital desk or school office.

                ## Milestones

                4. Written permission letters from the legal parents in place for the situations agreed.

                ## Notes

# Blended & Step-Family Life

This area is for couples bringing children from earlier relationships under one roof, and for the step-parents, step-children and step-siblings who then have to learn to live together. It starts with the foundations (paced introductions, the expectations talk, telling the children, a residence map, the step-parent's role, names, bedrooms, house rules, contacts and permissions), then the routines that keep a blended household steady, the skills step-parents and parents need, the bigger decisions about homes, money, names and wills, the milestone events, versions for step-parents without children, part-time and teenage step-children, later-life and bereaved families and an 'ours' baby, and finally specialist work on repairing a strained bond, keeping step-relationships after a separation and finding peer support.

What repeats is a Sunday couple check-in, weekly one-to-one time with your own children, a Friday arrival routine for children coming from the other home, a midweek all-together dinner, monthly step-parent outings, money reconciliations and adjustment checks on varied days, a quarterly rules and roles review and a yearly special-days plan. The Meeting notes, Monthly budget and bills, Reading queue and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
