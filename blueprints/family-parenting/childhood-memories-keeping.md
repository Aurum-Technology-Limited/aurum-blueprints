                ## Purpose
                Photos of a typical child are scattered across two parents' phones, an old laptop, a nursery app, grandparents' chat threads and a box in the loft.             - name: Keeping policy for keepsakes and photos
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Without a rule, every drawing, certificate and party invitation feels too precious to bin, and by age ten the loft is full of crates nobody opens. A one-page policy agreed by both parents, such as one archive box per child per school stage and twenty favourite photos a month, makes each keep-or-go decision quick and guilt-free. Children later value a curated box far more than an unsorted hoard.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page policy naming what is always kept, what is photographed then let go, and what is never kept.
                2. A physical limit set per child, such as one box per school stage.
                3. A digital limit set for favourites, such as twenty starred photos a month.
                4. Both parents signed up to the same policy.

                ## Notes
                Typical always-keep items: the hospital bracelet, first shoes, school reports and a handful of letters and drawings in the child's own writing. Typical photograph-then-let-go items: most artwork, certificates and party cards.
              priority: high

                output_kind: decision
