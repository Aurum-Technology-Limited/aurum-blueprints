            - name: Telling the children about the separation together
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Children remember how they were told long after they forget the reasons they were given. One conversation delivered by both parents together, with the same simple explanation, the same reassurance that it is not their fault and a clear picture of what happens next week, spares them from piecing it together from two different versions. Plan it before either parent moves out, if it is safe to be in the same room.

                ## Milestones
                1. A shared script of five or six sentences agreed by both parents, with no blame and no adult details.
                2. A date and quiet place chosen, with no exam, party or school trip the next morning.
                3. Answers agreed in advance for the questions children ask most: why, where will I live, and will I still see you both.
                4. The children told together, with time left for questions and a normal evening afterwards.
                5. A one-to-one follow-up held with each child within the next week.

                ## Notes
                If being in the same room is not safe, each parent can tell the children separately using the agreed words. A family support service can help you shape the script.
              priority: high

              frontmatter:

                output_kind: event-completion
                success_criteria: "Both parents have told the children using an agreed written script, and each child has had a one-to-one follow-up within seven days."
                cadence: one-shot

            - name: First eight weeks interim care arrangement
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The weeks straight after one parent moves out are when routines collapse and arguments start over who has the children on Saturday. A short interim arrangement, written down and agreed to run for eight weeks, gives children predictability while the long-term plan is worked out. Labelling it interim also stops a stopgap pattern quietly becoming the permanent one.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page interim schedule covering school days, weekends, pickups and drop-offs.
                2. Both parents' agreement recorded in a message, with an end date eight weeks away.
                3. The children shown the schedule in a form they understand, such as a colour-coded wall calendar.
                4. A review conversation booked for week six.

                ## Notes
                Write the word interim on the schedule and in the message. If you are worried that a temporary pattern could affect later decisions, ask a family law adviser how that works where you live.
              priority: high

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Messages spread across texts, emails, voicemails and notes in school bags get missed, and missed messages turn into arguments. Agreeing one channel for children's business, whether a dedicated co-parenting app, a single email thread or one messaging chat, puts every arrangement in one searchable place. It also keeps late-night replies out of the personal chats you used as a couple.

                ## Milestones
                1. Two or three options compared on cost, shared calendar, expense tracking and message history.

                3. Reply expectations set, such as within 24 hours for routine matters and a phone call for anything urgent.
                4. Every other channel kept for emergencies only from an agreed start date.

                ## Notes
                Some co-parenting apps keep messages that cannot be edited or deleted, which helps when trust is low. A plain email thread works well when cooperation is good.

            - name: Choosing a weekly time-sharing schedule
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Alternating weeks, a 2-2-3 rotation, 2-2-5-5, or school weeks in one home with alternate weekends in the other each suit different ages, distances and work patterns. Drawing the options out side by side against your children's ages, the school run and both work schedules leads to a pattern chosen for the children, not one that splits time to the hour to keep score.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three candidate patterns drawn out on a blank four-week calendar.
                2. Each pattern checked against travel time to school, both work schedules and the youngest child's age.
                3. The children's views heard in a way that suits their age, without asking them to pick a parent.
                4. One pattern agreed in writing with a three-month trial and a review date.

                ## Notes
                Very young children usually cope better with shorter gaps between seeing each parent, while teenagers often want fewer handovers. A family mediator can help if you are stuck.

# Co-Parenting After Separation

This area is for separated parents raising children across two homes, whether the split happened last month or years ago. It starts with the foundations (telling the children, an interim arrangement, one message channel, an information sheet, the weekly schedule, the parenting plan, a shared calendar, the second home, telling school and doctors, and who decides what), then the routines that keep two homes in step, the communication skills that keep children out of the middle, decisions on costs, child support, house rules, mediation, documents and new partners, the holidays and big occasions, versions for babies, teenagers, long distance, shift work and additional needs, and finally plans for refusal, parallel parenting, safety concerns and court orders.

What repeats is a weekly rhythm (a Tuesday video call for long-distance families, a Wednesday reread of sent messages, the Thursday bag check, Friday school letters and the Sunday update), monthly slots for costs, child support, the business meeting, the calendar, the record and rota-based schedules on different days, quarterly plan check-ins and yearly reviews of the plan, passports, festive holidays and the summer split. The Operational checklist, Meeting notes, Monthly budget and bills, Course and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them.
