              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Children rarely announce that they are being bullied; they show it through Sunday night stomach aches, lost or broken belongings, missing dinner money, a new route home or a sudden wish to skip a club they loved. Writing down the signs that fit your child's age, and what is normal for them, means both parents notice a change within days rather than months.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page list of physical, emotional, social and online signs relevant to your child's age.
                2. A short note of your child's usual sleep, appetite, mood and friendships to compare against.
                3. The list shared with the other parent and any regular carer, such as a grandparent who does pickups.
                4. Any sign already present circled and dated.

                ## Notes
                One sign on its own often means nothing. A cluster of two or three that arrive together, or a change that lasts more than a fortnight, is worth a gentle conversation.
              priority: high

            - name: Telling bullying apart from a falling out
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Schools usually define bullying as behaviour that is repeated, intended to hurt and involves an imbalance of power, and they respond to it differently from a one-off argument between friends. Knowing the difference helps you choose the right words when you contact the school and stops a normal friendship wobble being escalated too fast, or a real pattern being brushed off as banter.

                ## Milestones
                1. The school's own definition of bullying found and copied into your notes.
                2. What has happened to your child sorted into one-off incidents and repeated behaviour.
                3. The type of behaviour named: physical, verbal, social exclusion, online or a mix.
                4. A one-sentence summary written that you could say to a teacher without exaggeration.

                ## Notes
                A single serious incident, such as an assault or a threat, needs reporting even if it has only happened once.
              priority: medium

            - name: First calm conversation after your child tells you
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The way you react in the first five minutes decides whether your child tells you anything next time. Planning that conversation, listening fully, thanking them for telling you, not promising to keep it secret and not storming to the school that evening, keeps their trust while you work out what to do.

                ## Milestones
                1. A quiet time and place chosen, such as a car ride or a walk, where your child does not have to look at you.
                2. Your child's account heard in full and written down afterwards in their words.
                3. Your child told clearly that it is not their fault and that you will decide the next step together.
                4. One small agreed next step, such as you emailing the teacher, explained to your child before you take it.

                ## Notes
                Avoid advising your child to hit back. It usually gets the bullied child into trouble and makes the school's job harder.

# Dealing With Bullying

This area is for parents who suspect, or have just been told, that their child is being bullied, and for the harder case where your child is the one doing it. It starts with the foundations (knowing the signs, the first conversation, a dated log, saved evidence and a named adult at school), then the routines that keep you informed and the school accountable, the skills your child and you can practise, the decisions about escalating, changing class or moving school, the dated moments that need a plan, versions for young children, teenagers, children with additional needs and children bullying others, and finally the specialist situations such as image sharing, an adult who bullies and long-term recovery.

What repeats is a short daily good-thing question and bedtime worry slot, a Sunday look at the incident log, a Friday update to the school while things are live, a monthly wellbeing and attendance check on varied days, a termly review of the school's plan and a yearly look back once it is over. The Metrics log, Meeting notes, Reading queue and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
