            - name: Choosing the day, time and length of family meetings
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Family meetings fail most often for a dull reason: no fixed slot, so they happen only when something has gone wrong. Picking one day, one start time and a maximum length that suits the youngest child turns the meeting into a fixture rather than a summons. Twenty minutes on a Sunday after lunch is a common starting point.

                ## Milestones
                1. Two or three candidate slots compared against every family member's regular commitments.
                2. One day, start time and maximum length agreed, with the length set by the youngest child's attention span.
                3. The slot added as a repeating entry in every adult's calendar.
                4. The children told the slot and why it was chosen.

                ## Notes
                Avoid the hour before bed and the first hour after school. Tired children turn every agenda item into a negotiation.

            - name: Four levels of family decision-making
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Children lose faith in family meetings fast if they are asked for a view and then overruled without warning. Sorting decisions into four levels before the first meeting, parents decide, parents decide after hearing the children, the family votes, and the child decides, tells everyone in advance how much their voice will count. Safety, health and money limits normally stay with the adults.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of twenty typical household decisions, from bedtimes to holiday destinations.
                2. Each decision placed in one of the four levels, agreed by both parents.
                3. The levels written on one page in words a seven-year-old would follow.
                4. The page explained to the children at a meeting, with questions answered.

                ## Notes
                Expect to move some decisions up a level as children grow. A decision that sits with parents at six may belong to the child at fourteen.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:

# Family Meetings & Decisions

This area is for parents who want a short, regular family meeting where the week gets planned, grumbles get aired and some decisions are genuinely made together with the children. It starts with foundations (the slot, who decides what, the agenda, ground rules, a talking object, an issues board, a decisions log and the first meeting), then the weekly machinery that keeps meetings alive, skills such as chairing, problem-solving, listening and choosing between a vote and a parent call, real decisions taken as a family, the occasions that need a meeting of their own, versions for toddlers, teenagers, mixed ages, step-families, single parents, neurodivergent children, away parents and live-in grandparents, and finally specialist work on children's written proposals, consent decisions, a family mission statement and teen-led meetings.

What repeats is the weekly meeting itself, a parents' huddle the day before, a mid-week action check, a monthly appreciation read-out, rota and format reviews, decision review dates, a start-of-term meeting each quarter and an annual family decisions day. The Meeting notes, Habit tracker, Reading queue, Trip and Purchase decision templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
