            - name: Audit of the traditions you already keep
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most families already keep more traditions than they realise: pancakes on a Saturday, the same film every December, the walk after a birthday lunch. Listing everything the household does on repeat, however small, shows what the children already count on before you add anything new. It also exposes the customs that survive only out of habit.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of every repeated family custom, weekly, yearly and occasional, with at least fifteen entries.
                2. Each entry marked with the adult who usually makes it happen.
                3. Each entry marked keep, adapt or question.
                4. The list saved where both parents can add to it.

                ## Notes
                Ask the children separately what they would put on the list. They often name small things adults never noticed, like a song in the car or the way the last biscuit is shared.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A written list of at least fifteen existing family customs, each tagged with a lead adult and a keep, adapt or question mark."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Write down every weekly custom the household keeps, however small"
                - "Add the yearly customs, from birthdays to festivals and holidays"
                - "Ask each child what the family always does that they would miss"
                - "Mark each custom keep, adapt or question and note who runs it"
            - name: Traditions each parent grew up with
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Two adults bring two sets of childhood customs into one home, and arguments about Christmas Eve or Eid morning are usually arguments about which childhood counts. Each parent writing down what their family did, and how it felt, makes the differences visible before the next festival rather than during it.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each parent's list of childhood traditions written separately, with the feeling each one gave.
                2. The two lists compared side by side and the overlaps marked.
                3. Three traditions from each side that each parent most wants to pass on.
                4. Any tradition one parent strongly does not want repeated named calmly.

                ## Notes
                Single parents can do this alone, or with a sibling or old friend who remembers your childhood. The point is to know what you are carrying, not to copy it.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "Both parents have a written list of childhood traditions, with three each they most want to pass on and any they would rather not repeat."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "Write your own list of childhood traditions before talking it over"
                - "Note beside each one whether it felt warm, dull or tense as a child"

# Family Traditions & Rituals

This area is for parents who want their children to grow up with traditions they love and can carry on, from the weekly family meal to the festivals that shape the year. It starts with foundations (an audit of what you already do, each parent's childhood customs, three family values, the children's favourites, a traditions calendar, a fair split of the hidden work and a budget), then the weekly, monthly and seasonal rituals that keep belonging alive, skills such as telling festival stories and learning family recipes, decisions on mixed-faith calendars, gifts and overloaded seasons, the yearly occasions, versions for babies, step-families, two homes, single parents, teenagers, grief and grown children, and finally specialist work on sensory-friendly festivals, moving abroad, family service and a written handbook.

What repeats is a weekly family meal and game night, a short highs and lows at bedtime, a monthly adventure day and gratitude jar read-out, a seasonal tradition each quarter, festival run-up checks, a debrief after each big occasion and a yearly review each January. The Habit tracker, Operational checklist, Meeting notes, Reading queue and Savings goal templates pair with the projects that name them.
