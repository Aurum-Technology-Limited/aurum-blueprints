            - name: Baseline week of homework observation
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before changing anything, watch one ordinary school week and write down what homework arrives, when it is started, how long it really takes, where it happens and where it goes wrong. Most families argue about homework from impressions; a week of notes shows whether the problem is the amount, the timing, the place or the help, and points to the one fix worth making first.

                ## Milestones
                1. Five school days of notes covering task, start time, finish time, place and mood.
                2. Total homework minutes for the week added up and compared with what the school expects.
                3. The two hardest moments of the week identified, with what led up to each.
                4. One change chosen to try first, written at the bottom of the notes.

                ## Notes
                Observe without stepping in more than usual. The point is an honest picture, not a good week.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge

                - "Set up a simple note with columns for task, start, finish, place and mood"
                - "Record every homework session for five school days"
                - "Add up the week's minutes and circle the two hardest moments"
                - "Choose the single change you will try first and tell your child"

                ## Notes

            - name: Deciding how much parents help
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Teachers set homework to practise a skill and to see what a child can do, so a page finished by a parent tells them nothing and teaches the child less. Deciding together, as parents, what help you will give (reading instructions aloud, asking questions, checking it is complete) and what you will not (writing answers, fixing every mistake) ends the slow drift into doing it for them.

                ## Milestones

                2. The same list shared with grandparents, childminders or anyone else who supervises homework.
                3. Your child told what kind of help to expect and how to ask for it.
                4. A check one month later on whether the line has held.

                ## Notes
                If your child cannot start a task even after you have read it through together, that is information for the teacher, not a signal to take over.
              priority: high

# Homework Support

This area helps parents set up homework so it gets done without nightly battles, and so children gradually learn to manage it themselves. It runs from the foundations (a baseline week, the school's expectations, a study spot, a fixed time and a clear line on how much you help) through the weekly routines that keep it going, the skills to learn on both sides of the table, decisions such as tutors, desks and practice apps, dated moments like parents' evening and a new school year, situations such as two homes or several children, and finally teenage independence and study habits that make learning last.

What repeats is a Sunday look-ahead, a midweek platform check, the evening bag pack, short spelling and tables practice, a Friday look at marked work, a monthly routine review and a termly reset. The Habit tracker, Operational checklist, Metrics log, Purchase decision and Meeting notes templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
