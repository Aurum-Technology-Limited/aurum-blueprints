            - name: Whole-home household job audit
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before giving children jobs, it helps to see the real workload: most families run 60 to 90 distinct tasks a week, from emptying the dishwasher to feeding the cat, and parents usually underestimate it by half. Listing every job for one ordinary week, with who does it and roughly how many minutes it takes, shows which jobs could pass to a child and stops the chart becoming a random wish list.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of every recurring household job, grouped by room or part of the home.
                2. Each job marked with who does it now and roughly how long it takes.
                3. Jobs a child could do, fully or in part, highlighted on the list.
                4. Jobs that must stay with adults for safety reasons marked clearly.

                ## Notes
                Walk the house room by room rather than working from memory; the forgotten jobs are the small daily ones like wiping the table or relining the bin.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A dated household job list covers every room, shows owner and minutes for each job, and flags at least ten jobs a child could take on."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "Walk each room and write down every job done there in a normal week"
                - "Add who does each job now and the minutes it takes"
                - "Highlight the jobs a child could do alone or with help"
                - "Mark the adult-only jobs, such as anything with sharp blades or strong chemicals"
                - "Repeat the job audit to catch new jobs as the children grow @recurring(yearly)"
            - name: Age-matched job list for each child
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Two-year-olds can put toys in a box and nine-year-olds can hang out washing, yet many families give every child the same few jobs or none at all. Matching jobs to each child's age, coordination and attention span means jobs are hard enough to matter and easy enough to finish, which is what builds the habit.

                ## Milestones
                1. A personal list of three to six jobs for each child, drawn from the household audit.
                2. Each job checked against the child's age, reach and attention span.
                3. One stretch job per child that they can grow into over the next few months.
                4. Both parents happy that the lists feel fair between the children.

                ## Notes
                Typical starting points: ages 2 to 4 tidy toys and put clothes in the basket, ages 5 to 7 set the table and feed pets, ages 8 to 11 empty the dishwasher and hoover, teens cook simple meals and clean a bathroom. Adjust for your child, not the list.

# Kids' Household Jobs Chart

This area is for parents who want children to pitch in at home in a way that lasts, from a toddler posting socks into the washing machine to a teenager cooking the family dinner. It starts with the foundations (a household job audit, age-matched job lists, a parents' agreement, the chart itself and a launch the children help shape), then the daily and weekly routines that keep jobs happening, the skills to teach, the decisions about rewards and missed jobs, a handful of dated moments, the family situations that change the plan, and finally adapted charts, teen cooking and children teaching each other.

What repeats is a few daily jobs at breakfast, after dinner and for the pet, a Sunday chart reset with weekly rotations and tidies, a monthly level-up review, seasonal job lists each quarter and a new grown-up job on every birthday. The Household chores, Purchase decision, Habit tracker, Operational checklist and Weekly meal plan templates pair with the projects that name them.
