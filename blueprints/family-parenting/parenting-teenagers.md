            - name: Two-week conversation log with your teenager
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most parents of teenagers feel they talk all the time, yet a log usually shows that nine in ten exchanges are reminders, logistics or corrections. Writing down every conversation longer than a minute for fourteen days, with where it happened, who started it and how it ended, shows when your teen actually opens up and what shuts them down. Every later project in this area works better with that picture in hand.

                ## Milestones
                1. A simple log on paper or phone with columns for time, place, topic, who started it and how it ended.
                2. Fourteen days logged, including the days with almost nothing to write.
                3. The two places and times your teen talked most freely circled.
                4. The three most common conversation enders, such as a lecture or a glance at your phone, named in writing.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Metrics log** template. Keep it private and about your side of the conversation, not a record of their behaviour.
              priority: high

            - name: Family non-negotiables for the teenage years
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Teenagers push on every rule at once, and parents who defend twenty rules equally end up defending none. Agreeing between the adults on four to six genuine non-negotiables, typically safety, honesty about where they are, school attendance and respect in the house, frees you to be flexible on everything else. The short list becomes the thing you never bargain over and the reason you can say yes more often.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every current house rule written on one list, however small.
                2. Each rule sorted by the adults into non-negotiable, negotiable or drop.
                3. A final list of no more than six non-negotiables, each in one plain sentence.
                4. The list explained to your teenager, with the reasons, in a calm conversation.

                ## Notes
                If two parents are involved, agree the list privately first. A teenager who sees parents disagree on a non-negotiable will treat it as negotiable.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A written list of no more than six non-negotiables, agreed by every parent in the house and explained to the teenager."
                cadence: one-shot

            - name: Proportionate consequences for broken agreements
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                When a curfew is missed by an hour, many parents reach for the biggest sanction available in the moment, a month's grounding or a confiscated phone, then quietly drop it a week later. A short ladder of consequences agreed in advance, linked to the agreement that was broken and ending in a clear path back, is fairer and much easier to enforce. Teenagers accept consequences they could predict far better than ones invented in anger.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three or four levels of consequence written, from a conversation up to a temporary loss of a freedom.
                2. Each consequence linked to the kind of agreement it follows, such as lateness or not messaging.
                3. A rule that every consequence has an end date and a way to earn trust back.
                4. The ladder agreed by every parent and explained to your teen.

                ## Notes
                Decide consequences the next day, not at the door at midnight. On the night, say only that you will talk tomorrow.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A written ladder of three or four consequences, each with an end date, is agreed by all parents and explained to the teenager."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "List the agreements your teen most often breaks"
                - "Write three or four levels of consequence from mildest to most serious"

# Parenting Teenagers

This area is for parents of thirteen to eighteen-year-olds who want to stay close to their teenager while handing over freedom at a sensible pace, through moods, curfews, friendships, first relationships and risky choices. It starts with the foundations (a conversation log, family non-negotiables, a freedoms map, the curfew agreement and rescue code word, a contact list, home alone and privacy rules, and proportionate consequences), then the weekly and monthly routines that keep communication open, the listening and talking skills that matter most, decisions on parties, sleepovers, appearance and jobs, the big nights and trips, versions for two homes, step-families, single and working parents, and finally the specialist plans for substances, a missing teen, broken trust, exploitation and constant conflict.

What repeats is a weekly rhythm (one-to-one time on Saturdays, a family meal your teen chooses on Wednesdays, the Sunday week-ahead chat, the Friday lift rota, a Thursday word of praise and a Tuesday listening practice), a school-day decompression habit, monthly freedoms, sleep and support check-ins on varied days, quarterly agreement reviews and a yearly reread of the non-negotiables and the freedoms map. The Metrics log, Habit tracker, Meeting notes, Operational checklist and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
