                ## Purpose
                Most parents who want to shout less have no idea how often they actually do it, and guesses swing wildly with mood.             - name: Parent trigger map for the hard moments
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Children rarely cause the shout on their own: it is usually their behaviour landing on a parent who is late, hungry, on the phone or replaying a hard day at work. Mapping your own triggers, physical and situational, lets you spot the moments you are most likely to snap and put a buffer in place before them. It is the half of discipline that most books skip.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of your top five personal triggers, such as lateness, mess after cleaning or being ignored.
                2. Each trigger matched to the body signal that warns you, such as a clenched jaw or raised shoulders.
                3. One buffer written for each trigger, such as leaving ten minutes earlier or putting the phone in a drawer at pick-up.
                4. The map shared with your partner so they can step in when they see the signs.

                ## Notes
                Use the two-week tally: your triggers are usually hiding in the busiest time slots, not in the child's worst behaviour.

# Positive Discipline Strategies

This area is for parents of children roughly four to twelve who want to guide behaviour with connection, clear limits and consequences that teach, and to stop relying on shouting, threats or smacking. It runs from foundations (a shouting tally, your own trigger map, five house rules, a consequences menu and a pause routine) through the weekly and monthly machinery, the core skills of calm limits, emotion coaching and problem solving, decisions on rewards and screens, plans for backchat, lying and taking things, school and family events, versions for step-parents, two homes, single parents and neurodivergent children, and finally making amends, getting outside help and an annual review.

What repeats is a short daily encouragement count and ten minutes at lights out, a Friday debrief between carers, weekly problem-solving chats and handover notes, monthly tally reruns and preteen privilege reviews on varied days, a quarterly refresh of the carers' sheet and the house rules, and a yearly review. The Metrics log, Habit tracker, Operational checklist, Reading queue and Meeting notes templates pair with the projects that name them.
