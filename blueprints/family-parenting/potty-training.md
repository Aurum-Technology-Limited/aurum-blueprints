              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most children show they are ready somewhere between 18 months and three years, and starting before the signs are there usually means weeks of accidents and a reluctant child. Watching for a fortnight for dry spells of two hours, hiding to poo, telling you about a wet nappy and being able to pull pants up and down gives you evidence rather than a guess based on a friend's child or a birthday.

                ## Milestones
                1. A written list of eight to ten readiness signs, covering body, communication and behaviour.
                2. Two weeks of notes on which signs your child shows and how often.
                3. A clear verdict recorded: ready now, nearly ready, or not yet with a date to look again.
                4. Your partner or co-parent agreeing with the verdict.

                ## Notes

                Withholding, where a child holds in poo because it hurts or scares them, is one of the most common reasons training stalls, and it can quietly turn into constipation and soiling. # Potty Training

This area takes a toddler from nappies to using the toilet alone, and later to dry nights, with every adult in their life doing the same thing. It starts with the foundations (readiness signs, a method you both agree, kit, shared words, a start date and a briefing for carers), then the daily and weekly routines that carry training, the skills to teach and learn such as wiping, standing to wee and coping with public toilets, the decisions that come up mid-way, the dated moments like an intensive weekend or the first nursery day in pants, the situations that change the plan, and finally night-time dryness, additional needs and early-start approaches.

What repeats is a few daily prompts and a last wee at bedtime, a weekly progress review, nursery handover and laundry rhythm, and monthly restocks of cleaning kit and spare clothes.
