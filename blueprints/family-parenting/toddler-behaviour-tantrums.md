                ## Purpose
                Memory is a poor witness after a bad week: one meltdown in the car park can feel like ten, and the quiet days vanish. Logging every tantrum, bite or hit for fourteen days, with the time, what happened just before, how long it lasted and what you did, turns a fog of bad moments into a pattern you can act on. Every later decision in this area is easier with that record in hand.

                ## Milestones
                1. A simple log set up on paper or phone with columns for time, setting, trigger, length and response.
                2. Fourteen consecutive days logged by whichever adult was present, including the calm days.
                3. The three most common triggers and the two worst times of day circled.
                4. A one-paragraph summary written and shared with the other main carer.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Metrics log** template. Log the boring details, such as whether a snack was late or a nap was skipped, because those are usually the pattern.

                ## Purpose

            - name: Shared first ten seconds tantrum script
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                When one parent kneels and soothes while the other says stop that right now, a toddler learns that the response depends on who is in the room, and the tantrums often get longer. Agreeing a short script for the first ten seconds, the words, the tone, where you stand and what you do with your hands, gives both of you the same starting move. It also takes the panic out of the moment because the decision is already made.

                ## Milestones
                1. Both parents have each described what they currently do in the first ten seconds.
                2. A script of no more than three short sentences agreed, with what to do if the child is in danger.
                3. The script practised out loud by both parents at least twice.
                4. The script written on a card and shared with any regular carer.

                ## Notes
                Keep it short enough to remember while being kicked. A sensible default is to get low, stay close, name the feeling once and keep everyone safe, then say very little until the peak passes.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:

                output_kind: artifact

                cadence: one-shot

# Toddler Behaviour & Tantrums

This area is for parents of children roughly one to four years old who are living with tantrums, biting, hitting, throwing and the word no, and who want to respond the same way every time instead of improvising under pressure. It starts with the foundations (a two-week log, a trigger map, a shared script, a handful of safety rules, a calm spot, a picture routine and your own plan for staying calm), then the daily and weekly machinery that keeps things steady, the skills toddlers and parents practise together, decisions on time-out, rewards and battles worth picking, the events that test everyone, versions for new babies, two homes, single parents and working evenings, and finally the specialist work on persistent biting, daily hitting and knowing when to ask for help.

What repeats is up to three short daily habits (ten minutes of child-led play, noticing gentle moments, a bedtime reconnection), a Sunday parents' check-in, a Friday word with the nursery, weekly puppet and sign practice, monthly log and routine reviews on varied days, and a quarterly refresh of the babysitter guide and the red flags check. The Metrics log, Operational checklist, Habit tracker, Reading queue, Trip and Meeting notes templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
