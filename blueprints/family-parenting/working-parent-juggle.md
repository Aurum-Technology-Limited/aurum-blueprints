            - name: Two-week log of work and family collisions
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before asking for new hours or reshuffling who does what, keep a plain record for ten working days of every moment work and family pulled in different directions: the 8:45 meeting that clashed with drop-off, the call from nursery at 11, the email answered during bath time. Two weeks of evidence shows which clashes are structural and which were bad luck, so the fixes that follow aim at the right problem.

                ## Milestones
                1. Ten working days logged with the time, what clashed, who covered and what it cost.
                2. Each clash tagged as structural (it happens most weeks) or one-off.
                3. The three most frequent structural clashes ranked by how much strain they cause.
                4. A short summary read by both adults in the household, or by you and one trusted helper.

                ## Notes
                Log on your phone the moment it happens; memories at the end of the day soften the pattern. Include the quiet costs too, such as leaving a meeting early and catching up at 10pm.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A ten-day collision log exists with every clash tagged structural or one-off and the top three structural clashes ranked in writing."

# Working Parent Juggle

This area is for parents holding down a demanding job, as an employee, a manager or self-employed, who want family life to stop living in the gaps. It starts with foundations (a log of where work and family collide, your employer's policies, an on-call parent for each weekday and a sick-child protocol), moves through the weekly and monthly routines that hold the line, the skills of doing a full job in fewer hours, decisions about patterns, hours and careers, dated moments such as crunch weeks and new jobs, situations from the first nursery year to the sandwich years, and ends with team-level and senior work such as job shares and parents' networks.

What repeats is a daily shutdown, a Friday clash check, protected evenings, a weekly slot with each child, a monthly load review with your partner, monthly hours and one-to-one checks, a quarterly look at your flexible arrangement and a yearly reset of the whole setup. The Operational checklist, 1:1, Habit tracker, Metrics log, Development plan and Onboarding 30/60/90 templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
