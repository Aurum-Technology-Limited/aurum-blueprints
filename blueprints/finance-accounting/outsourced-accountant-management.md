              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most owners hire an accountant without deciding which jobs they actually want gone, so they pay for work they would happily do and keep work they hate. Listing every finance task in the business, who does it now and how long it takes, gives you a clear split between what the firm will do, what you will do and what a bookkeeper might do in between.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every recurring finance task listed with who does it now and the hours it takes each month.
                2. Each task marked as outsource, keep or undecided, with a reason.
                3. The jobs that must legally stay with the owner or director identified.
                4. A one-page split of responsibilities agreed with any business partner.

            - name: Engagement letter review before signing
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The engagement letter is the contract: it sets out what the firm will do, what you must provide, the fees, how either side can end it and the limits on the firm's liability. Reading it line by line before signing, and asking for changes where it does not match what was promised, prevents most of the disputes that sour accountant relationships.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every service in the letter checked against the proposal you accepted.

                3. Fee terms, payment dates and the basis for extra charges understood.
                4. Notice period, liability cap and complaints route noted.
                5. Any mismatches raised in writing and a corrected letter signed.

                ## Notes
                Liability caps are normal, but check the figure. If a service you discussed is missing from the letter, assume it is not included until the letter says otherwise.

# Outsourced Accountant Management

This area is for owners, founders and freelancers who pay someone outside the business to keep the books, prepare the accounts or file the returns, and who want that arrangement to work rather than drift. It starts with the foundations (deciding what to hand over, writing a brief, shortlisting and vetting firms, comparing fees and signing a clear engagement letter), then the routines that keep the work flowing, the knowledge that makes you a sharp client, the decisions about switching, splitting or adding advisers, the dated moments such as the first ninety days and the year-end planning meeting, situations for freelancers, founders and partners, and finally the specialist work of service levels, data terms and a formal tender.

What repeats is a monthly document drop, a weekly answer to the firm's queries, a monthly check-in call and fee check, a quarterly look at the reports you receive and the deadlines ahead, and a yearly scope review, scorecard, planning meeting and year-end pack. The Purchase decision, Vendor, Meeting notes and Onboarding plan templates pair with the projects that name them.
