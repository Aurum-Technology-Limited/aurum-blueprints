              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A long-only fund manager, a long/short hedge fund pod and an equity research desk want different pitches: a three-to-five-year compounder, a six-to-twelve-month catalyst trade, or a coverage-style rating with a price target. Writing down the seats you are interviewing for, and what each one expects, decides the holding period, the length and the tone of every pitch you build after this.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of the roles in your pipeline, each tagged long-only, long/short, multi-manager or equity research.
                2. For each type, the expected horizon, pitch length and whether a short is likely to be asked for.
                3. Two or three people in those seats asked what a good pitch sounds like at their firm.
                4. A one-paragraph pitch brief that every later project in this area is built against.

                ## Notes
                If you are interviewing across seat types, build for the strictest one (usually the hedge fund pod) and trim for the others. A pitch built for a long-only interview rarely survives a catalyst-focused PM.

            - name: Screening for a pitchable company
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The idea itself is half the pitch: a mega-cap everyone owns invites the question "what do you know that 40 analysts do not?", while an illiquid micro-cap makes a fund manager switch off. Screening for a business you can explain in two sentences, with enough liquidity for the seat and some sign the market is mispricing it, gives you a shortlist worth real hours.

                ## Milestones
                1. Screening filters written down: market capitalisation range, daily traded value, sector and analyst coverage.
                2. A first list of fifteen to twenty names produced from a free or university screener.
                3. Each name scored for understandability, data availability and a possible disagreement with consensus.
                4. A shortlist of three to five candidates with a sentence on why each might be mispriced.

                ## Notes
                Avoid stocks with a pending takeover, a very recent IPO with no filings history, or a company your interviewer's fund is known to own heavily, unless you are prepared for that conversation.

            - name: Writing the variant perception statement
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                "Why is the market wrong?" is the question that decides most pitch interviews, and "it is a great company" is not an answer. Writing down, in two sentences each, what consensus believes, what you believe and what evidence will settle the difference turns research into a thesis an interviewer can test.

                ## Milestones
                1. Consensus view written from sell-side estimates, recent coverage and the share price reaction to news.
                2. Your differing view written in two sentences with the key driver named.
                3. Two or three pieces of evidence that support your view, each with a source.
                4. The observable event or data point that would prove you right or wrong.

                ## Notes
                If you cannot state what consensus expects, you cannot claim to disagree with it. Check estimates for the next two years before writing your view.
              priority: high

# Stock Pitch Preparation

This area is for anyone who will be asked to pitch a stock in an asset management, hedge fund or equity research interview, from a student with no coverage experience to a sell-side analyst crossing to the buy side. It starts with the foundations (which seat you are pitching for, choosing the idea, compliance and a variant view), moves through the operating routines that keep a pitch current, the analytical skills behind the thesis, valuation, catalysts and risks, then the written and spoken versions, the interview events themselves, situations for different backgrounds and a few specialist pitch styles.

What repeats is a weekly news check on your names, a weekly idea log and rehearsal, a monthly refresh of prices, multiples and catalyst dates, and a quarterly earnings update so the pitch never goes stale between interviews. The Reading queue, Feedback themes, Portfolio piece and Meeting notes templates pair with the reading habit, mock feedback, the one-pager and practitioner calls. Nothing here is investment advice: an interview pitch is a demonstration of reasoning, not a recommendation to buy or sell.
