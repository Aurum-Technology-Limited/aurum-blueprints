                ## Purpose
                Superday invitations often arrive as a phone call or a short email that leaves out the things you most need to know: how many rounds, who is interviewing, whether there is a case or modelling exercise, and when it all finishes.                 ## Milestones

                2. The number and type of rounds known, including any case, written test, group exercise or lunch.
                3. Interviewer names or at least their seniority and group, if the recruiter will share them.
                4. Dress code, documents to bring, travel and expenses rules and the recruiter's direct contact saved in one note.

                ## Notes
                Ask once, in a single message. Recruiters are handling dozens of candidates, so a clear list of three or four questions gets answered faster than a run of separate emails.

# Superday Preparation

This area is for the candidate who has just been invited to a final round in finance, whether a student heading to an analyst superday, an MBA at an associate final round, or a working professional moving laterally or switching careers. It runs from foundations (the invitation details in writing, the format, the interviewers, the bank brief, your story and logistics) through the short daily machinery of the prep window, the skills each round tests, from deal discussions to the case exercise and the group task, the decisions around clashes, preferences and other offers, the dated events of the night before, the day itself, thank-you notes and the decision call, situations for students, professionals, MBAs, laterals, career changers and international candidates, and finally specialist superdays and sitting on the panel yourself.

What repeats is a daily answer deck and morning markets read, mocks three times a week, a Saturday back-to-back stamina rehearsal, weekly deal tracking and debrief reviews, and a pipeline review for anyone juggling several final rounds.
