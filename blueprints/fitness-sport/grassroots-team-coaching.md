              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Ask ten grassroots coaches why they coach and you get ten answers, but players and parents only see what you do on a wet Tuesday in November. Writing one page on what you want players to get from a season, how you balance winning against development and enjoyment, and how you will share minutes gives you something to decide from when selection gets awkward in week twelve.

                ## Milestones
                1. A page stating why you coach, who the team is for and what a good season looks like.
                2. Your stance on playing time, positions and results written in plain words.
                3. Three behaviours you will model at every session and match listed.
                4. The page shared with the club's coach coordinator and with players or families.

                ## Notes
                Keep it short enough to read aloud in two minutes. A philosophy nobody can remember settles no arguments.

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Anyone coaching under-18s needs an in-date safeguarding course and a criminal record check arranged through the club, such as a DBS check in the UK, before working with players. Getting both done before the first session protects the children, the club's affiliation and you, and it is usually the first thing a league or school asks to see.

                3. The check cleared and confirmed in writing by the welfare officer.

                ## Notes

# Grassroots Team Coaching

This area is for the volunteer, teacher or player-coach running a junior or amateur team, in football, netball, rugby, hockey, cricket, basketball or any other team sport. It starts with the foundations (a coaching philosophy, your first qualification, safeguarding, first aid, the squad register and an emergency plan), then the weekly machinery of session plans, reflection, selection and minutes, family updates, venue checks and volunteer rotas, the coaching skills that make practice worth turning up for, decisions about positions, formations, behaviour and spending, the season's events, versions for parent coaches, teachers, player-coaches and mid-season takeovers, and finally qualifications, mentoring, club curriculum and talent pathways.

What repeats is a Sunday session plan and Monday family update, a Thursday squad announcement and Sunday minutes log, a Wednesday reflection note, and monthly rhythms for the volunteer rota, attendance, mentor catch-ups, goal and net inspections and the first aid kit, with termly player reviews and yearly certificate checks. The Operational checklist, Training program, Skills matrix, Purchase decision, Trip and Development plan templates pair with the projects that name them.
