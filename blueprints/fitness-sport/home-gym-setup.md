              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Equipment listings give footprints, but they never mention the swing of a barbell, the bar sleeves sticking out past a rack or the ceiling you hit on an overhead press. A floor plan with real measurements, including ceiling height, door widths and where the sockets are, tells you what fits before anything is ordered. It is also what you will send to a seller or installer when asking whether their kit will work.

                ## Milestones

            - name: Floor strength check before heavy kit goes upstairs
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A concrete garage slab will take almost anything, but a suspended timber floor in a bedroom or loft was never designed for a loaded rack plus dropped weights. Before putting more than a few hundred kilos of kit on an upper floor, find out which way the joists run and whether someone qualified should look at it. Getting this wrong means cracked ceilings below at best.

                ## Milestones
                1. The floor type of the chosen room identified: concrete slab, suspended timber or something else.
                2. The total weight of the planned kit and its heaviest loaded use estimated.
                3. Joist direction found and the rack placed across several joists near a load-bearing wall where possible.
                4. A structural engineer or experienced builder consulted for any upper floor plan above a modest dumbbell set-up.
                5. Their advice, and any limits such as no dropping, written into the gym rules.

                ## Notes
                This is about finding the right person to ask, not a calculation to do yourself. If in doubt, keep the heaviest training on the ground floor.
              priority: high

            - name: Garage damp, ventilation and temperature check
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Garages and outbuildings swing from freezing in January to stifling in July, and the damp that comes with them rusts bars and plates within a season. A cheap thermometer and hygrometer left out for a fortnight tells you what you are dealing with before you spend on heaters, fans or a dehumidifier. Spare rooms in the house have their own version of this when the window cannot open.

                ## Milestones
                1. A thermometer and hygrometer in the room with readings noted for two weeks.
                2. The highest humidity and lowest temperature recorded.
                3. Sources of damp found, such as a leaking door seal, a wet wall or a car parked inside.
                4. A plan chosen for each problem: dehumidifier, door seal, extra ventilation or a safe heater.
                5. Steel kit stored off the bare floor and away from external walls.

                ## Notes
                Many people aim for relative humidity below about 60 percent to slow rust. Use only heaters rated for garages and never run fuel-burning heaters in an enclosed space.
              priority: medium
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "Two weeks of temperature and humidity readings are recorded and every damp source found has a named fix."

                - "Walk the walls and door with a torch looking for damp patches"
