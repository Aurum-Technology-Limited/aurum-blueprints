                Before you push any joint toward its end range, it helps to know which ones already complain.             - name: Baseline range of motion test battery
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Feeling tight tells you very little about where the restriction actually sits. Six simple tests, measured or filmed once, give you numbers to beat: knee-to-wall ankle distance, sit and reach, shoulder flexion against a wall, seated hip rotation, thoracic rotation and a deep squat hold. They take about forty minutes and turn vague stiffness into a short list of specific joints.

                ## Milestones
                1. Knee-to-wall distance for both ankles measured in centimetres.
                2. Sit and reach, wall shoulder flexion, hip rotation and thoracic rotation recorded on the same day, left and right where relevant.
                3. A deep squat hold timed and filmed from the side.
                4. Every result written in one place with the date, the time of day and the warm-up used.

                ## Notes
                Test at the same time of day and after the same short warm-up every time, or retests will measure your morning stiffness rather than your progress.

            - name: Stretch intensity and pain rules
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Pushing a stretch until it hurts tends to make the nervous system guard the joint, and the range you force today is gone by tomorrow. Learning to work at a firm but tolerable intensity, roughly five to seven out of ten, and knowing which sensations mean stop, keeps progress steady and keeps you out of trouble.

                ## Milestones
                1. A personal zero to ten stretch intensity scale written with examples.
                2. A working level of five to seven out of ten practised in three sessions.
                3. A list of stop signs written: sharp, burning, tingling or numb sensations and pain that lingers afterwards.
                4. A rule agreed for what to do when a stop sign appears.

                ## Notes
                Nerve stretches, such as a slumped hamstring stretch, often tingle and need a much gentler approach. If tingling persists after stretching, ask a physiotherapist.

# Mobility & Flexibility Work

This area is for anyone who wants to move through a fuller range, from the office worker who cannot touch their toes to the lifter whose squat tips forward and the runner whose stride has shortened. It starts with foundations (a pain screen, a baseline test battery, two priority joints, the right kit and a ten-minute routine), then the daily and weekly machinery that keeps range improving, the skills for ankles, hips, upper back, shoulders and hamstrings, the decisions that fix a stalled squat or a plateau, dated events such as a thirty-day challenge and a ninety-day retest, versions for runners, lifters, cyclists, the over-fifties, hypermobile people, parents and manual workers, and finally splits, pancake and bridge work.

What repeats is a daily ten-minute routine and an evening stretch, joint prep before training, a weekly long session, a monthly retest on the 9th, a new four-week block each month and a quarterly goal review. The Purchase decision, Metrics log, Habit tracker and Training program templates pair with the projects that name them.
