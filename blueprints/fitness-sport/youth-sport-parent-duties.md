            - name: Choosing a club that fits your child
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Clubs differ far more than their websites suggest: some play every child equal minutes, others select from the age of nine, some train once a week and others expect three evenings and a weekend. Visiting two or three before signing up, and asking the same questions at each, puts your child somewhere they will enjoy and you can sustain for a full season.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of clubs within a realistic drive or bus ride, with training nights, age groups and fees for each.
                2. At least two taster sessions attended, with your child's own verdict written down after each.
                3. The same five questions asked at every club: playing time policy, coach qualifications, welfare officer, costs and expected commitment.
                4. One club chosen and the reason recorded in a sentence.

                ## Notes
                Ask other parents at the taster, not only the coach. They will tell you how the club behaves when a team is losing, which matters more than the trophy cabinet.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30

                mode: research
                output_kind: decision

                - "Ask each club about playing time, welfare officer, fees and commitment"

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every club needs a registration form, emergency contacts, consent for photos and transport, and a note of any medical condition such as asthma, an allergy or epilepsy. Filling them in properly once, and keeping copies, means a coach on a cold touchline at an away ground knows who to call and what your child needs.

                ## Milestones
                1. Club and governing body registration complete, with your child's player number or ID noted.
                2. Two emergency contacts given who can actually answer on match days.
                3. Medical conditions, medicines and allergies described in plain words on the club's form.
                4. Photo, transport and first aid consent choices made deliberately rather than ticked by default.
                5. Copies of every form saved in one family folder.

                ## Notes
                If your child carries an inhaler or adrenaline pen, ask how the coach keeps it on the touchline and who checks it is in date.

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before you leave your child with adults you have just met, it is reasonable to know who the club's welfare officer is, whether coaches have had background checks, and how coaches are expected to message young players. A parent who knows the rules can spot when something is off and knows exactly who to tell.

                ## Milestones
                1. The welfare or safeguarding officer's name and contact details saved in your phone.
                2. The club's safeguarding policy and codes of conduct for players, parents and coaches read.

                4. A short, age-appropriate talk with your child about who they can tell if something feels wrong.

                ## Notes

# Youth Sport Parent Duties

This area is for parents and carers who do the driving, washing, paying and cheering behind a child's sport, whatever the sport and whatever the level. It starts with the foundations (choosing a club, forms and safeguarding, one family calendar, a season budget, kit, the match bag and a lift share), then the weekly machinery that keeps it running, the things worth learning about sideline behaviour, concussion, weather and selection, the decisions every sport parent meets, the dated events of a season, versions for busy and complicated households, and finally the specialist ground of volunteering, academies, online profiles and scholarships.

What repeats is a Sunday evening logistics check and a Monday kit wash, monthly checks on fees, the lift rota, the match bag, the training load and any volunteer duties, quarterly size checks and conversations with your child, and yearly form renewals, a budget review and a concussion refresher. The Purchase decision, Operational checklist, Meeting notes, Trip and Savings goal templates pair with the projects that name them.
