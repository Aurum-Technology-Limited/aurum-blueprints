            - name: Safe grill position and fire safety kit
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A grill pushed against a fence, under a gazebo or near a back door is how garden fires and carbon monoxide scares start. Choosing one permanent spot with clear space around it, on a stable non-flammable surface, and keeping a way to put out a fire within reach means every later cook begins from a safe default.

                ## Milestones
                1. A spot chosen at least three metres from fences, sheds, overhanging branches and doors, or the distance your grill manual gives.
                2. A level, non-flammable base in place, such as paving slabs or a heat-resistant mat.
                3. A fire blanket, suitable extinguisher or bucket of sand kept near the grill.
                4. A household rule written down that charcoal and gas grills are never used indoors, in a garage or in a tent.

                ## Notes
                Lit and cooling charcoal gives off carbon monoxide for hours. Never bring a grill inside to shelter it from rain, even when it seems to have gone out.

            - name: Thermometer accuracy check and doneness card
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Lid thermometers on many grills read 20 degrees or more away from the cooking grate, and guessing doneness by colour is the commonest cause of both dry chicken and undercooked burgers. An instant-read probe checked in iced water, plus a card with your food safety authority's minimum temperatures, replaces guesswork with a number.

                ## Milestones
                1. An instant-read digital thermometer in the kit.
                2. The probe tested in a glass of crushed ice and water, reading within a degree of 0°C, or its error noted.
                3. A doneness card written with your food safety authority's minimum temperatures for poultry, minced meat, pork and fish.
                4. The card laminated or kept in a plastic sleeve with the tool kit.

                ## Notes
                The card should use your own country's published figures, not a number from a recipe blog. Rested steak cooked to preference is a taste choice; poultry and minced meat are safety thresholds.

                ## Purpose
                Lighter fluid leaves a chemical taste and encourages the habit of squirting more onto a slow fire, which is how flash burns happen. A chimney starter with two firelighters or a twist of newspaper lights a full load of coals evenly in 15 to 20 minutes, with nothing to taste and nothing to flare.

                ## Notes
                Set the chimney on the charcoal grate, never on a wooden table or deck, and use gloves to pour. The handle and outer wall get hot enough to burn.

            - name: Two-zone fire for direct and indirect heat
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Almost every barbecue disaster, from black sausages raw in the middle to chicken burnt outside and pink at the bone, comes from cooking everything over full heat. Banking coals on one side, or running only half the burners, gives a hot zone to sear and a cooler zone to finish, and it is the single skill that changes results most.

                ## Milestones
                1. Coals banked to one side of a charcoal grill, or burners set high on one side and off on the other for gas.
                2. The temperature at the cool side measured with the lid closed.
                3. One cook done by searing first and finishing on the cool side, such as thick sausages or bone-in chicken.
                4. A second cook done the other way round, slow on the cool side then seared hot.

                ## Notes
                Keep the lid down when cooking indirect. Every look costs heat and adds minutes.

            - name: Gas grill leak test and hose check

                ## Purpose
                A cracked hose or loose regulator on a gas grill can leak for weeks unnoticed outdoors, then ignite at the next push of the starter button.                 4. The hose replacement date noted in the cook log.

                ## Notes

            - name: After-cook clean-down and safe ash disposal
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Ash that looks cold can hold live embers for a day or more, and tipping it into a plastic bin or a compost heap is a common cause of garden and bin fires. Brushing the grates while warm and moving fully cold ash into a lidded metal bin keeps the grill ready and the house safe.
