              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Oven dials are often out by 20 degrees or more, which is the difference between a risen sponge and a dense one. An inexpensive oven thermometer and a shelf of white bread slices show you the true temperature and where the hot spots sit, so you can correct the dial and turn trays at the right moment.

                ## Milestones
                1. A free-standing oven thermometer bought and placed on the middle shelf.
                2. Readings taken after 20 minutes at 160, 180 and 200 degrees, with the gap to the dial written down.
                3. A hot spot map made by toasting a shelf of white bread slices and noting which brown first.
                4. A note taped inside a cupboard giving the dial setting for each real temperature and which way to turn trays.

                ## Notes
                Check whether each recipe gives a fan or a conventional temperature. Fan settings usually run about 20 degrees lower for the same result.

                Cup measures can vary by a quarter depending on how the flour is scooped, which is why the same recipe works one week and not the next.             - name: Shortcrust pastry by hand and blind baked
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Shortcrust is the base for tarts, pies and quiches, and making it by hand teaches how fat, flour and cold water behave before anything fancier. A tart case that holds its shape and cuts cleanly with a crisp base is the first real pastry milestone.

                ## Milestones
                1. A shortcrust made by rubbing cold butter into flour, using no more water than needed.
                2. Pastry rested, rolled to an even 3 mm and eased into a loose-bottomed tin without stretching.
                3. The case blind baked with baking beans, then finished uncovered until the base is dry and golden.
                4. A tart case with no more than a few millimetres of shrinkage achieved twice and logged.

                ## Notes
                Shrinkage nearly always means the pastry was stretched or not rested. Leave an overhang and trim it after baking.
              priority: high

This area is for anyone who bakes for pleasure, from the person who makes every family birthday cake to a creative baker working towards patisserie.
