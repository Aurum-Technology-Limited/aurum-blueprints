            - name: Pizza stone or baking steel for the home oven
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A domestic oven heats air, but pizza needs heat driven hard into the base in the first two minutes, which only a thick, preheated surface delivers. A cordierite stone is cheap and forgiving, while a 6 to 10 mm steel conducts heat roughly twice as fast and gives a crisper base in an oven that tops out at 250°C. Choosing between them, and sizing it to the actual oven shelf, is the single purchase that changes home pizza most.

                ## Milestones
                1. The inside width and depth of the oven and the shelf measured.
                2. Stone, steel and cast iron pan compared on price, weight, heat-up time and base colour.
                3. A surface bought that leaves at least 2 cm of air gap on each side of the shelf.
                4. A wooden or metal peel chosen to match the surface size.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Purchase decision** template. A 10 mm steel can weigh over 10 kg: check the oven shelf rating and your wrists before buying the thickest one.

            - name: Home oven heat test
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Oven dials lie by 20°C or more, and a stone that feels hot after 20 minutes may still be 100°C short of where it needs to be. Measuring the real stone temperature after 30, 45 and 60 minutes of preheating, with the grill on and off, tells you the shelf position and preheat time that give your oven its best pizza.

                ## Milestones

                3. The effect of switching to the top grill for the last 10 minutes measured.

                ## Notes

# Homemade Pasta & Pizza

This area is for anyone who wants pasta and pizza from their own kitchen that beats the takeaway, whether that means a first tray of tagliatelle or a leopard-spotted crust from a 450°C oven. It starts with the foundations (flours, a stone or steel, an oven heat test, a first egg pasta, a first pizza dough, two tomato sauces and a bake log), then the weekly pizza night, pasta sessions and freezer stocks that keep it going, the rolling, shaping, filling and stretching skills, the dough trials and kit decisions, the dinners and parties worth planning, versions for busy weeknights, children, small kitchens and mixed diets, and finally the specialist styles, extruders and signature formulas of an experienced maker.

What repeats is a Wednesday dough and a Friday bake, a Sunday pasta session and a quick Tuesday plate, monthly stocks of dough balls, frozen pasta and sauce, a restock list and a log review, quarterly kit care and a new shape or style, and a yearly oven and formula check.
