                Kitchens are usually equipped for an imagined cook who bakes weekly and makes pasta by hand, while the real household fries eggs, roasts trays of vegetables and reheats leftovers. # Kitchen Equipment & Layout

This area is for homeowners and renters who want a kitchen that works for the cooking they actually do, rather than one cluttered with gadgets and short of the three tools used every day. It starts with foundations (a cooking log, an inventory, a clear-out, a zone map, core knives and an honest pan audit), then the routines that keep knives sharp and appliances clean and serviced, the knowledge of cookware materials, ovens, induction and simple fault-finding, the decisions on what to buy, repair or replace, the events of deliveries, moves and building works, situations such as renting, small kitchens, young children and reduced grip, and finally the specialist work of a gas-to-induction switch and a renovation brief.

What repeats is a Sunday worktop clear, a quarterly sharpening, an oven clean on the 12th, a dishwasher filter on the 8th, board oiling on the 15th, seasoning upkeep on the 22nd, a drawer reset on the 26th, a fund top-up on the 3rd, quarterly fridge, hood and small appliance rounds, and a yearly equipment review. The Purchase decision, Household chores, Operational checklist and Vendor templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
