              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A board that slides is behind a large share of kitchen knife accidents, and a cramped, cluttered counter makes every cut slower. Setting up one station with a damp cloth under a large board, a bowl for scraps and the knife always in the same spot gives every later skill a safe place to happen.

                ## Milestones
                1. A board of at least 40 by 30 cm sitting on a damp cloth or non-slip mat that does not move when pushed.
                2. The board at a comfortable working height, raised on a second board if the counter is low.
                3. A scrap bowl and a tray for cut ingredients set to one side of the board.
                4. A fixed home for the knife at the top of the board, blade facing away, during every session.

                ## Notes
                A wrung-out tea towel laid flat grips better than most mats. Glass and stone boards blunt knives fast, so keep them for serving.

            - name: Pinch grip and claw hand practice
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most home cooks hold a chef's knife by the handle with a finger along the spine, which gives poor control and tires the wrist. Learning the pinch grip on the blade and the claw with the guiding hand, knuckles forward and fingertips tucked, is the single change that makes cutting both faster and safer.

                ## Milestones
                1. Pinch grip held with thumb and forefinger on the blade just ahead of the handle.
                2. Guiding hand curled into a claw, with the flat of the knife resting against the knuckles.
                3. Three sessions of ten minutes slicing carrots and celery with both grips held throughout.
                4. Slices of even thickness produced without looking away from the guiding hand.

                ## Notes
                Slow and correct beats fast and sloppy. Speed arrives on its own after a few weeks of the right grip.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: learning
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "Three practice sessions of at least ten minutes each completed with pinch grip and claw hand, producing even slices of carrot and celery."
                cadence: phased

            - name: Sharpness test and honing steel technique
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A blunt knife skids off an onion skin and needs more force, which is exactly when cuts happen. Learning to test the edge on a sheet of paper or a ripe tomato, and to hone it on a steel at a steady angle, keeps a sound edge working between proper sharpenings.

                ## Milestones
                1. Your main knife tested by slicing a sheet of printer paper and a ripe tomato.
                2. Honing practised at a steady, shallow angle with five light strokes a side.
                3. Honing done before each cutting session until it is automatic.
                4. A note made of whether the knife needs a full sharpening, not just honing.

                ## Notes
                Honing straightens the edge; it does not remove metal. If the paper still tears after honing, the knife needs sharpening, by you or by a professional.
              priority: medium

            - name: Onion dice, slice and mince
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Onions start more savoury dishes than any other ingredient, so cutting one well pays back several times a week. Learning the cuts that leave the root intact to hold the layers together turns a tearful five-minute job into a tidy one-minute one.

                ## Milestones
                1. An onion halved through the root and peeled with the root left on.
                2. Even dice of about 5 mm produced with vertical cuts that stop short of the root.
                3. Thin half-moon slices cut for soups and stir-fries.
                4. A fine mince cut for dressings and sauces.
                5. One onion diced in under two minutes with even pieces.

                ## Notes
                A sharp knife crushes fewer cells and releases less of the compound that stings your eyes. The horizontal cut is optional on small onions.

# Knife Skills & Kitchen Technique

This area is for anyone who wants to cook with confidence rather than follow recipes line by line, whether you have never held a knife properly or already cook most nights. It starts with the foundations (a safe station, grip and claw, knife rules, a sharp edge, a skills baseline, the onion, mise en place and salting), then the routines that build skill week by week, the knife cuts and the heat, sauce and egg techniques that sit under most recipes, the decisions about ratios, thermometers, hob quirks and classes, dated milestones such as a recipe-free three-course meal, versions for teenagers, left-handed cooks, weak grips and shared kitchens, and finally the whetstone, classical cuts, stocks and professional kitchen work of an advanced cook.

What repeats is a Saturday knife drill, Sunday vegetable prep and a fresh dressing, mise en place on the two busiest nights, a monthly technique, recipe-free dinner and knife video, a monthly read of your cook notes, quarterly timed benchmarks, skills matrix reviews and whetstone sessions, and a yearly showcase dinner. The Skills matrix, Training program, Metrics log and Purchase decision templates pair with the projects that name them.
