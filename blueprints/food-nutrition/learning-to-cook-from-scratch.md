                ## Purpose
                Most people who want to cook more have no clear picture of what they eat now, only a vague sense that it is too many takeaways.             - name: Hob, oven and raw meat safety for new cooks
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                New cooks rarely get hurt by a recipe; they get hurt by a pan of oil left on high, a handle sticking out over the edge or raw chicken juices on the salad board. Learning a handful of rules before the first big cook prevents the burns, small fires and food poisoning that put people off cooking for good.

                ## Milestones
                1. What to do if oil catches fire known by heart: heat off, lid or fire blanket on, never water.
                2. Pan handles turned inward and tea towels kept away from the hob as a habit.
                3. A separate board, or a wash in between, used for raw meat.
                4. Your health service's guidance on cooking chicken and mince through read and noted.

                ## Notes
                Check that the kitchen has a working smoke alarm nearby and know where the fire blanket is, if you have one. These notes organise standard safety guidance; follow your local fire service's advice where it differs.
              priority: high

# Learning to Cook From Scratch

This area is for anyone who mostly eats ready meals, meal deals and takeaways and wants to cook real food from fresh ingredients without it becoming a second job, including students about to feed themselves for the first time. It starts with the foundations (an honest audit of what you eat now, the ten dinners worth learning, a small kit, a starter cupboard, safety basics and a first cooked dinner), then the weekly habits that make cooking stick, the staple dishes every home cook leans on, the decisions about takeaways, meal kits and courses, milestones such as a first meal for guests and thirty days without ordering in, versions for students, busy workers, tired bodies and people whose partner always cooked, and finally the projects of a cook who has outgrown the recipe card.

What repeats is a daily tick on the dinner tracker, a weekend plan for the coming week's cooking nights, one new dish each Wednesday and a Friday read of your kitchen notebook, a monthly review of your repertoire, takeaway count and slow cook, a quarterly cook from memory and a yearly look back. The Purchase decision, Habit tracker, Weekly meal plan, Metrics log and Course templates pair with the projects that name them.
