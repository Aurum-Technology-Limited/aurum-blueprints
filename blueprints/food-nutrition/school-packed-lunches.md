              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every school sets its own packed lunch rules, and they are often buried in a starter pack nobody reread: nut-free sites, no fizzy drinks, no glass, no sweets, sometimes a fruit-only morning snack. Knowing those rules, plus whether there is a fridge, a microwave or only twenty minutes to eat, decides what can go in the box before you plan a single meal.

                ## Milestones

                3. Lunch length, eating place and access to a fridge, microwave or water fountain confirmed for each child.
                4. Any differences between siblings' schools or year groups noted.

                ## Notes
                If the policy is vague, ask the class teacher what actually gets sent home or confiscated. The written rule and the lunchtime practice are not always the same.

# School Packed Lunches

This area is for parents who pack school lunches most days and are tired of boxes coming home full, warm yoghurts and seven o'clock panic. It starts with the foundations (the school's rules, the right kit, an audit of what comes back, a balanced formula, the packed lunch versus school meal decision and a first rotation), moves through the weekly machinery of planning, prepping, packing and washing, the skills that keep food cold, crisp and appealing by noon, and the decisions about waste, treats and budget, then dated plans for a new school year, trips, sports days and exams, versions for allergies, vegetarian children, two households, teenagers and children with additional needs, and finally the recipe books, seasonal menus and school policy work of a parent who has done this for years.

What repeats is a Thursday lunch plan, a Sunday prep hour with a Wednesday top-up, packing the night before, an after-school unpack and wash, a Friday chat with the children, a weekly bottle clean, monthly staples, freezer and cost checks, and a termly rotation refresh.
