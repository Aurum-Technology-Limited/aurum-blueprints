            - name: Choosing your everyday cooking salts
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Salt is the single biggest lever on flavour, yet a teaspoon of fine table salt is nearly twice as salty as a teaspoon of flaky salt, which is why recipes go wrong. Settling on one cooking salt for the pot, one finishing salt for the plate, and learning what your own pinch weighs, makes seasoning repeatable.

                ## Milestones
                1. One cooking salt and one finishing salt chosen and decanted into open containers by the hob.
                2. Your three-finger pinch of the cooking salt weighed on a scale, five times, and averaged.
                3. The salt type used in your favourite recipe sources noted.
                4. A conversion note written for when a recipe calls for a different salt.

                ## Notes
                This is about consistency, not quantity. If your clinician has given you a salt limit, the measuring here helps you keep to it.
              priority: medium
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision

                cadence: one-shot

              tasks:

                - "Put the cooking salt in an open pot or cellar beside the hob"
                - "Weigh five of your normal pinches on a gram scale and average them"

                ## Notes

# Spice & Flavour Building

This area is for anyone whose cooking is fine but somehow flat, and who wants to understand why a pinch of salt, a squeeze of lime or a spoon of miso changes a dish. It starts with the foundations (clearing out tired jars, a core collection, labelled storage, a palate baseline and the salt, acid and umami shelves), then the routines that keep spices fresh and blends on hand, the skills of blooming, toasting, layering aromatics and balancing, decisions about whole spices, home blends and where to buy, a few dated events worth cooking for, versions for mixed households, students, movers and travellers, and finally the signature blends, rare spices and chilli work of an experienced cook.

What repeats is a taste-and-adjust moment at every meal, a weekly flavour practice dish and fresh herb shop, a monthly stock check, blend jar, freezer paste session and staple upgrade, a quarterly seasonal drawer swap and a yearly purge of stale spices. The Purchase decision, Operational checklist and Reading queue templates pair with the projects that name them.
