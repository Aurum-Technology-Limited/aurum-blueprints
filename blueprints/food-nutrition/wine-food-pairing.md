                Most people who say they know nothing about wine actually have clear preferences they have never tested.             - name: One-page pairing principles cheat sheet
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Pairing comes down to a handful of interactions: acid in food makes wine taste softer, sweetness in food makes dry wine taste thin, salt and fat calm tannin, chilli heat amplifies alcohol and tannin, and the weight of the wine should match the weight of the dish. Writing these on one page you can glance at in the shop removes most of the guesswork before you learn a single region.

                ## Milestones
                1. Six pairing interactions written as plain one-line rules.
                2. One example dish and one example wine beside each rule.
                3. A short list of awkward foods, such as artichokes, asparagus and egg yolk, with a workaround for each.
                4. The sheet saved on your phone and printed for the kitchen.

                ## Notes
                Match the wine to the sauce or the strongest flavour, not the protein. Chicken in a cream sauce and chicken with tomato and olives want different bottles.
              priority: high

              frontmatter:

# Wine & Food Pairing

This area is for anyone who wants to pick a bottle for tonight's dinner, or for a table of guests, with confidence rather than guesswork. It starts with the foundations (a style tasting, a style map, a pairing cheat sheet, a log, glasses, serving and opened-bottle basics, a merchant, a budget and label reading), then the routines that keep the house shelf stocked and the lessons coming, the tasting and pairing skills from grapes and climate to cheese, spice and restaurant wine lists, the decisions on decanting, storage, clubs and buying, the occasions from dinner parties to weddings and winery trips, versions for tight budgets, single drinkers, families, plant-based tables and gift-givers, and finally cellaring, a formal qualification, tasting menus and fine wine buying.

What repeats is a Friday pairing test, a weekly aroma session and a weekly study slot if you take a course, a monthly comparison night, shelf restock, offers check, budget check, grape tasting and leftover clear-out, a quarterly log review, inventory check, seasonal refresh and merchant tasting, and a yearly spending review, cellar review, storage check and festive plan. The Metrics log, Purchase decision, Trip and Course templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
