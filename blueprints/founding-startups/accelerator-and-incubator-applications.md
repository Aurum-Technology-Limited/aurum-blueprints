            - name: Accelerator or incubator fit decision
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Accelerators typically buy a slice of the company for cash, a fixed-length cohort and a demo day, while incubators usually offer space, support and time with little or no equity taken. Writing down what the company is actually short of, whether money, network, structure, credibility or a market it cannot reach alone, tells you whether either model helps or whether the honest answer is to keep building.

                ## Milestones
                1. The company's three biggest gaps for the next six months written down in plain words.
                2. Each gap marked as one a programme could close, one it could not, or one money alone would fix.
                3. A written decision: apply to accelerators, apply to incubators, both, or neither this year.
                4. Your co-founders' agreement with the decision recorded.

                ## Notes
                A programme is worth its equity only if it closes a gap you cannot close cheaper. Strong teams with traction and investor access often gain least.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A one-page written decision on whether to apply to accelerators, incubators or neither, signed off by every co-founder."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "List the three things the company most lacks for the next six months"
                - "Mark which of those gaps a programme could realistically close"
                - "Talk the list through with your co-founders in one sitting"
                - "Write the apply or do not apply decision and its reasons on one page"

            - name: Standard accelerator deal terms primer
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Accelerator offers are usually written as a simple agreement for future equity, a convertible note or a straight equity purchase, and the same headline percentage can cost very different amounts once a valuation cap, post-money basis or pro rata right is applied. Learning the handful of terms that recur in these deals means you can read an offer in an evening and know which questions to take to a lawyer.

                ## Milestones
                1. Plain-language notes on SAFEs, convertible notes, valuation caps, discounts, post-money and pro rata rights.

                3. A list of five questions to ask any programme about its terms.
                4. A startup lawyer or experienced founder identified who will review a real offer.

                ## Notes
                This is for understanding the paperwork, not legal advice. Have a qualified lawyer in your jurisdiction review any agreement before you sign it.

# Accelerator and Incubator Applications

This area is for founders weighing up an accelerator or incubator, applying to one, or already sitting in a cohort and wanting the most from it. It starts with foundations (whether a programme fits at all, a longlist, the standard deal terms, alumni calls, an intake calendar, a company fact sheet, clean paperwork, founder bios and the application video), then the machinery of an application season, the writing and interview skills that decide most outcomes, the choices between offers, the dated moments from submission to demo day, variants for solo, non-technical, idea-stage, regional, parent and sector-specific founders, and finally specialist work on running several applications at once and judging what the equity bought.

What repeats is a weekly writing sprint and pipeline review during an application season, a monthly traction note to programmes that said not yet, weekly cohort goals, metrics and mentor follow-ups while you are in a programme, and a quarterly look at reapplying and at your alumni network. The **Meeting notes**, **Metrics log** and **Trip** templates pair with mentor sessions, the weekly cohort report and relocating for an in-person programme. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
