              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Searchers who look at everything end up buying nothing, or worse, buying the first business that says yes. A one-page buy box naming the industries, size range, geography, owner situation and deal breakers you will accept turns hundreds of listings into a handful worth a call, and gives brokers a reason to send you their best ones first.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page buy box covering industry, revenue and earnings range, location, owner involvement and customer mix.
                2. Five hard deal breakers written down, such as heavy customer concentration or a business that depends on the owner's licence.
                3. Three reasons you would be a better owner for this kind of business than the typical buyer.
                4. The buy box tested on ten real listings, with each sorted as pass, maybe or no.

                ## Notes
                Write the buy box before you see a business you love. A thesis written after the fact always fits the deal in front of you.

# Acquiring a Small Business

Acquiring a Small Business is for anyone who would rather buy a working company with customers, staff and cash flow than start one from nothing, whether you are a first-time searcher, an owner adding a second business or a founder buying a competitor. It starts with foundations (the buy box, your own money, the search model, a deal team and a financing map), then the weekly machinery of sourcing, screening and owner outreach, the skills of reading small-company accounts, normalising earnings and structuring a deal, the decisions from letter of intent through diligence to go or no-go, the dated events from the first site visit to closing day and the announcements, variants for couples, career changers, existing owners and relocating buyers, and finally roll-ups, working capital true-ups, escrow claims and refinancing.

What repeats is a Monday sourcing block, weekly screening and outreach, monthly broker calls, investor or lender updates and a searcher metrics log, and after the close a weekly operating review, seller check-ins and quarterly covenant reporting. The Meeting notes, Metrics log, Risk register, Operational checklist and Onboarding 30/60/90 templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
