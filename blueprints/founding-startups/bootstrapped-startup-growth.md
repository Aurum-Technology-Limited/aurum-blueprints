              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Founders often drift into bootstrapping because nobody would fund them, or into fundraising because everyone around them does, and both drifts lead to half-hearted versions of each. Writing a one-page memo on why this company should grow on customer revenue, what that rules out and what would make you reconsider gives you a decision you can point back to when an investor calls or growth feels slow.

                ## Milestones

                2. The trade-offs written plainly: slower hiring, founder pay below market for a while, no paid blitz.

                4. The memo shared with any co-founder or partner and signed off by both.

                ## Notes

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every bootstrapped decision comes back to one number: the monthly revenue at which the company covers its costs, including the founder's minimum draw. Knowing it to the nearest hundred, and how many customers at your current price that means, turns a vague hope of profitability into a target you can count down towards.

                ## Milestones

                ## Notes
