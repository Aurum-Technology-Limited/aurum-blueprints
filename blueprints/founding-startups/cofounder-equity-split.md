            - name: Ground rules for the first equity conversation
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Founders who leave equity until after the first customer or the first term sheet end up negotiating against value, pride and hindsight at the same time. Agreeing when, where and how the split will be discussed, before anyone names a number, keeps the first conversation about principles rather than positions.

                ## Milestones
                1. A date within the next two weeks fixed for the equity conversation, with every co-founder attending.
                2. Three or four ground rules written down, such as no numbers before the principles are agreed.
                3. A list of the topics the conversation must cover: split, vesting, leavers, roles and future hires.
                4. Agreement that nobody signs anything on the day itself.

                ## Notes
                The best time for this talk is now, while the company has no revenue and no investors. Every month of delay makes the conversation harder, not easier.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 7

                4. The differences carried into the agenda for the equity conversation.

            - name: Founder vesting schedule and cliff
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Without vesting, a founder who leaves after three months keeps the same stake as one who stays for five years, and the remaining team carries a passenger on the cap table. A schedule with a cliff, commonly four years with a one-year cliff, protects every founder from every other, including from their own possible early exit.

                ## Milestones
                1. A vesting length, cliff and vesting frequency agreed for every founder.
                2. A decision on whether time already worked counts towards vesting.
                3. Any difference between founders' schedules agreed and explained in writing.
                4. The terms passed to the lawyer drafting the share agreements.

                ## Notes
                Vesting is not a sign of distrust. Investors will usually require it anyway, and founders who agree it among themselves first get to set the terms.

# Co-Founder Equity Split

This area is for co-founders who need to agree who owns what, on what terms, while the company is still worth almost nothing and the conversation is cheap. It starts with the foundations (ground rules for the talk, each founder's written expectations, a contribution inventory, a first cap table, the split itself, vesting and the signed share paperwork), then the records that keep ownership accurate, the mechanics worth learning properly, the decisions around leavers, cash, IP and control, dated moments such as the offsite and the cliff, variants for pairs, larger teams, part-timers, family and cross-border founders, and finally the specialist work of investor re-vesting, share classes and dynamic models.

What repeats is a monthly cap table and vesting check, a weekly hours log while anyone is part-time, quarterly contribution check-ins and document vault reviews, and a yearly look at the whole agreement and the option pool. The **Meeting notes**, **Reading queue**, **Vendor** and **Operational checklist** templates pair with the check-ins, the study projects, choosing a lawyer and signing day.
