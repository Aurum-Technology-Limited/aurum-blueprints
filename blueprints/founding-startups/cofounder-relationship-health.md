                Most co-founders talk about the product every day and about the partnership almost never, until something breaks.             - name: Roles and decision rights map
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Co-founders fall out far more often over who decides than over what is decided. Mapping every recurring decision area, from hiring and pricing to the roadmap and spending, to a single owner with a clear consult or veto list removes the daily friction of two people both thinking they are in charge.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of at least twenty recurring decision areas in the company.
                2. One owner named for each area, with who must be consulted.
                3. The small set of decisions that need both founders written down explicitly.
                4. The map shared with the team so people know whom to ask.

                ## Notes
                Keep the jointly owned list short. If more than a handful of areas need both of you, you have not really split the roles.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A one-page map assigns every recurring decision area to a single owner, lists the joint decisions and has been shown to the team."
                cadence: one-shot

              tasks:

                - "Assign a single owner and a consult list to each decision area"

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Agreements made in a coffee shop in month one are remembered differently by month eighteen. A two or three page charter covering commitment, roles, how you disagree, how you treat each other under stress and what happens if one of you wants out turns those early promises into a document you can both point at.

                ## Milestones
                1. A draft charter covering commitment, roles, conflict, communication and departures.
                2. Each founder's edits and objections discussed rather than silently accepted.

                ## Notes

# Co-Founder Relationship Health

This area is for founders who share a company with one or more co-founders and want the partnership to survive the pressure of the early years. It starts with the foundations (a first honest conversation, a roles and decision rights map, a written charter, an escalation ladder and a deadlock rule), then the recurring check-ins that keep things honest, the conversational skills that make hard topics survivable, the decisions and repairs that come up when roles or trust shift, a handful of dated moments such as a fundraise or a new executive, variants for particular co-founder set-ups, and finally the work of founders who have kept a partnership healthy for years.

What repeats is a weekly co-founder one-to-one, a monthly scored health check, a Friday decision log, a quarterly offsite and an annual review of the charter and of what each of you wants from the company. The 1:1, Meeting notes, Feedback themes, Reading queue and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them.
