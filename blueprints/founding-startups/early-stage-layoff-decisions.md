            - name: Written case for why a layoff is on the table
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Founders often drift toward cutting staff on a bad week's feeling, then struggle to explain the decision to the board, the team or themselves. Writing one page that states the problem, the numbers behind it and what happens if nothing changes forces the question into the open. If the page cannot make the case, you have saved yourself a mistake; if it can, every later step starts from the same facts.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page statement of the problem, with current monthly burn, cash in the bank and months of runway.
                2. The specific trigger named: a missed raise, a lost customer, a revenue plan that slipped or a strategy change.
                3. What happens in six and twelve months if headcount stays the same written in plain words.
                4. The page read by one trusted person outside the company who challenged it.

                ## Notes
                Keep this document private and dated. If the case rests only on fear, park the layoff and revisit it in a month with fresh numbers.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 7
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A dated one-page case for or against cutting staff exists, with burn, runway and the trigger written down and reviewed by one outsider."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "Pull this month's bank balance and last three months of spend into one sheet"
                - "Write the trigger for considering cuts in two sentences"
                - "Describe what the company looks like in twelve months with no change"
                - "Ask a trusted founder or adviser to challenge the page"
            - name: Alternatives to cutting staff tested first
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Teams forgive a layoff more readily when they can see the founder tried everything else first, and sometimes the alternatives are enough. Listing and costing the options that do not remove people, from a hiring freeze and vendor cuts to temporary pay reductions and reduced hours, shows what each would save and how fast. The result is either a layoff avoided or a layoff you can explain honestly.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of at least six non-layoff options with the monthly saving and time to take effect for each.
                2. Software, office, agency and contractor spend reviewed line by line.
                3. Founder and leadership pay reductions costed as a separate line.
                4. A written verdict on whether the alternatives close the gap, alone or combined.

                ## Notes
                Voluntary pay cuts and reduced hours can carry legal conditions in many countries. Ask employment counsel before proposing them to anyone.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14

            - name: Employment counsel briefed before any decision
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Notice periods, consultation duties, protected characteristics and the wording of release agreements vary by country and sometimes by state or province, and a misstep can cost more than the layoff saves. Bringing in an employment lawyer before roles are chosen, not after, means the plan is shaped around the rules instead of patched afterwards. A two-hour briefing is cheap insurance.

                ## Milestones

                4. The fee arrangement and turnaround times agreed in writing.

# Early-Stage Layoff Decisions

Early-Stage Layoff Decisions is for the founder who has started to suspect the team is bigger than the company can carry, and who wants to make the call properly rather than in a panic. It starts with foundations (a written case for the cut, alternatives tested, cost scenarios, counsel, the board and a fixed notification date), then the weekly machinery that carries a layoff from planning to recovery, the skills of fair selection, severance and hard conversations, the decisions about roles, equity and pay, the dated events of notification day and the all-hands, variants for solo founders, friends, visa holders, remote teams and live fundraises, and finally second rounds, collective consultation and a playbook for next time.

What repeats is a Monday planning stand-up while the layoff is being prepared, a Friday decision log, weekly one-to-ones and workload reviews with the people who stay, monthly checks that severance is paid and the savings are real, and quarterly reviews of headcount risk, rehire rules and the alumni network. The Board meeting, Risk register, Operational checklist, Meeting notes, 1:1 and Metrics log templates pair with the projects that name them.
