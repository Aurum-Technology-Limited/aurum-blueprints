              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Plenty of good companies start with one founder, and plenty of bad partnerships start because someone heard investors prefer teams. Writing down what a co-founder would actually change, in skills, speed, money and morale, and setting that against hiring, contracting or advisers, tells you whether to search at all and what kind of person to look for.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of the five hardest problems in the next eighteen months of the company.
                2. Each problem marked as needing an owner, an employee, a contractor or an adviser.
                3. The cost of going solo written down, including the work you would stop doing.
                4. A one-line decision recorded: search for a co-founder, stay solo, or revisit by a set date.

                ## Notes
                If the only reason is loneliness or fundraising optics, a founder peer group or a strong first hire may solve it with far less risk than giving away a large stake.

# Finding a Co-Founder

This area is for founders who want a partner to build with and know that the wrong one can sink a company faster than a bad idea. It starts with the foundations (whether you need a co-founder at all, an honest inventory of your own gaps, a role profile, dealbreakers and a search deadline), then the weekly and monthly machinery of sourcing and follow-up, the skills of running first conversations and checking track records, the trial project and the commit decision, a few dated events, variants for founders in particular situations, and finally the work of founders who have assembled teams before.

What repeats is a weekly sourcing block and match review, monthly introduction requests, events, nurture updates and a search review against your stop rule, a weekly check-in during any trial, and a quarterly sweep of your long-list. The Skills matrix, Person, Meeting notes and Metrics log templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
