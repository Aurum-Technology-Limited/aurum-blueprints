            - name: Snapshot of the culture you already have
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every team of three or more already has a culture, made of who speaks first, when messages get answered, what gets praised and what gets quietly ignored. Writing down what actually happens over two normal weeks, before deciding what you want, stops you designing values for a team that does not exist.

                ## Milestones
                1. Two weeks of observations noted: meeting habits, response times, who decides, what gets celebrated.
                2. Each team member asked privately to describe the team in three words.
                3. A one-page snapshot listing five habits worth keeping and five that worry you.
                4. The snapshot shared with your co-founders as the starting point for the values work.

                ## Notes
                Write what people do, not what you hope they do. The gap between the two is the most useful thing you will find.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A one-page snapshot of current team habits, including three-word descriptions from every team member, exists and has been shared with the founders."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:
                - "Start a private note titled culture observations and add three things you noticed this week"
                - "Ask each team member for three words that describe working here"
                - "Sort your observations into habits to keep and habits that worry you"
                - "Write the one-page snapshot and share it with your co-founders"

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Founders usually assume they want the same culture until a hiring call or a missed deadline shows otherwise. A two-hour conversation about the company each of you would be proud to work at in three years, and the one you would hate, surfaces the differences while they are still cheap to resolve.

                ## Milestones

                4. A short shared statement of culture intent that both founders sign off.

                ## Notes

            - name: Founders' first draft of team values
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Values become useful when they help someone choose between two reasonable options, and useless when they read like a poster: honest, innovative, customer-focused. Drafting three to five values that each name a real trade-off, such as speed over polish on internal tools, gives the team something it can actually use on a Tuesday afternoon.

                ## Milestones
                1. A long list of candidate values drawn from the snapshot and the intent statement.
                2. The list cut to three to five, each phrased as a choice between two good things.
                3. Each value paired with one real example from the company's history.
                4. A draft ready to take to the whole team, labelled clearly as a draft.

                ## Notes
                If no reasonable company would choose the opposite of a value, it is not doing any work. Cut it or sharpen it.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before changing anything, it helps to know whether people feel able to admit a mistake, disagree with a founder or ask a basic question. # First Team Culture Setting

This area is for founders and the first handful of people around them who want the team's habits, values and norms chosen on purpose rather than inherited from whoever was loudest in month two. It starts with foundations (an honest snapshot of the culture you already have, a founders' intent conversation, draft values, a working agreement, decision and meeting norms and a culture document), then the rituals that keep it alive, the skills of feedback, facilitation and writing, the decisions about remote work, transparency, pace and titles, a few dated moments such as the first offsite and the first non-founder joiner, variants for remote, contractor-heavy and multinational teams, and finally the specialist work of measuring culture and passing it on.

What repeats is a Friday all-hands with the numbers, weekly one-to-ones and thank-yous, a monthly retro and pulse survey, a quarterly values check-in and ritual review, and a yearly rewrite of the working agreement. The Meeting notes, 1:1, Feedback themes, Onboarding 30/60/90, Reading queue, Risk register and Metrics log templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
