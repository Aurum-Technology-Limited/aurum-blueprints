            - name: Eighteen-month hiring plan tied to runway
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Founders who hire whenever someone good turns up usually find, eight months later, that the payroll has outrun the milestones the next round depends on. Writing down which roles you will hire, in what order and in which month, and checking each one against the cash you have, turns hiring from a series of impulses into a plan an investor or co-founder can challenge.

                ## Milestones
                1. The company milestones for the next eighteen months written down, each with the capability it needs.
                2. A list of roles in hiring order, each linked to the milestone it serves.
                3. A target start month for every role, with the runway in months after each hire.
                4. The plan reviewed with your co-founder or lead investor and their objections noted.
                5. A rule agreed for what moves a hire earlier or later, such as revenue or a funding close.

                ## Notes
                Hire for the bottleneck that blocks the next milestone, not for the work you personally dislike. Those are often different roles.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A dated hiring plan for the next eighteen months exists, each role linked to a milestone and showing runway after the hire."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "6"
              tasks:
                - "List the three milestones the next funding round or profitability depends on"
                - "Write the capability each milestone needs that nobody on the team has today"
                - "Put every planned role in hiring order with a target start month"
                - "Recalculate runway after each planned hire using your current burn"
                - "Walk your co-founder or lead investor through the plan and note their pushback"
            - name: Role scorecards before any job post
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Without a scorecard every interviewer quietly judges a different job, and the candidate who interviews best wins rather than the one who would do the work best. A one-page scorecard per role, stating the mission, the four or five outcomes expected in the first year and the competencies that matter, gives every later step something to measure against.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-sentence mission written for each open role.
                2. Four to six measurable first-year outcomes listed per role.
                3. Five to eight competencies chosen, split into must-have and nice-to-have.
                4. Each scorecard read and agreed by everyone who will interview for it.

                ## Notes
                If an outcome cannot be checked at the end of the year, rewrite it until it can. "Owns the backend" is not an outcome; "payment flow handles a thousand orders a day without manual fixes" is.
              priority: high

            - name: Hire, contract or outsource for each need
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Not every gap in a ten-person company needs a permanent employee: bookkeeping, design sprints and a one-off data migration are often better bought by the hour. Deciding for each need whether to hire, contract or outsource keeps permanent seats for the work that is core, continuous and hard to hand over.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every current and expected gap listed with hours per week and how long it will last.

                4. A conversion rule written for when a contractor role becomes a permanent hire.

                ## Notes
                Check how your country treats long-running contractors who work like employees. Misclassification can bring back taxes and penalties, so ask your accountant or employment lawyer before a contract runs past a few months.

                Before the first employee starts, the company usually has to register as an employer with the tax authority, run payroll correctly from the first pay day, and hold the insurance and records the law requires.                 Early employees accept lower pay partly for equity, so a founder who improvises grants offer by offer ends up with unfair gaps that surface the first time two employees compare notes.             - name: Structured interview loop design
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Unstructured chats are among the weakest predictors of job performance, yet they are how most first hires are made.                 ## Notes

# First Ten Hires

The first ten people a startup hires decide what it can build, how fast it moves and who will want to join next, and most founders make these hires with no recruiter, no process and very little time. This area starts with the foundations (a hiring plan tied to runway, role scorecards, the cost of each hire, the employer paperwork, the option pool and a written bar), then builds the operating machinery of sourcing, interviewing, references and follow-up, the skills a founder needs to run it, the big decisions about which roles come first, the dated hiring moments, the situations different founders face, and a few specialist moves for when the team is nearly ten.

The rhythms that repeat are a Monday pipeline review, a sourcing hour every Tuesday and Friday, a Thursday interviewer debrief, a Friday sweep so no candidate waits, monthly referral asks, metrics and a hiring retrospective, and a quarterly check of the plan, contractors and option grants against the runway. The Onboarding 30/60/90, Metrics log, Vendor and Skills matrix templates pair with the projects that point to them.
