                ## Purpose
                Founders who start posting without a reason drift into vanity metrics and stop after six weeks. Writing down who you want to reach (buyers, future hires, investors or other founders), what you want them to do and what you will not trade for attention gives every later post a test to pass.

                ## Milestones
                1. One primary audience named, with a sentence on what they care about.
                2. One desired action written down, such as joining the waitlist or replying with a problem.
                3. Three things you will not do for reach, recorded in plain words.
                4. The page shared with your co-founder and pinned where you draft posts.

                ## Notes
                Pick one primary audience. Posts written for buyers and posts written for other founders read very differently, and mixing them pleases neither.

              description: |-

                The hardest part of building in public is not writing, it is knowing what must never appear: customer names without permission, salaries, a staff member's struggles, the cash balance during a raise, security details.                 ## Milestones

                3. Contracts, NDAs and investor documents checked for confidentiality clauses that bind you.

                ## Notes

# Founder Building in Public

This area is for founders who want to share how the company is really going, in public, so that customers, future hires and supporters find them, without giving away what should stay private. It starts with the foundations (a goal and audience, a written disclosure line, consent from co-founders and investors, channels, a profile, an origin post and account security), then the weekly and monthly machinery of build logs, numbers posts, replies and a newsletter, the writing and demo skills that make posts worth reading, decisions on open dashboards, roadmaps and ghostwriters, dated moments like milestone posts and a funding announcement, variants for the employed, the bootstrapped, the enterprise seller and the slow deep tech company, and finally specialist work on pile-ons, copycats and publishing the company handbook.

What repeats is a daily one-line capture, a Friday build log, twice-weekly reply blocks, a monthly numbers post and newsletter, a monthly changelog and livestream, and a quarterly look at which posts actually brought customers, with a yearly public letter. The **Metrics log**, **Content pipeline**, **Risk register** and **Marketing campaign** templates pair with the audience baseline, repurposing, the copycat review and the milestone announcement.
