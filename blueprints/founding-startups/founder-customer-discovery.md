              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Founders who start interviewing without written assumptions tend to hear whatever confirms the idea. Listing the eight to twelve beliefs the business depends on, and ranking them by how fatal it would be if each were wrong, tells you what the first twenty conversations must actually test.

                ## Milestones
                1. Eight to twelve assumptions written as falsifiable statements about customers, the problem and current behaviour.
                2. Each assumption scored for impact if wrong and for the evidence you already hold.
                3. The three riskiest assumptions chosen as the focus of the first interview round.
                4. The list dated and saved where your co-founder can see and challenge it.

                ## Notes
                Write assumptions about the customer, not the product. 'Operations managers rebuild this report by hand every week' can be tested in an interview; 'they will love our dashboard' cannot.
              priority: high

            - name: Interview guide built on past behaviour
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Questions such as 'would you use this?' or 'how much would you pay?' invite kind guesses about the future. A guide built on what the person did the last time the problem happened, what it cost them and what they tried gets facts you can make decisions from.

                ## Milestones
                1. Eight to twelve open questions, each anchored on a specific recent occasion.
                2. Every question checked so that none of them mentions your solution.
                3. A short opening that explains you are learning, not selling, and asks permission to record.
                4. A closing block that asks for referrals and permission to follow up.
                5. The guide trimmed until it fits in 25 minutes with time left to follow threads.

                ## Notes
                A guide is a safety net, not a script. The best material comes from follow-up questions about something the person just said, so leave room for them.

# Founder Customer Discovery

Founder Customer Discovery is the craft of talking to the people you hope to serve and coming away with facts rather than flattery. It runs from foundations (riskiest assumptions, a narrow segment, a recruiting list and a past-behaviour interview guide) through the weekly machinery of booking, debriefing and synthesis, then interviewing skills, decisions drawn from the evidence, dated events such as sprint weeks and readouts, variants for employed, technical, regulated, consumer and international founders, and finally specialist work with churned users, surveys and teaching the team.

The rhythm is weekly: outreach on Mondays, referral counts midweek, synthesis on Thursdays and debrief checks on Fridays, with monthly evidence summaries, assumption reviews and call-backs, and a quarterly refresh of the interview guide. The Meeting notes, Feedback themes, Metrics log, Purchase decision and Training program templates pair with it.
