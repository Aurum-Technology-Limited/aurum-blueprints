                Founders join peer groups for very different reasons: some want accountability on targets, some want a room where they can admit they are scared, and some want introductions.             - name: Confidentiality agreement for the group
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Nothing said in a founder group is safe to share unless every member has agreed in plain words what stays in the room. A short written agreement covering company names, numbers, people and personal matters, signed or affirmed by everyone, is what lets a founder bring a layoff plan or a marriage under strain before it is public.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page confidentiality agreement drafted in plain language.
                2. Clear lines on what may never leave the room and what a member may share about their own issue.
                3. The consequence of a breach agreed, up to and including removal.
                4. Every member's agreement recorded before the first full meeting.

                ## Notes
                Keep it a promise between people rather than a legal contract. If a member needs it formalised for their investors, ask a lawyer to review a version rather than drafting legal terms yourselves.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:

            - name: Moderating a founder peer meeting
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Moderating is a skill of its own: holding time, drawing out the quiet member, stopping advice, and closing an issue cleanly. Preparing properly for your turn, and asking for feedback afterwards, means you leave the role a better leader of your own team meetings too.

                ## Milestones
                1. The running order, timings and open issues reviewed before your first meeting.
                2. Every member given airtime in each meeting you run.

# Founder Peer Groups

This area is for founders who want a small group of peers they can tell the truth to: the people who understand a missed payroll or a co-founder fight because they have lived one. It starts with foundations (what you want from a group, the options near you, confidentiality, composition, invitations, a founding meeting and a charter), then the monthly machinery of updates, issues, commitments and moderation, the skills of sharing experience rather than advice, the hard decisions about members who drift or break trust, dated events such as retreats and crisis meetings, variants for first-time, bootstrapped, remote, small-ecosystem and post-exit founders, and finally the specialist work of playbooks, networks of circles and ending a group well.

What repeats is a monthly meeting cycle with a written update, an issue queue and a commitments follow-up, a mid-month check-in, quarterly moderator rotation, attendance, money and conflict reviews, and a yearly retreat and health survey. The **Purchase decision**, **Meeting notes**, **Trip** and **Feedback themes** templates pair with choosing a paid programme, logging commitments, planning the retreat and reading the annual survey. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
