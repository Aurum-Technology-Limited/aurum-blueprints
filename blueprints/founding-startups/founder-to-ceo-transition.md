            - name: Inventory of every job the founder still holds
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Founders at twenty or thirty people are usually still the head of product, the top salesperson, the recruiter and the final approver of every expense, without ever having decided to be. Listing each job you hold, with the hours it takes and who could own it instead, turns a vague sense of overload into a list you can actually hand over.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every recurring responsibility you hold written down, including the informal ones such as approving copy or answering support escalations.
                2. Each responsibility tagged with rough weekly hours and how often only you can do it.
                3. A named person, a role still to hire, or the word nobody written beside every item.
                4. The five items that cost the most hours with the least CEO judgement highlighted.

                ## Notes
                Ask two people who work closely with you to add what you missed. The jobs founders forget are the ones people quietly wait on them for.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A written inventory of at least twenty responsibilities, each with hours, a possible owner and the top five hand-off candidates marked."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:
                - "List every responsibility you held in the last month in one document"
                - "Add rough weekly hours beside each responsibility"
                - "Ask two close colleagues what people still wait on you for"
                - "Highlight the five costliest items that need little CEO judgement"

            - name: Company stage diagnosis for the next 18 months
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The CEO a twelve-person company needs is not the CEO a sixty-person company needs, and the change usually arrives a year before the founder notices. Writing down where headcount, revenue, customers and funding are likely to be in eighteen months tells you which new demands the seat will carry and what has to change in how you work.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page forecast of headcount, revenue, customer count and funding position at six, twelve and eighteen months.
                2. The three biggest new demands on the CEO at that scale named, such as managing managers or a formal board.
                3. The current ways of working that will break at that scale listed.
                4. The diagnosis discussed with at least one investor or experienced founder.

                ## Notes
                Use the plan you already have, not a fresh optimistic one. The point is the shape of the job, not a precise number.

            - name: Written CEO role for the next stage
              description: |-

                Few founders have ever seen their own job written down, so nobody, including them, can tell what the CEO should stop doing. A one-page role covering the four or five things only the CEO can own, such as strategy, the leadership team, capital and culture, becomes the test for every request on your time.

                ## Milestones

                3. The role reviewed by your chair or lead investor and one senior colleague.

                ## Notes
                Write it for the company you will be in eighteen months, not the one you run today, or it will simply describe your current overload.

            - name: Decision rights register for the leadership team
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                In a founder-led company most decisions drift upward to the founder by default, and the queue outside your door is the clearest sign the company has outgrown that. A register naming who decides, who must be consulted and who is simply told for the thirty most common decisions lets the team move without waiting for you.

                ## Milestones
                1. The thirty most frequent company decisions listed, from pricing exceptions to hiring approvals.
                2. Each decision assigned one decider, the people consulted and the people informed.
                3. The register walked through with the leadership team and corrected.
                4. Five decisions formally moved away from the CEO and announced.

                ## Notes
                One decider per decision, never two. Shared ownership is how decisions come back to the founder.
              priority: high

# Founder to CEO Transition

This area is for founders whose company has outgrown the way they run it, and for the managers working alongside them while the seat changes shape. It starts with foundations (an inventory of every job you still hold, a written CEO role, decision rights and a first round of honest feedback), then the operating machinery of a leadership team and a board, the skills the CEO seat asks for, the hand-offs and hiring decisions that build the team, the dated events that mark the change, variants for technical founders, co-founders, early employees and distributed companies, and finally the specialist questions of succession, board composition and whether you stay CEO at all.

What repeats is a weekly leadership meeting and Friday hand-off review, executive 1:1s, a monthly business review, strategy memo, skip-levels and reflection, quarterly goals and board meetings, and yearly reviews of your own performance, spending authority, pay and succession. The **Development plan**, **Feedback themes**, **1:1**, **Board meeting**, **Skills matrix** and **Onboarding 30/60/90** templates pair with your growth plan, the 360, executive 1:1s, board preparation, the team gap analysis and the first executive's onboarding.
