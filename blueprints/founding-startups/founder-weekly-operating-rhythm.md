            - name: Two-week audit of where founder hours go
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most founders believe they spend half their week on product and customers, and a fortnight of honest logging usually shows a third of it disappearing into messages, status meetings and other people's urgencies. Logging every half hour for ten working days gives you the real baseline that every later change to the week is measured against.

                ## Milestones
                1. Ten working days logged in 30-minute blocks, with nothing left blank.
                2. Every block tagged with one of six to eight categories, such as customers, product, team, hiring, fundraising, admin and reactive.
                3. Total hours per category calculated and shown as a percentage of the fortnight.
                4. Three findings written down that surprised you, each with the hours behind it.

                ## Notes
                Log as you go, not from memory at the end of the day. Reconstructed days flatter you by roughly the amount you were trying to measure.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A ten-day time log with category totals and percentages, plus three written findings, exists before any calendar change is made."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:
                - "Pick six to eight time categories and write them at the top of a sheet"
                - "Log each 30-minute block of today before you finish work"
                - "Total the hours per category at the end of day ten"
                - "Write the three findings that most surprised you"
            - name: Three company priorities for this quarter
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Designing a week around priorities only works if the priorities exist in writing, and a founder holding seven goals in their head will spend each day on whichever one shouted loudest. Narrowing the quarter to three outcomes, each with a measure and an owner, gives every meeting, block and refusal something to point at.

                ## Milestones
                1. A long list of everything the company could pursue this quarter, written in one sitting.
                2. Three priorities chosen, each phrased as an outcome with a number and an end date.
                3. One owner named for each priority, even in a team of two.
                4. The three priorities pinned where the team sees them every week, such as the top of the team channel or the kickoff agenda.

                ## Notes
                If a priority has no number, it is a theme. Keep themes out of the list, or the list grows back to seven.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "Three quarterly priorities, each with a measurable outcome, end date and named owner, are written down and visible to the whole team."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "Write every goal competing for this quarter on one page"
                - "Cross out all but three, keeping the ones that move revenue or survival"
                - "Rewrite each survivor as an outcome with a number and a date"
                - "Post the three priorities with owners in the team channel"

            - name: Recurring meeting inventory and cull
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Recurring meetings breed quietly: a weekly sync set up for a launch six months ago is still running, now with nine people in it. Listing every recurring meeting with its cost in people-hours and cutting or shrinking the ones nobody would recreate today often hands back five to ten hours a week across a small team.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every recurring meeting you attend or own listed with length, frequency and attendees.

# Founder Weekly Operating Rhythm

This area is for founders whose week is decided by whoever messaged last. It starts with foundations (a two-week audit of where your hours actually go, three quarterly priorities, an ideal week drawn on the calendar, a meeting cull and protected thinking blocks), then the routines that run the week, the skills that make those routines work, the decisions that reshape the calendar, the dated weeks that need a different plan, variants for part-time, remote, technical, caregiving, co-founding and travelling founders, and finally the specialist cadence work of a growing company.

What repeats is a short daily plan and two message blocks, a Monday numbers read and team kickoff, Tuesday one-on-ones, a Wednesday thinking session, a Thursday customer slot and a Friday review, with a monthly all-hands, investor update, priorities check and rhythm retro, and quarterly planning and time-allocation reviews. The **Meeting notes**, **1:1**, **Metrics log**, **Board meeting** and **Purchase decision** templates pair with the Monday kickoff, one-on-ones, the numbers read, board week and the tool choice.
