            - name: Mission statement and theory of change
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every later decision in a social enterprise, from pricing to hiring to which grant to chase, gets tested against what change you exist to make. A one-page theory of change links the problem, your activities, the outputs they produce and the outcomes they should cause, with the assumptions in between written down so they can be tested rather than defended.

                ## Milestones
                1. A mission statement of one or two sentences naming who benefits and what changes for them.
                2. A one-page theory of change running from problem to activities, outputs, short-term outcomes and long-term impact.
                3. The three riskiest assumptions in the chain written beside the arrows they sit on.
                4. The page reviewed by at least two people affected by the problem and one outsider.

                ## Notes
                Keep outputs and outcomes apart: 200 people trained is an output, 80 of them in work six months later is an outcome. Funders and investors will ask for the second.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A one-page theory of change with named assumptions exists and has been reviewed by two affected people and one outsider."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "8"
              tasks:
                - "Write the problem you exist to solve in two sentences, naming who it affects"
                - "Draw the chain from activities to outputs to outcomes on one page"
                - "Mark the three assumptions you are least sure of"
                - "Ask two people with lived experience of the problem to challenge the chain"

            - name: Who pays versus who benefits model
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                In most social enterprises the person who benefits cannot pay the full cost, so the money has to come from somewhere else: a corporate buyer, a council contract, a premium customer, a donor or a grant. Drawing the flow of value and money between beneficiaries and payers early is what separates a sustainable model from a charity that sells a little.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every beneficiary group and every possible payer drawn on one diagram with arrows for value and money.
                2. One primary income model chosen, such as direct sale, cross-subsidy, contract or employment of beneficiaries.
                3. A rough check that the payer's willingness to pay covers the cost of serving the beneficiary.
                4. The two weakest links in the money flow named, with a test planned for each.

                ## Notes
                Common models include selling to customers who are also beneficiaries, a paying market subsidising a free one, public or corporate contracts for outcomes, and trading businesses that employ the people they exist to help.
              priority: high
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A value and money flow diagram exists with one primary income model chosen and its two weakest links named with tests planned."
                cadence: one-shot

              tasks:

                - "Estimate the cost of serving one beneficiary for a year"

                - "Pick the primary income model and note why the others lost"
            - name: Choosing the legal structure for a mission-led company
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The structure decides who can invest, whether you can take grants, how profits may be used and how hard the mission is to strip out later. The options differ by country, for example a community interest company in the UK, a benefit corporation in many US states, a cooperative, a company limited by guarantee or a charity with a trading subsidiary, and switching after investment is slow and costly.

                ## Milestones
                1. The structures available in your country listed with what each allows on grants, equity, profit distribution and asset locks.
                2. Each option scored against your funding plan, governance wishes and mission protection needs.
                3. Advice taken from a lawyer or social enterprise support body who knows mission-led structures.
                4. A structure chosen and the reasons recorded in a short decision note.

                ## Notes
                This is a structure decision, not legal advice. Ask whether the funders you most want will fund that form: some grant makers only fund charities, and most equity investors cannot buy shares in a company limited by guarantee.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 60
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A written decision note records the chosen legal structure, the options compared and the professional advice taken."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "10"
              tasks:
                - "List the mission-led legal forms your country recognises"
                - "Note for each whether it can take grants, equity and loans"
                - "Book a session with a lawyer or social enterprise support body"
                - "Write the decision note with the structure chosen and why"
            - name: Mission lock in the founding documents
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Missions that live only in the founders' heads can be dropped by the next board, the next investor or a buyer. Writing the purpose, an asset lock where the structure allows one, and the decisions that need special approval into the articles or bylaws makes the mission survive the people who wrote it.

                ## Milestones
                1. A purpose clause drafted that names the social or environmental objective in plain words.
                2. The decisions needing a special majority or a mission guardian's consent listed, such as changing the purpose or selling the company.
                3. An asset lock or profit distribution cap included where the structure provides for one.
                4. The final documents reviewed by a lawyer and filed with the company registry.

                ## Notes
                Some founders add a golden share held by a mission trust or a body of beneficiaries, so the purpose cannot change without their vote. Ask your lawyer what is enforceable where you are.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 90

# Founding a Social Enterprise

This area is for founders building a company whose social or environmental mission sits at its core, and for charity leaders turning a programme into something that trades. It starts with the foundations (a theory of change, evidence of the problem, who pays and who benefits, the legal structure, a mission lock and a first impact budget), moves through the routines that keep impact and money honest, the skills of measurement, grant writing and social finance, the hard choices about income mix, certification and hiring, dated moments like a first funding close or a public tender, variants for charities, community-owned and lived-experience ventures, and ends with specialist work on evaluation, outcome contracts, replication and mission-protected ownership.

What repeats is a weekly funding pipeline and impact data check, a monthly impact and finance dashboard, beneficiary feedback and restricted funds review, quarterly board, income mix and mission drift reviews, and yearly safeguarding, filing, carbon and impact report cycles. The **Metrics log**, **Feedback themes**, **Board meeting**, **Compliance control** and **Risk register** templates pair with the dashboard, beneficiary feedback, the quarterly board, safeguarding and the mission drift watch.
