            - name: Shutdown decision memo with the numbers
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Founders usually feel the end coming months before they say it aloud, and the delay burns cash that could have paid staff and creditors in full. A two-page memo that sets out cash, burn, the realistic funding options left and what each extra month costs turns a dread into a decision the board can act on. If the memo argues for carrying on, that is a result too, as long as it names a date to look again.

                ## Milestones
                1. A memo stating cash in the bank, monthly net burn and the date the company can no longer pay its debts as they fall due.
                2. Every live funding, sale or revenue option listed with an honest probability and the date it would land.
                3. The cost of waiting one, two and three more months written as money that would otherwise go to staff and creditors.
                4. A recommendation to close, continue to a named date or pursue a sale, read by your co-founders and one outside adviser.

                ## Notes
                Pivot questions belong elsewhere; this memo assumes the main options have been tried. Date it and keep it, because it may matter later if anyone asks when the directors knew.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 10
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision

# Winding Down a Startup

Winding Down a Startup is for the founder who has run out of road, or can see the end of it, and wants to close the company properly rather than let it fade. It starts with foundations (the decision memo, a cash and liabilities snapshot, the solvency question, advisers, the board resolution and a dated timeline), then the weekly machinery that carries a company through its last months, the knowledge of directors' duties, creditor priority and investor instruments, the decisions about a sale, the code, the data and remaining cash, the dated events from telling the team to the final filing, variants for solo, bootstrapped, visa-holding and hardware founders, and finally the public announcement, the post-mortem and what you do next.

What repeats is a Monday wind-down stand-up, a Thursday cash and payables review, a Friday creditor log, daily support triage until switch-off, monthly investor updates and vendor sweeps, a watch on statutory notices, and yearly checks on the records you must keep after dissolution. The Board meeting, Operational checklist, Risk register, Meeting notes and Development plan templates pair with the projects that name them.
