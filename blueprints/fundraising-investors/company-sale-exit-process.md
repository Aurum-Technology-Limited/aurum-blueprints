            - name: Exit objectives and walk-away terms
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Owners who go to market without written goals tend to anchor on the first number a buyer says and discover too late that they cared more about the team, the timing or their own role afterwards. Writing down what a good exit means to you, in price, timing, involvement and legacy, gives every later adviser and negotiation a fixed point, and it costs an afternoon now rather than a regret later.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page note of what you want from a sale: net proceeds, timing, your role afterwards and what happens to staff and the brand.
                2. Each goal ranked, so you know which you would trade for which.
                3. Walk-away terms written: the lowest net figure, the longest earn-out and the role you would refuse.
                4. Co-founders or co-owners have written their own version and the differences are discussed.

                ## Notes
                Write the minimum as cash in your pocket after tax, fees and debt, not as a headline valuation. The two can be far apart.

                Before you start: this plan is general information to help you organise your own money decisions. It is not financial, investment, tax or legal advice. Check decisions that matter with a qualified, licensed adviser in your country, and remember that investing and trading can lose money.

            - name: Exit readiness self-assessment
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Buyers discount for the same problems again and again: the owner holds every key relationship, one customer is a third of revenue, contracts are missing, the accounts need explaining. Scoring the business against those known issues a year or two before a sale shows which gaps you can close in time and which you will have to price in.

                ## Milestones
                1. The business scored against a fixed list: owner dependence, customer concentration, recurring revenue, records, management depth, legal tidiness and growth story.
                2. Each gap rated by how much it might cost in price or terms and how long it takes to fix.
                3. A remediation plan with an owner and date for the five gaps that matter most.
                4. The assessment shared with your accountant or adviser for a second view.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Remediation plan** template. Be harsh: a buyer's diligence team will be.

                ## Purpose
                The choice between an investment bank, an M&A boutique, a business broker or running the sale yourself shapes who sees the business, how competitive the process gets and what it costs.             - name: Exit proceeds waterfall at five prices
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Headline price and what each person actually receives can be very different once debt, liquidation preferences, option exercises, adviser fees and escrows come out. A waterfall at five sale prices shows founders, employees and investors their real outcome, reveals the price below which a deal makes no sense for the team, and stops nasty surprises on completion day.

                ## Milestones

                2. Liquidation preferences, participation and conversion applied for every share class.
                3. Option holders' net proceeds shown after exercise price and any withholding.
                4. Results at five prices, from a weak offer to a stretch outcome, checked by your lawyer or accountant.

                ## Notes
                Include the fees you will actually pay: adviser success fee, legal, accounting and any warranty insurance premium. They often total several per cent of the price.

                ## Purpose
                Whether a buyer takes your shares or buys the assets, how consideration is split over time and who holds the shares before the sale can change what you keep by a large margin. # Company Sale & Exit Process

This area is for founders and owner-managers who expect to sell their company in the next few years, or who already have a buyer at the door. It starts with the foundations (what you want from an exit, an honest readiness check, the adviser team, shareholder alignment and what the proceeds really look like), sets up the operating rhythms that keep numbers and records buyer-grade, teaches the mechanics of valuation, letters of intent and deal terms, then works through the improvements and decisions that move price, the dated events of a live process, the situations that change the shape of a sale and finally the specialist work around clearances, insurance and life after completion.

What repeats is a monthly readiness scorecard and a monthly close fit for a buyer, quarterly housekeeping of contracts and records, a refreshed buyer map, and, while a process is live, a Monday deal team call, twice-weekly diligence triage, a weekly check that trading stays on plan and monthly check-ins with key people. After completion, earn-out metrics and escrow dates get their own monthly and yearly checks. The Vendor, Remediation plan, Metrics log, Month-end close, Meeting notes, Purchase decision and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them.
