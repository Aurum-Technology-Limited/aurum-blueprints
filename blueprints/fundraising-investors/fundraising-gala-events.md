            - name: Gala go or no-go decision with a net target
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Galas are among the most expensive ways to raise money, and many charities run one out of habit while netting less than a well-run appeal would. Deciding up front what the evening must clear after costs, and whether a gala beats the alternatives for your supporters, protects staff time and keeps the event honest.

                ## Milestones
                1. Gross income, costs and staff hours from previous events, or comparable local galas, gathered in one place.
                2. A net income target agreed with the director or board, with the most the charity will spend to reach it.
                3. The gala compared against at least two alternatives, such as a direct appeal or a small donor dinner.
                4. A go or no-go decision recorded with the reasons in two or three sentences.

                ## Notes
                Count staff time as a cost. A gala that hits its net target but swallows three months of the fundraising team may still lose money overall.

                Before you start: this plan is general information to help you organise your own money decisions. It is not financial, investment, tax or legal advice. Check decisions that matter with a qualified, licensed adviser in your country, and remember that investing and trading can lose money.
              priority: high

                output_kind: decision

            - name: Gala budget with break-even and cost ratio
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A gala budget built backwards from a hoped-for headline number tends to grow its costs and shrink its income as the date nears. Building it line by line from quotes, with a break-even point and a cost-to-raise ratio, shows the committee early what must be true for the night to pay off.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every cost line listed: venue, catering, drinks, audiovisual, entertainment, decoration, printing, staff time and payment fees.
                2. Income split by tickets, sponsorship, auction, paddle raise and donations, each with a cautious and a likely figure.
                3. The break-even number of tickets and sponsors worked out.
                4. A target cost ratio, such as costs at no more than a third of gross income, agreed and written at the top.
                5. A contingency of around ten percent held back on costs.

                ## Notes
                Use the cautious income figures for decisions and the likely ones for morale. Committees that plan on the likely case usually overspend.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A line-by-line gala budget with a break-even point, cost ratio and contingency is approved by the director or finance lead."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "8"
              tasks:
                - "Copy last year's event costs into a new budget sheet"
                - "Request written quotes for venue, catering and audiovisual"

                Good venues for a seated dinner of 200 to 400 often book a year ahead, and a date that clashes with another charity's ball, a school holiday or a big match can halve the room. Checking the local events calendar first, then comparing venues on capacity, minimum spend and what is included, avoids the most expensive mistake a gala can make.

                ## Milestones

                3. Site visits made at the time of day the gala will run.
                4. A provisional hold placed on the chosen date, with the date the hold lapses written down.
                5. The contract reviewed for cancellation terms and the deposit schedule before signing.

            - name: Paddle raise design and script
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The paddle raise, where guests lift a numbered paddle to pledge a set amount towards the cause, is often the largest single income line at a gala, and it works best when it is scripted rather than improvised.                 ## Milestones

# Fundraising Gala Events

This area is for charities, community groups and fundraising teams planning a gala, auction or fundraising dinner that has to clear a real net figure, not just fill a room. It starts with the foundations (a go or no-go decision, a budget, the date and venue, the committee, prices, licences and the case for the evening), then the routines that run planning week to week, the skills behind sponsorship, the auction, the paddle raise and the programme, the decisions about platforms, format, costs and games, the dated moments from the invitation launch to the board report, situations from a first small gala to a workplace team dinner, and finally the work of reading years of results and reinventing a signature event.

What repeats is a weekly stand-up with weekly checks on ticket sales, sponsors, auction lots and the guest register, monthly reconciliation, risk, supplier and committee reviews, a quarterly look at how many first-time guests give again, and the yearly cycle of holding the next date and reporting results to the board. The Compliance control, Risk register, Vendor, Purchase decision, Operational checklist and Metrics log templates pair with the projects that name them.
