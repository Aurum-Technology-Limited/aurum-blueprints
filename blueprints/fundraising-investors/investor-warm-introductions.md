              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most venture funds see far more decks than they can read, and many partners still book first meetings mainly from introductions they trust. Deciding up front how many first meetings the round needs, how many intros that takes and how much founder time you will give it turns intro asking from an awkward favour into a planned part of the raise. Doing it before outreach opens leaves time to build the paths you do not have yet.

                ## Milestones
                1. The number of first meetings the round needs written down, with the intros required at a cautious conversion rate.
                2. A date by which the first wave of intro asks must go out.
                3. Weekly founder hours set aside for intro work during the raise.
                4. The plan shared with co-founders, with each connector assigned to one founder.

                ## Notes
                Start with a cautious conversion assumption and replace it with your own rate after the first ten asks. Intros are one channel among several, so keep the plan honest about what it can deliver.

            - name: Forwardable introduction email
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The kindest thing you can do for a connector is send them an email they can forward without editing: a subject line, three or four sentences on what the company does, the proof that matters and the round, with the deck link underneath. Investors decide whether to accept an intro from that forwarded text in seconds, so it deserves the same care as the first slide of the deck.

                ## Milestones
                1. A forwardable email under 150 words with subject line, one-line description, two proof points and the round.
                2. A version tailored for each of your three most common investor types.
                3. The email tested on two founders who have raised, and revised from their reactions.
                4. The current version saved where every co-founder can find it.

                ## Notes
                Write it so it reads well with the connector's own line on top. Keep the traction figures current, because a stale number in a forwarded email travels further than you expect.
              priority: high

# Investor Warm Introductions

This area is for founders who want investor meetings to start with a trusted introduction rather than a cold email. It begins with the foundations (an intro plan, a map of who you know, ranked paths to your top investors, a forwardable email and a tracker), then the weekly and monthly routines that keep intros moving and connectors warm, the skills of asking well and reading the replies, decisions on tools, paid introducers and going direct, the dated moments from a sprint week to the thank-you round, situations such as solo, first-time and relocating founders, and specialist work with scouts, intro exchanges and back-channel calls.

What repeats is a weekly pipeline review and thank-you round while a raise is live, a daily reply sweep in the busiest weeks, monthly connector touches, conversion counts and help given back, and quarterly notes to your top connectors with a refresh of your network sheet. The Person, Metrics log, Purchase decision and Operational checklist templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
