            - name: Deciding whether to raise pre-seed money now
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Outside money starts a clock: once angels own part of the company, they expect a venture-sized outcome and a next round within about eighteen months. Before writing a single investor email, a first-time founder should test whether customer revenue, a grant, a part-time job or savings could carry the company to the same point without giving away a slice of it.

                ## Milestones
                1. The next twelve months of costs listed, with what each source of money (savings, revenue, grants, outside investment) could cover.
                2. A written answer to whether the business could plausibly return a large multiple of an angel's money.
                3. A raise or wait decision recorded with the date and the two or three reasons behind it.
                4. If waiting, a trigger written down that would reopen the question, such as a first paying customer.

                ## Notes
                Many good businesses are a poor fit for angel money because they will grow steadily rather than very fast. That is a reason to fund them differently, not a judgement on the idea.
              priority: high

              tasks:

            - name: Pre-seed readiness check before the first ask
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Angels forgive a lot at pre-seed, but they still expect a founder who can explain the problem, show some proof that people want the solution and say clearly what the money is for. A one-afternoon readiness check against those questions shows the gaps while they can still be filled quietly, rather than in front of the investors you most wanted.

                ## Milestones
                1. Answers drafted to the ten questions pre-seed investors ask most, each in two or three sentences.
                2. The weakest three answers marked, with what would make each stronger.
                3. A working product, prototype or demand test that can be shown in under five minutes.
                4. A start date for outreach set, or a list of gaps to close first.

                ## Notes
                Typical questions: what problem, for whom, why now, why you, what evidence, how big, how it makes money, who else is trying, what the money buys and what happens next.

            - name: Deciding how much to raise at pre-seed
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Raise too little and the company runs out before the milestones; raise too much and the founder gives away more of the company than needed, or chases a target nobody fills. A pre-seed amount usually comes from the cost of reaching the milestones plus a buffer of several months, then a check against what is normal in your market.

                ## Milestones
                1. Monthly burn estimated for the period the round must cover, with team, product and other costs separated.
                2. A target amount set that covers the milestones plus a three to six month buffer.
                3. A minimum and a maximum written beside the target.
                4. The amount compared with recent pre-seed rounds for similar companies in your region.

                ## Notes
                Investors often ask how long the money lasts. Have the month count ready, not just the amount.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A target, minimum and maximum raise amount, each tied to a runway month count, are recorded and agreed by the founders."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "6"
              tasks:
                - "Estimate monthly spend for team, product and other costs"
                - "Multiply by the months to the milestones and add a buffer"
                - "Write a minimum, target and maximum amount"
                - "Compare the target with three recent pre-seed rounds near you"

                ## Purpose
                Most pre-seed raises take longer than founders expect, often three to six months, and many first-time founders go unpaid throughout. Knowing exactly how many months your household can last, and what you will do if the raise is slower, stops money worries from pushing you into a bad deal at the end.

            - name: Co-founder equity split and vesting before outside money
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Angels look at the founder split early, and an uneven or unvested split is one of the few things that stops a pre-seed deal outright. Agreeing the split, putting founder shares on a vesting schedule and writing down what happens if someone leaves is far easier before investors are involved than after.

                ## Milestones
                1. A written split agreed by all founders, with the reasoning recorded.
                2. Founder shares placed on a vesting schedule with a cliff, drafted by a lawyer.
                3. Leaver terms agreed for a founder who departs early.
                4. Signed documents stored where the lawyer and every founder can find them.

                ## Notes
                Four-year vesting with a one-year cliff is a common pattern, but your lawyer should advise on what suits your country and situation. Some tax filings tied to founder shares have short deadlines, so ask about them at the same meeting.
              priority: high

# Pre-Seed & Angel Round

This area takes a first-time founder from the question of whether to raise at all to the last signed cheque of a pre-seed round from angels, friends and family and small pre-seed funds. What repeats is the raise rhythm: a Monday plan and Friday review, a weekly commitment reconciliation, product check and founder handover, and monthly progress notes to interested angels, runway and traction checks and a pass reasons review.
