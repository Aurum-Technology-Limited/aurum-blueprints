            - name: Deciding whether to raise a fund now
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most first-time managers spend 12 to 24 months raising Fund I, often with no salary, and many stop short of a first close. Before quitting a job or telling anyone, test the decision against three things LPs will test it against: a track record you can attribute to yourself, deal access others lack, and enough personal money to live through a long raise. A written answer of yes, not yet or no saves a year if the honest answer is not yet.

                ## Milestones
                1. An honest list of the investments you led or sourced, with your role in each.
                2. Your personal runway in months written down, assuming no fund income for 18 months.
                3. Three experienced GPs or LPs asked whether they would back you today, and their answers recorded.
                4. A written decision: raise now, build more record first, or stay an angel, with the reason.

                ## Notes
                Not yet is a common and sensible answer. The SPV project later in this area is a way to build the record that changes it.

                Before you start: this plan is general information to help you organise your own money decisions. It is not financial, investment, tax or legal advice. Check decisions that matter with a qualified, licensed adviser in your country, and remember that investing and trading can lose money.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:

                output_kind: decision

            - name: One-page fund thesis
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                LPs hear dozens of generalist pitches a month, and the managers they remember can say in two sentences what they invest in, at what stage, why they win those deals and why the opportunity exists now. A one-page thesis forces those choices and becomes the source for the deck, the website and every introduction email.

                ## Milestones
                1. Sector, stage, geography and typical cheque size stated in one paragraph.
                2. The edge written as a reason founders choose you, backed by at least two examples.
                3. A why-now paragraph naming the shift that opens this window.
                4. The page tested on three people outside venture, who can repeat it back accurately.

                ## Notes
                A narrow thesis is easier to raise on than a broad one, but leave room for the best deal you will see that does not fit perfectly. Agree with your co-GP how much of the fund may sit outside it.

            - name: Attributable track record schedule
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Institutional LPs and many family offices will ask what you personally did in each past deal, and they check the answer with founders and co-investors. A schedule listing every investment with date, amount, round, your role and the current mark, separating deals you led from deals you joined, is the document most likely to decide whether a first meeting becomes a second.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every past investment listed with date, cheque, round, entry valuation and current status.
                2. Your role in each deal marked as sourced, led, joined or advised.
                3. Current marks backed by the latest priced round, or written down where the company has struggled.
                4. Losses and write-offs included, not just the winners.
                5. A note on which deals were made with your own money and which for an employer.

                ## Notes
                Check with counsel, and with any former employer, before showing deals made for someone else's fund; some firms restrict how their deals can be claimed. Never present a mark as a realised return.

            - name: Choosing the fund vehicle and structure
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Five vehicles are common for a first fund: a traditional closed-end limited partnership, a rolling fund that raises quarter by quarter, a series of deal-by-deal SPVs, a scout fund backed by a larger firm, or an angel fund pooling a network. Each changes how much you can raise, how long LPs commit, what it costs to run and what record you build for Fund II. Compare them on your own numbers before counsel starts drafting, because changing vehicle later means redoing the documents.

                ## Milestones
                1. The vehicles available to managers in your country listed with counsel's help.
                2. Each compared on minimum workable size, running cost, LP commitment period and fundraising rules.
                3. A preferred vehicle chosen, with a fallback if the target size is not reached.
                4. The choice confirmed in writing with formation counsel.

                ## Notes
                Domicile and tax treatment are for your fund counsel and tax adviser to settle. This project prepares the questions and records the decision.
              priority: high

            - name: Fund size and portfolio construction model
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Fund size should come from the strategy, not from the number that sounds credible: number of companies, initial cheque, ownership target, reserves for follow-ons, fees and the years of investing. A spreadsheet that ties those together shows LPs the fund is built to work, and shows you the smallest close at which the strategy still holds.

                ## Milestones
                1. Number of initial investments, average cheque and target ownership set.
                2. Reserves as a share of the fund, and the rule for using them, stated.
                3. Management fees and fund expenses over the fund's life deducted to show investable capital.
                4. A minimum workable fund size identified, below which the strategy breaks.
                5. The model reviewed by one GP who has raised a fund of similar size.

                ## Notes
                Do not show projected returns as promises. LPs want to see the construction logic; any return scenarios belong in clearly labelled illustrations agreed with counsel.
              priority: high

            - name: GP commitment and personal runway plan
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                LPs expect the general partner to put personal money into the fund, often quoted at 1 to 2 percent of commitments, and they notice when it is missing. You also need to live through the raise and the years before any carried interest arrives.                 ## Milestones

                ## Notes

# Raising a Venture Fund

This area is for operators, angels, founders after an exit and investment professionals raising a first venture or angel fund from limited partners. It starts with the foundations: the decision to raise, a thesis, an attributable track record, the vehicle, fund size, GP commitment, counsel and the offering rules. Then come the LP materials and pipeline machinery, the knowledge an LP expects you to have, the term and structure decisions, the dated milestones from launch to final close, situations such as raising while employed or as a solo GP, and specialist work like operational due diligence and planning Fund II.

Each week the pipeline tracker is updated on Monday and reviewed on Friday; monthly come the deal flow log, the letter to prospective LPs, the objection review, a piece of public writing and checks on runway, expenses and the data room, with quarterly track record, thesis and LP report rhythms and a yearly LP meeting. The Vendor template suits counsel and administrator selection, Compliance control the offering rules, Person your references, Metrics log the deal flow, Meeting notes your pitch practice and Risk register operational due diligence.
