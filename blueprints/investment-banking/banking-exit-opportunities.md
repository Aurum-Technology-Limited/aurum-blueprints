              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every buy-side interviewer asks why you want to leave banking, and a vague answer about wanting to invest rather than advise is the one they hear forty times a week.             - name: Exit timing window against your analyst class
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Timing decides more exits than talent does: large private equity firms may recruit very early in an analyst programme, others hire off-cycle all year, and corporate roles care little about class year. Setting a window that fits your bonus dates, deal milestones and the recruiting rhythm of your target seats means you leave when you are strongest, not when a headhunter happens to call.

                ## Milestones
                1. The usual recruiting timing for each of your top destinations written down, from people who went through it recently.
                2. Your bonus payment date, any deferred pay vesting dates and your contract notice period on one timeline.

                4. A target window of no more than three months chosen, with a fallback window six to twelve months later.

                ## Notes
                Recruiting timetables shift from year to year. Treat anything you read online as last year's pattern and confirm it with headhunters and recent hires.

# Banking Exit Opportunities

Most bankers start thinking about the exit within months of joining, and most of them drift into it: a headhunter calls, a process starts before the CV is ready and an offer arrives before they have decided what they want. This area makes the move deliberate. It starts with foundations (the options open from your seat, a written exit thesis, a timing window, the contract terms that bind you, a deal sheet and CV for the buy side and a confidentiality line you will not cross), then the operating routines of headhunters, networking, a pipeline and practice, the skills each destination tests (paper and timed LBOs, PE case studies, stock pitches, growth and venture fluency, corporate development interviews), the big choices between seats and offers, the recruiting events and the resignation itself, variants for first-years, associates, boutique and regional bankers, visa holders, parents and Caribbean bankers, and specialist routes into private credit, infrastructure, search funds and family offices.

What repeats is a weekly networking slot, a Friday pipeline review, weekend modelling practice, a Monday read of recruiting signals and a weekly entry in your investment idea notebook, with monthly headhunter check-ins, a readiness review of your deal sheet and stories, and a quarterly look at the whole plan with a mentor. The **Resume**, **Person**, **Job application**, **Skills matrix**, **Onboarding 30/60/90** and **Savings goal** templates pair with the CV, the headhunter register, the process tracker, the skill gap review, your first months on the buy side and a startup runway. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
