            - name: Written case for moving banks or staying
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most lateral moves start on a bad week, and a move made to escape one MD or one deal often lands you in the same problem with a different logo. Writing down what you want more of (deal flow, product, city, title, pay, team) and what a move would cost you gives you a test for every call from a headhunter.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three to five reasons for moving written down, each one specific to your current group rather than to banking in general.
                2. For each reason, a note on whether an internal fix (a new staffer, a group transfer, a frank talk with your VP) could solve it.
                3. A short list of what you would not give up in a move, such as a sponsor MD or a vesting date.
                4. A one-line decision: search actively, take calls only, or stay for now and review in three months.

                ## Notes
                If the honest answer is that you want to leave banking altogether, that is a different plan. A lateral move only makes sense if you still want the job, somewhere else.

            - name: Employment contract and policy terms review
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Notice periods, garden leave clauses, non-solicitation of clients and staff, and the conditions on deferred awards are all set in documents most bankers signed in a hurry. Reading them before you speak to another bank tells you how long you would be off the market, what you could not do afterwards, and which questions to take to an employment lawyer.

                ## Milestones
                1. Your contract, offer letter, staff handbook and deferred award plan rules gathered in one private folder outside the bank's systems, using only documents you are entitled to keep.
                2. Notice period, garden leave terms, non-solicitation and non-compete clauses summarised in plain words, with clause numbers.
                3. The rules on what happens to unvested awards and paid bonuses when you resign noted, including any clawback.
                4. A list of questions for an employment lawyer, or a note that none are needed.

                ## Notes
                Clauses mean different things in different jurisdictions and courts enforce them differently. Treat your summary as a list of questions, not a conclusion, and take anything unclear to an employment lawyer in your country.

# Lateral Moves Between Banks

This area is for a banker moving from one bank to another at the same or a nearby level: an analyst going from a middle-market firm to a bulge bracket group, an associate changing product, or a VP being hired with a guarantee. It starts with foundations (why you are moving, what your contract and unvested pay say, what you may and may not take with you, a deal sheet and a market map), moves to the routines that keep a quiet search organised, then interview skills, the decisions on offers, buyouts, title and deal credit, the dated moments from superday to resignation and garden leave, variants for analysts, senior bankers, visa holders, parents and regional bankers, and specialist work such as team moves and multi-year guarantees.

What repeats is a weekly pipeline update and Friday check on your current deals, monthly headhunter and lateral market reviews on different days, a quarterly deal sheet and compensation refresh, and a yearly look at vesting dates and references. The **Resume** template suits the deal sheet and CV, **Job application** suits each bank in the pipeline, **Person** suits headhunters and contacts, **Meeting notes** suits diligence calls, and **Onboarding 30/60/90** suits the first months at the new bank.
