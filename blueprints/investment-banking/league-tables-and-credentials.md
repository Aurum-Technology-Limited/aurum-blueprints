                ## Purpose
                Juniors often treat a league table as a fact, when it is the output of one provider's eligibility rules, credit rules, value definitions and cut-off dates. Reading the published criteria of the two or three providers your group quotes explains why the bank can be third in one table and ninth in another for the same quarter, and stops you promising a ranking that the rules will not give.

# League Tables and Credentials

League tables and credentials are the evidence a bank shows when it says it has done this before: the deal record behind every tombstone, the submissions that decide where the group ranks, and the credentials pages that land in front of clients. This area starts with foundations (how the tables are built, which rankings your group quotes, who owns submissions, a baseline audit, consent rules, a standard deal record and value conventions), then the operating routines from closing capture to quarterly submissions and reconciliation, the skills of reading and footnoting a table, the decisions about criteria and databases, the dated pushes and awards, variants for new analysts, boutiques, lateral hires, regional banks and senior bankers, and specialist work on ranking strategy, claim audit trails and merged banks.

What repeats is a Friday capture of newly closed deals, a Monday scan of announcements, a weekly clear of credentials requests, monthly reconciliation against provider data and library refreshes on varied days, a quarterly submission cycle and a yearly sign-off of the full record. The **Onboarding plan**, **Operational checklist** and **Metrics log** templates pair with the first month in the role, the closing capture routine and the ranking tracker.
