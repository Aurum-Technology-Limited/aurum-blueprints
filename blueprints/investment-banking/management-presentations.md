            - name: Management presentation scope and timetable
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Management teams agree to a sale process long before they understand that it will take two or three weeks of their diaries, often in the same quarter as a budget or a year-end. Pinning down where the presentations sit in the process, how many bidders are likely to be invited and how long each session runs lets the client protect the time before it is gone.

                ## Milestones
                1. The window for presentations fixed against the bid round dates in the process timetable.
                2. A working estimate of how many bidders will be invited, with a range for best and worst cases.
                3. Session length and format agreed in principle with the deal lead.
                4. Two clear weeks blocked in the diaries of everyone likely to present.

                ## Notes
                Leave a buffer day between the last first-round session and second-round bids: bidders always ask for one more call.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 10

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Bidders read the presenting team as a preview of who will run the business after closing, so who sits at the table matters as much as what is on the slides.                 3. A decision recorded on anyone senior who will not attend, with the explanation bidders will hear.
                4. The line-up agreed with the chief executive and the shareholder representative.

# Management Presentations

Management presentations are the hours in a sale process where bidders stop reading about a business and start judging the people who run it, and the banker's job is to make sure those hours go to plan. This area starts with foundations (scope and timetable, the presenting line-up, the storyline, the first deck, the question bank, the number tie-out and the disclosure boundaries), then the operating routines that run the presentation fortnight, the coaching and delivery skills, the format and sequencing decisions, the rehearsal, session and site visit events, variants for founders, sponsor-backed teams, carve-outs, family businesses and distressed sales, and specialist work such as competitor sessions and a group playbook.

What repeats is mostly weekly while sessions run: a Monday tracker update and diary check, mid-week rehearsals and follow-up chases, and Friday reviews of the question bank and debrief patterns, with a monthly refresh of the current trading pages once management accounts close. The **Meeting notes**, **Feedback themes** and **Operational checklist** templates pair with the debriefs, the bidder feedback calls and the day-before readiness check.
