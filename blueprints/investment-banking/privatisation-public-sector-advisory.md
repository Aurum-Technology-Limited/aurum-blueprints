              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Governments sell or open up state assets in perhaps seven distinct ways: a public offering, a trade sale to a strategic buyer, a staged sell-down, a concession, a lease, a public-private partnership or a management and employee buyout. Each carries different proceeds, timelines, political optics and legal steps, and a banker who can explain the trade-offs on one page is useful in the first ministerial meeting. Building that page from real public cases fixes the vocabulary before you need it.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page table of seven sale or participation methods, each with typical proceeds, timeline and approvals needed.
                2. Two public cases noted for each method, drawn from published government or development bank reports.
                3. A sentence on the usual failure pattern added to each row.
                4. The table checked by a colleague who has worked a government mandate.

                ## Notes
                Use published case reviews from development banks and audit offices. They are franker about what went wrong than press releases.

            - name: Conflict and independence checks on a state mandate
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A bank advising a government on selling an asset may also cover likely bidders, lend to them or hope to underwrite the buyer's financing. Public clients and auditors look hard at these overlaps, and an undeclared conflict found after award can void the appointment and reach the news. Running the conflict search properly, and agreeing ring-fences in writing, protects the mandate and the bank's name.

                ## Milestones
                1. A conflict search run through the bank's conflicts team covering the asset, the ministry and the probable bidder list.
                2. Every relationship with a likely bidder declared to the client in the form it asked for.
                3. Written ring-fence arrangements agreed with compliance where the bank keeps other roles.
                4. A decision recorded on whether the bank will offer buyer financing, with the client's view.

                ## Notes
                Your bank's compliance and legal teams decide what is a conflict and how it is handled. The project is about raising every overlap early, not judging it yourself.

# Privatisation and Public Sector Advisory

This area is for bankers who advise governments, privatisation agencies and state enterprises on selling shareholdings, opening assets to private partners and running public-private partnerships. It starts with foundations (sale methods, who decides inside government, adviser tenders, conflicts, transparency rules, the legal basis and the political calendar), moves to the routines that keep a public mandate documented and watched, then the skills governments pay for, such as value for money, fiscal impact and valuing enterprises with public service obligations, the decisions on method and process design, the dated events from cabinet approval to completion and audit review, variants for each seat and career move, and finally specialist work on portfolio reviews, asset recycling and integrity frameworks.

What repeats is a weekly status note and mandate record check on a live deal, weekly media and team reviews, monthly watches on pipelines, politics, parliament and information requests, quarterly fiscal and multilateral updates, and yearly refreshes of the rules, budgets and past sales you rely on. The Meeting notes, Risk register, Operational checklist, Onboarding 30/60/90, Development plan and Compliance control templates pair with the projects that name them. Client documents stay on the bank's systems, and legal, regulatory and procurement questions go to the client's counsel and your compliance team.
