            - name: Recognising inside information in deal work
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Inside information is usually defined along the lines of precise, not public and likely to move the price if it were known, and in banking almost every live mandate meets that test long before anyone says so. Bankers rarely get into trouble with the obvious signed deal; they get caught by a hint in a pitch meeting, a draft model left on a shared drive or a client's offhand comment about results. A short note that applies your firm's definition to real kinds of deal material makes the call faster when it matters.

                ## Milestones
                1. Your firm's definition of inside or material non-public information copied word for word.
                2. Ten kinds of deal material sorted into likely inside, possibly inside and public, with a reason for each.
                3. The grey cases taken to compliance and their answers recorded.
                4. A rule written for yourself: when unsure, treat it as inside and ask.

                ## Notes
                Regimes differ, for example the Market Abuse Regulation in the EU and UK and insider trading law in the US, so use your firm's definition rather than a textbook one.
              priority: high

# Wall Crossing and Compliance

This area is for bankers who handle inside information every week and want their own compliance record to be beyond question: analysts in their first months, associates and VPs supervising a deal team, and senior bankers with board seats and public profiles. It starts with foundations (your obligations, what counts as inside information, holdings and connected persons disclosed, approved channels and the information barrier map), moves to the routines that run all year, then the skills of sounding, cleansing and careful writing, the decisions about your own investments and public profile, dated events such as a surveillance query or an announcement day, variants for each stage of a banking career and finally specialist work on cross-border deals, expert calls and barrier breaches.

What repeats is a weekly look at the restricted list and a Friday desk sweep, monthly checks on pre-clearance approvals, the trade log, open wall crossings, gifts and off-channel messages, quarterly statement reconciliations and household account questions, and yearly attestations, outside interest reviews and a conversation with compliance. The Compliance control, Reading queue, Operational checklist, Remediation plan, Meeting notes and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them. Every project here is organisational: your firm's policies and its compliance team decide what is permitted, and legal questions go to compliance or your own lawyer.
