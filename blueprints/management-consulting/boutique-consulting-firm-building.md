            - name: Choosing the boutique's niche
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A boutique wins against large firms by being the obvious choice for one kind of client with one kind of problem, such as pricing for mid-sized distributors or regulatory change for regional insurers. Choosing the niche on paper, from your own track record and the buyers who already call you, gives every later decision on brand, hires and offers something to test against.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of your last ten engagements with sector, problem, fee and how the work arrived.
                2. Three candidate niches, each written as a client type plus a problem they pay to solve.
                3. Each candidate scored on your credibility, number of reachable buyers, fee level and repeat need.
                4. One niche chosen and written as a single sentence a client would recognise.

                ## Notes
                A niche that feels too narrow usually is not. Most boutiques need a few hundred reachable buyers, not thousands.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A one-sentence niche statement naming client type and problem, chosen from three scored candidates and recorded with the scores."

                - "Draft three candidate niches as client type plus problem"

                - "Test the leading niche sentence on three past clients by phone"

                ## Purpose
                Trading as a sole trader is fine for one person, but a firm with co-founders, staff and larger clients usually needs a company and a written agreement between the founders. # Boutique Consulting Firm Building

This area is for the founder turning a consulting practice into a firm with a name, a team and a reputation of its own, and for the small business adding an advisory arm. It starts with the foundations (niche, firm plan, entity, insurance, contracts, money), moves into the operating rhythm of pipeline, economics and delivery standards, then covers the skills a founder has to learn, the big decisions, the dated moments, the situations founders find themselves in and the specialist work of alliances, new practice lines and sale readiness.

The rhythm is weekly for pipeline, invoices and capacity, monthly for the firm P&L, cash, engagement health and founder time, and quarterly for client concentration, associate check-ins and a founders' offsite, with an annual plan to close the year.
