            - name: Written reason for leaving consulting
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Leaving because of one bad engagement or one brutal manager leads to a role chosen to escape rather than to build. Writing down what you want more of, what you want less of and the three things you will not trade away gives every later conversation, from headhunter calls to offer negotiation, a test to run against.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of what you want more of (ownership, depth, pay, time) and less of (travel, slides, hours), ranked.
                2. Three non-negotiables written as testable statements, for example no more than one night a week away.
                3. A one-paragraph answer to why now, separated from frustration with the current project.
                4. The statement read by one trusted person outside the firm who pushed back on at least one point.

                ## Notes
                Write it after a normal week, not the night a deck was torn apart. If the reason is mostly one person or one project, a staffing change may solve it more cheaply than an exit.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A one-page exit statement with ranked wants, three testable non-negotiables and a why-now paragraph exists and has been reviewed by one outside person."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "List five things you want more of and five you want less of"
                - "Rank both lists and circle the top three in each"
                - "Write three non-negotiables as statements someone else could check"
                - "Ask a friend outside the firm to challenge the draft over coffee"

# Consulting Exit Options

Most consultants leave within a few years, and the ones who land well treat the exit like an engagement: a clear question, a short list of hypotheses about where to go, evidence gathered from people already in those roles, and a decision made before the next staffing call forces one. This area starts with foundations (your reason for leaving, your runway, your contract terms, an inventory of what you have actually done, a map of the exit lanes and the timing call), then the routines that keep a search moving, the skills that industry strategy, private equity, corporate development and startup interviews test, decisions about offers, level and geography, the dated events from recruiting sprints to your last day, variants for analysts, managers, carers, visa holders and people counselled out, and finally the specialist lanes of portfolio operations, chief of staff work and joining a former client.

The rhythm is a Monday pipeline review and a Friday tracker update, Thursday alumni outreach, monthly market and performance checks on varied days, quarterly headhunter, reference and plan reviews, weekly practice blocks while you are learning modelling, SQL or a sector, and a yearly look at the long arc. The Resume, Job application, Person, Reading queue, Onboarding 30/60/90 and Development plan templates pair with the CV rewrite, the opportunity tracker, contact records, sector reading, your first months in the new role and the operator skills plan. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
