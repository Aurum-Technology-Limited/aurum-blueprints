              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every firm staffs differently: some run a central staffing team with an availability system, some let partners pull people directly, and most mix the two with informal favours. Until you know who actually decides, when requests are made and what information they look at, every attempt to influence your next project is guesswork.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page description of the formal staffing process, including the system or spreadsheet used and how far ahead requests are made.
                2. The names of the staffing coordinator for your level and office, and of the partners who staff most often in your practice.
                3. Three colleagues one or two years ahead asked how they got their last two projects, with the answers noted.
                4. A short list of the informal routes that work at your firm, such as being asked onto proposals or a manager requesting you.

                ## Notes
                The formal process and the real process are rarely the same. Write both down and do not judge colleagues who use the informal one; ask them how.

            - name: Travel and location limits statement

                Consultants who never state their limits get staffed as though they have none, then burn out or turn down work late and badly. # Consulting Staffing and Project Choice

Staffing decides most of what a consulting career feels like: which industries you learn, which partners know your name, how many nights you spend in hotels and what your reviewers can say about you. This area starts with foundations (how staffing works at your firm, a log of past projects, a skills gap map, written goals and a current profile), then the routines that keep you visible to the people who staff work, the skills of reading and pitching for a staffing request, decisions between offers and about staying or leaving a project, the dated moments such as roll-offs and the staffing preferences round, variants for new joiners, lateral hires, parents, part-time consultants and small offices, and finally the specialist moves of being requested by name and shaping your own role.

The rhythm is a weekly look at the sales pipeline, a monthly pulse with the staffing coordinator, monthly partner touchpoints and a roll-off check, with a quarterly balance review of travel and growth and a yearly portfolio review before the evaluation cycle. The Metrics log, Skills matrix, Person, 1:1 and Development plan templates pair with the staffing history log, the gap map, the partner network, the counsellor conversations and the plan for breaking out of a typecast role. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
