            - name: Sponsor profile and personal stakes
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every sponsor carries two agendas: the stated objective in the statement of work and a personal one, such as a promotion case, a budget fight or a predecessor's mess to clear. Writing a one-page profile of what this person is measured on, who they answer to and what a good outcome would do for them tells you which findings will land easily and which will need careful handling.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page profile covering role, tenure, reporting line, targets and known priorities.
                2. The personal stakes of the engagement for the sponsor written in two or three sentences.
                3. Three things the sponsor has said they care about, quoted from early meetings.
                4. Gaps in the profile marked, with a named person on the team who will fill each.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Person** template. Keep the profile in the internal team folder only and write nothing in it you would be embarrassed for the sponsor to read.
              priority: high

            - name: Expectations conversation in the first fortnight
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Sponsors rarely read the statement of work twice, and what they now expect may include a board paper, a named saving or a reorganisation the contract never mentioned. A forty-minute conversation in the first two weeks, asking what they expect to see, when and in what form, surfaces the gap while it is still cheap to close.

                ## Milestones
                1. A meeting of at least thirty minutes held with the sponsor alone in the first fortnight.
                2. The sponsor's expected outputs, dates and audiences written down in their own words.
                3. Any expectation outside the agreed scope flagged to your partner within a day.
                4. A short follow-up email restating what was agreed, acknowledged by the sponsor.

                ## Notes
                Ask "what would make you call this a failure in three months?" The answer is often more useful than the stated goal.

                cadence: one-shot

            - name: Agreed check-in rhythm with the sponsor
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Without a fixed slot, sponsor contact drifts to whenever something has gone wrong, which teaches them to associate your name with problems. Agreeing a standing weekly or fortnightly slot, its length and who attends, gives the relationship a steady pulse that does not depend on a crisis.

                ## Milestones
                1. A recurring slot of thirty minutes or less agreed and in both calendars.
                2. Attendees decided, usually the sponsor, your engagement lead and your partner when needed.
                3. A standing agenda of no more than four items shared with the sponsor.
                4. A rule agreed for what happens when the slot is cancelled, such as a written update instead.

                ## Notes
                Short and reliable beats long and occasional. A fifteen-minute call that always happens is worth more than an hour that is moved three times.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "A recurring sponsor check-in with a written four-item agenda is in both calendars for the length of the engagement."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "1"
              tasks:

# Managing Client Sponsors

The sponsor is the one client executive whose support decides whether your work gets used, and most engagements that fail do so in that relationship long before the analysis goes wrong. This area starts with foundations (knowing what the sponsor needs personally, an expectations conversation, an agreed rhythm and escalation path), then the weekly routines that keep the relationship honest, the skills of early bad news, private disagreement and short senior updates, decisions about resets and reluctant stakeholders, the dated moments such as the mid-point review, then situations like a new or departing sponsor, and finally specialist work on sponsors under pressure and support that has to last beyond the readout.

The rhythm is a weekly one-to-one and a Friday note to the sponsor, a weekly commitments check, monthly reviews of stakeholder sentiment and the bad news log, and a quarterly feedback conversation on long engagements. The Person, 1:1, Risk register and Feedback themes templates pair with the sponsor profile, the standing one-to-one, the early warning log and the end-of-engagement feedback interview.
