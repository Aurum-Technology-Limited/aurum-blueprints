            - name: Governing thought in one sentence for the current problem
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most weak summaries fail before the first word is written, because the writer has a pile of findings but no single answer. Forcing the current problem into one sentence of 25 words or fewer, which a senior reader could agree or disagree with, gives every later page a spine and exposes early whether you actually know what you think.

                ## Milestones
                1. One governing thought for your current problem written as a full sentence of 25 words or fewer.
                2. The sentence makes a claim a reader could dispute, not a description of the work done.
                3. Your manager or a peer has read it and said, in their own words, what it asks the reader to believe or do.
                4. The agreed sentence saved at the top of your working document and dated.

                ## Notes
                Test it with the question 'so what should the client do?' If the sentence cannot answer that, it is a topic heading, not a governing thought.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 7

                mode: building

# Synthesis and Executive Summaries

Synthesis is the moment analysis becomes an answer: one governing thought, a few supporting points that a busy reader can test, and recommendations someone can act on by Monday. This area is for consultants and other knowledge workers who have to write the page that senior people actually read, and it runs from foundations (the governing thought, the reader, the pyramid rules and a personal one-page template) through the daily and weekly synthesis habits, the core writing skills, decisions about grouping and cutting, the dated summaries on an engagement, versions for analysts, managers, in-house teams, solo consultants and second-language writers, and the advanced work of programme-level and contested summaries.

The rhythm is a three-line synthesis note at the end of each working day, a Friday answer-so-far email, a Thursday team synthesis session, a Monday thirty-second answer rehearsal, a Sunday drill on a published report, and monthly rewrites, scores and reviews, with quarterly rereads of your best-summaries collection and feedback themes. The Metrics log, Reading queue, Purchase decision and Feedback themes templates pair with the practice score, the collection of strong summaries, the choice of outlining tool and the record of reviewer comments.
