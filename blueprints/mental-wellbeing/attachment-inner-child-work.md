            - name: Recurring relationship pattern inventory
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most people come to attachment work because the same scene keeps playing out: panicking when a partner goes quiet, going cold when someone gets close, apologising for needing anything at all. Writing down five to eight of these repeating moments, with who was there and what you did next, gives the rest of the area a concrete target instead of a vague wish to be different.

                ## Milestones
                1. At least five relationship moments that have happened more than once written down.
                2. For each one, the trigger, the feeling and what you did next recorded in a line each.
                3. The two patterns that cost you most marked as priorities.
                4. A one-sentence description of what you would like to do instead, for each priority pattern.

                ## Notes
                Write in the first person and stick to what happened. Explaining why comes later, and doing it too early tends to turn the list into self-blame.

                Before you start: this plan is general information to help you look after your own wellbeing. It is not medical or psychological advice and does not replace a doctor, therapist or counsellor. If you are in crisis, in danger or thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building

# Attachment & Inner Child Work

This area is for anyone who keeps meeting the same feelings in close relationships, panic when someone goes quiet, the urge to leave when things get close, a voice that says you are too much, and wants to understand where they started and loosen their hold. It begins with foundations (a pattern inventory, an attachment baseline, a family timeline and genogram, a pacing plan and a decision about support), then the weekly and monthly machinery of check-ins, activation logs and repair, the core skills of attachment theory, parts and schema work, mentalising and coherent storytelling, followed by decisions about parents, partners and old roles, the occasions worth preparing for, plans for parents, carers, adoptees and people after a breakup, and finally the specialist work usually done alongside a therapist.

What repeats is a daily two-minute reparenting moment, a Sunday inner child check-in, a midweek scan of the activation log, a Friday repair conversation with a partner, a Saturday play hour, a monthly pattern review and parent contact note, a quarterly look at who you turn to, and an annual review with the attachment questionnaire retaken. The Reading queue, Metrics log and Habit tracker templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
