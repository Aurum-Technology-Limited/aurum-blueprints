            - name: First 90 days after a bipolar diagnosis
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A new bipolar diagnosis usually arrives at a hard moment, often straight after an episode, with a prescription, a follow-up date and a lot left unsaid. Treating the first three months as a set-up period, one appointment, one question list and one routine at a time, stops the important pieces falling through the gaps while you are still recovering.

                ## Milestones
                1. Your diagnosis, its type and the name of the clinician who made it written down in one note.
                2. A first follow-up appointment attended with a prepared question list.
                3. Every prescribed medicine, and the reason for it, recorded in the same note.
                4. One daily routine running for a month: a fixed wake time or a mood chart, not both at once.
                5. A short list of the projects in this area you will start next, and the ones archived for now.

                ## Notes
                Recovery from an episode often takes longer than the diagnosis conversation suggests. Pace this area to your energy, not to the length of the list.
              priority: high

              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: knowledge

                - "Archive the projects here that can wait until you feel steadier"

                ## Milestones

# Bipolar Disorder Stability Plan

This area is for anyone living with bipolar disorder who wants long stretches of stability, and for the partners and relatives who help protect them. It starts with the foundations (understanding your type, a two-way mood chart, personal warning signs and a relapse plan agreed with your team), then the daily and weekly routines around sleep, rhythm and medicines, the skills of telling a good week from a high, the decisions on money, medicines and advance planning, the events that shake routines, situations such as pregnancy, parenting and study, and finally the pattern work of an experienced self-manager.

What repeats is a daily mood and sleep entry with a fixed wake time, a weekly chart review and rhythm score, a monthly check-in with your trusted person, quarterly blood test and review preparation, and an annual care plan review. The Metrics log, Habit tracker, Meeting notes, Course and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
