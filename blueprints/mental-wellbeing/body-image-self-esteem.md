                ## Purpose
                Two short, freely available questionnaires, the Body Appreciation Scale and the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale, give you a number to start from in about ten minutes. Scoring them now means that in three months you can tell whether anything has actually shifted, instead of judging by how you feel on a bad mirror day.

                ## Milestones
                1. Both questionnaires found from a reputable source and completed on the same day.
                2. Both scores written in a metrics log with the date.
                3. The three items you scored lowest on noted as early targets.
                4. A date set to repeat the questionnaires in twelve weeks.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Metrics log** template. These scales describe how you feel; they do not diagnose anything, so treat a very low score as a reason to talk to your doctor, not as a verdict.

It is not medical or psychological advice and does not replace a doctor, therapist or counsellor. If you are in crisis, in danger or thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now.

            - name: Mirror exposure practice
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Mirror exposure, describing your whole body from head to toe in neutral, factual language, is one of the best-supported parts of cognitive behavioural therapy for body image. Practising it in short, structured sessions over four weeks teaches you to look at yourself without either avoiding the mirror or picking apart the parts you dislike.

                ## Milestones
                1. A full-length mirror and a private, well-lit time identified.
                2. A neutral description script written that covers every part of the body.
                3. Two sessions a week held for four weeks, with distress rated before and after.
                4. End-of-session distress lower in week four than in week one.

                ## Notes
                Describe, do not judge: say broad shoulders, not huge shoulders. If sessions become overwhelming or you start checking more, pause and talk to a therapist experienced in body image.
              priority: medium

# Body Image & Self-Esteem

This area is for anyone who spends more time than they would like worrying about how they look, and for students facing changing rooms, group chats and photos every day. It starts with foundations (baseline scores, a map of triggers, an honest inventory of checking and avoidance, and a red flags list), then the daily and weekly routines that keep the work going, skills such as mirror exposure and media literacy, decisions about cosmetic procedures, photos and where to exercise, the occasions that test body image, the life stages that change the body, and finally specialist work on body dysmorphic concerns, peer-led groups and fairer policies.

What repeats is a one-line daily note of what your body did and a daily qualities entry, a weekly check-in, comparison review and enjoyable movement session, a monthly feed tidy and household body talk check, a quarterly re-score and wardrobe changeover, and an annual review. The Metrics log, Habit tracker and Reading queue templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
