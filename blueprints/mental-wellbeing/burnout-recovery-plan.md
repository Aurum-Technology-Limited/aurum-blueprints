              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Exhaustion, growing cynicism about work and a sense that nothing you do is effective are the three signs most researchers use to describe burnout, and people usually notice only the first. Scoring yourself on all three, with examples from the last month, tells you how far things have gone and gives you something concrete to show a doctor or manager instead of saying you are just tired.

                ## Milestones
                1. A written score from 0 to 10 for exhaustion, detachment or cynicism, and reduced effectiveness.
                2. Two specific examples from the last month noted against each sign.
                3. A short list of physical symptoms such as headaches, frequent illness or broken sleep.
                4. A one-line summary of how long the signs have been building.

                ## Notes
                If you notice hopelessness, thoughts of not wanting to be here, or you cannot get through a normal day, contact your doctor or a crisis line now rather than working through this list.
              priority: high

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Burnout shares symptoms with depression, anaemia, thyroid problems, sleep apnoea and long infections, and some of those are quick to test for. An appointment booked with your self-check in hand rules out the physical causes, gets a professional view on whether this is burnout or something that needs other treatment, and opens the door to a fit note if you need time off.

                ## Milestones
                1. An appointment booked with your doctor and the reason given as exhaustion and burnout symptoms.
                2. Your self-check, symptom list and working hours brought to the appointment.
                3. Any blood tests or screening the doctor suggests booked and completed.
                4. The doctor's view and agreed next steps written down the same day.

                ## Notes
                Ask for a double appointment if your practice offers one. Ten minutes is rarely enough to cover symptoms, work and time off together.

# Burnout Recovery Plan

Burnout is what happens when demand runs ahead of recovery for long enough that rest stops working, and it tends to hit the people who care most about their work: knowledge workers, managers carrying a team and founders carrying a company. This area starts with the foundations (an honest self-check, a doctor's appointment, a baseline of energy and hours, and the first cuts to your load), then the weekly and monthly routines that protect recovery, the skills of pacing, saying no and planning below capacity, the bigger decisions about workload, role and whether to stay, the milestones worth marking, the versions for managers, founders and remote workers, and finally the plans that keep it from coming back.

What repeats is a daily floor routine and a hard stop to the working day, a Friday energy ledger and Monday workload triage, a midweek recovery activity, a monthly burnout score and support check-in, and quarterly audits of meetings, role and your relapse plan. The Metrics log, Habit tracker, 1:1, Reading queue, Household chores and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
