            - name: Choosing a thought record format you will keep
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Whichever thought record you actually open on a bad evening is the best one, and people abandon the practice more often because the format is awkward than because the method fails. Deciding between a paper notebook, a printed seven-column sheet, a notes app template or a dedicated CBT app, and setting it up once, removes friction before the first difficult moment arrives.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three candidate formats listed with where each would live day to day.

                ## Notes

            - name: Three-column records for the first fortnight
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Jumping straight to the full seven-column record is the most common reason beginners give up, because challenging a thought you have not learned to catch feels forced. Two weeks of three columns only (situation, emotion with a rating, and the thoughts that were there) builds the noticing muscle first and gives you raw material for every later skill.

                ## Milestones
                1. At least ten three-column records written over fourteen days.
                2. Each record has an emotion rating and at least one thought written as a sentence, not a feeling word.
                3. No challenging attempted yet, by design.
                4. A one-paragraph note at the end on which situations came up most.

                ## Notes
                If a thought column keeps filling with feeling words such as anxious or rubbish, ask yourself what that feeling was saying about you, others or the future, and write that sentence instead.

            - name: Evidence for and against without cherry-picking

# CBT Thought Record Practice

Thought records are the workhorse of cognitive behavioural therapy: a written way of slowing down the moment your mood dropped, catching what went through your mind, and testing it against the facts until a fairer view is possible. This area starts with the foundations (checking the method suits you, a format you will keep, the five-areas map, emotion ratings, the three-column start and your own list of thinking traps), then the routines that keep practice going, the individual skills from evidence gathering to decatastrophising, the decisions about books, apps and therapists, the dated milestones, versions for students, workers, parents, carers and older adults, and finally the specialist work on assumptions, rules for living and core beliefs.

What repeats is a short evening record, a Sunday review of the week's records, monthly tallies of thinking traps and belief shifts, a monthly reread of your best balanced thoughts and a quarterly check that the practice is still earning its time. The Habit tracker, Metrics log and Purchase decision templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
