                ## Purpose
                Frustration with a long-term illness is often aimed at the wrong thing because the losses have never been named: the job, the long walks, the spontaneity, the version of you who said yes to everything. Writing down what has changed, and beside it what is still yours, gives grief for the old life a specific shape and shows where adjustment is possible. It takes one evening and becomes the reference point for most of the projects that follow.

                ## Milestones

                2. A second list of what remains possible, even in a smaller or slower form.

                Expect this to stir things up. Do it on a reasonable-energy day rather than in the middle of a flare, and plan something gentle for afterwards.

If you are in crisis, in danger or thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now.

            - name: Raising the emotional impact with your doctor
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Appointments for a long-term condition tend to fill up with results and medicines, and the frustration, fear or flatness of living with it rarely gets airtime unless you bring it. Going in with two or three prepared sentences about how the illness is affecting your mood, sleep and daily life makes it far more likely you will be offered support, screening or a referral. Doctors expect this conversation: roughly a third of people with a long-term physical condition also struggle with their mental health.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three sentences written on how the illness is affecting mood, sleep and daily life.
                2. An appointment booked where coping is on the agenda from the start.
                3. The doctor's suggestions, screening results or referral options written down.
                4. One agreed next step recorded with a date to review it.

                ## Notes
                If low mood has lasted most days for two weeks or more, or you have thoughts of not wanting to be here, say so plainly. That changes what your doctor will offer, and urgent help is available the same day through your health service or emergency number.
              priority: high

              frontmatter:
                mode: building

                success_criteria: "The emotional impact of the illness has been raised at an appointment and one agreed next step is written down with a review date."

                ## Purpose
                On the worst days of a flare, thinking clearly about what helps is exactly what you cannot do, so the plan has to be made on a good day. A one-page plan with a permission statement, comfort items, easy food, who to text and what can be cancelled turns a bad day from a crisis into something you already know how to get through.

                2. A short permission statement written in your own words, such as rest is treatment today.
                3. A box or shelf with the physical comfort items ready to use.
                4. The plan used on at least one bad day and adjusted afterwards.

                ## Notes

# Chronic Illness Emotional Coping

Living with a long-term physical illness means coping with more than symptoms: the frustration of a body that will not cooperate, grief for the life you had planned, and the slow work of finding out who you are now. This area starts with the foundations (naming what has changed, raising it with your doctor, a bad-day plan, a support circle and a psychologist who knows your condition), then the routines that keep feelings and flares in view, the skills for frustration, uncertainty and being doubted, the decisions about hobbies, aids, treatment and relationships, the events worth preparing for, the versions for young adults, parents, older people and those living alone, and finally the specialist work of advance care conversations, mentoring and telling your own story.

What repeats is a short morning capacity check, a Sunday flare and feelings check-in, a midweek booking of planned rest, a monthly look at what you still managed and a catch-up with your support circle, a quarterly reflection, and the yearly anniversary and hard-season preparations. The Habit tracker, Metrics log, Meeting notes, Reading queue and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them.
