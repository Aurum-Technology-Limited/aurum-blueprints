            - name: Naming the transition you are in
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Big changes rarely arrive as one clean event: a move, a separation or a retirement usually sets off several smaller endings at once, and it is hard to cope with something you have not named. Writing down what is changing, when it started and which of three phases you seem to be in (an ending, an uncertain middle or a new beginning) gives you a shared language for the months ahead and stops every bad day looking like proof that something is wrong.

                ## Milestones
                1. One sentence naming the transition and the date it began or will begin.
                2. A list of the knock-on changes it has triggered, such as routine, income, home or role.
                3. A note of which phase you seem to be in now, with one reason.
                4. The page read to one person who is close to the change.

                ## Notes
                Wanted changes count. A promotion, a wedding or a long-planned move can unsettle you as much as an unwelcome one, and that is normal rather than ungrateful.

                Before you start: this plan is general information to help you look after your own wellbeing. It is not medical or psychological advice and does not replace a doctor, therapist or counsellor. If you are in crisis, in danger or thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now.

# Life Transition Adjustment

This area is for anyone adjusting to a big change in life, chosen or not: moving home or country, separating, retiring, becoming a parent or a carer, or watching the children leave. It starts with foundations for naming the change and steadying the days, then the weekly and monthly routines that carry an adjustment, the skills of living with endings and uncertainty, decisions about sequencing and testing changes, the dated moments from moving day to the first anniversary, versions for particular transitions, and finally the playbook and support work of someone who has been through several.

What repeats is kept small: three daily anchors, a Sunday check-in, two short journal entries and a standing call each week, a monthly scorecard and experiment, a quarterly identity review and a yearly reread of your playbook. The Habit tracker, Metrics log, Reading queue and Development plan templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
