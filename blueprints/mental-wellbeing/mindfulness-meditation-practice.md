              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Knowing why you want to meditate decides which kind of practice to choose and gives you something to read on the mornings you would rather not sit. People start for very different reasons, such as a scattered mind at work, reactivity at home, or curiosity after a course, and each points to a slightly different practice.                 4. The note saved where you will see it during the first month.

                ## Notes
                Keep the reason modest. Mindfulness tends to change how you relate to stress and distraction, slowly; it is not a quick fix for sleep, anxiety or a crisis.

            - name: Meditation corner and seat set-up
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A fixed place to sit removes one decision from every session and quietly reminds you to practise when you walk past it. It does not need to be a shrine: a chair with a firm seat, a cushion or kneeling bench, a blanket and somewhere to put the phone out of reach is enough. Trying two or three seated positions before buying anything stops you spending money on a cushion your knees cannot use.

                ## Milestones
                1. A spot chosen that is quiet enough at the time you plan to sit and free of clutter in your eyeline.
                2. Three positions tried for ten minutes each: a chair, a cushion and kneeling on a bench or folded blankets.
                3. One seat chosen that lets you sit upright for fifteen minutes without numbness or sharp pain.
                4. A blanket, a timer and a place for the phone kept at the spot.

                ## Notes
                A chair is a full and respectable meditation seat. Sit forward with feet flat, back away from the backrest, hands resting on the thighs.

            - name: Two-week attention and mood baseline
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Without a before picture, it is hard to tell months later whether practice has changed anything, and memory tends to flatter or dismiss the effect. Rating attention, mood and reactivity once a day for two weeks, and taking one published mindfulness questionnaire, gives you numbers to compare against at one, three and six months.

                ## Milestones
                1. Daily 0 to 10 ratings recorded for focus, mood and how easily you were rattled, for fourteen days.
                2. One published self-report mindfulness questionnaire, such as the Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire, completed and scored.
                3. Two or three concrete markers noted, such as how often you checked the phone during an hour of focused work.
                4. The baseline averages written at the top of the meditation log.

                ## Notes
                Rate at the same time each evening. The point is a rough trend, not precision, so do not agonise over a 6 or a 7.

                ## Purpose
                Five minutes a day is short enough to keep on the worst day of the fortnight and long enough to meet the basic experience of meditation: the mind wanders, you notice, you come back.             - name: Checking when meditation needs extra support
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Meditation is safe for most people, but a minority meet unsettling experiences such as old memories surfacing, panic, dissociation or very low mood, and the risk is higher with a history of trauma, psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe depression. Checking your own history honestly, and talking to your clinician or therapist first where it applies, lets you start with the right style, length and support rather than finding out on a retreat.

                ## Milestones
                1. A short private note on any history that might make intensive practice harder.
                2. Your clinician or therapist asked about starting a practice, where that history applies.
                3. A plan written for what to do if a sit becomes distressing: open the eyes, move, shorten or stop.
                4. One person or service named to contact if difficult experiences continue between sits.

                ## Notes
                Distress that continues after practice is a reason to pause and get support, not to push through. Trauma-sensitive teachers adapt practice with open eyes, shorter sits and choice of anchor.

# Mindfulness Meditation Practice

Mindfulness meditation is simple to describe and hard to keep doing: sit, pay attention to something, notice when the mind wanders, come back, repeat. This area starts with the foundations (a reason, a seat, a time of day, a baseline and the first ten days), then the daily, weekly and monthly routines that keep a practice going, the specific skills of noting, open awareness, walking and loving-kindness, decisions about styles, teachers and guided audio, retreats from a half day at home to a residential weekend, versions for parents, shift workers, students, desk workers and older adults, and finally the long sits, long retreats and teaching that experienced practitioners work towards.

What repeats is a short daily sit and one moment of informal practice, a longer sit on Saturdays, a Sunday review, a monthly check of attention and mood against your baseline, a quarterly reset and a yearly practice plan. The Purchase decision, Metrics log, Habit tracker, Course, Reading queue and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
