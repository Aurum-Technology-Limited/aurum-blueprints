                ## Purpose
                Before searching, it helps to know whether you want people who share a diagnosis, people who share a situation such as caring or job loss, or simply company on bad weeks.                 A peer group is people with lived experience helping each other, not treatment, and the best groups say so plainly. # Mental Health Peer Support Groups

This area is for anyone who wants the company of people who have been there: finding a mental health peer group, in a room or online, and getting real value from it. It starts with the foundations (knowing what you want, vetting a group and getting through the first meeting), moves through the routines and listening skills that make a group work, then the choices about format and structure, the occasions worth marking, variants for students, carers, parents, older people, shift workers and people leaving hospital, and finally facilitating, moderating and starting groups yourself.

What repeats is the weekly meeting and a weekly peer check-in, a monthly look at whether the group still helps, quarterly checks on your contacts and on the balance of giving and receiving, and a yearly refresh of your shortlist and crisis numbers. The Habit tracker, Metrics log, Course, Operational checklist, Feedback themes and Development plan templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
