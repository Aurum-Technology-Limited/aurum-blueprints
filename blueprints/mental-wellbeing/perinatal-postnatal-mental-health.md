                Anyone with a history of depression, anxiety, an eating disorder, bipolar disorder or a previous postnatal illness is more likely to become unwell in pregnancy or after birth, and maternity teams can only plan for what they are told. # Perinatal & Postnatal Mental Health

Pregnancy and the first year after birth are when many people meet anxiety or depression for the first time, and when existing conditions are most likely to return, for birthing parents and partners alike. This area begins with the foundations to put in place during pregnancy (telling your midwife, a warning signs card, a support team, medicine and birth plans, a rota), then the weekly routines for mood, sleep and connection, the knowledge that helps you recognise what is happening, the choices about referrals, therapy, feeding and help, the key dates from the last month of pregnancy to the first birthday, versions for partners, solo, adoptive and neonatal parents, and finally specialist planning after severe illness.

What repeats is kept small: a daily recovery minimum and medicine routine, weekly mood scores, sleep shifts and a partners' check-in, and a monthly review through the baby's first year. The Metrics log, Sleep review, Habit tracker, Household chores and Meeting notes templates pair with the projects that keep a mood log, a night plan, daily basics, household jobs and appointment notes.
