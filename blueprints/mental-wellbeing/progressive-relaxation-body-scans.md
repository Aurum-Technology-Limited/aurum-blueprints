              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Tensing muscle groups is safe for most people, but a recent injury or operation, a hernia, uncontrolled high blood pressure, pregnancy or a history of cramps can change how hard to tense, or whether to tense a group at all. Ten minutes of checking before the first session tells you which groups to skip or squeeze gently, so the practice relaxes you rather than straining something.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of current injuries, recent operations and conditions that affect muscles, joints or blood pressure.
                2. Each of the sixteen muscle groups marked as fine, gentle only or skip.
                3. Your doctor or physiotherapist asked about any group you are unsure of.
                4. Your adaptations written at the top of your practice log.

                ## Notes
                The usual instruction is to tense to about half your maximum effort, not as hard as you can. If a group cramps, let it go at once and use relaxation by recall for that group instead.

                Before you start: this plan is general information to help you look after your own wellbeing. It is not medical or psychological advice and does not replace a doctor, therapist or counsellor. If you are in crisis, in danger or thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now.

            - name: First fortnight of sixteen-group progressive relaxation
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The full method works through sixteen muscle groups, from the dominant hand to the feet, tensing each for about seven seconds and releasing for thirty. It takes around twenty-five minutes and feels slow, but two weeks of the long version is what teaches you to feel the difference between tense and relaxed in each group, which every shorter version later depends on.

                ## Milestones

                3. Before and after ratings logged for every session.
                4. Any group that will not let go noted for extra attention.

                ## Notes
                Tense to about half effort and release all at once, not gradually. Notice the release for the full thirty seconds: that noticing is the skill being trained.

# Progressive Relaxation & Body Scans

This area is for anyone whose stress shows up in the body: a clenched jaw, shoulders up by the ears, tension headaches or a back that never quite lets go. It starts with the foundations (a health check, a week-long tension baseline, a practice spot and a fixed daily slot), then the routines that keep practice going, the classic ladder of skills from sixteen-group progressive relaxation down to release in thirty seconds, decisions about dentists, headaches, aids and teachers, occasions worth preparing for, adaptations for children, pregnancy, older adults, chronic pain, trauma and carers, and finally the methods an experienced practitioner works on.

What repeats is a daily session with quick tension checks through the day, a Saturday long body scan, a Wednesday imagery session, a Sunday look at the log, a monthly re-rating of your tension map, a quarterly refresh of your recordings and an annual review.
