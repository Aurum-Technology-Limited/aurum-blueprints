            - name: First 72 hours after a major setback
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Straight after a redundancy call, a bad diagnosis or a failed bid, the mind races toward worst cases while sleep, food and judgement all take a hit. Setting a short holding pattern for three days, with basic care, one trusted person told and no irreversible decisions, gives the shock time to settle before you act on it.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three days passed with meals, water and some sleep, even if broken.
                2. One trusted person told what happened within the first day.
                3. A written list of decisions deliberately parked until day four.
                4. Nothing irreversible signed, sent or resigned in the first 72 hours.

                ## Notes
                Angry emails, resignation letters and social media posts written on the first night are the classic regret. Draft them if it helps, but keep them unsent until day four.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 7
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "No irreversible decision was made in the first 72 hours, and a written parked-decisions list exists with a date to revisit it."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Text one trusted person a two-line summary of what happened"
                - "Write a list of decisions you are parking until day four"
                - "Eat one proper meal and drink some water before the evening"
                - "Save any angry drafts to a folder instead of sending them"

# Resilience After Life Setbacks

This area is for anyone knocked off course by a setback such as redundancy, a failed exam, a business closing, a financial loss or a diagnosis that puts plans on hold, and who wants to find their feet again and be better prepared for the next one. It starts with the foundations for the first weeks (a 72-hour holding pattern, a plain account, an impact map, deadlines and who to tell), then the routines that carry a recovery, the skills that research links with bouncing back, the decisions about appealing, retrying or changing route, dated moments like a first interview or an anniversary, versions for particular setbacks and family situations, and finally the playbook and mentoring work of someone who has come through.

What repeats is kept light: a daily anchor and an evening wins note, a Sunday check-in, a weekly call with your support circle and a sort of what you can influence, a monthly plan review and budget check, quarterly buffer checks and a yearly refresh of your playbook. The Habit tracker, Metrics log, Reading queue, Monthly budget and bills, Job application and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
