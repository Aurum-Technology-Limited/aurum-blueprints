                ## Purpose
                Most people overestimate how much rest they get, because evenings spent half-working or scrolling feel like time off while they are happening. Going back through the last four weeks of calendar, messages and memory shows how many hours were genuinely free, what filled them and how you felt afterwards. That picture is the starting point for every plan in this area.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every evening, weekend day and day off from the last four weeks listed with what actually happened in it.

                3. A count of genuinely restful hours per week, written as a single number.
                4. The two biggest thieves of free time named.

                ## Notes

# Restorative Rest Planning

This area is for anyone whose time off passes without leaving them rested, and for knowledge workers whose evenings and holidays keep leaking back into work. It starts with foundations (a rest audit, the seven kinds of rest, a tested list of what restores you, a rest floor and a household agreement), then the weekly and monthly routines that keep rest happening, the skills of detaching, idling and saying no, the decisions about weekends, holidays and commitments, the breaks worth planning as events, versions for parents, carers, shift workers, remote workers, founders and retirees, and finally a full personal rest rhythm.

What repeats is a Friday rest planning slot, two protected evenings, daily micro-breaks, weekly time outdoors and an unproductive hobby hour, a monthly quiet weekend and rest review, quarterly restoration days and leave checks, and a yearly rest calendar. The Metrics log, Household chores, Habit tracker, Reading queue and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them.
