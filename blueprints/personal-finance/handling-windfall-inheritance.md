            - name: Ninety-day pause before big money decisions
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most regret around inherited or windfall money comes from decisions made in the first few weeks, when grief, excitement or other people's suggestions are loudest. Writing a pause rule now, with the few things that are allowed to happen straight away, buys time to think while the money sits somewhere safe. Ninety days is a common default; shorten it for a small sum and lengthen it for a life-changing one.

                ## Milestones
                1. A written pause rule naming the end date and the per-purchase spending limit that applies until then.
                2. A short list of actions allowed during the pause, such as clearing a payday loan or paying the funeral bill.
                3. The rule shared with a partner or one trusted person who will hold you to it.
                4. The end date in the calendar, with a planning session booked for that week.

                ## Notes
                The pause covers commitments, not safety steps. Moving the money to a protected account and securing your bank logins should still happen in the first week.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 7
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A dated pause rule with a spending limit and an allowed-actions list exists, and one other person has seen it."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "1"
              tasks:
                - "Write the pause end date and the per-purchase spending limit on one page"
                - "List the two or three things allowed to happen before the pause ends"
                - "Tell your partner or a trusted friend the rule and ask them to check in"
                - "Book a two-hour planning session in the week the pause ends"

            - name: Protected holding account for the windfall
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Leaving a large sum in a current account invites spending and may leave part of it outside the deposit protection scheme. A separate easy-access account, at a bank you already know, keeps the money visible, earning something and away from the debit card while decisions are made. Deposit guarantees usually apply per banking licence rather than per brand, so sums above the limit may need splitting.

                ## Milestones
                1. The deposit protection limit for your country, and any temporary high balance cover after an inheritance, written down.
                2. One or two easy-access accounts chosen, each under a separate banking licence if the sum needs splitting.
                3. The windfall moved, or ready to be paid straight into the holding account.
                4. The holding account nicknamed so it is obviously not for spending.

                ## Notes
                Some schemes give extra temporary protection for high balances from an inheritance or house sale for a few months, so check your scheme's rules. Avoid fixed-term products until the allocation plan is written.

This area is for anyone who has received, or is about to receive, a lump sum they did not earn month by month: an inheritance, a large bonus, an insurance or compensation payout, or a family gift.
