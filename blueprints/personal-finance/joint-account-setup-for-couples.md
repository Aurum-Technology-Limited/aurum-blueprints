            - name: Joint, separate or hybrid accounts decision
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                There are three broad models: pool everything into joint accounts, keep everything separate and split bills, or run a hybrid with a joint account for shared costs and personal accounts for the rest. Choosing deliberately, against criteria you both care about, beats drifting into whatever the first shared bill forced on you.

                ## Milestones
                1. The three models written out with how each would handle rent, groceries, savings and personal spending.
                2. Four or five criteria agreed, such as fairness, simplicity, privacy and protection if one of you loses income.
                3. Each model scored by both partners separately, then compared.
                4. One model chosen and the reason recorded in a sentence.
                5. A date set to review the choice after six months.

                ## Notes
                The hybrid model suits most couples starting out. It keeps a shared pot for shared life while protecting each person's independence and privacy over small purchases.

# Joint Account Setup for Couples

This area is for any couple deciding how to share money, whether you have just moved in together, married after years of separate finances or are untangling a set-up that never quite felt fair. It starts with the foundations (the honest money conversation, the full picture, the choice between joint, separate and hybrid accounts, the contribution rule and the written agreement), then the routines that keep a shared account healthy, the things worth understanding about joint liability and credit, the decisions that refine the arrangement, the moments that test it, the situations that change its shape and finally the specialist protections every couple should have in place.

What repeats is a monthly money date, a payday check that both contributions landed, a monthly settle-up of costs paid personally, a weekly five-minute spending check-in, a quarterly fairness conversation and a yearly reset of contributions, agreement, cards and credit files. The Purchase decision, Monthly budget and bills, Meeting notes and Savings goal templates pair with the projects that name them.
