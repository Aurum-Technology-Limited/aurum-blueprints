            - name: Written essentials and exceptions rulebook
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every no-spend month runs into the same questions on day three: is a birthday card allowed, does petrol count, what about a replacement phone charger? Writing one page that lists what is essential, what is banned and the few named exceptions means each decision takes seconds and nobody argues about it later.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of allowed essentials: rent or mortgage, bills, debt payments, groceries from a list, fuel or fares for work, medicines.
                2. A list of banned categories drawn from your baseline.
                3. No more than three named exceptions, each with a cap.
                4. A rule for genuine emergencies, such as a broken boiler, that names who decides and how.
                5. The rulebook printed or pinned where everyone in the household can see it.

                ## Notes
                Health, safety and work costs always stay on the essentials list. A no-spend month that makes you skip a prescription or drive on a bald tyre has gone wrong.

# No-Spend Months

This area is for anyone who wants to stop all non-essential spending for a weekend, a week or a whole month, and come out the other side with money set aside and a clearer sense of what they actually value. It starts with the foundations (length and timing, a spending baseline, a written rulebook, grey areas settled early, a named pot for the savings and a scan of costs already committed), then the routines that carry you through the month, the skills of cooking, mending and socialising without a card, the decisions about what to keep afterwards, the dated events that test the rules, versions for families, couples, housemates, students, freelancers and retirees, and finally the work of someone who has run several months and now helps others.

What repeats is a daily cross on the calendar, a Sunday check-in, a Saturday essentials shop, a Friday meal plan, a mid-month rules review, a month-end transfer of the money saved, a quarterly no-spend weekend and a yearly look at which months to run. The Savings goal, Habit tracker, Weekly meal plan, Purchase decision, Reading queue and Metrics log templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
