            - name: Sold-listing price research method
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Asking prices tell you what hopeful sellers want, not what anything is worth, which is why so many listings sit unsold for months. Learning to filter for completed sales and average three recent comparables gives you a defensible price for selling and a ceiling for buying, in about five minutes per item.

                ## Milestones
                1. The completed or sold filter found on each platform you use.
                2. A simple rule written for comparables: same model, similar condition, sold in the last three months.
                3. Five items from the sell list priced using three sold comparables each.
                4. A note made of which items had too few sales to price this way.

                ## Notes
                Strip out postage when comparing. A low price with high postage and a high price with free postage are often the same sale.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30

# Secondhand Buying & Selling

This area is for anyone who wants to spend less by buying used and turn the things they no longer need back into money, without wasting weekends on no-shows or losing a parcel to a scam. It starts with the foundations (an inventory of what to sell and what to buy, the right marketplaces, a pot for the proceeds, safety rules and the price research habit), then the weekly and monthly routines that keep listings moving, the skills of inspecting, listing, negotiating and spotting fakes and scams, the decisions about when used beats new, dated events such as a car boot stall or a pre-move sell-off, versions for parents, students, people downsizing and families clearing a home, and finally the specialist work of small-scale reselling, auctions, tax records and running a local swap group.

What repeats is a Saturday listing session and a Wednesday reprice, saved-search checks on Sundays, parcel runs twice a week, a monthly clear of the sell box, a month-end ledger of sales, buys and fees, a quarterly seasonal rotation and a yearly review of what the whole habit saved.
