            - name: Case for admitting a new partner
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Partners are usually added for one of four reasons: more capacity than the current owners can carry, a skill or specialty the practice lacks, a succession plan, or keeping a star associate from leaving. Writing down which reason applies, and what the practice expects to look like three years after the admission, stops the existing partners agreeing to the idea while each imagining a different person and a different deal.

                ## Milestones
                1. The main reason for admitting a partner written in one paragraph, with any secondary reasons ranked below it.
                2. The expected effect on revenue, workload and profit per partner sketched for the next three years.
                3. Alternatives considered and noted: a senior employee, a salaried professional, a locum or no change.
                4. Every current partner has read the note and recorded agreement or objections.

                ## Notes
                If the honest reason is that one partner wants to work less, say so. It changes who you look for and how the profit share should move.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:

                output_kind: decision

# Admitting New Partners

This area is for practice owners bringing someone new into ownership, whether a sole practitioner taking a first partner, a three-partner clinic adding a fourth, or a firm hiring a lateral partner with a client following. It starts with foundations (the reason for admitting anyone, what your agreement and regulator already require, written criteria and a candidate pack), then the recurring machinery of a candidate pipeline, capital accounts and buy-in repayments, the knowledge behind buy-in pricing and liability, the decisions on amount, structure and tier, the dated events from due diligence to the vote and signing day, situations such as family members, internal candidates and healthcare practices, and finally specialist work on group admissions, team moves and failed trials.

What repeats is a quarterly look at the candidate pipeline, a monthly capital account and loan repayment check, monthly trial and integration check-ins, and a yearly refresh of the buy-in formula and admission policy. The Vendor, Board meeting, 1:1, Feedback themes, Risk register and Metrics log templates pair with the projects that keep those records.
