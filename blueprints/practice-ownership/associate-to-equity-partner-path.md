            - name: Reasons for offering a route to ownership
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Owners who open a partner track without agreeing why tend to make it either impossible to reach or too easy to grant. Writing down what the practice needs from new owners, whether that is succession, retention, more client capacity or capital, decides almost every later design choice. It also gives you a straight answer when an associate asks why the route exists.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page statement of the two or three reasons the practice is offering ownership, agreed by every current owner.
                2. The time horizon recorded, such as a first new partner within five years.
                3. The things the route is not for, such as rewarding long service alone, written down.
                4. The statement filed where it will be read at each annual readiness review.

                ## Notes
                If the honest reason is that a key associate has threatened to leave, say so in the owners' discussion. A route built around one person rarely survives the next candidate.
              priority: high

            - name: Career stage ladder from associate to equity partner
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Associates cannot aim at a route they cannot see. A ladder that names each stage, such as associate, senior associate, salaried partner and equity partner, with what changes at each step in responsibility, pay basis, voting and risk, turns a vague hope into a plan. It is the document every later conversation refers to.

                ## Milestones
                1. Four or five named stages written in order, with a short definition of each.
                2. For each stage, the pay basis, decision rights and any capital or liability stated.
                3. The usual time spent at each stage given as a range, not a promise.
                4. The ladder approved by the owners and ready to share with staff.

                ## Notes
                Keep titles honest. Calling someone a partner when they have no vote and no share of profit can mislead clients and, in some professions, the regulator.
              priority: high

# Associate to Equity Partner Path

This area is for practice owners who want their best associates to stay and one day own part of the firm, and for the associate or manager who wants to know what that route really looks like. It starts with foundations (why you are offering ownership at all, the ladder of stages, written criteria, the regulator's ownership rules and the owners' appetite for dilution), then the operating rhythm of career conversations, development plans and readiness reviews, the knowledge a future partner needs about accounts, buy-ins and liability, the design decisions on the salaried step, funding and phased equity, the dated moments from launching the route to a first promotion, the situations of part-time, clinical and two-owner practices, and specialist work on multi-site equity and long-range modelling.

What repeats is a monthly development check-in and promise log, quarterly career conversations, stay interviews and pipeline updates, a standing item at the partners' meeting, and a yearly readiness review and feedback round.
