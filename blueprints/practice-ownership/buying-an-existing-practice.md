            - name: Acquisition criteria for the practice you want
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Buyers without written criteria end up chasing whatever a broker sends next and lose months on practices they were never going to buy. One page that fixes the location radius, size range, type of work, price ceiling and deal breakers lets you say no in ten minutes and yes with conviction.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page criteria sheet covering location, revenue range, number of professionals, mix of work and maximum price.
                2. Five deal breakers written down, such as a short lease, a single dominant referrer or no willing seller handover.
                3. The criteria tested against two practices currently advertised to see whether they are realistic.
                4. The sheet shared with your partner or co-buyer and your accountant for comment.

                ## Notes
                Write the criteria before you talk to a single broker. It is far harder to set a price ceiling once you have fallen for a particular building.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21

# Buying an Existing Practice

This area is for the professional who would rather buy a running practice than build one from nothing: an associate eyeing the boss's retirement, a clinician or lawyer ready to leave employment, or an owner adding a second site by acquisition. It starts with foundations (your criteria, your own finances, advisers, the regulator's ownership rules and a lender's appetite), moves through the search and due diligence machinery, the skills of reading a practice's accounts and a sale agreement, the decisions on what to offer and when to walk away, the dated events from letter of intent to completion day and the first 100 days, the situations different buyers face, and finally specialist work on earn-outs, warranty claims and turnarounds.

What repeats is a weekly search and diligence rhythm while you are hunting, weekly handover meetings with the seller, and then monthly cash, staff, retention and loan reviews through the first year of ownership, with quarterly checks on deferred payments and broker contacts. The Purchase decision, Risk register, Meeting notes, Metrics log, Onboarding 30/60/90 and 1:1 templates pair with the projects that keep those records.
