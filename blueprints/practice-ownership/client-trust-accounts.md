              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Client money arrives in more ways than most owners realise: deposits on account of costs, completion funds, settlement cheques, tax refunds, card payments taken at reception and money paid to a fee earner personally.                 4. The map saved where the cashier, partners and compliance lead can find it.

# Client Trust Accounts

This area is for the owner of a law, accounting or other professional firm that holds money belonging to clients, and who carries the personal responsibility when that money goes missing or sits in the wrong place. It starts with foundations (knowing what counts as client money, the rules that apply, a properly designated bank account, a ledger per client and named people with authority), moves to the operating routines that keep the account clean, the skills fee earners and cashiers need, improvements to banking and software, the dated events of the annual report and inspection, situations such as sole practice, conveyancing, estates and closing a firm, and finally specialist work on settlements, currency and spot audits.

What repeats is a daily posting routine, a weekly check of unidentified receipts and pending costs transfers, a monthly three-way reconciliation with an ageing report and breach review, quarterly client statements and ledger samples, and yearly mandate, interest and training reviews. The Operational checklist, Month-end close, Risk register, Purchase decision and Compliance control templates pair with the projects that keep those records.
