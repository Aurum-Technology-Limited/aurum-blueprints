                ## Purpose
                Most practices hold more controlled medicines than the owner can name from memory: a strong opioid in the cabinet, a sedative in the surgery, a benzodiazepine on the shelf and a stimulant ordered for one patient.                 ## Milestones

                2. Each line marked with its legal schedule or class under your country's controlled drugs law.
                3. Each line marked for register required, safe custody required and destruction witness required.
                4. The list dated and saved where the accountable person and every key holder can find it.

                ## Notes
                Schedules differ between countries and are revised from time to time, so check the current list on the regulator's or health ministry's website rather than an old textbook.

            - name: Liquid controlled drug volume reconciliation
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Oral liquids rarely balance to the millilitre because of measuring losses, overfill from the manufacturer and residue left in the bottle. Recording how volumes are checked, agreeing what small difference the accountable person will accept and reconciling each bottle when it is finished stops honest losses from hiding a real one.

                2. A written tolerance for measuring loss agreed by the accountable person.
                3. Each finished bottle reconciled against the register and the result recorded.
                4. Repeated differences beyond the tolerance investigated and logged.

                ## Notes

# Controlled Drugs and Pharmacy Registers

This area is for the owner of a pharmacy, medical or dental clinic or veterinary practice who holds controlled medicines and carries the legal responsibility when a register does not balance or a bottle goes missing. It starts with foundations (knowing which drugs you hold and under which schedule, the rules that apply, a named accountable person, a compliant cabinet and keys, and a clean opening register), then the operating routines that keep every entry and balance right, the skills staff need, improvements to storage and systems, the dated events of destruction, inspection and handover, the situations that differ for vets, clinics, dental sedation and single-handed pharmacists, and finally specialist work on instalment dispensing and diversion analysis.

What repeats is a daily check that yesterday's entries are complete, a weekly running balance count, a weekly look at patient returns and vehicle or bag stock, monthly full stock checks, liquid reconciliations, expiry and discrepancy reviews, quarterly authority and usage reviews, and yearly procedure, training and self-assessment work. The Operational checklist, Purchase decision, Risk register, Compliance control, Training program and Remediation plan templates pair with the projects that keep those records.
