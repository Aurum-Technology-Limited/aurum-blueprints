            - name: Professional capacity gap for the next two years
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most practices hire a professional only once the diary is full and patients or clients are already waiting, which forces a rushed hire. Measuring how many hours of professional work are booked, turned away and likely to arrive over the next two years tells you whether you need a full-time associate, a part-time one, or a different fix such as delegating work to support staff.

                ## Milestones
                1. Booked professional hours per week for the last twelve months pulled from the diary or time records.
                2. Work turned away, waiting times or overflow to other firms estimated for the same period.
                3. A two-year demand estimate written down, including known retirements, leave and new services.
                4. The gap expressed in hours per week and in full-time equivalents, with the assumptions listed.

                ## Notes
                Count the owner's own hours honestly. A gap that exists only because the owner wants to work fewer days is still a gap, but it changes the role you design.
              priority: high

            - name: Role profile for a qualified associate
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Vague adverts such as "associate wanted, competitive package" attract everyone and no one. A written role profile, covering the caseload or work mix, the hours, who supervises, which systems they will use and what good looks like after a year, lets candidates rule themselves in or out and gives the interview panel something to score against.

                ## Milestones
                1. The work mix written as percentages, such as routine, complex, new clients and admin.
                2. Required registration, years of experience and specific competencies separated from the nice-to-haves.
                3. Days, hours, on-call or out-of-hours duties and supervision lines stated.
                4. Three outcomes for the end of year one written as measurable results.
                5. The profile reviewed by whoever will work most closely with the new hire.

                ## Notes
                Keep the essential list short. Every extra must-have shrinks the field, often without improving the hire.

# Hiring Associates and Professionals

This area is for practice owners and the managers who hire for them, bringing qualified dentists, doctors, solicitors, accountants, engineers, architects, vets, pharmacists and therapists into a practice as employed or contracted associates. It starts with foundations (the capacity gap, the engagement model, a role profile, pay bands, regulator conditions, a scored interview process, the contract and credential checks), then the recurring machinery of a candidate pipeline, pay and retention reviews and new-hire file audits, the skills of interviewing, referencing and incentive design, decisions on employment status, agencies and sign-on terms, the dated events from campaign to probation review, situations such as a first hire, a graduate, a lateral hire or a recruit from overseas, and finally specialist work on team hires, bad hires and hiring metrics.

What repeats is a monthly pipeline review and candidate contact, weekly supervision for new associates in their first months, quarterly retention conversations and file audits, and a yearly pay benchmark, contract refresh and pay round. The Vendor, Onboarding plan, Onboarding 30/60/90, 1:1, Feedback themes, Skills matrix and Metrics log templates pair with the projects that keep those records.
