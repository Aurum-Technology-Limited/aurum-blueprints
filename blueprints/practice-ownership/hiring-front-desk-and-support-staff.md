            - name: Map of front desk and support roles
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before hiring anyone, list every non-professional task the practice depends on: answering calls, booking, check-in, taking payments, chasing balances, scanning post, ordering stock and covering the desk at lunch. Most practices find two or three tasks that belong to nobody and one person quietly doing the work of a role and a half, and the map shows which hire would actually fix the gap.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every support task listed with how often it happens and roughly how long it takes.
                2. Each task assigned to a current person or marked as unowned.
                3. Weekly hours of support work compared with the hours actually contracted.
                4. The roles the practice needs, such as reception, billing clerk or office administrator, named with hours per week.

                ## Notes
                Shadow the desk for a full Monday morning before writing the map. The busiest session shows the real workload; a quiet Thursday hides it.
              priority: high

            - name: Pay bands for reception, billing and office roles
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Support staff in practices often leave for a supermarket or a call centre paying slightly more, and owners usually find out at the exit interview. Setting a written band for each support role, based on local adverts and what similar practices pay, makes offers quicker and fairer and stops two people doing the same job on very different rates.

                ## Milestones
                1. At least ten current local adverts for comparable roles collected with their pay.
                2. A band with a starting point and a top point agreed for each support role.
                3. Current staff placed on the bands, with any anomalies listed.
                4. A plan, with dates, for closing any gap where a current team member sits below the band.

                ## Notes
                Check the statutory minimum wage and any sector pay rules in your country before setting the bottom of a band. An employment adviser or accountant can confirm the rules on overtime and holiday pay.

# Hiring Front Desk and Support Staff

This area is for the owner or manager of a clinic, dental, veterinary, therapy, law, accounting or design practice who has to hire, train and keep the people who answer the phone, book the diary, take payments and run the office. It starts with foundations (a map of the support roles, job descriptions, pay bands, contracts, checks and a written service standard), then the operating routines of rotas, cover, checklists and one-to-ones, the skills of phone scripts, difficult callers, confidentiality and urgent call escalation, the decisions about where to advertise, agencies and desk design, the dated events of inductions, assessment days and pay reviews, the situations of a first hire, a new manager or a sudden resignation, and finally specialist work on career ladders, performance processes and remote admin staff.

What repeats is a weekly rota and Monday huddle, daily opening and closing checks, monthly one-to-ones, test calls, cover checks and phone metrics, a quarterly script review, skills matrix update and turnover review, and a yearly pay review, peak season plan and staff survey. The Operational checklist, 1:1, Skills matrix, Metrics log, Onboarding plan and Onboarding 30/60/90 templates pair with the projects that keep those records.
