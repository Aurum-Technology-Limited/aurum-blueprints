            - name: Twelve-month locum cover map
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most practices book locums in a panic the week someone falls ill, then pay the highest rates for whoever is free. Mapping every known absence for the next twelve months (annual leave, courses, parental leave, the owner's own time off and seasonal peaks) shows how many sessions or days of cover you actually need and which ones can be booked months ahead at normal rates.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every professional's booked and expected leave for the next twelve months listed in one calendar.
                2. Seasonal peaks such as winter illness, school holidays or tax deadlines marked against the same calendar.
                3. Total cover needed expressed in sessions or days per month, with the busiest months highlighted.
                4. Cover already arranged separated from cover still to book.

                ## Notes
                Add a margin for unplanned absence. Looking back at the last two years of sickness and short-notice gaps gives a realistic figure rather than a guess.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30

                - "Total the sessions or days of cover still to book for each month"
            - name: Status check for each locum and contractor arrangement
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Calling someone a locum or a freelancer does not settle whether they are self-employed in the eyes of the tax authority or employment law. Control over how the work is done, who supplies cover if they cannot attend, and how long they have been with you all count, and getting it wrong can leave the practice owing back taxes, holiday pay or employment rights. Reviewing each regular arrangement with your accountant settles the question before it is raised for you.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every locum and contract professional used in the last year listed with how often they worked and how they were paid.
                2. Each arrangement described against the tests your tax authority publishes, such as control, substitution and integration.
                3. Your accountant's or employment adviser's view on each arrangement recorded.
                4. Any arrangement that looks like employment changed, put through payroll or ended, with the date noted.

                ## Notes
                Long-running locums who work the same days every week for years are the usual problem. Some countries also have specific rules for contractors working through their own companies, so ask your adviser which apply.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 60
              frontmatter:

            - name: Locum rate card and annual cover budget
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Without a rate card, every booking becomes a fresh negotiation and the practice ends up paying different people very different amounts for the same session. Setting rates by session type, notice period and day of the week, then multiplying them by the cover map, turns locum spend into a budget line you can plan for instead of a monthly surprise.

                ## Milestones
                1. Current local rates for your profession gathered from at least two agencies, a professional body survey and two peers.
                2. A rate card set for standard sessions, short notice, evenings, weekends and public holidays.
                3. What the rate includes stated plainly: travel, mileage, accommodation, indemnity and breaks.
                4. An annual cover budget calculated from the rate card and the twelve-month cover map.

                ## Notes
                Check whether a rate is quoted per hour, per session or per day, and whether an agency rate includes its fee. Comparing the wrong units is the most common budgeting mistake.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30

            - name: Pre-booking credential and registration check
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Even a locum arriving tomorrow on a colleague's recommendation can have a lapsed registration, conditions on their licence or a gap in their record that nobody asked about. # Locums and Contract Professionals

This area is for practice owners and managers who bring in locum doctors, dentists, vets, pharmacists and therapists, or contract solicitors, accountants, engineers and architects, to cover leave, vacancies and busy seasons. What repeats is a Monday cover gap check, a Friday handover review, monthly invoice, spend, registration and feedback checks on different days, quarterly bank and quality reviews and a yearly rate and agreement refresh.
