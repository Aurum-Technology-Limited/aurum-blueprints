              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Many partnerships share profit by a mix of an old agreement clause, a spreadsheet the senior partner keeps and side deals nobody has written down. Recording exactly how last year's profit was actually divided, step by step, is the only honest starting point for any change and often surfaces disagreements before they become disputes.

                ## Milestones
                1. The profit sharing clause of the current agreement copied into one document.
                2. Last year's allocation reconstructed step by step from the accounts.
                3. Every difference between the clause and what actually happened listed.
                4. Any side arrangements, guarantees or informal adjustments recorded and confirmed by the partners involved.

                ## Notes
                Keep this factual. It is a record of the present, not the place to argue for a new model.

            - name: Distributable profit pool definition
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Arguments about shares are often really arguments about the size of the pot: whether salaried partner costs, notional rent on premises a partner owns, interest on capital, retained working capital or movements in work in progress come off before the split.             - name: Tax reserve rules for partner shares
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Partners in many tax systems are taxed on their share of profit whether or not it has been drawn, and a partner who spends every drawing can face a bill the practice ends up covering. Agreeing with the accountant how much of each share is held back for tax, where it is held and when it is released protects both the partner and the practice's cash.

                ## Milestones
                1. The accountant's explanation of how partner shares are taxed in your jurisdiction recorded.
                2. A reserve rule agreed, such as a fixed proportion of each drawing or a calculated amount per partner.
                3. A separate account or ledger set up for the reserves.
                4. Reserve balances reported to each partner at least twice a year.

                ## Notes
                This project organises the reserve; it does not decide anyone's tax position. Each partner should take their own tax advice.

# Partner Profit Sharing Models

This area is for the partners of a law, accounting, engineering, architecture, medical, dental or veterinary practice who have to decide, every year, who gets what share of the profit. It starts with the foundations (writing down what you do today, defining the profit pool, agreeing what the model is for and setting reserves), then the yearly and monthly machinery of drawings, forecasts, scorecards and allocation, the skills of modelling and credit splitting, the decisions about lockstep, merit, hybrids and origination credit, the events around adopting a new model, the situations small partnerships meet, and finally specialist work such as mergers, bonus pools and incorporated practices.

What repeats is a monthly drawings run, tax reserve transfer, scorecard update and origination register check, a quarterly profit forecast, and a yearly round of self-assessments, committee meetings, allocation, true-up and model review.
