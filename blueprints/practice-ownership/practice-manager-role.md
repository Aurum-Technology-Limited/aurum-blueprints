              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Owners usually feel the need for a manager long before they decide, because the evidence is spread across late invoices, staff questions at the treatment room door and evenings spent on rotas. Counting the hours owners and professionals spend on admin each week, and pricing those hours at their billing or production rate, turns a vague frustration into a decision with a number attached.

                ## Milestones
                1. Two weeks of admin hours recorded by each owner and senior professional.
                2. The value of those hours calculated at each person's fee or production rate.
                3. The cost of a full-time, part-time or shared manager set beside that value.
                4. A recorded decision from the owners: hire now, hire later with a trigger, or not at all.

                ## Notes
                A common rule of thumb is that once owners spend more than a day a week each on admin, a manager pays for themselves, but test it against your own figures rather than the rule.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30

                output_kind: decision

                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "6"
              tasks:
                - "Give each owner a simple sheet to log admin time for two weeks"
                - "Price the logged hours at each person's fee or production rate"

                - "Record the owners' decision and any trigger for revisiting it"
            - name: Practice manager job description and scope
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Without a written scope the manager becomes whoever picks up whatever the owners drop, and the role fails within a year because nobody agrees what success looks like. A job description that lists the areas the manager owns outright, the areas they support and the areas that stay with the professionals gives both sides something to hold each other to.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of every management task in the practice, sorted into owns, supports and excluded.
                2. A one-page job description with purpose, responsibilities, reporting line and working hours.
                3. Clinical or professional decisions explicitly listed as outside the manager's scope.
                4. The description signed off by every owner, not only the one who drafted it.

                ## Notes
                Keep professional judgement out of the role on paper: the manager can schedule, staff and resource the work but not decide how a patient is treated or how a client is advised.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:

                cadence: one-shot

              tasks:

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most friction between owners and their manager comes from unwritten limits: the manager orders a new autoclave or issues a warning and an owner overturns it in front of staff. A short matrix of spending limits, staffing decisions, supplier contracts and client or patient complaints, with who decides, who is consulted and who is told, settles those questions once.

            - name: Recruiting and selecting a practice manager
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Charming interviews tell you little about whether someone can chase a debtor list, build a rota around three part-time clinicians and say no to a partner. A selection process with a written exercise drawn from your real practice, a panel that includes a senior staff member and structured references shows how the candidate actually works.

                ## Milestones
                1. An advert placed with the job description, salary range and closing date.
                2. A shortlist scored against the same five criteria by each panel member.
                3. A practical exercise completed by each shortlisted candidate, such as a rota or a debtor plan.
                4. Two references taken, including one from a previous line manager.
                5. An offer accepted and a start date agreed.

                ## Notes
                Use anonymised practice data for the exercise. Never share real patient or client records with candidates.
              priority: high

# Practice Manager Role

This area is for owners deciding whether to hire a practice manager and for the person stepping into the job, whether in a clinic, a dental or vet practice, or a law, accounting or engineering firm. It starts with foundations (the case for the role, the job description, delegated authority, the reporting line, pay, recruitment and the first ninety days), then the operating machinery of reports, check-ins, huddles, rotas, deadlines and budgets, the skills a manager needs to hold authority among professionals, decisions that move admin off the professionals, the dated events of the manager's year, situations from internal promotion to a family member in the role, and finally specialist work on bonuses, multi-site operations and the manager's own successor.

What repeats is a daily huddle, a weekly owner check-in, monthly one-to-ones, a monthly report, scorecard and budget check on varied days, quarterly reviews of contracts, policies and the escalation tree, and a yearly appraisal round and review of the manager. The Onboarding 30/60/90, 1:1, Metrics log, Vendor, Board meeting and Course templates pair with the projects that keep those records.
