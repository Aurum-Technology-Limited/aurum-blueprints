              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most owners carry a vague idea of retiring at some point, and that vagueness is why successors leave, staff worry and the eventual exit is rushed. A one-page statement of when you want to step back, how far (fully out, part-time, consulting) and what matters most to you in the outcome gives every later decision something to be measured against.

                ## Milestones
                1. A target year for stepping back from ownership written down, with an earliest and latest acceptable date.
                2. Your preferred end state named: fully retired, reduced clinical or professional hours, or a paid advisory role.
                3. Your three priorities ranked, for example price, continuity of care for clients, jobs for staff.
                4. The statement shared with your spouse or partner and any co-owners, and their reactions noted.

                ## Notes
                Write the latest acceptable date as well as the ideal one. Health, family and market events tend to move the date earlier, rarely later.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A signed one-page statement records your target step-back year, end state and three ranked priorities, discussed with the people it affects."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"

                - "Decide whether you want to stop practising entirely or keep some hours"

                - "Talk the statement through with your spouse or partner"

                ## Notes

# Practice Succession Planning

This area is for the practice owner who knows that one day someone else will see the patients, sign the reports or run the client files, and wants that day to be planned rather than forced. It starts with foundations (a succession goal and date, the duties only you perform, the options compared, an emergency plan and the documents that let someone act for you), then the recurring machinery that keeps the plan alive, the knowledge you need about financing, tax touchpoints and handing over relationships, the decisions about who succeeds you and on what terms, the dated moments of telling staff, clients and the regulator, the situations of single-handed, family, co-owned, ill-health and small-market practices, and finally specialist work such as rehearsing the emergency plan, preparing your executor and capturing your professional judgement before you go.

What repeats is a weekly delegation of one owner task, a weekly shadowing slot for your successor, monthly relationship handover and procedure library updates, quarterly successor check-ins, readiness scores and emergency vault checks, and a yearly review of the whole plan, its insurance funding and your powers of attorney. The Development plan, Vendor, Meeting notes, Risk register and Operational checklist templates pair with the projects that keep those records. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
