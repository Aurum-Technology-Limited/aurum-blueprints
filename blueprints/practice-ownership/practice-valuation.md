              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A practice has no single value: the figure for a partner buy-in, a sale to an outside buyer, a divorce settlement and an insurance policy can differ by a wide margin because each uses a different standard of value and date. Writing down why you need the number, as at what date and on which basis (fair market value, fair value under an agreement, or investment value to one buyer) stops you paying for the wrong report.

                ## Milestones
                1. The reason for the valuation written in one sentence, with the person or body who will rely on it.
                2. A valuation date chosen and recorded.
                3. The standard of value named, with any definition in your partnership or shareholder agreement copied out.
                4. Your accountant or lawyer asked to confirm the basis before any valuer is engaged.

                ## Notes
                If your partnership agreement contains a valuation formula, that formula usually governs internal transactions whatever the market would pay. Find it first.

            - name: Normalised earnings schedule with add-backs
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Reported profit includes one-off legal fees, a year of double rent during a move, personal expenses and other items that will not recur for a new owner. A normalised earnings schedule adjusts each year for those items, line by line with evidence, and gives the maintainable profit that every valuation method starts from.

                ## Milestones

                ## Notes

# Practice Valuation

This area is for the owner who wants to know what the practice is really worth, and why, long before a buyer, a new partner, a bank or a court asks. It starts with foundations (the purpose of the valuation, three years of accounts, owner pay and add-backs normalised, the revenue mix, assets, debt and an honest owner dependence score), moves to the methods valuers and buyers use, then the recurring machinery that keeps a value current, the improvements that move it, the dated occasions that need a formal figure, the situations of retiring, single-handed and multi-site owners, and finally specialist work on minority discounts, deferred payments and defending a number under challenge.

What repeats is a monthly normalised profit tracker and add-back register, a monthly check of retention and collections, quarterly reviews of value drivers, concentration and key people, and a yearly valuation refresh with updated comparables and asset values.
