            - name: How claims-made cover works in your policy
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Professional indemnity is almost always written on a claims-made basis: the policy that responds is the one in force when the claim is made or notified, not the one in force when the work was done. Owners who grasp that one idea stop letting cover lapse, understand why run-off matters and know why late notification is so costly.

                ## Milestones

                2. Your policy's trigger clause found and read, with the notification wording highlighted.
                3. Three scenarios worked through: a claim during the policy, after a lapse and after retirement.
                4. Questions you could not answer sent to the broker and their replies filed.

                ## Notes
                Some healthcare malpractice cover is written on an occurrence basis or as discretionary membership of a defence organisation. Find out which yours is before applying the scenarios.

# Professional Indemnity for Practices

This area is for the owner or managing partner who signs the indemnity proposal form each year and would be the first to hear about a claim. It starts with foundations (what you hold today, what your regulator requires, how claims-made cover works, the limit, excess, retroactive date and who is insured), then the operating routines that keep cover valid, the knowledge needed to read a wording and spot a notifiable circumstance, the decisions about quotes, layers and neighbouring policies, the events of a claim letter, a risk audit or a change of insurer, the situations of opening, merging, retiring and working alone, and finally specialist work on group schemes, claims history and project policies.

What repeats is a weekly incident log and contract check, monthly reviews of notifications, certificates and premium payments, quarterly fee declarations, risk meetings and cover checks for staff and locums, and a yearly renewal, wording refresh and team training. The Vendor, Risk register, Operational checklist, Meeting notes, Training program and Purchase decision templates pair with the projects that name them.
