              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Sellers who go to market without written goals end up negotiating against themselves, accepting a price, a handover period or an employment role they would have refused a year earlier. A one-page statement of what you need from the sale (net cash, last working day, how long you will stay, what must happen for staff and patients or clients) becomes the test every offer is measured against.

                ## Milestones
                1. A target net figure and a minimum figure below which you will not sell, both written down.
                2. A preferred last working day and the longest transition period you would accept.

# Selling a Practice

This area is for the owner who wants to sell a practice to an outside buyer, whether that is two years away or already on the table, and especially for the retiring practitioner who gets one chance to do it well. It starts with foundations (exit goals, a timeline, the adviser team, a readiness check, buyer eligibility, confidentiality and a data room), then the routines that keep the practice sale-ready and the process moving, the knowledge a seller needs about agreements, warranties, earn-outs and tax, the decisions on broker, price and offers, the dated steps from teaser to completion and announcements, the situations of sole practitioners, co-owners, ill health and small markets, and finally specialist work on competitive bids, failed deals and post-completion claims.

What repeats is a monthly set of sale-ready management accounts and trading figures, a weekly process meeting and buyer log while the practice is on the market, monthly checks on owner dependence and receivables, a quarterly staff retention check, and after completion a monthly ledger of deferred and earn-out receipts with a quarterly earn-out review. The Meeting notes, Metrics log, Risk register and Vendor templates pair with the projects that keep those records.
