                Before any money is spent, settle which Caribbean country the practice will be in and whether you can lawfully practise and own a business there.                 ## Purpose
                Most Caribbean professions require you to be on the local register before you see a single patient or client, whatever you hold elsewhere: the medical council or board, the general legal council or law association, the institute of chartered accountants, the engineers' or architects' board. Applications often need certified copies, certificates of good standing from every place you have practised, police certificates and sometimes an interview or exam. Start early, because the meetings that approve applications may sit only once a month.

                4. Registration or a provisional licence issued and its number recorded.
                5. The annual practising certificate requirement and renewal date noted.

                ## Notes
                Good standing letters from overseas regulators usually expire after a few months, so request them last, once everything else is ready.

# Setting Up a Caribbean Practice

Setting up a practice in the Caribbean means two sets of paperwork running side by side: getting yourself onto the local professional register, and getting the business registered, taxed, insured and licensed. This area starts with eligibility, council registration and the company, tax, national insurance and bank foundations, moves through the monthly and yearly filings, the local law worth learning and the decisions and events around opening, and ends with the variants for returning nationals, CARICOM movers, work permit holders and practices that cross borders.

Payroll levies, sales tax returns, the foreign currency run, the generator test and the statutory filing session come round monthly, tax instalments quarterly, and the registry return, compliance certificate and hurricane season check once a year. The Compliance control, Vendor, Risk register and Month-end close templates pair with the licence map and renewal register, the choice of local advisers, the storm plan and the filing archive.
