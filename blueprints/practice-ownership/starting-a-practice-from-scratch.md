            - name: Practice concept and target client one-pager
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Founders who open a practice for everyone in town usually spend the first year competing on price with every established firm nearby. Writing one page that names the services you will offer, the patients or clients you want, the problem you solve better than the incumbents and what you will refuse gives every later decision, from location to the first hire, something to be tested against.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of no more than eight core services, each with the type of patient or client who buys it.
                2. Three named competitors within your catchment, with their hours, prices where public and obvious gaps.
                3. A two-sentence statement of why someone would switch to you.
                4. A short list of work you will refer out rather than take on in year one.
                5. The one-pager reviewed by two professionals you trust who are not your friends.

                ## Notes
                A narrow concept is easier to market and staff. You can widen the service list once fees are coming in; it is much harder to narrow it after the signage is up.

            - name: Monthly cash runway check against the plan
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Even a busy start-up practice can run out of cash because fees are slow to arrive or costs crept up during fit-out. Comparing actual cash, spend and receipts with the cash model each month shows how many months of runway remain and gives you time to act, by cutting costs, chasing debts or arranging funding, before the account goes dry.

# Starting a Practice from Scratch

This area is for the professional opening a practice where none existed before: a doctor, dentist, vet, therapist, lawyer, accountant, engineer or architect who is leaving employment to put their own name on the door. It starts with the foundations (the concept, personal runway, the cash model, ownership structure, licences, location, timeline and insurance), then the operating routines that run before and after opening, the owner skills a salaried professional rarely needed, the early decisions on prices, systems, equipment, money and people, the dated events from regulator approval to the first anniversary, the situations particular founders face, and finally specialist work such as niche launches, owner pay and outside backing.

What repeats is a weekly pre-opening review, a weekly enquiries log, weekly bookkeeping and billing, daily opening and closing checks in the first months, a monthly runway check and scorecard, a monthly filing calendar and quarterly founder reviews. The Savings goal, Compliance control, Month-end close, Metrics log, Operational checklist and Onboarding plan templates pair with the projects that name them.
