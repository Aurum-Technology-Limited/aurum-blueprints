            - name: How co-investment works for GPs and LPs
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Co-investment means an LP or third party puts money directly into one deal next to the fund, usually at reduced or no fee and carry. GPs use it to write bigger cheques and deepen LP relationships, while LPs use it to lower their blended fee load and build exposure faster. Understanding both motives before your first process stops you treating it as a favour rather than a product with its own rules.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page explainer covering why GPs offer co-investment and why LPs want it.
                2. The difference between pre-signing co-underwriting and post-closing syndication written down with an example of each.
                3. The common fee and carry arrangements listed, from no fee and no carry to a reduced fee with carry.
                4. Three open questions about your own firm's practice answered by a senior colleague.

                ## Notes
                Industry bodies such as ILPA publish guidance on co-investment and allocation. Read it alongside your own firm's documents, not instead of them.

# Co-Investment Deals

This area is for the deal professional, investor relations lead or LP investment team member who offers co-investment alongside a fund or takes it. It starts with foundations (how co-investment works, the LPA provisions, an allocation policy, a co-investor map, an LP mandate, SPV structure, standard terms, wall-crossing and the information pack), then the weekly and quarterly machinery of pipelines, allocation logs, timelines and co-investor updates, then the underwriting and presenting skills, the fee, vehicle and expense decisions, the dated first offers, closings and exits, variants for associates, IR leads, new LP team members and employee co-invest, and finally co-underwriting, follow-on equity, programme attribution and rescue situations.

What repeats is a Monday pipeline review, a Wednesday intake triage on the LP side, a Thursday timeline check on live deals, monthly soundings, allocation log reviews and wall-crossing checks, quarterly co-investor updates and expense reconciliations, and a yearly programme review. The Compliance control, Operational checklist, Meeting notes, Vendor and Metrics log templates pair with the projects that name them. Everything here is organisational: allocation, fee and structuring questions go to fund counsel and your compliance officer, tax questions go to a tax adviser, and confidential deal material stays inside the systems your firm and each NDA allow.
