            - name: How the independent sponsor model works
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                An independent sponsor finds and negotiates a buyout without committed capital, then raises equity for that one deal from capital partners such as family offices, mezzanine funds or high net worth investors. The sponsor is usually paid a closing fee, an annual management or monitoring fee and a promote on the capital partners' profits, but only if the deal closes. Knowing how every party thinks about that bargain before your first owner meeting saves months of learning it from rejections.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page explainer of the model covering sponsor, capital partner, seller and lender motives.
                2. The three usual sponsor economics (closing fee, ongoing fee, promote) described with how each is typically triggered.
                3. Two published independent sponsor surveys or reports read and their main findings noted.
                4. A list of five questions about the model answered by someone who has closed a sponsor deal.

                ## Notes
                Market surveys from law firms and industry groups give ranges, not rules. Use them to frame conversations, and let counsel tell you what is normal for your deal size.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: learning
                output_kind: knowledge

# Independent Sponsor Deals

This area is for the founder doing private equity one deal at a time: finding a business first, then lining up family offices, mezzanine funds or other capital partners to buy it, and earning a closing fee and promote instead of a fund's management fee. It runs from foundations (how the model works, your runway, criteria, entity, credibility pack and adviser bench), through the weekly machinery of pipeline, intermediary and owner coverage and capital partner relationships, into the skills of pricing, modelling the promote and negotiating sponsor terms, then the key decisions, the dated events from first backed LOI to exit, variants for different routes into sponsoring, and finally the specialist work of add-ons, recaps, disputes and programmatic partnerships.

What repeats is a Monday pipeline review, weekly intermediary and owner outreach, monthly capital partner touches, a dead-deal cost ledger and passed-deal log, quarterly strategy and lender reviews, and a yearly update letter to everyone who has backed or nearly backed you. The Vendor, Metrics log, Operational checklist, Board meeting and Meeting notes templates pair with the projects that name them. Everything here is organisational: fee, promote, tax, securities and structuring questions go to deal counsel and a tax adviser, and nothing here is investment advice.
