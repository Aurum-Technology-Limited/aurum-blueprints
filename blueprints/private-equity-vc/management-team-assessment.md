            - name: Management assessment scorecard for your deals
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most management write-ups say the CEO is impressive and the CFO is solid, which tells a committee nothing it can test. A one-page scorecard with six to eight criteria tied to what the plan needs (commercial drive, financial grip, hiring record, integrity, change leadership, board handling) forces every interviewer to score the same things and write down the evidence behind each score.

                ## Milestones
                1. Six to eight criteria written, each with a one-line definition and what a 1, 3 and 5 look like.
                2. A separate weighting for each role, so a CFO is not scored on the CEO's criteria.
                3. The scorecard tested on one past deal where you know how the team turned out.
                4. A partner has agreed the criteria and the scorecard is saved where the deal team can use it.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Skills matrix** template. Score evidence, not charm: every score above 3 needs a specific example the executive gave or a referee confirmed.

# Management Team Assessment

This area is for the deal professional who has to tell an investment committee whether the people running a target can deliver the plan, and what happens if they cannot. It starts with the foundations (a scorecard, a role map, a question bank, confidentiality rules and the back, change or walk decision), then the routines that keep people diligence moving across live deals, the interviewing and referencing skills behind a sound judgement, the decisions about gaps, the CFO, the CEO and retention, the dated moments such as management presentations and committee, the variants for founder-led, family, carve-out, secondary, minority, cross-border and distressed deals, and finally the advanced work of pricing leadership change and building a house playbook.

What repeats is a Monday update of the people tracker, a Thursday check-in with the deal team, monthly reviews of the reference log, the accuracy log and your executive network, a quarterly refresh of the question bank, and a yearly look at search firms and assessment providers. The Skills matrix, Meeting notes, Person, Risk register and Purchase decision templates pair with the projects that name them. Keep candidate and reference notes inside the systems your firm and each NDA allow, and take employment and data protection questions to counsel. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
