              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A traditional search usually means two years of modest salary, a high chance of not finding a company and, if you do, five to ten years running it. Before telling a single investor you are searching, score yourself honestly on operating appetite, tolerance for rejection, financial runway, family support and what you will do if the search ends without a deal.

                ## Milestones
                1. A written scorecard covering operating appetite, rejection tolerance, runway, family support and a fallback plan, each rated with evidence.
                2. Three former searchers and one search investor asked for a candid reaction to the scorecard.
                3. A go, no-go or not-yet decision written down with the conditions that would change it.
                4. The decision shared with the people at home whose lives it changes.

                ## Notes
                The question that matters most is not whether you can find a company but whether you want to be the CEO of a 30-person business in a town you did not choose. Answer that one first.

            - name: Traditional versus self-funded search decision
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Raising search capital from investors buys salary, budget and a ready board, but it gives away much of the equity and adds people to answer to. Self-funding keeps more of the upside and the control, at the cost of your own savings and a harder acquisition raise later. Comparing the two on your own numbers, rather than on folklore, settles which route you are on.

                ## Milestones
                1. Both models laid out side by side on salary, search budget, equity kept, governance and acquisition financing.
                2. Two searchers from each model interviewed about what they would choose again.
                3. The choice recorded with the three reasons that decided it.
                4. A note of what would make you switch models later, kept with the decision.

                ## Notes
                Independent sponsorship and buying a business with a personal loan and no outside investors are other routes again. Rule them in or out explicitly so the decision is complete.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A recorded choice between traditional and self-funded search, with a side-by-side comparison on five dimensions and interviews with four searchers."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "8"
              tasks:
                - "Build a two-column comparison of traditional and self-funded search on five dimensions"
                - "Find two traditional and two self-funded searchers to interview"

            - name: Search fund economics model
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Searchers who cannot explain how search capital converts, how the step-up works and how their own equity vests in tranches lose credibility in the first investor meeting. A small spreadsheet that follows one dollar of search capital and your own stake through a few acquisition and exit outcomes makes the economics concrete and shows what the deal has to look like for everyone to do well.

                ## Milestones
                1. A model showing search capital, the step-up into acquisition equity and the investors' preferred return as commonly structured.
                2. Searcher equity split into its usual tranches, with the conditions that trigger each one.
                3. Three illustrative outcomes worked through: a weak, a typical and a strong exit.
                4. The model checked by one search investor or experienced searcher for errors.

                ## Notes
                Use the model to understand the mechanics, not to promise returns. The figures in it are illustrations, and the real terms are whatever your term sheet and counsel settle.

# Search Fund Launch

This area is for the would-be searcher who wants to buy and run one small company with investors behind them: the business school graduate, the mid-career manager, the pair of partners, the founder outside the usual markets. It starts with the foundations (whether to search at all, traditional or self-funded, solo or partnered, the economics, your runway, the budget, counsel, the entity and the offering memo), then the weekly and monthly machinery of a raise and a search, the knowledge worth having before investors test it, the decisions about investors, terms, tools and base, the dated events from search conferences and the first close to the six-month review, variants for different searchers' lives, and finally the harder cases of an extension, a wind-down or a second search.

What repeats is a Monday investor tracker, a Friday search scorecard, monthly reviews of burn, the books, a mentor call and the home front, quarterly investor letters and self-reviews, and a yearly filing calendar. The Vendor, Purchase decision, Month-end close, Habit tracker and Meeting notes templates pair with the projects that name them. Everything here is organisational: entity, securities, tax and employment questions belong with search fund counsel and your accountant, and nothing in these projects is legal, tax or investment advice. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
