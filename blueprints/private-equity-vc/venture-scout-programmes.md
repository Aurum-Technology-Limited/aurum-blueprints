            - name: Reading the scout agreement clause by clause
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Scout agreements are short, but a few lines decide almost everything: who owns a referral, how long your carry survives if you leave, whether the fund can decline a deal you sourced and then invest later, and what confidentiality you owe. Reading the draft with a list of questions before signing is far easier than renegotiating after your first referral turns into a round.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every clause summarised in one line in plain words.
                2. Referral ownership, attribution window, carry vesting and leaver terms written out explicitly.
                3. Confidentiality, non-solicit and publicity restrictions listed with what they stop you doing.
                4. Open questions sent to the fund and answered in writing before signing.
                5. A lawyer's review obtained or a recorded reason for signing without one.

                ## Notes
                Ask what happens to a referral the fund passes on and then backs at a later round. A clear attribution window, often 12 to 24 months, avoids awkward arguments.

            - name: Scout economics worksheet
              description: |-

                4. Non-cash returns you expect written beside the numbers.

                ## Notes

# Venture Scout Programmes

This area is for anyone scouting for a venture fund on the side of a day job or a company of their own: the engineer or operator with a strong founder network, and the founder who sees promising peers before any investor does. It starts with foundations (the time and employer checks, reading the scout agreement, the economics and your lane), moves into the weekly and monthly machinery of sourcing, referral write-ups and closing the loop with founders, then the skills of first calls, deck screens and short memos, decisions about programmes, channels and tax, the demo days and reviews worth preparing for, situations such as scouting from an employer, as a founder or from a smaller ecosystem, and finally specialist work on carry tracking, a verifiable record and the step beyond scouting.

What repeats is a weekly sourcing block, monthly pipeline calls with your fund contact and founder follow-ups, a monthly look at the deal log, quarterly updates from scout portfolio companies and a yearly agreement and tax review. The Meeting notes, Metrics log, Person and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
