            - name: Vision horizon and scope decision
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A vision for the next eighteen months reads like a roadmap, and one for the next twenty years reads like a slogan. Deciding up front how far ahead it looks, whether it covers the whole company or one product line, and what it is not responsible for keeps the drafting focused and stops it turning into a feature list.

                ## Milestones

                2. The scope stated: which products, segments and markets the vision covers.
                3. A short list of questions that belong in strategy or the roadmap instead.
                4. The decision agreed with the CEO, founder or head of product.

            - name: Evidence pack behind the vision
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Visions written from conviction alone are easy to dismiss in the first tough meeting. Gathering the research findings, usage patterns, lost-deal reasons and market shifts that point towards the future you describe gives the draft a spine, and lets you answer the question of why you believe it with something better than opinion.

                ## Milestones
                1. The ten strongest customer research findings summarised on a page.
                2. Three usage or revenue trends charted with their source.
                3. Lost-deal and churn reasons from the last year grouped into themes.
                4. Four or five market or technology shifts listed with evidence for each.
                5. Each item tagged with how confident you are in it.

                ## Notes
                Keep the pack to about ten pages. Its job is to support the vision, not to replace the research repository.
              priority: high

# Product Vision Writing

This area is for founders and product leaders who need a product vision people can repeat from memory and actually use when they decide what to build. It starts with foundations (finding the statements already in circulation, setting the horizon, gathering evidence and leadership views, and writing the first one-page draft), then the routines that keep the vision current, the writing and facilitation skills behind a good one, the principles, non-goals and companion pieces that make it usable, the events where it is agreed, launched and reviewed, the situations that force a rewrite, and finally the platform, multi-horizon and coaching work of an experienced product leader.

What repeats is a monthly trace from roadmap themes to the vision, monthly reviews of the assumption register, sampled decisions, customer stories, spec spot-checks and new starter walkthroughs, quarterly evidence checks, understanding pulses and board paragraphs, and a yearly refresh timed to finish before annual planning. The Meeting notes, Risk register, Feedback themes, Reading queue and Onboarding 30/60/90 templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
