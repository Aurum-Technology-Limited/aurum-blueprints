              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Architecture looks like sketching beautiful buildings, but most working weeks go on drawings for planning and building approval, coordinating engineers, answering contractors and revising details after a client changes their mind.                 4. A dated decision to pursue architecture, keep exploring or change direction.

                ## Notes
                Ask about hours and pay in the first five years as well as the design work. Long studio nights and modest early salaries surprise more students than any technical subject.

            - name: Comparing architecture routes and accreditation
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The title architect is protected by law in many countries, and the route to it varies: three parts ending in a practice exam in the UK, an accredited professional degree plus logged experience and a licensing exam in the US, a certified degree, internship and exam in Canada, and national registration boards across the Caribbean. Choosing a programme whose degree your intended country recognises avoids discovering at twenty-five that a qualification does not count.

                ## Milestones
                1. The protected title and registration body identified for each country you might work in.
                2. The stages of each route (degree, experience, exam) set out side by side on one page.
                3. Each target programme checked for accreditation or validation recognised by your chosen registration body.
                4. A preferred route chosen, with a fallback recorded.

                ## Notes
                Accreditation applies to a specific programme at a specific time, not to the whole university. Check the registration body's own published list, not only the university's website.

# Architecture Career

This area is for anyone drawn to designing buildings, from a fourth-form student weighing CSEC Technical Drawing and Visual Arts to a graduate logging experience towards registration and an architect thinking about a practice of their own. It starts with the foundations (testing the fit, subjects, routes and accreditation, the admissions portfolio, funding and applications), then the routines that build a professional record, the drawing, modelling, BIM, climate and detailing skills every architect needs, the decisions about placements, programmes and sectors, the milestones from thesis to registration, the situations that change the route, and finally leading projects, conservation work and running a practice.

What repeats is a daily sketch, a weekly studio pin-up, experience log and software practice, monthly precedent, CPD and log sign-offs, quarterly mentor meetings, code checks and portfolio updates, and the yearly round of renewals, salary checks and career review. The Portfolio piece, Job application, Cover letter, Development plan, Onboarding 30/60/90 and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them.
