            - name: Classroom observation week before committing
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Plenty of people choose teaching from memories of being a pupil, which is the wrong side of the desk. Spending five days watching real lessons, ideally in two different schools and age groups, shows you the marking pile, the break duty and the class that will not settle, and lets you decide with your eyes open before paying any fees.

                ## Milestones
                1. Two schools agreed to host you for at least two and a half days each.
                2. Notes kept on at least ten lessons, covering what the teacher did before, during and after each one.
                3. A list of what energised you and what drained you, written on the evening of each day.
                4. A yes, not yet or no decision on applying for teacher training, with your reasons written beside it.

                ## Notes
                Former teachers, a church or community school, or the school you attended are often the easiest doors to knock on. Ask to see a full day including the staffroom and lunch duty, not only the best lessons.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A written decision on whether to apply for teacher training, based on notes from at least ten observed lessons in two schools."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "40"
              tasks:
                - "Email or call two schools asking to observe lessons for a few days"
                - "Prepare a one-page observation sheet with before, during and after columns"
                - "Ask one teacher what their week looks like outside lesson time"
                - "Write your decision and three reasons for it in a short note"
            - name: Choosing between primary and secondary teaching
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Primary teachers usually teach most subjects to one class all year, while secondary teachers teach one or two subjects to several hundred students across many classes. The choice shapes your training route, the qualifications you need and the kind of day you will have for decades, so make it deliberately rather than by default.

                ## Milestones
                1. Time spent with both a primary class and a secondary department, even if only a day each.
                2. Both phases scored against five things that matter to you, such as age group, subject depth and workload pattern.
                3. Your teaching subject or subjects chosen if secondary, with shortage subjects noted.
                4. A phase decision written down and talked through with someone whose judgement you trust.

                ## Notes
                Shortage subjects such as mathematics, physics, chemistry, computing and modern languages often attract bursaries and more job offers. Check the current list where you plan to train, as it changes from year to year.
              priority: high

                success_criteria: "A written choice of phase, plus subject for secondary, scored against five personal criteria after time in both settings."
                cadence: one-shot

                ## Purpose
                There are several roads to qualified status: a teachers' college diploma, a Bachelor of Education, a subject degree followed by a postgraduate certificate or diploma in education, a salaried school-based route, or teaching while untrained and qualifying in service.             - name: Safeguarding and child protection training

                ## Purpose
                Every teacher is a person a child may choose to tell, and how you respond in the first minute matters. Completing recognised child protection training, learning your school's reporting route and knowing who the designated person is means you can act correctly on the day a disclosure happens, rather than looking it up afterwards.

# Becoming a Teacher

Becoming a Teacher follows the route from wondering whether teaching suits you to leading a department, for school leavers, graduates, career changers and serving teachers in the Caribbean, the UK, the US and Canada. It starts with observation, entry qualifications, funding and choosing a training route, moves through lesson planning, behaviour, assessment and inclusion skills, the teaching practice placement and registration, then the first post, the induction year and the routines that keep a teacher going, and closes with career changers, untrained teachers, teaching abroad, exam classes, mentoring, further study and leadership.

In term the rhythm is weekly: planning on Sundays, marking on Tuesdays, mentor meetings on Wednesdays and an hours check on Fridays, with monthly progress records, reading and resource sorting, and yearly registration, safeguarding, appraisal and pay checks. The Savings goal, Portfolio piece, Job application, 1:1, Reading queue and Development plan templates pair with the projects that point to them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
