            - name: Checking a programme's accreditation before applying
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Professional registration later depends on whether your degree was accredited by a recognised engineering body, and an unaccredited programme can add years of extra assessment. International agreements such as the Washington Accord mean an accredited degree from one signatory country is usually recognised in the others, which matters if you might work abroad. An hour checking each programme now is the cheapest insurance in the whole career.

                ## Milestones
                1. The body that accredits engineering degrees in your country, and in any country you might work in, identified.
                2. Each shortlisted programme checked against that body's published list, with the year of accreditation noted.
                3. Programmes that are unaccredited or only provisionally accredited marked clearly.
                4. The shortlist trimmed to accredited programmes, or the extra steps for an unaccredited one written down.

                ## Notes
                Accreditation applies to a specific programme and intake year, not a whole university. Check the exact degree title you would enrol on.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 75
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "Every programme on the shortlist has its accreditation status and accrediting body recorded from the official published list."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Find which body accredits engineering degrees in your country"
                - "Look up each shortlisted programme on that body's accredited list"
                - "Check whether that accreditor is a Washington Accord signatory"
                - "Remove or flag any programme without current accreditation"

# Civil Engineering Career

This area is for anyone drawn to roads, bridges, drainage and water systems, from a fourth-form student weighing CSEC Mathematics and Physics to a graduate engineer working towards registration. It starts with the foundations (testing the fit, subject choices, routes, accreditation, funding and applications), then the routines that build a professional record, the technical skills every civil engineer needs, the decisions about placements, sectors and specialisms, the milestones from first registration to professional review, the situations that change the route, and finally the work of leading projects and a practice.

What repeats is a weekly problem sheet and site diary, monthly competence evidence, CPD, reading and branch meetings, quarterly mentor reviews and portfolio updates, and the yearly round of renewals, salary checks and career review. The Skills matrix, Development plan, Job application, Portfolio piece, Onboarding 30/60/90 and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them.
