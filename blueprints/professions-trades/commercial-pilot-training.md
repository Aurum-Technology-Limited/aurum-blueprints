                ## Purpose
                Airlines need pilots who hold the highest class of aviation medical, called Class 1 under most authorities and first class in the US, and a problem found after you have spent heavily on training can end the plan overnight. Booking the initial examination with an approved aviation medical examiner before any deposit or loan means you only invest once you know you can hold the certificate. Colour vision, eyesight correction, hearing and some long-term conditions are the usual surprises.

# Commercial Pilot Training

This area is for anyone who wants to fly for a living, whether a fourth or fifth-form student weighing CSEC and CAPE subjects, a school leaver in the UK, US or Canada, a career changer funding lessons around a full-time job, or a new first officer finding their feet on the line. It starts with the foundations (a trial lesson, the medical, your licensing authority's route, the real cost, the school and the training model), then the routines that keep study, logbook, money, weather reading, rest and expiry dates in order, the skills from first solo to multi-crew flying, the decisions about hour building, equipment, licence conversion and the kind of flying you want, the dated tests and assessments, the situations that change the route, including training abroad from the Caribbean, and finally pay, command and flying overseas.

What repeats is twice-weekly theory study with a weekly timed paper, a daily look at the coded weather, monthly logbook, spend and hours checks on varied days, a monthly expiry and recruitment check, quarterly preparation for simulator checks once you are employed, and the yearly medical and insurance renewals.
