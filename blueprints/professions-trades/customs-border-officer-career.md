              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Border officers spend long shifts checking passports, opening suitcases and barrels, reading manifests and refusing people who will be upset, often in a hot cargo shed or a busy arrivals hall at 2 a.m. Finding out what the work is really like, before you sit an entrance exam or leave another job, gives you an honest answer.                 4. A dated decision to apply now, prepare for a year or look at another career.

# Customs and Border Officer Career

This area is for anyone heading for a customs or immigration post, from a fourth-form student choosing CSEC subjects to a security guard, soldier or clerk changing direction, and for serving officers aiming at specialist units and promotion. It starts with the foundations (whether the work suits you, which side of the border to choose, entry requirements, vetting, fitness and an application file), then the routines that carry you through recruitment and the early years, the knowledge the exams and the job demand, from tariff classification and valuation to document fraud and trafficking indicators, the decisions about agencies and postings, the dated steps from entrance exam to confirmation, situations such as outstation postings, parenting on shifts and working abroad, and finally the intelligence, investigation, audit and leadership work of an experienced officer.

What repeats is a monthly check of vacancy notices, a weekly news briefing, language and exam practice, and, once you are in, daily note checks, monthly legislation updates, payslip checks, wellbeing check-ins and roster planning, a quarterly development log entry and a yearly declaration of interests.
