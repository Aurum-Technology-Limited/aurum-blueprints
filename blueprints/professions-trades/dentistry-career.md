              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Dentistry attracts students who are strong in science and like the idea of a respected, well-paid health career, but the daily reality is close, precise handwork in a small space for eight hours, with patients who are often nervous and sometimes in pain. Checking your reasons against what dentists actually do, before choosing subjects or paying for applications, saves a costly change of course and gives you honest material for a personal statement.

                ## Milestones
                1. A written list of your reasons for dentistry, each tied to something you have seen or done.
                2. A conversation held with at least one practising dentist about the hardest parts of their week.
                3. Dentistry compared with two neighbouring careers, such as medicine or dental hygiene, on training length, cost and daily work.
                4. A dated decision to pursue dentistry, keep exploring or look elsewhere.

                ## Notes
                Being good at science is necessary but not enough. Ask yourself honestly whether you enjoy fine, fiddly work with your hands and talking calmly to anxious people.

# Dentistry Career

This area is for anyone heading towards the dental chair as the person holding the mirror, from a fourth-form student weighing biology and chemistry at CSEC to a final-year student facing licensing exams and a new graduate signing a first associate contract. It runs from the foundations (testing your reasons, choosing between dentist, hygienist and therapist routes, subjects, work experience, schools, funding and applications) through the routines of study and clinical practice, the core skills from anatomy to local anaesthesia, the big decisions on contracts, kit and specialty, the dated milestones of admissions tests, finals and licensing, situations such as studying overseas or returning after a break, and finally the work of an experienced dentist who teaches, serves a community or owns a practice.

What repeats is a weekly study plan with daily anatomy flashcards, a weekly procedure log, monthly CPD entries, case reflections and infection control checks, quarterly emergency drills, radiograph audits and mentor meetings, and the yearly round of registration, indemnity and life support renewals. The Monthly budget and bills, Skills matrix, Compliance control, Purchase decision, Job application and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them.
