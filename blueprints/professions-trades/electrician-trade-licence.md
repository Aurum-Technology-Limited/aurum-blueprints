            - name: Safe isolation procedure practised until automatic
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                More electricians are hurt working on circuits they believed were dead than in any other way. The safe isolation sequence (identify the circuit, isolate it, lock it off, prove your tester on a known source, test the circuit dead, prove the tester again) has to be second nature before you work alone. Practising it on a training board until you never skip a step is the single most important safety habit in the trade.

                ## Milestones
                1. The safe isolation sequence your course or regulator teaches written in your own words.
                2. The sequence carried out under supervision on a training board at least ten times without a missed step.
                3. A supervisor has watched you isolate a real circuit on site and signed your logbook.
                4. A laminated step card kept in your tool bag.

                ## Notes
                Use an approved two-pole voltage indicator, not a neon screwdriver or a multimeter on the wrong setting. If a lock-off is not possible, do not work live; tell your supervisor.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "The safe isolation sequence has been carried out correctly ten times on a training board and once on site, signed off by a supervisor."
                cadence: phased

              tasks:

            - name: Cable sizing and voltage drop calculations
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Choosing a cable means accounting for the load, the protective device, the installation method, ambient temperature, grouping with other cables and voltage drop over the length of the run.                 ## Milestones

# Electrician Trade and Licence

This area is for anyone heading into electrical work, whether a fourth-form student taking Electrical and Electronic Technology, a school leaver on a TVET course, a career changer starting an apprenticeship at thirty or a journeyman working towards a contractor licence. It starts with the foundations (testing the fit, the licensing route, a course, an apprenticeship, the first tools and safe isolation), then the routines that keep hours, records, instruments and licences in order, the core electrical knowledge from Ohm's law to inspection and testing, the decisions about specialism, tools, insurance and pricing, the exam and assessment days, the situations that change the route, including working abroad and coming home, and finally the specialist and business work of an experienced electrician.

What repeats is a weekly hours log signed by your supervisor, a weekly session with the wiring regulations, weekly lock-off kit checks and invoicing, monthly filing of certificates, stock counts, near-miss reviews and client follow-ups, and the yearly round of calibration, insurance, storm-season preparation and licence renewal. The Monthly budget and bills, Job application, Purchase decision, Metrics log, Operational checklist and Development plan templates pair with the projects that name them.
