            - name: Testing whether farming is the career for you
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Farming rewards patience and planning, but the reality is early starts, heat, a cash flow that arrives months after the money goes out, and losses to weather, pests and thieves that nobody can fully prevent. Spending a few weeks on working farms, and asking farmers about their worst season, before you commit savings or family land gives you an honest answer and a clearer idea of which kind of farming suits you.

                ## Milestones
                1. A written list of what draws you to farming and what worries you, made before any visits.
                2. Conversations held with at least three working farmers, including one who has given up.
                3. At least five days worked on a farm doing ordinary jobs, not just a tour.
                4. A dated decision recorded: farm full time, farm part time, or try a related agricultural career.

                ## Notes
                Ask each farmer what a bad year cost them and how they got through it. That answer tells you more than any prospectus.
              priority: high

            - name: Finding land to farm
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Land is the first wall most new farmers hit. Options include leasing private land, applying for a state land lease or agricultural tenancy, farming family land with a written agreement among the heirs, or renting a plot from a farmer who is cutting back. Securing written access for long enough to recover what you spend on clearing, fencing and water, usually several years, protects you from losing the farm just as it starts to pay.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three possible parcels identified, each with size, road access, water source and terms.
                2. The owner's right to lease confirmed through the land registry or the state lands office.
                3. A written lease or family agreement covering length, rent, improvements and early ending.
                4. The agreement reviewed by a lawyer before signing.

                ## Notes
                Family land held by many heirs is common across the Caribbean. A short written agreement signed by the heirs who can be reached avoids disputes once the farm starts earning.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 180
              frontmatter:

                output_kind: decision

                cadence: one-shot

# Farming as a Career

This area is for anyone thinking of farming for a living, from a student weighing CSEC Agricultural Science and a college of agriculture to an adult with family land, a returning resident or a working farmer ready to grow. It starts with the foundations (testing the fit, training, a season on someone else's farm, land, soil, water, the first enterprise, registration, safety and a business plan), then the records and routines that keep a farm running, practical skills in soil, protected growing, small ruminants, poultry and post-harvest care, the money and selling decisions, the first planting and harvest, situations such as succession, part-time farming and seasonal work abroad, and finally specialist work in certification, export, processing, climate adaptation and mentoring.

What repeats is a daily field diary, weekly scouting walks, sales entries and banking, monthly checks of the chemical store, livestock health calendar, equipment and enterprise margins, quarterly price reviews, and the yearly round of storm preparation and season reviews. The Job application, Operational checklist, Metrics log, Purchase decision and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them.
