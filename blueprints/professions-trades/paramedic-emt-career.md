              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Pre-hospital care has a ladder, and the rungs have different names in different countries. In the US it runs from emergency medical responder to EMT, advanced EMT and paramedic; Canada uses primary, advanced and critical care paramedic; in the UK the paramedic is a registered professional with support roles such as emergency care assistant below; and Caribbean ambulance services set their own EMT and paramedic grades, often modelled on the US or UK ladder. Knowing what each level may legally do where you want to work stops you training for a qualification that will not be recognised.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every certification level used in your country or target country listed, from first responder to paramedic.
                2. The scope of practice for each level written in one line: skills allowed, medicines allowed, supervision needed.
                3. The body that certifies, licenses or registers each level named, with its website saved.
                4. The level you will aim for first chosen and written down.

                ## Notes
                Scope of practice is set by law or by each service's medical director, so check the regulator or the ambulance service itself rather than a forum post.

# Paramedic and EMT Career

This area is for anyone drawn to emergency care on the road, from a fifth-form student choosing CSEC subjects to a security guard, nursing assistant or soldier changing career, and for working EMTs planning the step up to paramedic and beyond. It starts with the foundations (a ride-along, the certification ladder where you live, entry passes, the first CPR card, clearances, licence, fitness, your route and your career folder), then the routines that keep you studying, logged, certified, rested and well, the clinical knowledge every exam and shift tests, the decisions about programmes, money and employers, the dated exams, applications and first months on the road, the situations that change the route, and finally critical care, advanced practice and teaching.

What repeats is weekly study and practice scenarios, a weekly placement log and vacancy check, twice-weekly strength work, a monthly look at certificates, guidelines, kit, payslips and the family rota, and the yearly renewal of your certification or registration. The Resume, Metrics log, Training program, Job application, Onboarding 30/60/90 and Development plan templates pair with the projects that name them.
