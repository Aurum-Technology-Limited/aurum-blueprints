            - name: Testing whether physiotherapy suits you
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Physiotherapy is often pictured as sports massage at the side of a pitch, but most physiotherapists spend their days on hospital wards, in outpatient clinics, care homes and patients' homes, helping people walk again after a stroke, clear their chests after surgery or manage years of back pain. Spending time with working physiotherapists before choosing subjects or applying shows whether you enjoy slow, hands-on progress with people who are in pain, which is the real work.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three physiotherapists or physiotherapy assistants asked what a normal week involves and what they find hardest.
                2. At least one day spent observing in a hospital or community setting rather than a sports club.
                3. Physiotherapy compared on one page with two neighbouring careers, such as occupational therapy, sports science or nursing.
                4. A dated decision to pursue physiotherapy, keep exploring or change direction.

                ## Notes
                Ask about lifting, standing all day and emotionally hard cases as well as the rewarding moments. People who leave the profession early tend to mention the physical load and the paperwork, not the patients.
              priority: medium

                output_kind: decision

            - name: Comparing physiotherapy degree routes by country
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Becoming a physiotherapist looks different depending on where you want to practise: a three or four-year bachelor's degree in the UK and much of the Caribbean, an entry-level master's in Canada, and a Doctor of Physical Therapy in the United States, usually after a first degree. Choosing the route with the end country in mind matters, because a degree that leads to registration in one place may need extra study, exams or supervised practice in another.

                ## Milestones
                1. The entry-level qualification required in each country you might work in written down.
                2. Every shortlisted programme checked for approval or accreditation by the regulator in that country.
                3. Total length and cost of each route, including any prerequisite first degree, set side by side.
                4. One preferred route and one backup route chosen and dated.

                ## Notes
                Ask each programme directly whether its graduates can register with the physiotherapy regulator in your home country. Some regional students train abroad and find that registration at home takes longer than expected.

            - name: Anatomy and physiology head start
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                First-year anatomy moves fast: hundreds of muscles with their attachments, nerve supply and actions, plus the bones and joints they cross, often examined through spotter tests and practicals. Learning the musculoskeletal basics in the months before the programme starts frees your first term for the clinical skills that build on them.

                ## Milestones

                2. Attachments, action and nerve supply learned for the main muscles of the shoulder, hip and knee.
                3. A flashcard or spaced repetition deck built and in daily use.
                4. A self-test of 50 muscles scored at 80 percent or better.

# Physiotherapy Career

This area is for anyone drawn to physiotherapy, from a fourth-form student weighing CSEC Biology and Physical Education to a graduate preparing for registration and a senior clinician thinking about a clinic of their own. It starts with the foundations (testing the fit, subjects, routes, observation hours, funding, applications and professional conduct), then the routines that keep a physiotherapist safe and current, the assessment and treatment skills every placement tests, the decisions about first posts, pay and specialty, the milestones from dissertation to registration and the first 90 days, the situations that change the route, and finally sports work, advanced practice, student supervision and private practice.

What repeats is daily anatomy review and same-day notes, weekly peer practice, placement logs and reflections, monthly CPD, research reading and clinic figures, quarterly supervision and outcome audits, and the yearly round of renewals, pay checks and career review. The Skills matrix, Reading queue, 1:1, Development plan, Job application and Onboarding 30/60/90 templates pair with the projects that name them.
