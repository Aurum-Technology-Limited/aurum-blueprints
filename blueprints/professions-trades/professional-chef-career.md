                ## Purpose
                Colleges and apprenticeship schemes usually ask for English and Mathematics, and they look kindly on a food subject.                 If your school does not offer a food subject, a strong Mathematics grade and a summer job in any kitchen count for a great deal. At CAPE, Tourism or Management of Business help later if you aim for hospitality management.

            - name: Food handler's permit and food safety certificate
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Almost every country requires anyone preparing food for the public to hold a food handler's permit or certificate, and in many Caribbean territories that means a short course plus a medical examination through the public health department. Hotels and good restaurants will not schedule you without it, so getting it before you apply removes the first question at every interview.

                ## Milestones
                1. The permit or certificate required where you will work confirmed with the health authority or employer.
                2. Any medical examination or tests the authority requires completed.
                3. The food safety course passed and the permit or certificate in hand.
                4. A copy stored with your job documents and the expiry date noted.

                ## Notes
                Requirements differ: a food handler's badge in much of the region, a Level 2 food hygiene certificate in the UK, a food handler card or ServSafe certificate in many US states. Check the rule for the place you will actually work.

                Burns, deep knife cuts, slips on wet tiles and back strains from lifting stockpots are the everyday injuries of the trade, and fryer fires are the dangerous ones. A first aid certificate plus knowing exactly where the fire blanket, extinguisher and burns kit are in your kitchen means you act in seconds instead of looking around.

                ## Milestones

                4. A personal rule written for hot pans, knives in the sink and calling behind and hot.

                ## Notes

            - name: Classic knife cuts practised to a standard
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every chef judges a new cook first by their knife work: a brunoise of even two-millimetre cubes, a fine julienne, a chiffonade without bruising. Practising the classic cuts at home with cheap onions, carrots and peppers until they are both even and quick gets you trusted with prep in your first weeks.

                ## Milestones
                1. The classic cuts and their sizes written on a single card: brunoise, small dice, julienne, batonnet, paysanne and chiffonade.
                2. Each cut practised on cheap vegetables until the pieces are visibly even.
                3. A timed test of one kilogram of onions diced, repeated a month later.
                4. A chef or tutor asked to check your cuts and name one correction.

                ## Notes
                Speed comes from technique, not effort. Keep the knife sharp, curl the guiding fingers and let the blade do the work; rushing is how prep cooks lose fingertips.

# Professional Chef Career

This area is for anyone heading into professional cooking, whether a fourth-form student taking Food, Nutrition and Health, a school leaver on a CVQ course, a home cook who wants to cook for a living or a commis already on the line and aiming for sous chef. It starts with the foundations (testing the fit, the right subjects, school or apprenticeship, food safety and first aid certificates, a knife roll and the first job), then the routines that keep a station, its records, costs and your body in working order, the core skills from the brigade and mother sauces to Caribbean repertoire and the pass, the decisions about kitchen type, promotion, pay and certification, the trial shifts, assessments and inspections, the situations that change the route, including working abroad, and finally the specialist work of choosing a specialism, running a kitchen and training others.

What repeats is a daily prep list and post-shift stretch, weekly knife sharpening, stock counts, temperature sheet reviews and home practice, monthly recosting, allergen checks, spec sheet updates and a photo of your best plate, and the yearly round of permit renewals and pay comparisons. The Purchase decision, Monthly budget and bills, Job application, Operational checklist, Development plan and Portfolio piece templates pair with the projects that name them.
