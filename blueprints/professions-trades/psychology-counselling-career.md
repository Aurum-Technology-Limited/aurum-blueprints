              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Psychologist, counsellor, psychotherapist and counselling psychologist sound interchangeable, but they lead to different degrees, different regulators, different lengths of training and different pay. In many countries a clinical psychologist needs a doctorate after a first degree, while a counsellor may qualify through a diploma or master's in two to four years. Laying the routes side by side before you choose a degree saves years spent on the wrong ladder.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page comparison of at least four titles covering qualification, years of training, regulator and typical settings.
                2. The titles that are protected by law in the country where you want to work identified.
                3. Two routes shortlisted that match your time, money and interests.
                4. A first-choice route written down with the reasons behind it.

                ## Notes
                In some countries anyone can call themselves a counsellor, while psychologist, or a specific title such as clinical psychologist, is protected by law. Check your regulator's list of protected titles before relying on a job advert's wording.

                ## Purpose
                Reading about therapy tells you little about sitting with distress for six hours a day, writing notes, chasing supervision and managing a waiting list. Three conversations with people at different stages, say a trainee, a school counsellor and an experienced psychologist, test your picture of the work against reality. Most clinicians cannot let you observe sessions for confidentiality reasons, so a good conversation is the closest you will get.

                ## Notes
                Do not ask to sit in on therapy sessions; it puts the clinician in an awkward position. Ask instead about a typical week, supervision, pay and what they wish they had known at your stage.

# Psychology and Counselling Career

Psychology and Counselling Career follows the route from wondering whether you could do this work to running a caseload, supervising others or opening a practice, for school leavers, graduates and career changers in the Caribbean, the UK, the US and Canada. It starts with choosing between the psychologist, counsellor and psychotherapist routes, entry subjects, accredited degrees and funding, then builds core skills from micro-skills and ethics to risk assessment and culturally responsive practice, takes you through placements, supervised hours, the licensing exam and registration, and ends with modalities, specialisms, online and private practice, working abroad and supervising.

Once you are seeing clients the rhythm is weekly: hours logged on Fridays, notes cleared on Thursdays, supervision prepared midweek and a Sunday self-check, with monthly outcome, caseload and CPD reviews, quarterly checks of crisis contacts and record security, and yearly insurance, pay and licence renewals. The Savings goal, Metrics log, 1:1, Development plan, Job application and Onboarding 30/60/90 templates pair with the projects that point to them.
