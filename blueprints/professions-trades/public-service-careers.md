            - name: Public service entry test preparation
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Several countries set a written test for entry to the public service, often covering English comprehension and writing, arithmetic, reasoning and general knowledge of government, and national services in the UK, US and Canada use online aptitude tests. A steady eight weeks of practice on the right question types usually beats cramming, and most candidates lose marks on timing and careless arithmetic rather than hard questions.

                ## Milestones
                1. The current test format, sections and booking rules confirmed from the official source.
                2. A baseline practice test taken under timed conditions and scored by section.
                3. Eight weeks of practice completed, with the two weakest sections drilled twice a week.
                4. A final timed practice test scored at or above your target.

                ## Notes
                Do not pay for 'guaranteed pass' coaching or leaked papers. Use official sample questions where they exist and general aptitude practice books where they do not.
              priority: high

# Public Service Careers

This area is for anyone aiming at a government job or already in one and planning the next grade, whether in a Caribbean public service or in the civil service of the UK, US or Canada. It starts with foundations (how government is organised, entry qualifications, vacancy channels, the entry test, applications, interviews and pre-appointment checks), then the routines that protect a public officer's career, the core skills from minutes and Cabinet notes to public finance and procurement, decisions about appointment types, promotion, transfers and scholarships, dated events such as confirmation, budget season and audit queries, versions for school leavers, graduates, career changers, returning nationals, parents and officers near retirement, and finally specialist routes into diplomacy, local government, regional bodies, donor-funded projects and senior leadership.

What repeats is a weekly look at vacancy circulars and file movement, monthly checks on payslips, leave, appraisal evidence, complaints and your network, quarterly updates of training and declarations, and yearly checks of your service record, increment and the budget call.
