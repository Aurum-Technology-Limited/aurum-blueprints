            - name: Licensing rules where you plan to sell property
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Whether you need a licence, and how you get one, depends entirely on where you work. Jamaica's Real Estate Board licenses salesmen and dealers, US states require a pre-licensing course and state exam, Canadian provinces have their own regulators, and in the UK there is no licence but agents must belong to a redress scheme and be supervised for anti-money laundering. Pinning down the exact rules in your country or state first stops you paying for the wrong course.

                ## Milestones
                1. The regulator or board for property sales in your country, state or province identified.
                2. Entry requirements written down: age, education, course hours, exam, background check and fees.
                3. Any rule about working under a sponsoring broker or dealer noted.
                4. A dated plan to meet each requirement in order.

                ## Notes
                Read the rules on the regulator's own website, not a course provider's sales page. School leavers should check whether passes in CSEC or GCSE English and Mathematics, or a high school diploma, are required.
              priority: high

# Real Estate Agent Career

This area is for anyone thinking about selling property for a living, or already licensed and trying to turn a few deals into a steady business, whether in Kingston, Nassau or Port of Spain, or in the UK, US or Canada. It starts with the foundations (what the work is really like, licensing rules, the course, start-up money, the brokerage and your first contact list), then the weekly machinery of prospecting, pipeline, market reports and past-client care, the skills of pricing, presenting, showing, negotiating and checking clients, the decisions about niche, leads and teams, dated events from the licence exam to your first closing, versions for part-timers, commission-only freelancers, diaspora clients and agents moving abroad, and finally commercial work, new developments, building a team and opening your own brokerage.

What repeats is a weekday prospecting block, a Monday pipeline review, Thursday seller feedback, a monthly market report, expense and tax set-aside routines, quarterly past-client touches and a yearly cycle of licence renewal, business planning and client appreciation. The Habit tracker, Metrics log, Monthly budget and bills, Vendor, Marketing campaign and 1:1 templates pair with the projects that name them.
