                Nursing looks very different on a medical ward at three in the morning than it does on television, and schools increasingly ask applicants what they have actually seen of the work.                 Most nursing programmes want English, mathematics and at least one science, usually biology or human and social biology, and some ask for chemistry too. In the Caribbean that typically means CSEC passes and sometimes CAPE units; in the UK, GCSEs plus A levels or an Access course; in the US and Canada, high school prerequisites with set grades. # Registered Nursing Career

This area follows the route to registered nurse from the first shadowing shift to senior and specialist roles, for school leavers, career changers and working nurses alike. It starts with entry subjects such as CSEC and CAPE sciences or their equivalents, school choice, funding and applications, moves through nursing school study and placements, the licensing exam and registration, then the first post, staying registered, and finally specialising, leading and teaching.

The recurring rhythms are a weekly study plan and reflection during school, daily question practice before the licensing exam, monthly checks on money, placement hours, pay, wellbeing and professional development evidence, and yearly reminders for licence renewal, life support certificates and appraisal. The Monthly budget and bills, Resume, Cover letter, Job application, Onboarding 30/60/90 and Development plan templates pair with it.
