              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                People drawn to veterinary work often picture healthy puppies, not the parts that decide who stays: euthanasia several times a week, owners who cannot pay, night calls and physical work in all weather. Testing your interest against the real job for a few weeks, before choosing subjects or paying any fees, saves years if the answer is no and makes the application far stronger if it is yes.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of what you expect the job to involve, written before you start.
                2. Conversations held with at least two people in veterinary practice, one vet and one nurse or technician.
                3. A week of volunteering or observation completed with animals that are not your own pets.
                4. A written yes, not yet or no, with the two reasons that decided it.

                ## Notes
                Liking animals is necessary but not enough. Most of the job is about people: owners, farmers and colleagues.
              priority: medium

            - name: Veterinarian or veterinary nurse route decision
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Two careers share the same consulting rooms: the veterinarian, who diagnoses, prescribes and operates after a long professional degree, and the veterinary nurse or technician, who runs anaesthesia, nursing care and the lab after a two to four year programme. They differ in entry grades, cost, pay and how much of each clinical decision sits on your shoulders, so choosing deliberately avoids drifting into the wrong one.

                ## Milestones
                1. Entry requirements, programme length and typical cost written side by side for both routes.
                2. The day-to-day duties of each role confirmed with someone who does it.
                3. The legal limits on what a nurse or technician may do in your country noted.
                4. A route chosen, with a fallback named in case grades or funding fall short.

                ## Notes
                Titles vary: veterinary nurse in the UK and much of the Commonwealth, veterinary technician or technologist in the US and Canada, and animal health assistant in some Caribbean government services. Check what the title means where you plan to work.

# Veterinary Career

This area is for anyone heading towards a consulting room, a farm gate or an operating theatre with animals as patients, from a fourth-form student weighing biology and chemistry at CSEC to a veterinary nurse thinking about the full degree and an experienced vet eyeing a residency or a practice of their own. It runs from the foundations (testing the reality of the work, choosing between vet and nurse or technician, subjects, animal experience, accredited schools, funding and applications) through the routines of study and practice, the core clinical skills from anatomy to euthanasia conversations, the big decisions, the dated milestones of rotations, licensing and the first job, situations such as studying overseas or returning after a break, and finally specialist, public service and practice ownership work.

What repeats is a weekly study plan with daily flashcards, weekly experience and placement logs, weekly case reflections and controlled drug checks, monthly CPD, wellbeing and dosimeter routines, quarterly mentor meetings and the yearly round of registration, indemnity and pay reviews. The Savings goal, Development plan, Skills matrix, Job application, Onboarding 30/60/90 and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them.
