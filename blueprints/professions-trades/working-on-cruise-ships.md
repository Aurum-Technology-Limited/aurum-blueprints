            - name: Spotting cruise job scams and fake recruiters
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Fake cruise job offers circulate on social media and messaging apps, promising a quick start and then asking for a fee for processing, training, a visa or a medical at a named clinic. Under the Maritime Labour Convention, legitimate recruitment and placement services should not charge seafarers for finding them work, so knowing the warning signs protects both your savings and your passport details.

                ## Milestones
                1. A written list of the warning signs of a cruise job scam.
                2. The official careers page and approved hiring partners checked for each line you are targeting.
                3. A rule written down that you never pay a recruiter fee or send passport scans to an unverified contact.
                4. Any suspicious offer reported to the cruise line and the relevant authority in your country.

                ## Notes
                Warning signs include an offer without an interview, a free email address, pressure to pay fast, and spelling that does not match the line's own website. When unsure, contact the line directly using details from its official site, never the ones in the message.
              priority: high

              frontmatter:

                output_kind: knowledge

# Working on Cruise Ships

This area is for anyone thinking about a contract at sea, from a school leaver in Kingston, Bridgetown or Port of Spain weighing up a first job to a waiter in Manchester or Toronto who wants to see the world and send money home. It starts with the foundations (whether ship life suits you, which department to aim for, how to tell a real recruiter from a scam and how to apply), then the certificates, medical, visa and joining day, the routines that keep you safe, paid and sane on board, the service and conduct skills ships expect, the decisions about tax, renewals and promotion, the first sign-off, situations such as parenting from sea or coming home for good, and finally the specialist routes into entertainment, concessions, expedition ships and hotel management.

What repeats is a weekly muster check, rest hours record, cabin routine, fitness log and call home, monthly pay slip, allotment, savings, home bills, guest score and wellbeing checks, quarterly certificate and promotion reviews, and a yearly tax and renewal round. The Job application, Operational checklist, Savings goal, Monthly budget and bills and Development plan templates pair with the projects that name them.
