              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Affordable means something precise and different in every system: social rent, affordable rent set against market rent, shared ownership, rent-to-own, or rents capped against a share of area median income. Developers lose months designing a product the local authority or funder does not recognise. A one-page definitions note for your target market keeps the design team, the appraisal and every partner conversation using the same words.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each affordable tenure recognised in your target area listed with its legal or policy definition.
                2. The rent or price cap method for each tenure written down, with its source document.
                3. The public bodies that set or police each definition named.
                4. The note shared with your architect, cost consultant and any partner already involved.

                ## Notes
                Examples help: area median income bands under a housing tax credit system, discounted market sale under a planning obligation, or a national housing corporation's income bands. Use the definition in the policy, not the one an agent remembers.

                Before you start: this plan is general information to help you organise your own affairs. It is not legal advice. Rules differ between countries and change often, so check decisions that matter with a qualified lawyer or the official authority in the country concerned.

Building a proper value line for each affordable tenure, checked with providers, shows how much of the scheme's profit the obligation really costs.

# Affordable Housing Development

Affordable housing development is ordinary development with a second set of rules: who can live in the homes, what they can be charged, who pays the gap and what happens if the terms are broken. This area is for founders and small developers meeting their first affordable obligation, builders who want to deliver for government housing agencies, and non-profits, charities and community land trusts building homes themselves. It starts with definitions, local requirements, partners, tenure mix and funding, moves through the running machinery of bids, grant claims and compliance, then covers the knowledge of subsidy, tax credits and design standards, the big choices on partners and unit placement, the dated submissions and handovers, situations for different kinds of developer and the specialist work of layered funding, regeneration and long-term stewardship.

The rhythms are a weekly housing provider bid check, monthly funding call, grant claim, unit schedule, risk and dashboard reviews, quarterly partner check-ins and board reports, and yearly rent limit updates, compliance certifications and stewardship reviews. Risk register suits the scheme risk log, Metrics log suits the dashboard, Board meeting suits non-profit reporting, Purchase decision suits choosing a housing provider and Meeting notes suits agency meetings. None of this is legal, tax or funding advice: confirm grant terms, tax credit rules and covenants with your solicitor, tax adviser and the funding agency itself.
