            - name: Income-based build-to-rent appraisal
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Investors value a rental scheme by dividing its net operating income by an investment yield, so the appraisal has to start from rents and running costs rather than sales values. Building an appraisal that runs from gross rent through voids, operating costs and management fees to net income, then to value and yield on cost, gives you the figure funders will check first and shows how much land you can afford.

                ## Milestones
                1. Gross rent by unit type taken from the comparable rents schedule.
                2. Voids, bad debt, operating costs and management fees deducted to reach net operating income.
                3. Net income capitalised at a yield range sourced from recent market reports.
                4. Yield on cost calculated against total development cost including finance.
                5. Residual land value shown at the target yield on cost.

                ## Notes
                Keep the yield an input with a source, not a number you choose to make the scheme work. A quarter point on the yield can move value more than a year of cost savings.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 60
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "An appraisal running from gross rent to net income, value, yield on cost and residual land value, with every assumption sourced."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "12"
              tasks:
                - "Set up the rent roll by unit type from the comparables schedule"
                - "Add voids, bad debt, operating costs and management fees as separate lines"
                - "Source a yield range from two recent market reports"
                - "Calculate yield on cost and the residual land value at your target"

# Build-to-Rent Development

Build-to-rent is development where the finished homes are let, not sold, so the scheme is designed, costed and valued around years of rent, running costs and resident turnover rather than a single sale price. This area is for founders and small developers taking on their first rental scheme or building a repeatable rental product. It starts with the foundations of a rental thesis, rent evidence, operating budgets and an income-based appraisal, then moves through the recurring machinery of market tracking and funder pipelines, the skills of reading funding agreements and modelling lease-up, the design and operator decisions, the dated moments from funder tour to stabilisation, situations for different kinds of developer and the specialist work of phased schemes and portfolio programmes.

The rhythms are a monthly rent tracker, a monthly funder pipeline update, a weekly lease-up review once letting starts, quarterly operating cost benchmarks and resident feedback, a monthly policy check and a yearly market review. Vendor suits choosing an operator, Purchase decision suits resident technology, Metrics log suits the lease-up tracker, Meeting notes suits funder visits, Feedback themes suits resident surveys and Risk register suits converting a for-sale scheme. None of this is investment, tax or legal advice: agree structures, tenancy terms and funding documents with your solicitor, accountant and a regulated adviser where your country requires one.
