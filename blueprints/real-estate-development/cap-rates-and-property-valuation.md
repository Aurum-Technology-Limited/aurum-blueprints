# Cap Rates and Property Valuation

Valuing income property comes down to a handful of methods applied honestly: capitalising a net income at a yield the market supports, checking it against what similar buildings actually sold for, testing it with a discounted cash flow and, where the building is unusual, asking what it would cost to replace. This area is for analysts who value property as part of their job and for founders who need to know what their own buildings, and the ones they want to buy, are really worth. It runs from foundations such as yield definitions, NOI and the basis of value, through the evidence routines that keep inputs current, the core valuation skills, the decisions about reconciling and challenging figures, dated valuation moments, situations for different readers, and specialist cases such as vacant buildings, the profits method and marriage value.

The rhythms are a weekly read of market research, monthly logs of sales, lettings, interest rate spreads and your own yield matrix, quarterly refreshes of published cap rate surveys, construction costs, discount rates and your own portfolio values, and a yearly lookback comparing your figures with real sale prices. The Reading queue template suits the standards and research reading, Metrics log suits the evidence logs, Purchase decision suits choosing data services and Vendor suits instructing a valuer. Nothing here is a formal valuation or investment advice: figures relied on by lenders, auditors, courts or tax authorities come from a registered valuer, and tax and legal positions from the right professional.
