            - name: Identifying the land registration system for a parcel
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Whether a parcel sits under a registered title system or an older deeds system decides what a search can prove, how long it takes and what the attorney must check. Trinidad and Tobago, for example, runs both old law deeds and certificates of title under the Real Property Ordinance, while Saint Lucia and the Cayman Islands moved to registered land decades ago. Knowing which applies to your site on day one sets the budget and the timetable for everything else.

                ## Milestones
                1. The jurisdiction, district and any parcel, volume and folio or deed reference for the site recorded.
                2. The registration system covering the parcel confirmed as registered title, deeds, or a mix across the site.
                3. The registry or office that holds the records named, with its opening hours and search fees noted.
                4. A one-paragraph note on what a search under that system can and cannot prove, agreed with your attorney.

                ## Notes
                A single development site can straddle both systems where one parcel was brought under registered title and its neighbour was not. Check each parcel separately.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge

# Caribbean Land Title Checks

Land title in the Caribbean is rarely as tidy as a single certificate: one island runs registered titles, the next still relies on chains of deeds, and almost everywhere there is family land held by heirs nobody has listed, people living on parcels they do not own, and boundaries that exist mainly in a neighbour's memory. This area is for founders and small developers who need to know, before they commit money, that a site can be bought, financed and built on. It starts with foundations such as knowing which registration system applies, appointing an attorney and a land surveyor, and walking the land, moves through the routines that protect land you already hold, the skills of reading titles, deeds and survey plans, the decisions on encroachments, co-owners, occupiers and title insurance, the dated days at the registry and at completion, situations for diaspora sellers, heirs and foreign buyers, and the specialist work of beachfront, customary and multi-owner land.

What repeats is a weekly registry chase and undertakings check during a live purchase, a monthly walk of held land and a monthly look at the official gazette, quarterly searches, boundary marker checks and pipeline reviews, and a yearly land tax payment, document custody check and panel review. The Risk register template suits the title findings register, Vendor suits the surveyor appointment, Operational checklist suits completion day and Remediation plan suits fixing a defective title. Nothing here is legal advice: title opinions, adverse possession claims and court applications belong to a licensed attorney-at-law in the jurisdiction, and boundaries to a licensed land surveyor, so each project is about asking, recording and deciding.
