# Caribbean Tourism Real Estate

Tourism property in the Caribbean is a business with its own rules: the season decides the income, airlift decides the season, a non-citizen often needs a licence to own the land, and one storm can close a villa for months. This area is for founders and small businesses buying, building or holding villas, condo-hotel units and small resorts across the region, and it starts with an investment thesis, an island comparison, foreign buyer rules, incentives, banking and the insurance baseline. It then moves through the owner's operating rhythms, the knowledge of rental pools, strata, citizenship by investment and policy wording, the decisions on managers, structures and upgrades, the dated purchases, licences, launches and claims, situations for diaspora returnees, unit buyers and local operators, and the specialist work of boutique resorts, branded residences and eco-lodges.

The rhythms are a monthly rental performance review, owner statement check, account reconciliation, compliance calendar and market watch, quarterly manager meetings and salt-air maintenance, and a yearly hurricane season readiness check, windstorm renewal, peak season inspection and incentive return. Metrics log suits the rental review, Meeting notes suits manager meetings, Operational checklist suits storm preparation, Vendor suits choosing a rental manager, Purchase decision suits furnishing and Risk register suits a first purchase. None of this is legal, tax, immigration or investment advice: confirm licences, structures, incentives and tax with a local attorney, an accountant who knows both countries and a licensed insurance broker.
