              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A joint venture gives away part of the profit and part of the control in exchange for land, money or skill you do not have. Before talking terms with anyone, compare the JV against buying the site outright, taking an option, raising debt or walking away, so you know what the partner is really worth to you and what you would accept.

                ## Milestones
                1. The scheme's shortfall in land, equity, track record or guarantees written down in one paragraph.
                2. At least three routes compared on profit kept, control kept and risk carried.
                3. A decision recorded to pursue a JV, or not, with the main reason.
                4. The minimum share of profit and control you would accept noted for later talks.

                ## Notes
                A JV is often chosen because it feels easier than raising money. Run the numbers on giving away half the profit before deciding it is cheaper than mezzanine or a smaller scheme.

# Development Joint Ventures

A development joint venture lets a landowner, a developer and a money partner do together what none could do alone, and most of them succeed or fail on what was written down before the first spade went in. This area is for founders and small developers bringing skill to a partner's land or cash, and for landowners and small builders being asked to join someone else's scheme. It starts with the foundations of deciding on a JV, checking the partner, agreeing contributions, heads of terms and the vehicle, moves through the running machinery of reporting, reserved matters, capital calls and controls, then covers the knowledge of profit shares, deadlock and exits, the decisions on land value, promote and guarantees, the dated signings and go or no-go points, situations for different partners and the specialist work of phased, programmatic and distressed JVs.

The rhythms are a weekly notice date check and partner update, monthly partner packs, bank reconciliations, waterfall updates and fee invoices, quarterly JV board meetings, capital forecasts and relationship check-ins, and yearly accounts and track record updates. Board meeting suits the JV steering committee, Metrics log suits the partner pack, Meeting notes suits the kick-off and Risk register suits a JV in trouble. None of this is legal, tax or investment advice: agree structures, clauses and tax treatment with your own solicitor and accountant.
