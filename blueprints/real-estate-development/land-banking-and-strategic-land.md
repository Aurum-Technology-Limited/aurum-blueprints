            - name: Written land banking thesis and holding horizon
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most small land banks grow by accident: a field bought because it was cheap, a plot kept because nobody decided otherwise. Writing down where you will hold land, why planning or growth should change its use, how long you can wait and what return justifies the wait turns a collection of parcels into a strategy you can test every year.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page thesis naming the target districts, the expected trigger for value (allocation, new road, station, settlement boundary review) and the use you expect.
                2. A maximum holding horizon in years and the point at which you would sell regardless.
                3. A minimum required return on the whole hold, including holding costs, agreed with your partners.
                4. Three rules for what you will not buy, such as land in a protected landscape or with no realistic access.

                ## Notes
                Keep the thesis short enough to read before every purchase decision. If a parcel cannot be explained by it, that is a reason to pause, not to rewrite the thesis around the parcel.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A signed-off one-page land banking thesis with target districts, value triggers, a maximum holding horizon and three exclusion rules."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "6"
              tasks:
                - "Write one paragraph on why you want to hold land rather than build on it now"
                - "List the districts you know well enough to judge growth in"
                - "Set a maximum holding horizon and the sale-regardless point in years"
                - "Write three rules for land you will not buy and share them with partners"
            - name: Patient capital plan for a long land hold
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Strategic land rarely produces income, so the danger is not a bad site but being forced to sell a good one two years before it is allocated. A capital plan that sets out how much equity is committed, how long it can stay tied up and how holding costs will be paid gives you staying power and stops lenders or partners setting your exit date for you.

                ## Milestones
                1. Total equity available for land, split between purchases, promotion spend and a holding cost reserve.
                2. A holding cost reserve sized to cover at least the planned horizon, with the assumption written down.
                3. Any borrowing secured on land listed with its term, review date and what happens if it is not renewed.
                4. A written rule for the maximum share of the business's capital that may sit in non-income land.

                ## Notes
                Talk to your accountant before committing. Land that earns nothing can still create tax, rates and insurance bills every year, and those come out of cash, not out of value.
              priority: high

              frontmatter:

# Land Banking and Strategic Land

Strategic land is land bought or held today because of what planning policy and growth might allow in five, ten or fifteen years, and it rewards patience, records and a clear view of when to let go. This area is for founders and small development businesses building or running a land bank, and for business owners and families sitting on land with hope value. It starts with the thesis, capital plan, advisers, scorecard, register and risk register, then builds the machinery of plan watches, holding cost ledgers, inspections and hold or sell reviews, the knowledge of local plans, hope value, contributions and long-hold returns, the promotion decisions from call for sites to vision documents and land assembly, the dated moments of examination hearings, allocation and sale, the situations of family, inherited, surplus and Caribbean land, and the specialist work of outside capital, new settlements and phased disposals.

The rhythms are monthly local plan and infrastructure watches, a monthly holding cost entry, quarterly site inspections, value evidence, neighbour contact and portfolio reviews, and yearly renewals, licence checks and a hold or sell decision on every parcel. Risk register pairs with the land bank risks, Metrics log with the holding cost ledger, Meeting notes with the quarterly review and Marketing campaign with the sale of an allocated site. None of this is planning, legal, tax or investment advice: every structure, representation and sale goes past the right professional.
