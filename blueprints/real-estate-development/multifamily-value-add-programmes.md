              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most value-add programmes drift because the plan lived in the acquisition model and nobody turned it into a list of works, rents and dates. Writing a short business plan for one building, naming which units get which scope, the target rent after works, the expense lines to attack and the month the building should be stabilised, gives the site team, the lender and your partners one document to work from.

                ## Milestones
                1. A two to four page plan covering units, amenities, other income, expenses and timing for one named building.
                2. Target post-renovation rents stated per unit type with the evidence behind each number.
                3. A stabilisation month named, with occupancy and net operating income targets for it.
                4. The plan shared with every partner and lender who has approval rights over capital spending.

                ## Notes
                Keep the plan short enough to reread every quarter. A forty-page plan is the acquisition memo again and nobody updates it.

# Multifamily Value-Add Programmes

Value-add is the work that starts after the keys change hands: renovating units as they turn, upgrading the amenities residents will pay for, bringing rents towards market within the law, cutting the costs the last owner ignored and proving each spend earned its keep. This area is for founders and small operators running that programme on one building or a handful. It begins with the foundations of a per-building plan, unit inventory, rent gap, legal briefing, budget and tracking ledger, then moves through the operating rhythms, the skills of reading scopes and return arithmetic, the renovation, amenity and expense decisions, the dated milestones to stabilisation, situations such as occupied renovations and rent-regulated buildings, and the specialist work of unit mix changes and multi-building programmes.

The rhythms are a weekly turn and renovation meeting, a monthly value-add scorecard, monthly checks on renovated-unit leasing, utility use, materials stock and renewal notices, quarterly reviews of contractors, expense lines and resident feedback, and yearly retenders and rent surveys. Metrics log suits the spend ledger, Meeting notes the weekly pipeline meeting, Vendor the contractor review, Feedback themes the resident pulse, Purchase decision the access technology choice and Risk register the older building plan. None of this is legal, tax or investment advice: confirm rent increase rules, notice periods and tenant protections with a local solicitor or housing adviser before acting.
