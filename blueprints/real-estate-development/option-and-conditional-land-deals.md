              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Founders often take whichever structure the landowner's agent proposes, without knowing what each one costs them up front, who carries the planning risk and who gets the uplift. Comparing the three main structures on one page, against your own cash position and appetite for planning risk, gives you a default to open negotiations with instead of a reaction to someone else's draft.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page comparison of option, conditional contract and promotion agreement covering up-front cost, planning risk, control of the application and share of uplift.
                2. Two real or past sites mapped to the structure that would have suited each, with a reason.
                3. A default opening structure chosen for sites of the size you usually pursue.
                4. The comparison checked with a land solicitor for anything missing.

                ## Notes
                A conditional contract usually fixes the price now and the landowner is bound once conditions are met. An option leaves you free to walk away. A promotion agreement shares the sale proceeds. The detail varies by jurisdiction, so treat the comparison as a working map and confirm it with your solicitor.

# Option and Conditional Land Deals

Option and conditional land deals let a developer control a site without buying it outright until planning, finance or other conditions are met, and they are where small developers either protect their capital or lose it on a missed date. This area is for founders and small development businesses structuring these deals on their own sites. It starts with choosing between an option, a conditional contract and a promotion agreement, appointing the right solicitor and protecting the deal on title, then builds the machinery that tracks fees, notices and long-stop dates, the knowledge of price mechanisms, notices and overage, the decisions on extending, renegotiating, exercising or walking away, the dated moments from signing day to completion, situations with family and farming landowners or Caribbean agreements for sale, and the specialist work of multi-owner equalisation, phased exercises and expert determination.

The rhythms are a weekly review of key dates across every live deal, monthly checks on option expiries, payment dates, contractual duties and recoverable spend, a monthly continue or drop meeting, quarterly landowner updates, market value evidence and title register checks, and a yearly lookback at what each deal cost and returned. The Meeting notes template suits the pipeline meeting and landowner presentations, Risk register suits the final run to a long-stop date, Operational checklist suits signing day and Board meeting suits the decision to buy unconditionally. None of this is legal or tax advice: every agreement, notice and tax position goes past your solicitor and tax adviser.
