            - name: What practical completion means in your build contract
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Practical completion is a contract term, not a feeling on site, and the standard forms define it differently or leave it to the certifier's opinion. Before the last month on site, read the clauses that say who certifies it, what must be finished, which documents are preconditions and what happens to damages, insurance and retention on that date. Knowing this turns completion arguments into a checklist.

                ## Milestones
                1. The completion clause, the certifier and any listed preconditions copied into a one-page note.
                2. Every contract consequence of the completion date listed: damages stop, retention halves, insurance passes, defects period starts.
                3. Any employer's requirements or specification items tied to completion, such as manuals or test results, identified.
                4. Questions you could not answer from the documents put to your contract administrator or solicitor.

                ## Notes
                Bespoke amendments often add preconditions, such as the health and safety file or warranty certificates. Read the amendments schedule, not just the standard form.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A one-page note quoting the contract's completion definition, certifier, preconditions and every consequence of the completion date, checked by the contract administrator."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:
                - "Find the signed building contract and its schedule of amendments"
                - "Copy the completion definition and the name of the certifier into a note"
                - "List every clause that changes on the completion date"
                - "Send your open questions to the contract administrator"

# Practical Completion and Handover

Practical completion is the day a building stops being the contractor's site and becomes your asset, your insurance risk and your buyers' or tenants' home, and most of the money and goodwill lost on a scheme leaks out in the few months either side of it. This area is for founders and small developers taking their own schemes over the line, and for builders completing their first own-account projects. It starts with the foundations of what the contract says and what documents you need, moves through the weekly machinery of snags, certificates and defects, then covers the skills of snagging and reading certificates, the decisions about accepting completion, the dated inspections and handover meetings, situations such as forward-funded schemes or a contractor failing near the end, and the specialist work of collateral warranties, latent defects and digital handover.

The rhythms are a weekly snag walk and tracker export, a weekly certificate chase and defects triage, weekly checks of empty units, a monthly defects meeting with the contractor, a monthly retention diary and maintenance check, and quarterly reviews of warranty expiries and seasonal plant performance. Operational checklist suits the inspections and empty unit rounds, Meeting notes the defects meetings, Purchase decision the choice of snagging inspector, Risk register a contractor insolvency and Metrics log a defects dashboard. None of this is legal or insurance advice: check notices, certificates and cover with your solicitor, contract administrator and broker.
