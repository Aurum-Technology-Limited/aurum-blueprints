                ## Purpose
                Pooling money with a few people lets you buy a better building than you could alone, but it also means shared control, shared mistakes and money you cannot easily get back. Before any partner conversation, compare a club against buying something smaller on your own, investing passively in someone else's deal or waiting, so you go in knowing what you are trading.

                ## Milestones
                1. The property you could buy alone and the one you could buy in a club written side by side.
                2. Control, liquidity, effort and relationship risk scored for each route.
                3. A decision recorded to pursue a club deal, or not, with the main reason.
                4. The two things that would make you walk away from any club written down.

                ## Notes
                Most club deals that go wrong do so between the people, not in the building. Weigh the friendship as an asset you could lose.

            - name: Personal commitment limit for club deals
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Club equity is usually locked up for five to ten years and may be called on again if the building needs a new roof or the loan has to be paid down. Setting a ceiling on what you put into any one club, and into clubs in total, protects your household and your business from one partner's bad year becoming yours.

                ## Milestones
                1. Money you could lock away for at least seven years worked out from your own budget.
                2. A per-deal ceiling and a total ceiling across all club deals written down.
                3. A separate buffer for possible capital calls of at least a fifth of each commitment set aside.
                4. The limits reviewed with your accountant or financial planner.

                ## Notes
                Count possible capital calls and any personal guarantee as part of the commitment, not as something extra that might never happen.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14

            - name: Choosing the club partners
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The right club is small enough to agree and large enough to fund the deal, usually somewhere between three and eight people. Choosing partners on money alone ignores what matters later: whether they can meet a capital call, keep confidences, make a decision in a week and accept a vote that goes against them.

                ## Milestones
                1. A long list of possible partners with what each could bring: cash, skills, contacts or time.
                2. Each candidate checked against the same five questions on capacity, temperament and horizon.
                3. A shortlist chosen with a target total equity that covers the deal and a reserve.
                4. Anyone left out told kindly and in person.

                ## Notes
                A partner who needs the money back in two years will force a sale at the wrong time. Ask about time horizon directly.

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The same building can be held through a limited company, a limited liability company, a limited partnership or direct co-ownership, and each choice changes liability, tax, lender appetite and how easily a partner can leave.                 ## Milestones
                1. At least three vehicle options listed for the jurisdiction where the property sits.

                3. Written advice obtained from a property lawyer and an accountant.

                ## Notes

# Real Estate Club Deals

A club deal is a handful of people, often friends, relatives, colleagues or fellow founders, putting their own money together to buy one property they could not buy alone. This area is for the founder or small business owner who either leads that group or joins one, and it starts with the foundations of deciding whether to club at all, setting a personal limit, choosing partners and the vehicle, then moves through the running machinery of packs, meetings, ledgers and distributions, the knowledge every partner needs, the decisions on voting, capital calls, guarantees and exit, the dated moments from bid to wind-up, the family, overseas and partner-exit situations, and the specialist work of repeat clubs and disputes.

What repeats is a monthly financial pack and capital account update, a monthly look at the property manager's report and the key dates calendar, quarterly partner meetings, distributions and reserve checks, and a yearly round of accounts, insurance, the hold or sell review and each partner's own estate and residency check. Meeting notes suits the first expectations workshop, Metrics log suits the monthly pack, Board meeting suits the quarterly partner meeting and Risk register suits a dispute. None of this is legal, tax or investment advice: agree the vehicle, the agreement and the tax treatment with your own lawyer and accountant.
