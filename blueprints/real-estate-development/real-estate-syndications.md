              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Investors back a sponsor who can say in two sentences what they buy and why, and a vague strategy is the first reason a raise stalls. A written buy box fixes the asset type, markets, deal size, business plan and the return range you will underwrite to, so every deal you bring is recognisably yours. Write it before you look at another offering memorandum.

                ## Milestones

                ## Notes

            - name: Offering exemption map for your jurisdiction
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Raising money from passive investors is selling securities almost everywhere, and each country offers a few private placement exemptions with different rules on who may invest, how you may advertise and what you must file. In the US these include Regulation D Rule 506(b) and 506(c); other countries have their own private placement or sophisticated investor regimes. Mapping the options with counsel before you market anything decides how the whole raise is run.

                ## Milestones
                1. The exemptions counsel says are realistic for you listed with their investor and advertising limits.
                2. Filing and notice duties for each written beside it, including any state or provincial notices.
                3. The exemption chosen for the first offering, with counsel's reasoning recorded.
                4. A one-paragraph summary of what that choice means for your marketing, shared with your team.

                ## Notes
                This is a map for conversations with counsel, not a legal conclusion. Rules change, so date the map and refresh it before each new offering.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:

                output_kind: knowledge

# Real Estate Syndications

A syndication is a deal where you, as sponsor, find and run the property and a group of passive investors put up most of the equity under a securities offering. This area is for founders and small operators sponsoring their own deals. It starts with the foundations of a buy box, counsel, the exemption you will raise under, the entity structure and your own co-investment, moves through the operating machinery of deal screening, the raise tracker and the compliance calendar, then covers the skills of reading offering documents and waterfalls, the decisions on terms and structure, the dated moments from launch to final sale, situations for different kinds of sponsor and the specialist work of funds, recapitalisations and succession.

The rhythms are a weekly deal screen and a weekly investor pipeline review during a raise, a weekly log of new investor relationships, monthly checks of marketing material, the asset against its offering plan and market terms, quarterly reviews of conflicts, sponsor fees and guarantees, and a yearly review of the whole sponsor business. Vendor suits appointing counsel, Compliance control suits the filing calendar, Metrics log suits the raise tracker, Purchase decision suits choosing a fund administrator, Meeting notes suits the launch webinar and Risk register suits an undersubscribed raise. None of this is legal, securities, tax or investment advice: agree every structure and document with securities counsel and an accountant in each country involved.
