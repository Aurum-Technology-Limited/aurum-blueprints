            - name: Choosing between LEED, BREEAM and EDGE for the scheme
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Each rating system suits a different market: buyers and tenants in some countries expect LEED, others know BREEAM, and EDGE is designed to be quicker and cheaper for housing and emerging markets. Picking the one your end buyers, tenants and lenders actually recognise, before the design is fixed, avoids paying for a badge nobody in your market values.

                ## Milestones
                1. The rating systems your target tenants, buyers and lenders ask for identified from at least five conversations or listings.
                2. LEED, BREEAM and EDGE compared on fees, assessor model, evidence load and recognition in your country.
                3. A preferred system chosen, with the reason written in two sentences.
                4. The choice recorded in the scheme's project brief.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Purchase decision** template. Ask two local agents which ratings appear in the leasing and sales particulars they see most often; that answer usually settles it.

                Before you start: this plan is general information to help you organise your own affairs. It is not legal advice. Rules differ between countries and change often, so check decisions that matter with a qualified lawyer or the official authority in the country concerned.

# Sustainable Building Certification

Sustainable building certification is the work of choosing a rating such as LEED, BREEAM or EDGE for your scheme, committing to a target early enough to design for it, and carrying the evidence all the way to an awarded certificate that buyers, tenants and lenders will pay attention to. It is written for founders and small developers who are paying for the rating themselves and need it to earn its cost. The projects run from the first decisions on system, target, budget and assessor, through the tracking machinery that keeps credits alive during design and construction, the knowledge you need to talk to energy modellers and commissioning agents, the design choices that win or lose points, the dated submissions, situations such as a first scheme, a refurbishment or a tropical site, and finally the specialist work on value evidence, net zero and portfolio reporting.

What repeats is a fortnightly credit tracker meeting, monthly evidence requests to the design team and contractor, monthly waste and submittal checks on site, a quarterly risk review, and yearly metering and recertification reviews once the building is occupied. Purchase decision suits the rating system choice, Vendor suits the assessor appointment, Risk register suits credit risk, Meeting notes suits the tracker meeting and Metrics log suits post-occupancy energy and water. Rating rules change between versions, so check the current manual of your chosen scheme before relying on any credit, and take legal, valuation and tax questions to the right professional.
