            - name: Honest stocktake of each sibling relationship
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most adults could not say when they last spoke to each sibling one to one, rather than in a family group or through a parent. Writing down, for each brother or sister, how often you are in touch, who starts it, how it feels and what you would like it to be gives you a true starting point instead of a vague guilt. Ten minutes per sibling is enough, and it shows which relationship needs attention first.

                ## Milestones
                1. One page per sibling with last one-to-one contact, usual frequency and who usually starts it.
                2. Each relationship scored from one to five for warmth and for ease, with a sentence on why.
                3. The relationship you most want to change marked, and the one that is fine as it is.
                4. One realistic hope for the next twelve months written beside each name.

                ## Notes
                Include half-siblings, step-siblings and siblings you rarely see. Leaving someone off the list is itself information worth noticing.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14

            - name: Asking each sibling what closeness means to them
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Closeness means a daily meme to one sibling and a long call at Christmas to another, and most friction between grown siblings comes from guessing. Asking each brother or sister directly how much contact they would like, and in what form, replaces silent scorekeeping with something you both agreed. It feels awkward for about thirty seconds and saves years of misread silences.

                ## Milestones
                1. A short, light question ready that you are comfortable asking each sibling.
                2. Each sibling asked, in person, on a call or by message, how they like to stay in touch.
                3. Their answers written down next to your own preference.
                4. Any big gap between what you want and what they want noted for the contact plan.

                ## Notes
                Ask in a calm moment, not straight after a disappointment. A light framing works: you are trying to get better at keeping in touch and want to know what suits them.

# Adult Sibling Relationships

This area is for anyone with brothers or sisters who wants the relationship to be chosen as adults rather than left on the settings it had at fourteen. It starts with the foundations (an honest stocktake, a family sheet, asking each sibling what closeness means, contact rhythms and an inventory of old grievances), then the routines that keep siblings in each other's lives, the skills of speaking as adults and hearing different memories of the same childhood, decisions about repair, money, partners and fairness, the occasions worth sharing, versions for siblings abroad, half and step siblings, twins and families with ageing parents, and finally the hard cases: addiction, estrangement, a newly discovered sibling and grief.

What repeats is a weekly one-to-one call rotation and a midweek photo or voice note, a monthly siblings' video call, monthly notes on each sibling's life and time with nieces and nephews, a quarterly sibling-only plan and relationship review, and each year a get-together hosted in turn, a hosting rotation for the wider family and a refreshed family sheet. The Person, Trip and Meeting notes templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
