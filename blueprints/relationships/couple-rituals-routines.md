              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most couples already have rituals they never named: the way Saturday breakfast happens, the text sent at lunch, the film on a sick day. Writing down what you already do, and how each of you feels about it, shows what to protect before adding anything new. It takes one relaxed evening and stops you building on top of habits one partner quietly dislikes.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of at least ten things you already do together daily, weekly or yearly.
                2. Each item marked by both partners as treasured, fine or tired.
                3. The two or three rituals you both treasure circled as ones to protect.
                4. Any ritual one partner finds a chore noted for a later conversation.

                ## Notes
                Count the small stuff. A shared crossword or the same takeaway every Friday counts as a ritual if it happens without anyone planning it.

            - name: Ten-minute morning coffee together
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Ten minutes sitting down together before the day starts, with coffee or tea and no phones, is one of the most reliable daily rituals because it attaches to something you already do. It gives each of you a sense of the other's day before it happens, which makes the evening reunion easier. For couples with different start times it may mean one of you sits with a cup while the other gets ready.

                ## Milestones
                1. A time agreed that works on weekday mornings for both of you.
                2. Cups, kettle or machine set up so the ritual needs no planning.
                3. Coffee taken together on at least five mornings a week for four weeks.
                4. A fallback agreed for early meetings, such as a longer weekend version.

                ## Notes

# Couple Rituals & Shared Traditions

This area is for couples who want the small repeated things, the morning coffee, the walk after dinner, the trip that happens every year, to be chosen rather than accidental. It starts with the foundations (a map of the rituals you already share, the traditions you each grew up with and a first three to try), then the weekly and seasonal routines that keep them going, the skills and decisions that keep rituals alive and honest, the occasions worth marking, versions for new parents, shift workers, blended families, intercultural couples, illness and retirement, and finally the long-running traditions an established couple builds to last decades.

What repeats is a daily coffee, a few fixed evenings for walks, cooking and winding down, a slow Sunday, a monthly outing and photo pick, a quarterly ritual check and seasonal tradition, and a handful of yearly bookings for the trip, the look-back and the volunteering day.
