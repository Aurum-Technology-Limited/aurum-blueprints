              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Couples who start planning side by side tend to agree too fast, with the quieter partner adopting the louder one's picture and discovering the mismatch years later. Writing your own ten-year vision alone first, covering where you live, how you work, family, friends, health and adventures, means both of you arrive with something to say. It takes an evening each and makes every later conversation more honest.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each partner has written a private ten-year picture covering home, work, family, money, friends and adventures.
                2. Each picture includes at least three things that would make the decade feel well spent.
                3. Each partner has marked the parts they feel unsure or shy about saying aloud.
                4. Both documents are finished before any joint planning conversation starts.

                ## Notes
                Write in the present tense as if it is ten years from now: where you wake up, what a Tuesday looks like, who is at the table on Sunday. Vague hopes become concrete when you describe an ordinary day.

            - name: Overlap and clash map of your two visions
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                With both solo visions written, the useful work is sorting them: what you already share, what one of you wants and the other has never thought about, and what genuinely conflicts. Most couples find the overlap is larger than they feared and the real clashes fit on one page. Naming them early stops a single disagreement, such as city versus countryside, colouring everything else.

                ## Milestones
                1. Both solo visions read aloud to each other without interruption or debate.
                2. Every item sorted into three columns: shared, one-sided, in conflict.
                3. The conflict column limited to the items that truly cannot both happen.
                4. An agreed order for tackling the conflict items, hardest last.

                ## Notes
                Read, do not rebut. The first session is for hearing each other; negotiation comes later and goes better when nobody felt ambushed.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A three-column map of shared, one-sided and conflicting vision items, with the conflict items ranked in the order you will discuss them."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "Book a two-hour slot at home with phones in another room"
                - "Read your solo visions aloud to each other, one at a time"
                - "Sort every item into shared, one-sided or in conflict"
                - "Rank the conflict items and agree which one to discuss first"
            - name: Non-negotiables and open questions for each partner
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every person carries a few things they will not trade: living within reach of an ageing parent, a career they trained a decade for, never living in a big city. Writing yours down, alongside the questions you are genuinely open on, tells your partner where the room to move is. Without this list, couples negotiate hardest over things that do not matter and give way on the ones that do.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each partner has a list of no more than five non-negotiables, each with the reason behind it.
                2. Each partner has a list of things they are genuinely flexible about.
                3. Both lists exchanged and any surprises talked through.
                4. Any non-negotiable that directly contradicts the other partner's flagged for a separate conversation.

                ## Notes
                If your list runs past five items, some of them are preferences. Keep the short list honest and the flexible list generous.

            - name: Five-sentence shared values statement
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Values are what you fall back on when a decision is new and the plan does not cover it, such as whether to take the higher salary or the shorter commute. A statement of five sentences, written together, gives you both a test to hold any opportunity against. Short enough to remember is the point: a page of values gets read once.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each partner has picked their top five values from a long list, separately.

                4. The statement placed somewhere you will both see it, such as the front of the vision document.

                ## Notes
                Write behaviours, not virtues: 'We choose time with people over extra money once the bills are covered' is usable; 'We value family' is not.

            - name: Ground rules for deciding big joint choices
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Big choices arrive at bad moments: a job offer with a 48-hour reply window, a house going to sealed bids, a parent's sudden illness. Couples who agreed in advance how they decide, who gets a veto, how long either can ask for, what happens when you are split, make those choices faster and with less damage. Agree the rules while nothing is at stake.

                ## Milestones
                1. A shared definition of what counts as a big joint decision, such as anything over a set amount or a move of more than an hour.
                2. An agreed minimum thinking time before either partner says yes to something big.
                3. A rule for what happens when you disagree, such as more time, a trusted third view, or the person most affected leads.
                4. The rules written in five lines or fewer and added to the vision document.

                ## Notes
                A common rule that works: either partner can ask for 48 hours on any big decision, and neither commits to an outside party until both have said yes.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "Written decision rules covering what counts as big, minimum thinking time and the tie-break method, agreed by both and stored with the vision."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "List three big decisions you made together and how each was actually made"
                - "Agree what amount, distance or commitment makes a decision big"
                - "Choose a minimum thinking time and a tie-break method"
                - "Write the rules in five lines and save them with the vision"

            - name: One-page shared life vision document
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Everything so far becomes useful when it fits on a single page you can both find in under a minute. One page forces choices: the values, a short description of the life you are building, one line each for where, work, family and adventure, and the decision rules. Couples who keep it to a page actually reread it; those with a twenty-page plan rarely do.

                4. A printed copy kept somewhere you will see it, such as inside a kitchen cupboard door.

                ## Notes

# Couple's Shared Life Vision

This area is for couples who want to build a life on purpose rather than drift into whatever the next lease, job offer or family event decides for them. It starts with the foundations (separate solo visions, the overlap and clash map, non-negotiables, a values statement, decision rules and a one-page vision), then the routines that keep it alive, the skills of dreaming and negotiating well together, the big decisions about place, careers, children and retirement, the moments worth marking, versions for parents, blended families, dual-career, cross-cultural and later-life couples, and finally the advanced work of stress-testing and extending the vision.

What repeats is a Sunday small step, a vision conversation on the 14th, a fund transfer check on the 3rd, an adventure list look on the 22nd and a decisions log on the 28th, quarterly scorecards and career syncs, and yearly rewrites, where-we-live checks and scenario reruns. The Meeting notes, Savings goal, Development plan, Trip and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them.
