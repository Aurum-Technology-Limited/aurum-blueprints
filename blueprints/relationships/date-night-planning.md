            - name: Agreeing your date night rhythm
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Couples who promise to go out more often rarely do, because a vague intention loses to every tired Friday. Agreeing a specific rhythm, such as one evening out a fortnight and one at-home date in between, turns romance from a wish into a fixture you can both defend. Settle it now, before the next busy month fills the gaps.

                ## Milestones
                1. A target frequency agreed, for example fortnightly out and weekly in.
                2. A default evening chosen that suits both work patterns and any childcare.
                3. A rule agreed for who plans each date.
                4. The rhythm written down where both of you will see it.

                ## Notes
                Start smaller than you think. A rhythm you keep for six months beats an ambitious one that collapses in three weeks.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A written date night rhythm stating frequency, default evening and who plans, agreed by both partners."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "1"
              tasks:
                - "Ask your partner for twenty minutes this week to talk about date nights"
                - "Compare how often each of you would ideally like a date"
                - "Pick a default evening that works with both diaries"
                - "Write the agreed rhythm on the shared calendar or the fridge"

# Date Night Planning

This area is for couples who want date nights to happen on purpose rather than by accident, including parents juggling sitters, bedtimes and tight budgets. It starts with the foundations (an agreed rhythm, a budget, a sitter shortlist and an idea bank), then the routines that keep dates booked and paid for, the skills that make the evenings better, the decisions that cut cost and break ruts, the occasions worth planning early, versions for new parents, shift workers, carers and blended families, and finally the more ambitious dates an experienced planner enjoys.

What repeats is a Sunday planning check, a Thursday at-home date, sitter bookings on the 20th, a spending check on the 28th, a quarterly review of how the dates are going and a few monthly prompts for ideas, tickets and deals. The Monthly budget and bills, Habit tracker, Purchase decision, Trip and Savings goal templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
