            - name: Honest readiness check before dating again
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Dating soon after a separation can turn into a search for comfort, reassurance or a replacement co-parent, and children feel the fallout when it ends quickly. A short written check on where you stand emotionally, practically and with your ex tells you whether to start now, start slowly or wait three months and look again.

                ## Milestones
                1. Answers written to five questions: why now, what you hope for, how settled the children are, how civil things are with your ex, and how much spare time you really have.
                2. One trusted friend asked for an honest read on those answers.
                3. A decision recorded: start now, start slowly, or revisit on a set date.
                4. If waiting, a date in the calendar to ask the five questions again.

                ## Notes
                Loneliness is a perfectly good reason to want company. It is a poor reason to rush children into meeting anyone.

# Dating as a Single Parent

This area is for single parents who want to date again without the children paying the price, whether you share care week on, week off, parent alone or are raising teenagers who notice everything. It starts with the foundations (readiness, priorities, a custody calendar, sitters, privacy and first-date safety), then the routines that keep dating in its place, the skills of talking to children and spotting risk, the big decisions about introductions and overnight stays, the first meetings and occasions, versions for different family set-ups, and finally the slower work of blending two families.

What repeats is a Sunday choice for the week's dating slot, sitter bookings on the 18th, a family time check on the 5th, a spending check on the 26th, weekly notes on how the children are coping, phone-free evenings and a quarterly review of dating and family balance.
