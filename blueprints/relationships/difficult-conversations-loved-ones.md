                ## Purpose
                Most people are carrying two or three conversations they keep meaning to have: a hurt never mentioned, a money worry, a question about the future.             - name: Time, place and medium for a hard talk
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The same words land differently at eleven at night after a long day than on a Saturday walk with nowhere to be. Agreeing a time when neither of you is rushed, hungry or about to leave, in a place with privacy, and choosing face to face over text for anything emotional removes half the reasons hard talks go wrong.

                ## Milestones
                1. A time chosen with at least an hour free afterwards.
                2. A private setting picked where neither of you will be overheard or interrupted.
                3. The other person told in advance that you want to talk about something that matters.
                4. Text and email ruled out for the main conversation unless distance makes them necessary.

                ## Notes
                A side by side setting such as a walk can make eye contact less intense for nervous talkers. Avoid the car if either of you might want to leave.

                ## Purpose
                The first thirty seconds set the tone, and many hard talks fail at the opening: a joke that undercuts it, a long preamble that alarms, or an accusation dressed up as a question.                 ## Notes

            - name: Sounding board for before and after
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Hard conversations are easier with someone in your corner who is not involved: a friend, sibling or counsellor who will hear your plan beforehand and your account afterwards.                 ## Milestones

                ## Notes

# Difficult Conversations With Loved Ones

This area is for anyone with something hard to say to a partner or close friend, about hurt, needs, money, health or the future. It starts with the foundations (listing what you are avoiding, choosing the first conversation, separating facts from stories, naming your aim and building a prep sheet), then the routines that stop things going unsaid, the skills of clear requests, steady listening and kind bad news, the specific conversations people most often put off, dated events, variants for parents, couples apart, older couples and friends who share a home, and finally the specialist work of facilitated sessions, letters and coaching others.

What repeats is a monthly sweep of things left unsaid, a weekly check against the seven-day rule, a monthly look at the agreements log and the themes page, a quarterly review of postponed conversations and a yearly swap of big unspoken topics with your partner. The Meeting notes, Feedback themes and Reading queue templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
