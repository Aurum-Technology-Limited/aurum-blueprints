                ## Purpose
                Before the next few dates pull you along, it helps to know whether you are looking for something casual, something serious or simply finding out. Writing two or three honest sentences about what you want this year, and what you do not, gives you something to check each new connection against when chemistry makes everything feel urgent.

                ## Milestones
                1. A short written statement of what you are looking for over the next twelve months.
                2. A note of what you are not looking for, such as a rebound or a weekend-only arrangement.
                3. A rough sense of timing, for example whether you want to be living with someone within three years.
                4. The statement reread and still true after one week.

                ## Notes
                Wanting something casual is a perfectly good answer. The point is to know it, so you can say it early and kindly.

                Going to someone's home, or inviting them to yours, for the first time is the point where a public-place first date stops protecting you. A simple routine of telling a friend where you are, keeping your own way home and having an agreed check-in text costs nothing and matters most with someone you have only known for weeks.

# Early Dating & New Relationships

This area covers the first months with someone new, from the third date to the point where you both know what this is, for anyone who wants to enjoy a new relationship without losing their head, their friends or their standards. It starts with foundations (what you want, your dealbreakers, a pace, safety and sexual health), then the light weekly and monthly machinery that keeps you clear-eyed, the skills of saying what you want and reading what you are shown, the decisions about exclusivity, apps, labels and compatibility, the first meetings with friends and family, situations such as dating a colleague, dating someone with children or dating with a health condition, and finally retrospectives and a first-year agreement.

What repeats is a short note after dates and a weekly look at time together and apart, a protected evening for your own life, a monthly review of how things feel alongside a flags log and a spending check, a monthly catch-up with a trusted friend, and a yearly sexual health check and retrospective.
