            - name: Talking about the empty house before the last child leaves
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Couples who wait until the car is unpacked at the hall of residence or the new flat to talk about life after the children often find each partner has been imagining a very different year. One unhurried conversation in the months before the last child leaves, covering hopes, worries and what each of you expects to change, turns a shock into a plan you both recognise.

                ## Milestones
                1. A date set for the conversation, at least four weeks before the last child moves out.
                2. Each partner's three hopes and three worries for the first year written down separately beforehand.
                3. The two lists compared, with the overlaps and the surprises noted.
                4. Two or three things agreed to try in the first month after the move.

                ## Notes
                Keep it away from the packing and the goodbyes. A walk or a quiet meal out works better than the kitchen table where the family logistics have always been sorted.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building

            - name: Honest stocktake of the relationship after the parenting years
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Two decades of school runs, homework and taxi duty can leave a relationship running on logistics, with little sense of how either partner actually feels about it. A private stocktake, each of you rating the same eight parts of your life together and then comparing answers, shows what is strong, what has gone quiet and where to put effort first.

                ## Milestones
                1. Eight shared areas chosen, such as conversation, affection, fun, friends, home, money, health and plans.
                2. Each area scored from one to ten by both partners separately.
                3. The scores compared, with the two largest gaps talked through calmly.
                4. One strength to protect and one area to work on chosen together.

                ## Notes
                Score privately before comparing so neither answer bends towards the other. A gap of three points or more is a conversation, not a verdict.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "Both partners have scored the same eight areas and recorded one strength to protect and one area to improve."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Agree the eight areas of your life together that you will both score"
                - "Score each area from one to ten on your own, without conferring"
                - "Compare scores and talk through the two biggest gaps"
                - "Write down one strength to protect and one area to work on"
                - "Repeat the stocktake on the anniversary of the last child leaving @recurring(yearly)"

            - name: Naming what each of you misses about the busy years
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Losing the daily role of parent is a real loss, and partners rarely feel it at the same time or in the same way: one may be relieved while the other is quietly bereft. Naming what each of you misses, out loud and without trying to fix it, stops the difference being read as coldness on one side or overreaction on the other.

                ## Milestones

                ## Notes

# Empty Nest Couple Reconnection

This area is for parents and new retirees whose house has gone quiet and who want the next chapter to be about the two of them, not an echo of the family years. It starts with foundations for the first months (the conversation before the last child leaves, an honest stocktake and a new weekly shape for two), then moves through shared routines, skills to learn together, decisions about the house and the money the children used to cost, events that mark the change, situations such as retirement, grandchildren and a child moving back, and finally bigger ventures like a sabbatical or a trial move.

The repeating rhythms are a weekly walk, two cooked dinners and two shared workouts a week, monthly first-time outings, a book club and meet-ups with other couples, a quarterly empty nest review and a yearly adventure planning evening. The Weekly meal plan, Meeting notes, Habit tracker, Reading queue, Savings goal and Trip templates pair with the projects that need them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
