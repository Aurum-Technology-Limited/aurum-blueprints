              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most couples argue about the five chores they can see, while dozens of smaller jobs, like renewing the car insurance, noticing the shampoo is low or booking the boiler service, go uncounted. Listing every recurring task in the house over two weeks, including the thinking ones, gives you one shared picture to divide instead of two different memories. It is the starting point for every other project here.

                ## Milestones
                1. A shared list of at least 80 household and life admin tasks, grouped into rooms, children, money admin, family, food and outside jobs.
                2. Each task marked with how often it comes round: daily, weekly, monthly, seasonal or yearly.
                3. Invisible tasks, such as remembering, noticing and booking, listed as tasks in their own right.
                4. Both partners have added to the list and agree nothing big is missing.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Household chores** template. Walk round the house room by room and scroll back through a month of messages and receipts; both turn up jobs nobody thinks of as jobs.

            - name: Deciding what fair means in your household
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Fair does not have to mean fifty-fifty. Some couples aim for equal free time, others split by hours of paid work, others by what each person minds least. Choosing your own definition, out loud, gives every later conversation a yardstick and stops each of you measuring against a different one.

                ## Milestones

                4. An agreement on what happens to the definition if circumstances shift, such as a new job or a baby.

                ## Notes

# Fair Division of Household Labour

This area is for couples who want the work of running a home shared in a way both of you think is fair, with or without children. It starts with the foundations (a full task inventory, a time log, a map of the mental load, your own definition of fair and a first written split), then the weekly and monthly routines that keep it working, skills such as handing over a job properly, decisions about cleaners and time-saving kit, plans for a new baby, holidays and recovery periods, versions for parents, shift workers, carers and remote workers, and finally the long-run measures experienced couples use.

What repeats is a Sunday planning meeting, a ten-minute evening reset, a Wednesday sort of the shared list, a Thursday school admin sweep, a Friday noticing walk and a Saturday food plan, then a fairness check-in on the 14th, an admin hour on the 20th, renewals on the 5th, family occasions on the 25th and a swap of the unloved jobs on the 28th, with quarterly deep-clean lists and time samples and a yearly review of the whole split. The Household chores, Meeting notes, Weekly meal plan, Reading queue, Vendor and Purchase decision templates pair with the projects that name them.
