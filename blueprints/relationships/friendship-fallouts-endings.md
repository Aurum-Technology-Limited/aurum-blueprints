              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Not every friendship that feels wrong is ending, and not every ending needs a confrontation. Drift from changed lives, a single rupture over something said, and a pattern that is actually harming you each call for a different response, and mixing them up is how people ghost a good friend or tolerate a bad one. Naming which of the three you are in, in writing, is the first decision everything else in this area hangs on.

                ## Milestones
                1. The friendship described in five or six plain sentences: how long, how close, what changed and when.
                2. The situation labelled as drift, rupture, harm or a mix, with the evidence for each label noted.
                3. Your honest goal written in one line: repair, recalibrate, let fade or end.
                4. The next project in this area chosen to match that label.

                ## Notes
                If the friendship involves threats, stalking, coercion or someone using private information against you, treat it as a safety matter first and start with the safety check project.

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most friendship damage that cannot be undone happens in the first two days: the long message sent at 1am, the screenshot shared with the group, the post everyone knows is about them.                 2. One person named who you can vent to safely without it reaching the friend.

                4. The 48 hours passed with no message sent that you later wish you had not.

            - name: Writing both sides of the fallout down
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Memory edits a row within days, usually in your favour, and the version you rehearse in the shower hardens into the only version. Writing what happened as plainly as a witness would, then writing it again as the friend would tell it, shows you what you actually know, what you are assuming and where a conversation could start.

                ## Milestones
                1. A factual account of the fallout with dates, what was said and what was done, without adjectives about character.
                2. A second account written from the friend's point of view, as fairly as you can manage.
                3. Assumptions about their motives separated from things you know for certain.
                4. Two or three points where the accounts differ that would need talking through in any repair.
              priority: medium
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "Two written accounts of the fallout exist, yours and the friend's likely version, with assumptions marked and differences listed."
                cadence: one-shot

            - name: Safety check when a falling-out turns to harassment
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Occasionally a fallout escalates into repeated unwanted contact, threats, sharing private photos or information, or turning up where you live or work. When that happens the priority is a record of what has happened and a plan for staying safe, not a better conversation, and the right people to help are the police, your workplace or a support service rather than the friend group.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every incident written down with date, time and what happened, and screenshots saved off the phone.
                2. The person blocked or muted on the platforms they are using, with evidence kept first.
                3. One trusted person told what is happening and how to reach you.
                4. Your local police non-emergency route or a harassment support service identified, and contacted if the behaviour continues.

                ## Notes
                If you are in immediate danger, call your emergency number. Keep the evidence even if you decide not to report yet; reporting later is much harder without it.

# Friendship Fallouts & Endings

Friendships rarely end with a clean conversation: more often they drift, blow up over something small or sour slowly, and the loss gets far less sympathy than a breakup. This area starts with the foundations for the first days (working out what is really going on, cooling off, writing both sides down, owning your part, a safety check where needed and the repair or release decision), then the routines that carry you through, the skills of listening, apologising and setting limits, the choices about repairing, fading or ending, the occasions where you will meet again, situations such as a fallout in a friend group, at work, at home or at the school gate, and finally pattern reviews, mediation and therapy.

What repeats is a weekly journal entry and a weekly clear-out of unsent drafts, a weekly plan for the slots the friend used to fill, monthly scans of your close friendships and your grievance list, a monthly look ahead at group events and hard dates, and quarterly and yearly reviews of the ending and of patterns across friendships. The Habit tracker, Reading queue and Meeting notes templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
