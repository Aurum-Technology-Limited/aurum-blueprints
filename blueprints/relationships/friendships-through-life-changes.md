            - name: Mapping which friendships a life change will touch
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The friendships most at risk from a baby, a move or a new job are rarely the ones you expect: it is the friend you saw at the gym every Tuesday, or the one whose contact ran on a shared commute. Listing each important friendship next to what currently keeps it going shows which ones depend on routines the change is about to break. Do it before the change lands, while you still have the time and energy to plan.

                ## Milestones
                1. Twelve to twenty friendships listed, each with the routine or place that currently keeps it going.
                2. Every friendship marked as safe, at risk or likely to end with the change.
                3. The five most valuable at-risk friendships picked out for a deliberate plan.
                4. One replacement routine sketched beside each of those five.

                ## Notes
                Contact that depends on geography or timetables (the school gate, the office, the Thursday league) is the most fragile. Friendships already kept alive by calls or by one of you travelling usually survive a change better.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A written map of at least twelve friendships, each marked safe, at risk or ending, with replacement routines sketched for the five most valuable at-risk ones."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Write the life change you are facing and its rough start date at the top of a page"
                - "List every friendship that matters and what currently keeps it going"
                - "Mark each friendship safe, at risk or likely to end with the change"
                - "Sketch a replacement routine for the five most valuable at-risk friendships"
            - name: Telling close friends about a big change in person
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Hearing about a pregnancy, a move or a resignation from a social media post leaves close friends feeling demoted, and that small hurt can colour the next year of the friendship. Deciding who hears first, in what order and in what format, then telling the inner few face to face or on a call, protects those friendships at exactly the moment they are about to be tested.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of who should hear the news personally, in order.
                2. Each person on the list told by meeting, call or video before any public announcement.
                3. Anything you will need from them in the coming months said out loud, even if small.
                4. The wider announcement made only after the list is complete.

                ## Notes
                If timing is sensitive, such as an early pregnancy or a job not yet resigned, ask each friend to keep it quiet and tell fewer people rather than more.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:

                output_kind: event-completion

                cadence: one-shot

# Friendships Through Life Changes

This area is for anyone whose friendships are being tested by a new baby, a move, a new career, caring, separation or retirement, their own or a friend's, when the rhythms that used to hold a friendship together stop matching. It starts with foundations (mapping which friendships a change will touch, telling people well, budgeting your time and resetting expectations), then the routines that keep contact going, the skills of asking, listening and handling envy, practical changes to when and how you meet, the moments worth turning up for, versions for parents, carers, job changers and retirees, and finally the longer view across decades.

What repeats is a Tuesday check-in message, a Thursday voice note and a Saturday walk, monthly anchors such as the standing friend date on the 9th, the childcare swap on the 14th and the update to friends in your old town on the 26th, a quarterly review and mixed-stage gathering, and a year-end note on how each friendship changed.
