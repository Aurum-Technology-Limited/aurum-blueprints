                Most friction with in-laws comes from not knowing who is close to whom, who is still sore about an old falling-out and whose opinion actually carries weight. Sketching your partner's family with them, names, partners, children, rough ages and the main alliances and rifts, turns a crowd of relatives into people you can understand.             - name: United-front agreement with your partner
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                In-laws notice quickly when a couple disagree, and some will press on the gap. Agreeing in private that you will not contradict each other in front of either family, and will settle differences later at home, protects the relationship from being split by well-meant pressure. It also makes clear that each of you puts the couple first, while still valuing both families.

                ## Milestones
                1. A shared sentence you both accept describing the couple as the primary unit, with both families valued.
                2. A discreet signal agreed for when one of you needs to step out or change the subject.
                3. A holding phrase agreed for questions you have not yet discussed, such as 'we will talk about it and let you know'.
                4. A rule for when a disagreement gets talked through at home, normally within a day.

                ## Notes
                A united front is about not being played off against each other, not about pretending to agree on everything. You can still disagree openly with an in-law about politics or football.
              priority: high

            - name: Boundaries list for both sides of the family
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Boundaries with in-laws fail when they are invented in the heat of a visit, so they come out sharp and feel personal. Writing a short list together in a calm week, covering drop-in visits, advice on how you live, comments on weight or work, and what is shared about your private life, means both of you know the line before anyone crosses it. # Getting On With the In-Laws

This area is for anyone who has gained a second family through marriage or partnership and wants it to be a source of warmth rather than strain. It starts with the foundations (a map of the family, its unwritten rules, a united front, shared boundaries and a holiday rotation), then the routines that keep things steady, the skills of handling advice, triangles and requests, decisions about visits, money and news, the big occasions, versions for new parents, cross-cultural couples, long-distance and multigenerational households, and finally the hard cases: limiting contact, shared businesses, future care and keeping bonds after loss or separation.

What repeats is a Sunday photo round to the grandparents and a midweek look at the family chat, monthly solo contact with each parent-in-law, a debrief with your partner and a thank-you sweep, a quarterly in-law review, and each year a fresh visits plan, holiday rotation and dates list. The Person, Operational checklist, Meeting notes and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
