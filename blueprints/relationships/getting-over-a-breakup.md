              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The first week is when people send the message they regret, stop eating properly and spend nights scrolling old photos. A simple plan written on day one, covering who you will see, what you will eat, where you will sleep and what you will not do, gets you through it with fewer things to undo later.

                ## Milestones
                1. One person who knows what happened and has agreed to check in daily for the week.
                2. Something to eat and somewhere to be planned for each of the seven evenings.
                3. Two or three rules written down for the week, such as no messages to the ex after 9pm.
                4. Any work or study commitments for the week adjusted or flagged to the right person.

                ## Notes
                Keep the plan small enough to fit on one card. The aim is to get through the week, not to make sense of the relationship yet.
              priority: high

                Every reply, like or late-night call restarts the hope and the hurt, which is why most people who recover faster go fully quiet for at least sixty days. # Getting Over a Breakup

A breakup is a loss, a logistics problem and an identity question all at once, and this area treats it as all three. It starts with the foundations for the first raw weeks (a survival plan, a safety check, a support circle, no-contact and the untangling of accounts, money, housing and belongings), moves through the daily and weekly routines that carry you through, the skills of grieving and thinking straight about what happened, the decisions about closure, mutual friends and keepsakes, the hard dates and encounters worth preparing for, variants for situations such as a long relationship, being the one who left, a shared workplace, a pet or student life, and ends with the deeper work of spotting patterns and choosing when to date again.

What repeats is a short daily check on sleep and food, a time-boxed worry window, weekly plans for the empty evenings and a standing date with friends, a weekly journal entry and urge review, a monthly healing score, and a quarterly and yearly look at the hard dates and what you still carry. The Habit tracker, Metrics log, Reading queue, Monthly budget and bills and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
