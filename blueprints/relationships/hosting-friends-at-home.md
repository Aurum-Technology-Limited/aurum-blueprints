              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before inviting anyone it helps to know the real numbers: how many people can sit down to eat, how many can stand with a drink, and how many matching plates, glasses and forks are in the cupboard. Most first-time hosts discover the limit is cutlery or chairs, not floor space. Counting once turns every later invitation into a simple question of which format fits.

                ## Milestones
                1. A seated-dinner number and a standing-drinks number written down for your home.
                2. Plates, bowls, glasses, cutlery and mugs counted, with the smallest set noted as the real limit.
                3. Every chair, stool and floor cushion in the house counted, including ones in bedrooms.
                4. Oven, hob and fridge space noted, with the largest pan and tray you own.

                ## Notes
                The smallest set wins: twelve plates and eight forks means eight guests for a sit-down meal.

# Hosting Friends at Home

This area is for anyone who wants friends round more often, whether you own a house with a garden or rent a flat with a sofa and four chairs. It starts with foundations (counting what your home can hold, a guest list, a format you enjoy, a budget, kit and a thirty-minute reset), moves to the standing machinery of monthly open houses, games nights, invitations and restocking, then the skills of cooking for a crowd, teaching games, introductions and allergy-safe food, the improvements and dated occasions from housewarmings to festive roasts, versions for renters, homeowners, parents, solo hosts and guests with access needs, and finally supper clubs, salons and a big annual open house.
