                Most people guess at their social limits and guess wrong, usually blaming the wrong event for the tiredness.                 ## Purpose
                Not all social time costs the same: a long walk with one old friend may leave you fuller than you started, while a ninety-minute networking drink can flatten a whole evening. Sorting your usual situations into drainers, neutrals and restorers gives every later decision in this area something solid to stand on.

            - name: Weekly social energy budget
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Putting a budget on social time makes the trade-offs visible before you commit, instead of on Thursday night when the week is already overspent. Giving each type of event a rough cost in points and setting a weekly total you can sustain lets you say yes to the things you want, knowing what they will take.

                ## Milestones
                1. A points scale agreed with yourself, for example one point for a coffee with a close friend and four for a large party.
                2. A sustainable weekly total chosen from the diary, with room for work demands.
                3. The budget tested against two typical weeks from the last month.
                4. The total adjusted after a four-week trial and the new figure written down.

                ## Notes
                Treat the numbers as a planning aid, not a score. The point is to notice when a week is heading for 18 points when you know 10 is your limit.

# Introvert Social Energy Pacing

This area is for anyone who likes people but finds that a full weekend of them leaves a flat, foggy Monday, and who wants a social life planned around the energy they actually have. It starts with foundations (an energy diary, a map of what drains and restores you, a weekly budget, protected recharge time and rules for saying yes), moves through the weekly and monthly routines that keep the budget honest, the skills of declining, leaving early and recovering mid-event, decisions about which commitments and formats to keep, plans for weddings, festive seasons and houseguests, variants for partners, parents, students and remote workers, and ends with guardrails against isolation and a graded return after burnout.

What repeats is a Sunday look-ahead at the week's social load, a short daily wind-down on people-heavy days, a weekly low-effort check-in with friends and a solo outing, monthly energy reviews and standing plans spread across different days, quarterly density and isolation checks and a yearly retrospective before the festive season.
