            - name: Distance, time difference and travel cost map
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before agreeing anything about calls or visits, it helps to know the hard numbers: how many hours apart you are by the fastest affordable route, what a return trip really costs door to door, and how many waking hours your days overlap. Couples who skip this tend to promise a visit every fortnight and then discover it costs a month's rent. One page of facts makes every later decision in this area honest.

                ## Milestones
                1. Door-to-door travel time on your usual route written down, including transfers and waiting.
                2. A typical return fare noted at normal, holiday and last-minute prices.
                3. The overlap of waking hours on a weekday and a weekend, shown in both local times.
                4. One page holding all of this, saved where both of you can see it.

                ## Notes
                Use real searched prices for dates a month out, not the cheapest fare either of you has ever seen. Include airport trains, parking and any unpaid leave where they apply.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A one-page distance map listing door-to-door travel time, typical return fares and daily overlap hours, shared with your partner."
                cadence: one-shot

            - name: Target window for ending the distance
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Distance with an end in sight feels very different from distance with no horizon, and open-ended arrangements are the ones that most often quietly fail. Agreeing even a rough window, such as after her degree or within eighteen months, gives both of you something to plan towards and a fair moment to ask what has changed if it slips.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each partner's honest timeline written separately, including what would need to happen first.
                2. The fixed constraints listed: contracts, courses, visas, children and leases.
                3. A target window agreed, expressed as a season and year or a life event.
                4. The window written in the shared calendar with a check-in six months before it.

                ## Notes
                A window is not a promise to move on a fixed day. It is the date by which you have either closed the gap or deliberately agreed a new plan.

# Long-Distance Relationship

This area is for couples living apart, whether you are students at different universities, separated by a job or a visa, or across an ocean from someone you met online. It begins with the foundations (a distance map, ground rules, a target window for ending the distance, a visit fund, trust and emergency contacts), moves into the weekly and monthly machinery of calls, visits and post, then the skills of calls, texts and goodbyes, the big decisions about who moves and how, the visits and occasions worth planning, versions for students, deployments and postings, and finally specialist work such as visa evidence and living apart by choice.

What repeats is a protected weekly long call, a small daily touchpoint, a weekly shared activity night and an evening of local life, monthly distance reviews, fare checks, post and visit fund transfers, a quarterly look at progress towards closing the gap, and yearly checks on clock changes, holidays, contacts and security. The Savings goal, Habit tracker, Meeting notes, Course, Job application and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them.
