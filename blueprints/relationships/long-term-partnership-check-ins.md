            - name: Proposing regular check-ins to your partner
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Suggesting a scheduled relationship talk can land as 'what have I done wrong?' if it arrives after a bad week or out of nowhere. Raising it on a calm day, framed as looking after something you both value, with a short trial rather than a forever commitment, makes a yes far more likely. Most couples who keep check-ins going started with a four-week experiment.

                ## Milestones
                1. A calm, unhurried moment chosen to raise the idea, away from any recent disagreement.
                2. The idea explained in two or three sentences that name what you hope it will add, not what is wrong.
                3. Your partner's hopes and worries about check-ins heard and written down.
                4. A four-week trial agreed, or a clear 'not yet' with a date to ask again.

                ## Notes
                Avoid proposing it in the middle of a row or as the solution to one. A partner who hears 'we need to talk more' as criticism may need to see the first session kept short and kind before they trust the format.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "Your partner has agreed to a four-week check-in trial, or a date to revisit the idea is written down."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "1"
              tasks:
                - "Write two sentences on why you want regular check-ins, without mentioning a complaint"
                - "Pick a relaxed moment this week to raise the idea, such as a walk"
                - "Ask your partner what would make a check-in feel worthwhile or worrying for them"
                - "Agree a four-week trial and the day the first one happens"
            - name: Ground rules for how you check in
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Check-ins go wrong in predictable ways: one partner lectures, old grievances are dragged in, phones buzz, and the talk runs past midnight. Five to seven ground rules agreed in advance, written in your own words, give either of you something neutral to point to when a session drifts. Typical rules cover one speaker at a time, no scorekeeping, a time limit and what stays private.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each partner's three must-have rules written separately, then compared.
                2. A shared list of no more than seven rules both of you would sign.
                3. A rule on privacy agreed: what can and cannot be repeated to friends or family.
                4. The rules written at the front of your check-in notebook and read aloud at the first session.

                ## Notes
                Keep rules about behaviour, not outcomes. 'We each finish our point before the other replies' works; 'we always resolve things' sets the session up to fail.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A written list of five to seven ground rules, agreed by both partners, sits at the front of the check-in notebook."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "1"
              tasks:
                - "List the three rules you most want, separately from your partner"
                - "Compare lists and merge them into one set of five to seven rules"
                - "Agree what from a check-in may be shared with friends or family"
                - "Copy the final rules onto the first page of your check-in notebook"
            - name: Protected time and place for check-ins
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A check-in squeezed between washing up and bedtime rarely gets past logistics. Choosing a fixed slot when neither of you is hungry, rushed or exhausted, and a place without screens or interruptions, does more for the quality of the talk than any question list. Many couples settle on a weekend morning or an early weekday evening, with a hard stop after 30 to 45 minutes.

                ## Milestones

                2. One recurring slot chosen and added to both calendars as a protected appointment.
                3. A setting agreed, such as a particular room, a cafe or a walk, with phones out of reach.
                4. A back-up slot agreed for weeks when the usual one is lost.

            - name: Pause signal and stop rules for check-ins
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Even good check-ins sometimes overheat, and once either partner is flooded with stress, listening drops sharply. Agreeing a pause word, a minimum break of twenty minutes and a promise to come back to the topic within a day protects the format from becoming a place either of you dreads. # Long-Term Partnership Check-Ins

This area is for couples who want to talk about the relationship on purpose, before small resentments harden, rather than only when something has gone wrong. It starts with the foundations (proposing the idea, ground rules, a protected slot, a first session, baseline ratings and a shared notebook), then the operating rhythm of weekly, monthly, quarterly and yearly check-ins, the listening and asking skills that make them work, the fixes and decisions when they stall, the events worth preparing for, variants for situations such as a new baby, shift work, caring or retirement, and finally the work of couples who have kept the habit for years.

What repeats is the weekly check-in and its preparation, a midweek logistics huddle, a monthly themed session with a satisfaction score and a clear-out of parked topics, a quarterly state of the relationship review, and a yearly review, debrief and retreat. The 1:1, Meeting notes, Metrics log, Reading queue and Feedback themes templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
