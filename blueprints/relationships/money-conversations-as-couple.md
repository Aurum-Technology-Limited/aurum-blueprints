            - name: Money histories swap, how you each grew up with money
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most money arguments between partners are really two childhoods talking: one of you watched parents fight over unpaid bills, the other never heard money mentioned at all. Telling each other those stories on purpose, before the next disagreement, explains reactions that otherwise look irrational or stubborn. It is the cheapest and kindest first step in this whole area.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each partner has answered the same six questions about money in their childhood home, in writing.
                2. One unhurried evening spent hearing each other's answers without debating them.
                3. Two or three money beliefs each of you inherited named out loud.
                4. One sentence each recorded on what the other needs when money gets tense.

                ## Notes
                Good questions: who handled money at home, was it discussed, what happened when it ran short, what did you get pocket money for, what did your parents argue about, and what did you promise yourself you would do differently. Listen first; save the solving for later projects.
              priority: high

            - name: Ground rules for talking about money
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Couples who argue about money rarely lack information; they lack a way of raising it that does not feel like an ambush. Agreeing a few written rules, such as no money talk after 9pm, no surprises in front of family and a pause word either of you can call, makes every later conversation in this area safer to start.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each partner has listed the money conversations that have gone badly and why.
                2. Five or fewer ground rules agreed and written on one page.
                3. A pause word chosen and a rule for when the conversation resumes.
                4. The rules tested in one real money conversation and adjusted afterwards.

                ## Notes
                Keep the list short enough to remember. Rules about timing and tone matter more than rules about content.
              priority: high

# Money Conversations as a Couple

This area is for any couple who wants to talk about money calmly and decide things together, whether you have just started sharing costs or have been married for twenty years. It opens with the foundations (money histories, ground rules, full disclosure, your styles and priorities, how to combine accounts and what fair means), then the routines that keep money visible, the conversation skills that stop it turning into blame, the shared decisions on giving, family help, big purchases and saving, the moments like a bonus, an inheritance or a sudden income drop, versions for unequal earners, career breaks, variable income, blended families, a new baby and retirement, and finally the harder ground of protected assets, hidden debt, compulsive spending and financial independence.

What repeats is a budget refresh on the 2nd, the monthly money date on the 5th, sinking fund top-ups on the 9th, debt payments checked on the 15th, savings progress on the 20th, payday transfers on the 26th, a short Sunday spending check-in, a quarterly net worth snapshot and a yearly money review with its related checks on credit files, benefits, giving and family support. The Meeting notes, Monthly budget and bills, Savings goal, Reading queue and Purchase decision templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
