            - name: Readiness conversation before moving in together
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Many couples slide into living together because a lease is ending or one flat is always empty, and only later discover they meant different things by it. A deliberate conversation about why now, what each of you expects and what would make you want to wait gives the decision a foundation you both chose. Have it before anyone signs a lease or gives notice.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each partner has written down, separately, three reasons to move in now and one reason to wait.
                2. The two lists compared in one unhurried conversation.
                3. A shared answer to what living together means to you both, such as a step towards marriage, a trial or a practical choice.
                4. A decision recorded: move in, wait until a named date, or revisit after a specific change.

                ## Notes
                If one of you is mainly motivated by saving rent, say so openly. It is a fair reason, but it should not be the only one either of you believes is true.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A written decision on whether and roughly when to move in, with each partner's main reasons noted and agreed by both."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"

                - "Write your own three reasons to move in now and one reason to wait"
                - "Ask your partner to write their own list before you talk"
                - "Set aside a phone-free evening to compare the two lists"
                - "Record the decision and, if you chose to wait, the date you will revisit it"

# Moving In Together

This area is for couples deciding whether to share a home and then making it work, whether you rent, own or are moving into a place one of you already has. It starts with the foundations (readiness, where to live, notice periods, upfront costs, the rent split and a cohabitation agreement), then the routines that keep a shared home running, the skills of living closely with someone, the decisions about furniture, space and insurance, the move itself, versions for parents, flat shares, later life and long-distance moves, and finally the legal and property questions experienced cohabiting couples face.

What repeats is a Monday look at the week, a Sunday settling-in check for the first three months, a Saturday shopping list, bill checks on the 3rd and contract checks on the 22nd, a monthly walk round for repairs, a quarterly living-together review and yearly checks of the rent split, insurance, wills and cohabitation agreement.
