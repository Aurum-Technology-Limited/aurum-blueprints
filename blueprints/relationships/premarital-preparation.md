              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Couples often agree to marry without ever saying out loud what they expect marriage to add to a relationship that already works. Writing your own reasons privately, then reading them to each other, surfaces quiet assumptions such as security, children, faith or simply family pressure while there is still time to talk about them. It sets the tone for every conversation that follows in this area.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each partner has written a private page answering why marry, and why now.
                2. Both pages read aloud to each other in one unhurried sitting.
                3. Any reason that surprised either of you noted as an open question.
                4. One shared sentence written on what you both want marriage to mean.

                ## Notes
                Write before you talk. Reasons heard first tend to shape the other person's answer.

# Premarital Preparation

This area is for engaged couples who want the marriage to get as much thought as the wedding, whether you are twenty-four and marrying for the first time or sixty and marrying again. It starts with the foundations (why you are marrying, a preparation calendar, choosing a course or counsellor, a relationship inventory, the legal effects of marriage and full disclosure), moves through the routines that keep the conversations going during a busy engagement, the communication and family-of-origin skills worth learning, and the decisions on values, roles, children, faith, money, family boundaries, intimacy and names, then the events that mark the preparation, versions for second marriages, stepfamilies, interfaith couples, later life, same-sex couples and short engagements, and finally prenuptial agreements, wills, mental health plans and the hard question of postponing.

What repeats is a Sunday preparation conversation, a Wednesday evening kept free of wedding admin, a short daily check-in, weekly counselling homework, speaking and listening practice and a chapter of a shared book, a monthly open-questions review on the 12th and an agreements register on the 24th, then quarterly check-ins through the first year of marriage and a yearly revisit of what you agreed. The Meeting notes, Reading queue and Course templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
