                Most adults can name a dozen people they were once close to and now only see on social media, but the list lives as vague guilt rather than something written down.             - name: Sorting lapsed friendships into rebuild, revive or let rest
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Not every lapsed friendship is worth the effort of rebuilding, and trying to revive twenty at once means none get the attention they need. Sorting your list into three groups (friendships to rebuild properly, ones to revive lightly and ones to let rest with good memories) gives you a short list of three to five people to start with. Letting some rest on purpose also removes the background guilt about them.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every name on the drift list placed in one of three groups: rebuild, revive or let rest.
                2. Three to five names chosen as the first people to contact.
                3. One sentence beside each rebuild name saying what you miss about that friendship.
                4. The let-rest group read through once more and accepted without guilt.

                ## Notes
                A useful test: would you be glad if this person messaged you tomorrow? If the honest answer is no, the friendship probably belongs in the let-rest group.

            - name: Drafting a low-pressure first message after years apart
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The first message is where most reconnections stall: people write and delete it for months because it feels like it must explain years of silence. A good first message is short, specific and asks for nothing, built from a shared memory, a line on why they came to mind and an easy door left open. Having a draft you are happy with removes the biggest barrier in this whole area.

                ## Milestones

                2. Each draft containing one specific shared memory.
                3. No draft that demands a reply, a call or an explanation for the silence.
                4. Every draft read aloud once and trimmed.

                ## Notes
                A workable shape: their name, the memory or reason they came to mind, one line about you, and something like 'no pressure to reply, I just wanted to say hello'.

# Reconnecting With Old Friends

This area is for anyone who has scrolled past an old friend's photo, thought about sending a message and then did not. It starts with the foundations (mapping who you drifted from, deciding which friendships to rebuild, finding contact details and sending the first messages), then the routines that keep reconnection moving, the skills of awkward first catch-ups and short honest apologies, the decisions about calls, meetings and old rifts, the reunions and visits worth preparing for, versions for parents, retirees and people returning from a hard patch, and finally the harder cases: decades of silence, serious illness and loss.

What repeats is a reach-out to one lapsed friend on the 9th, a Sunday check of replies, catch-up calls proposed on the 3rd, file updates on the 6th, light touchpoints on the 18th, occasion hooks on the 26th, a stopping-rule check on the 28th, a quarterly review of rekindled friendships and a yearly audit of new drift.
