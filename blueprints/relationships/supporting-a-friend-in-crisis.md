            - name: First 48 hours after a friend's bad news
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The first reply to news of a diagnosis, a death or a breakdown is the one your friend remembers, and most people either say nothing for fear of saying the wrong thing or arrive with far too much. A short acknowledgement, one concrete offer and a named day for your next check-in covers the first two days without crowding them.

                ## Milestones
                1. A short message sent acknowledging the news without asking for details.
                2. One specific practical offer made, such as food left at the door or a lift.
                3. A day for your next check-in chosen and written in your calendar.
                4. Anything you heard second-hand kept to yourself until your friend shares it.

                ## Notes
                Silence hurts more than clumsy words. A line saying you are sorry, you are thinking of them and they need not reply is enough for a first message.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 7

                cadence: one-shot

                - "Send a two-line message that asks for nothing back"
                - "Make one specific offer with a day and time attached"

                - "Hold off telling anyone else until your friend has shared the news themselves"

# Supporting a Friend in Crisis

This area is for anyone whose friend is going through illness, grief, a breakup, a mental health crisis or another hard stretch, and who wants to help well for as long as it lasts. It starts with the foundations (the first two days, asking what help is wanted, crisis numbers, warning signs and an honest look at your own capacity), then the routines that keep support going, the skills that make you useful, the decisions about money, boundaries and handing over, the occasions you may be asked to show up for, the situations that need a particular approach, and finally the work of coordinating care through long illness, the end of life and recovery after a suicide attempt.

What repeats is a weekly no-reply check-in, a meal and errand rota, a standing walk or cup of tea, a weekly recharge slot of your own, a monthly review of the plan with your friend, a monthly debrief and self-check for you, and a quarterly look at the long haul.
