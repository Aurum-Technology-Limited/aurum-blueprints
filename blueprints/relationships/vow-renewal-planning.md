              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Couples renew vows for very different reasons: a round-number anniversary, coming through illness or a hard patch, giving children a wedding they never saw, or simply wanting to say it again with more meaning than the first time. Naming your reason together before booking anything shapes every later choice, from the size of the guest list to whether the day is solemn, joyful or both. Ten minutes of honest talk now saves weeks of planning the wrong celebration.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each partner's reason for renewing written down separately, then shared.
                2. One or two sentences agreed that say what this renewal is for.
                3. A short list of things the day must include and things it must not become.
                4. The statement saved at the top of your planning notes where both of you will reread it.

                ## Notes
                If one of you is keener than the other, say so now. A renewal one partner is quietly enduring is worse than none at all.

            - name: What a vow renewal is and is not legally
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                In most places a renewal celebrates an existing marriage rather than creating a new one, so there is usually no notice to give and no new certificate, though some faiths have rites with their own requirements. Knowing this in advance avoids paying for paperwork you do not need and sets expectations for guests who assume it is a second wedding.

                ## Milestones
                1. The position where you live checked with an officiant or the local registration office.
                2. Any faith requirements for a blessing of an existing marriage noted.
                3. Your original marriage certificate located in case an officiant asks to see it.
                4. A one-line explanation ready for guests who ask whether you are remarrying.

                ## Notes
                This is organisational, not legal advice. If anything about your marriage's legal status is uncertain, such as a ceremony held abroad, ask a family lawyer before relying on the renewal for anything.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: learning

# Vow Renewal Planning

This area is for couples planning to renew their vows, whether for a tenth anniversary or a sixtieth, and for the adult children who sometimes organise it for them. It starts with the foundations (why you are renewing, the scale, the date, the budget, the guest list, the officiant and your original wedding records), then the routines that keep planning, money, replies and family in order, the skills of rewriting and speaking your vows, the choices about venue, rings, clothes and food, the dated steps from invitations to the loose ends afterwards, versions for older couples, blended families, faith blessings, hard seasons and serious illness, and finally a destination renewal, recorded stories and keepsakes for the next generation.

What repeats is a Sunday planning half hour, a budget check on the 14th, supplier payments on the 3rd, a Wednesday reply sweep, a weekly memory note and vow practice, a monthly family update and, once the day is done, rereading your vows each anniversary. The Savings goal, Meeting notes, Purchase decision, Vendor, Operational checklist and Trip templates pair with the projects that name them.
