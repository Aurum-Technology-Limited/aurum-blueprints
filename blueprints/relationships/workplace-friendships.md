            - name: Map of your current work relationships
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most people can name their teammates but have never looked at which colleagues they actually trust, laugh with or would call if they left tomorrow. Sorting everyone you deal with into rings (friend, friendly, neutral, strained) shows where your support at work really sits and where it is thin, before any change in team or job forces the question.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of everyone you work with at least monthly, inside and outside your team.
                2. Each person placed in one of four rings: friend, friendly, neutral or strained.
                3. The two or three people you would miss most if you left marked.
                4. A one-line note on any ring that is empty or crowded, such as no friends outside your own team.

                ## Notes
                Keep this private and off company systems. A list of colleagues labelled "strained" is not something to leave on a shared drive.
              priority: high

            - name: Personal boundaries for friendship at work
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most trouble between work friends starts in predictable places: a confidence shared that later reaches a manager, a friend who expects cover for missed deadlines, money lent across a desk. Deciding in advance what you will share, what you will not, and where friendship stops and the job starts makes those moments easier to handle calmly when they arrive.

                ## Milestones
                1. A short list of topics you are happy to share at work and topics you keep outside it.
                2. Your position written down on covering for a friend's mistakes, lending money and sharing salary details.
                3. A sentence you can use to decline gracefully when a request crosses a line.
                4. The boundaries tested against one real situation from the past year.

                ## Notes
                Boundaries are for you, not a rulebook for others. You do not need to announce them, just act on them consistently.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A one-page note of what you share, what you keep private and how you handle requests to cover, lend or disclose, with one prepared decline sentence."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:

            - name: Separating work and personal contact channels

# Workplace Friendships

This area is for anyone who spends forty hours a week beside people and would like a few of them to be real friends, without making work awkward when roles, teams or jobs change. It starts with foundations (a map of who you know, your boundaries, the company's written rules and the colleagues worth investing in), moves through the weekly and monthly routines that keep those ties warm, then the skills of inviting, disagreeing, keeping confidences and declining gracefully, followed by decisions and repairs, the events that mark arrivals and departures, variants for managers, remote workers, parents, contractors and people facing redundancy, and finally the specialist work of onboarding buddies, staff networks and friendships at senior level.

What repeats is a weekly coffee or walk, a weekly remote check-in and a Friday thank-you note, monthly cross-team coffees, people notes, celebrations, lunch-and-learn swaps and messages to former colleagues, and a quarterly review of the whole map. The Person, Onboarding 30/60/90, Habit tracker, Reading queue, 1:1 and Onboarding plan templates pair with the projects that name them.
