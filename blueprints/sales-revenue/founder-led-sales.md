            - name: Weekly selling hours blocked in the founder's calendar
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Selling is the first thing a founder drops when a release is late, and a pipeline that only gets attention in quiet weeks never builds. Blocking a fixed number of hours each week, at times your buyers actually answer, turns sales from a mood into a commitment the rest of the calendar has to work around.

                ## Milestones
                1. A weekly selling target in hours chosen and written down, typically eight to fifteen at this stage.
                2. Recurring calendar blocks set for the next eight weeks at the times your buyers are reachable.
                3. The blocks shared with co-founders or staff so nobody books over them.
                4. Two consecutive weeks in which at least four fifths of the blocked hours went on selling.

                ## Notes
                Mornings early in the week suit many business buyers; ask three of your contacts when they prefer to take calls rather than guessing.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 7
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "Eight weeks of selling blocks sit in the calendar and two consecutive weeks show at least four fifths of those hours spent selling."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Pick the number of selling hours you will commit to each week"
                - "Block those hours in the calendar for the next eight weeks"
                - "Tell your co-founder or team which blocks cannot be moved"
                - "Note which blocks got moved in the first fortnight and why"
            - name: One-page founding sales hypothesis
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before the first conversation, write down who you think will buy, what problem pushes them to act, what they do about it today and roughly what they would pay. A written hypothesis can be proved wrong, which is the point: without one, every friendly call feels encouraging and nothing is learned.

                ## Milestones
                1. A single page naming the buyer's role, the type of company, the triggering problem and the current workaround.
                2. A price guess written down with the reasoning behind it.
                3. The three riskiest assumptions marked, each with the evidence that would disprove it.
                4. The page dated and saved where the Friday review can find it.

                ## Notes
                Keep it rough. Fuller targeting rules for a team come later, once twenty or thirty conversations have tested this page.
              priority: medium

            - name: Opening price for the first ten customers
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Founders tend to underprice the first deals out of nervousness, then struggle to raise prices on customers who signed cheap. Setting a deliberate list price, with a founding-customer discount that has an end date, protects later revenue and tests whether the problem is worth real money.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three reference points gathered: what buyers spend on the current workaround, competitor prices and the yearly cost of the problem.
                2. A list price written down with the reasoning.
                3. A founding-customer discount set, with an expiry or review date in writing.
                4. The price quoted aloud in at least five conversations without changing it on the spot.

                ## Notes
                Discount from a visible list price rather than lowering the list price itself. Customers who know the full price and their saving are far easier to move to standard terms later.

# Founder-Led Sales

Founder-led sales is the stretch when the founder is the sales team: finding the first buyers, learning what makes them say yes, closing the first customers and writing it all down for whoever sells next. It is for startup founders and for small business owners who do every sale themselves. The projects run from foundations (protected selling hours, a written hypothesis, a first hundred names, a simple tracker, a founder story, an opening price and plain terms), through the weekly and monthly routines, the skills founders usually lack, the decisions about segments, pilots and the first hire, dated moments such as the first customer and the first renewal, situations like selling part-time or after a pivot, and finally handing selling over to a team.

What repeats is a daily first hour of outreach, a Monday priorities talk with your co-founder, a Tuesday follow-up sweep and a Friday sales review, then monthly checks on messaging, product requests, runway, deal debriefs and paying customers, and a quarterly retrospective.
