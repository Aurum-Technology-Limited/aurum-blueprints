            - name: Three to five strategic questions for the board
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A session titled "strategy update" invites a tour of everything and decides nothing. Boards do their best work when management names the few questions that genuinely need directors' judgement, such as whether to keep investing in a loss-making segment or how fast to expand abroad, and says which of them it wants decided this year.

                ## Milestones
                1. A long list of open strategic questions gathered from the executive team.
                2. The list cut to three to five questions that need board judgement rather than management action.
                3. Each question written as a choice with a timeframe for deciding it.
                4. The questions tested with the chair and adopted as the year's strategy agenda.

                ## Notes
                A good board question has at least two credible answers. If management already knows the answer, it is an update, not a question.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "Three to five strategic questions, each written as a choice with a decision date, agreed with the chair as the board's strategy agenda for the year."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "5"
              tasks:
                - "Ask each executive for the two strategic questions that worry them most"
                - "Cut the list to questions that need board judgement"
                - "Rewrite each question as a choice with a date to decide it"
                - "Review the final questions with the chair"

            - name: Strategic fact base for directors
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                People around the board table arrive at strategy sessions with very different levels of knowledge about the market, the cost base and the competition, and the first hour is often spent levelling them up. A standing fact base of twenty or so pages, refreshed before each session, gives everyone the same starting facts and frees the session for judgement.

                ## Milestones
                1. The core facts chosen: market size and growth, share, segment margins, customer concentration and the competitor set.

                3. A fact base of no more than twenty pages assembled and sent to directors.
                4. A short list of facts directors asked for that are not yet available.

                ## Notes

            - name: Strategy pre-read deadline and dispatch
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A strategy paper that arrives two days before the meeting gets skimmed on the train, and directors then spend the session asking questions the paper answers. A firm rule that strategy papers reach directors seven days ahead, with the dispatch date worked back into the executive calendar, is the single cheapest improvement most boards can make.

                ## Milestones

                3. Paper owners told the dates at the start of each quarter.
                4. Three board cycles in a row where every strategy paper went out on time.

                ## Notes
                Late papers should be the exception the chair approves, not the norm the secretary absorbs. If a paper is late, the item usually moves to the next meeting.

            - name: Framing a strategic choice as options
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Boards asked to approve a single recommendation can only say yes or nitpick it. Learning to present two or three genuinely different options, each with its costs, risks and what you would have to believe for it to work, gives directors something to weigh and makes the eventual decision far sturdier.

                ## Milestones
                1. Two published examples of options papers read and their structure noted.
                2. One live strategic question rewritten as three real options, including one management does not favour.
                3. For each option, a short list of what would have to be true for it to succeed.
                4. The options paper reviewed by a director or adviser before it goes to the board.

                ## Notes
                A straw man option that nobody would choose wastes the board's time and its trust. Every option on the page should be one a reasonable director could back.
              priority: medium

# Board Strategy Sessions

This area is for chief executives, founders, strategy directors and company secretaries who bring strategy to a board and want directors to sharpen the choices rather than sit through a slide show. It starts with the foundations (a look back at past board strategy time, an agreed purpose with the chair, the strategy calendar, the questions and the line between board and management), then the recurring machinery between meetings, the framing and facilitation skills, the format decisions, the dated sessions themselves, the situations that change the dynamics, such as a new chief executive or investor directors, and finally the specialist work of capital allocation, assumption tracking and decision look-backs.

What repeats is a monthly chair and chief executive check-in, a monthly sweep of board strategy actions and a monthly call with one director, a quarterly strategy topic at the board with its pre-read deadline, risk discussion, progress report, horizon scan and feedback round, and a yearly cycle that books the strategy day, refreshes the director experience map and looks back at past decisions. The Skills matrix, Meeting notes, Risk register, Feedback themes, Purchase decision and Board meeting templates pair with the projects that name them.
