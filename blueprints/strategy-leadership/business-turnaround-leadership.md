            - name: First fortnight turnaround situation diagnosis
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                In the first two weeks of a turnaround, everyone wants a plan and almost nobody has the facts. A short, structured diagnosis of cash, profitability by line, debt terms, customer health and staff mood, written before any cuts are announced, stops you fixing the wrong problem and gives lenders a reason to believe you understand the business.

                ## Milestones
                1. Cash on hand, committed payments and available facilities confirmed to the nearest week.
                2. Profit or loss by product, customer group or site laid out for the last twelve months.
                3. Every loan, lease and covenant listed with its next test date.
                4. Twenty interviews with staff, customers and suppliers summarised in one page of themes.
                5. A five-page situation report shared with the board or owner.

                ## Notes
                Resist announcing anything structural before the report is done. Early staff and supplier conversations are the cheapest source of truth you will get.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A five-page situation report covering cash, profitability by line, debt terms and stakeholder views is delivered to the board within three weeks."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "30"
              tasks:
                - "Ask finance for today's bank balances and every payment due in the next four weeks"
                - "Book twenty short interviews across staff, customers and suppliers"
                - "List every loan, lease and covenant with its next test date"
                - "Write the five-page situation report and send it to the board"
            - name: Thirteen-week rolling cash flow forecast
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Profit tells you whether the business deserves to survive; cash tells you whether it will. A direct, receipts-and-payments forecast for the next thirteen weeks, built line by line from the ledgers rather than from the budget, is the single document lenders, boards and insolvency advisers ask for first in any distressed situation.

                ## Milestones
                1. Opening cash reconciled to the bank statements for every account.
                2. Customer receipts forecast by major account using actual payment behaviour, not terms.
                3. Payroll, tax, rent, debt service and key supplier payments scheduled by week.
                4. The lowest cash point and its week identified, with headroom against facilities shown.
                5. The forecast template handed to a named owner in finance for weekly roll-forward.

                ## Notes
                Forecast receipts on how customers actually pay. A ninety-day debtor on thirty-day terms is still a ninety-day debtor in a crisis.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A direct thirteen-week cash forecast, reconciled to bank balances and showing the lowest cash week, is owned by a named person in finance."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "20"
              tasks:
                - "Reconcile opening cash across every bank account to the statements"
                - "Pull the debtor ledger and note how each large customer really pays"
                - "Schedule payroll, tax, rent and debt payments week by week"
                - "Mark the lowest cash week and the headroom left against facilities"
            - name: Immediate cash preservation measures
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Once the forecast shows a gap, every week of delay narrows the options. A short list of measures that release or protect cash within thirty days, such as collecting overdue debts, pausing discretionary spend, deferring capital projects and agreeing payment plans with the tax authority, buys the time a proper plan needs.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of at least ten cash measures, each with a value and an owner.
                2. Overdue debtors over a set threshold called personally by a senior person.
                3. Non-essential capital projects paused with suppliers notified.
                4. Any payment arrangement with the tax authority requested in writing.
                5. Cash released in the first month tracked against the target.

                ## Notes
                Stretching suppliers without telling them is a loan they did not agree to give. Agree it openly or expect your credit to be stopped.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: deliverable
                success_criteria: "At least ten cash measures with values and owners are in place, and cash released in the first thirty days is reported against target."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "15"
              tasks:
                - "List every lever that could release cash within thirty days"
                - "Call the ten largest overdue debtors personally this week"
                - "Pause capital projects that are not safety critical or contracted"
                - "Ask your accountant whether a tax payment arrangement is worth requesting"
            - name: Root cause analysis of the decline
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Symptoms such as falling margins, rising debt and staff turnover usually trace back to two or three underlying causes: a market that moved, a cost base that never adjusted, poor pricing, a bad acquisition or weak management. Naming those causes with evidence, and admitting which were self-inflicted, is what separates a turnaround from a round of cuts that buys a year.

                ## Milestones
                1. Five years of revenue, gross margin and overhead trends charted side by side.
                2. External causes such as market, regulation and competition separated from internal ones.
                3. Two or three root causes stated, each backed by a figure or a document.
                4. The diagnosis tested with at least three people who knew the business before you arrived.

                ## Notes
                Most declines are mainly internal. If your list blames only the market, test it again.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A written diagnosis naming two or three root causes of decline, each supported by evidence, has been reviewed by the board."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "16"
              tasks:
                - "Chart five years of revenue, gross margin and overheads on one page"
                - "Sort every suspected cause into external or internal"
                - "Find one hard figure or document behind each root cause"
                - "Test the diagnosis with three long-serving managers"

            - name: Fix, sell or close viability decision
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Not every business can or should be saved, and owners who keep trading on hope often destroy the value a sale would have preserved. Setting out the three honest options side by side, with the cash needed, the likely outcome for creditors, staff and owners, and the time each takes, lets the board make the hardest call deliberately rather than by drift.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three options written up: fix and keep, sell as a going concern, close in an orderly way.
                2. Cash requirement, timeline and outcome for each stakeholder group estimated for each option.
                3. Professional advice taken on the creditor and legal consequences of each.
                4. A board decision recorded with the reasons and the conditions for revisiting it.

                ## Notes
                Write down in advance what evidence would make you switch option. Without that, the decision quietly becomes permanent.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 60
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "The board has recorded a choice between fix, sell and close, with cash needs, stakeholder outcomes and switch conditions documented."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "20"
              tasks:
                - "Write a one-page case for fix, sell and close"
                - "Estimate the cash each option needs and how long it takes"
                - "Ask your advisers how creditors and staff fare under each option"
                - "Hold a board session dedicated to the choice and minute the decision"

            - name: Weekly cash forecast variance review
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A thirteen-week forecast is only useful if it is rolled forward and checked against reality every week. Comparing last week's forecast to actual receipts and payments, explaining every large miss and updating the remaining weeks keeps the forecast honest and catches a looming shortfall while there is still time to act.

                ## Milestones
                1. The forecast rolled forward one week, every week, without gaps.
                2. Variances above a set threshold explained in a sentence each.
                3. Forecast accuracy over the last four weeks tracked as a percentage.
                4. Any new shortfall escalated to the steering committee the same day.

                ## Notes

# Business Turnaround Leadership

This area is for the leader handed a business in decline: the owner of a small firm watching the overdraft creep up, the manager parachuted into a loss-making division, or the executive asked to rescue a whole company. It runs from the first fortnight of diagnosis and cash preservation, through the operating rhythm of cash, spend, lender and morale controls, the skills of reading distress and negotiating with creditors, the hard decisions on costs, people, sites and products, the dated moments such as the lender meeting and the hundred-day checkpoint, situations for owners, divisions and interim managers, and finally the specialist work of a funded recovery plan and the pivot back to growth.

What repeats is the turnaround drumbeat: a daily cash call while the position is tight, weekly forecast variance, spend approvals and initiative reviews, monthly lender reporting, board reports, morale pulses and customer and supplier watches, and quarterly checks on the recovery plan. The **Risk register**, **Metrics log**, **Board meeting**, **Vendor** and **Remediation plan** templates pair with the steering committee, the cash variance log, the monthly board report, supplier renegotiation and fixing the worst unit.
