            - name: Succession readiness snapshot for the senior team
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most boards believe they have a succession plan until someone asks who would run the organisation on Monday if the chief executive resigned on Friday. Spending a fortnight recording, role by role, whether a named successor exists, how ready they are and whether the board has ever been told gives an honest starting point and shows where the real exposure sits.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every executive committee role listed with its incumbent, tenure and any known departure date.
                2. A named successor, or a blank, recorded against each role with a readiness rating of ready now, one to two years or three to five years.
                3. Roles with no successor, and incumbents within two years of a likely exit, flagged in red.
                4. The snapshot shared with the chair and chief executive with a short covering note.

                ## Notes
                Be honest about blanks. A snapshot that names a weak successor to avoid an empty box is worse than one that admits the gap.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A one-page readiness snapshot covering every executive committee role, with readiness ratings and red flags, has been sent to the chair and chief executive."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "6"
              tasks:
                - "List every role that reports to the chief executive, plus the chief executive"
                - "Ask the chief executive who could step into each role today"
                - "Rate each named successor ready now, one to two years or three to five years"
                - "Mark roles with no successor or an incumbent near retirement"
                - "Send the snapshot to the chair with the three questions it raises"
            - name: Critical roles list beyond the executive committee
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Succession risk does not stop at the leadership chart: the head of a regulated function, the person holding the largest client relationship or the only engineer who understands the core platform can hurt the organisation more than some executives. Agreeing a short list of fifteen to thirty critical roles, scored against written criteria, keeps succession effort pointed where a vacancy would do the most damage.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three to five written criteria for criticality, such as revenue at risk, regulatory duty, scarcity in the market and time to fill.
                2. A list of critical roles scored against the criteria and agreed by the executive team.
                3. Each critical role tagged with an owner responsible for its succession cover.
                4. The list approved by the board committee that oversees succession.

            - name: Written succession policy for senior roles
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Without a policy, succession happens by habit: the chief executive picks a favourite, the board hears late and nobody knows whether internal candidates must be considered. A short policy approved by the board sets who owns succession for which roles, how often plans are reviewed, how candidates are assessed and what happens in an emergency.

                ## Milestones
                1. A draft policy of two to four pages covering scope, ownership, review frequency, assessment, emergency cover and confidentiality.
                2. Comments from the chair, chief executive, head of people and company secretary incorporated.
                3. The policy approved by the board, with its approval date recorded.
                4. The policy filed with the governance documents and referenced in the lead committee's terms of reference.

                ## Notes
                Check whether any governance code, regulator or major funder that applies to you sets expectations on succession, and name them in the policy rather than restating their wording.

            - name: Emergency chief executive succession plan
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Sudden death, serious illness, a scandal or an abrupt resignation can leave the organisation leaderless within hours, and boards that have not prepared lose days debating who is in charge. An emergency plan names who acts as chief executive immediately, who has authority to sign and speak, and what the board does in the first 48 hours.

                ## Milestones
                1. A named acting chief executive and an alternate, both of whom have agreed to the role.

                3. A first 48 hours checklist for the chair covering the board call, staff message, key stakeholders and media.
                4. The plan approved by the board and a copy held securely by the chair and company secretary.

                ## Notes
                The acting chief executive in an emergency is not automatically the long-term successor. Say so in the plan, so the appointment does not prejudge a later search.

            - name: Succession slates for each senior role
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A slate is the working document of succession: for each critical role, the two or three people who could step in and how ready they are. Keeping slates current every quarter, rather than rebuilding them the week before the annual review, means the board always sees the real picture and a resignation is absorbed in days.

                ## Milestones
                1. A slate for each critical role naming up to three successors, with readiness ratings and the main gap for each.
                2. External benchmark candidates noted where the internal slate is thin.
                3. Each slate confirmed by the role's succession owner.
                4. Slates updated at least four times a year, with the date of the last update visible.

                ## Notes
                Watch for the same two or three names appearing on every slate. One person cannot succeed five executives at once, so count each name once when judging bench strength.
              priority: high
              frontmatter:
                mode: operating
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "Every critical role has a slate of up to three successors with readiness ratings and gaps, updated within the last three months."
                cadence: rolling
              tasks:
                - "Create one slate page per critical role with successors, readiness and main gap"
                - "Ask each succession owner to confirm their slate"
                - "Count how many slates each named successor appears on"
                - "Update every slate with the quarter's changes @recurring(quarterly)"

# CEO and Executive Succession

This area is for chairs, nomination committee members, chief executives, heads of people and nonprofit trustees who need the top jobs covered before anyone leaves, and for the executives who may be in line for them. It starts with foundations (a readiness snapshot, critical roles, policy, ownership, emergency cover and success profiles), moves to the operating machinery of slates, talent reviews, development plans and retention watch, then the skills of assessing potential and interviewing, the decisions about insiders, search firms and timing, the dated work of a search, announcement and handover, situations for founders, nonprofits, small teams and named successors, and finally specialist work on cascades, disclosure and learning from each transition.

What repeats is a quarterly round of slate updates, talent reviews, successor board appearances and bench strength measures, monthly retention and development check-ins on different days, and a yearly cycle of the board succession review, the tenure conversation, emergency plan testing, profile refresh and external talent mapping. The Skills matrix, Development plan, Risk register, Metrics log, Vendor and Onboarding plan templates pair with the projects that name them.
