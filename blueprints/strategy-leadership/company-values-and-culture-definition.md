              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most companies already have values somewhere: a poster, a handbook page, a slide from an old offsite. Setting those words beside who was last promoted, praised and let go shows the gap between the culture you announce and the one you pay for, which is the honest starting point for any values work.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every existing values wording collected in one document with its source and date.
                2. The last ten promotions, awards and dismissals listed with the behaviour each one rewarded or punished.
                3. Each stated value marked as reinforced, ignored or contradicted by those decisions.
                4. A one-page summary of the gap shared with the sponsor.

                ## Notes
                Keep names out of the summary. The point is the pattern of what the organisation rewards, not a verdict on individuals.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:

            - name: Values working group drawn from every level
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Lists written by the executive team alone tend to describe how executives like to see themselves. A working group of six to ten people, mixing tenure, function, site and seniority, with one sponsor who can decide, gives the work credibility with staff and stops it becoming a branding exercise.

                ## Milestones
                1. One executive sponsor named with authority to approve the final set.
                2. Six to ten members chosen across levels, functions and sites, including at least one sceptic.
                3. A one-page charter agreed covering scope, time commitment, decision route and confidentiality.
                4. Managers of each member have agreed the time away from the day job.

                ## Notes
                Invite someone known for saying the values are nonsense. If the group can convince them, it can convince the building.
              priority: medium
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A signed one-page charter names the sponsor, six to ten members from different levels and the route by which the final values will be approved."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:
                - "Agree with the chief executive who sponsors the values work"

            - name: Culture listening interviews across levels and sites
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Surveys tell you how many people agree; interviews tell you why, and what they would never write in a form. Twenty to thirty confidential conversations, spread across levels, sites and tenure, surface the stories and unwritten rules the values must speak to.

                ## Milestones
                1. An interview guide of eight open questions agreed by the working group.
                2. Twenty or more interviews completed, with no level or site left out.
                3. Notes stored without names and coded into recurring themes.
                4. A themes paper with quotes, shared with the working group.

                ## Notes
                Start from the **Meeting notes** template for each conversation. Ask about specific moments: when did you feel proud here, and when did you think about leaving.

            - name: Founder's non-negotiables written down
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                In a founder-led company the real values often live in one head and come out as surprise vetoes. Writing down the five or six things the founder would close the company rather than compromise on makes them discussable, separates genuine principles from personal preferences, and gives the working group something firm to build around.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of the founder's non-negotiables, each with a real example of when it mattered.
                2. Each item sorted into principle, preference or habit.
                3. The principles discussed with two senior colleagues who can push back.
                4. A short note of which items go to the working group as inputs.

                ## Notes
                Preferences such as working hours or tidy desks are legitimate but they are not values. Keep them out of the final set.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A written list of the founder's non-negotiables, each sorted as principle, preference or habit with an example, has been reviewed by two colleagues."

              tasks:

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Any value is real only if the organisation would pay a price for it: lose a deal, slow a launch, part with a high performer. Testing each candidate against that question shrinks a list of fifteen pleasant words to four or five that genuinely shape choices.

                2. Each candidate tested with the question: when would this cost us money or speed?
                3. Candidates that no one can attach a cost to removed or moved to the code of conduct.
                4. A shortlist of four to six values with a cost example for each.

            - name: Behaviour statements for each value
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                One word like integrity means something different to everyone who reads it. Writing three to five observable behaviours under each value, plus what it does not mean, turns the list into something a manager can coach to and an interviewer can test for.

                ## Milestones

                2. One 'this does not mean' line per value to block common misreadings.
                3. Every statement checked to pass the test of two observers agreeing whether it happened.
                4. The full set of behaviours fitting on a single page.

                ## Notes
                Write behaviours as actions in the present tense: 'says no to work that cannot be done well', not 'is quality focused'.

            - name: Values-based questions in the interview loop
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Hiring is where values either become real or stay decorative, because every hire either strengthens or dilutes the culture. Assigning each value to one interviewer, with two behavioural questions and a scoring guide, replaces the vague 'culture fit' gut call with evidence that can be compared across candidates.

                ## Milestones
                1. Two behavioural questions written for each value, asking for past examples.
                2. A scoring guide with what weak, adequate and strong answers sound like.
                3. Each value assigned to a named interviewer in the standard loop.
                4. A quarterly calibration where interviewers compare scores on the same candidates.

                ## Notes
                Score values separately from skills. A strong skills score must not lift a weak values score in the debrief.
              priority: high

            - name: Values in performance reviews and promotion criteria
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                If people are promoted for results regardless of how they got them, staff will believe the promotion list over the poster.                 ## Notes

              priority: high

# Company Values and Culture Definition

This area is for the founder putting the company's values on paper for the first time, the manager asked to make a list of words mean something on the shop floor, and the enterprise leader replacing values that staff quietly mock. It starts with evidence about the culture you actually have, moves through choosing values, writing behaviours and testing them with staff, then builds the routines that put them into hiring, induction, reviews and recognition, and ends with situations such as rapid growth, frontline teams, a founder stepping back and the specialist work of measuring culture.

What repeats is deliberately modest: a monthly values story and recognition round, a monthly look at exit themes and the hard-decisions log, a quarterly culture pulse and leadership behaviour check, and a yearly values health review. The **Meeting notes** template pairs with the listening interviews, **Feedback themes** with the survey and focus groups, **Onboarding plan** with induction and **1:1** with the manager conversations.
