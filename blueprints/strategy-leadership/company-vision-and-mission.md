              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most organisations already have a mission somewhere: on the website, in the annual report, on a poster in reception, and the versions rarely match. Gathering every live version and checking which one leaders actually quote shows whether you need a light edit or a real rewrite before anyone starts wordsmithing.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every published version of the vision, mission and purpose collected in one document with its source and date.
                2. Differences between versions marked line by line.
                3. Five leaders asked to recite the mission from memory, with their answers recorded verbatim.
                4. A one-paragraph verdict written: keep, edit or rewrite.

                ## Notes
                Include job adverts, investor decks and the staff handbook. Old wording often survives in the places nobody owns.
              priority: high

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Vision work stalls when everyone can comment and nobody can decide. Naming one sponsor, a working group of four to six people and the exact route to final approval before drafting starts prevents the endless redraft loop and the late veto from someone who was left out.

                ## Milestones
                1. One executive sponsor named and agreed with the chief executive.
                2. A working group of four to six people chosen across functions and levels.
                3. The decision route written down: who drafts, who is consulted, who approves.
                4. A timetable with the board or owner approval date agreed.

                ## Notes
                Keep the chief executive or founder as approver, not drafter. Statements written by one person tend to read like a personal manifesto.

                Senior leaders often hold quite different pictures of where the company is going, and a group workshop tends to hide that.             - name: First draft mission statement with the refusal test
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                If any competitor could also sign your mission, it has no power to guide choices. Drafting three versions and testing each against what it would make you refuse, which customers, products and kinds of growth, separates a working mission from a decorative one.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three alternative mission drafts written, each under forty words.
                2. Each draft scored on whether a named competitor could use it unchanged.
                3. For each draft, two things it would rule out written beside it.
                4. One preferred draft chosen by the working group with notes on the others.

                ## Notes
                If a draft rules nothing out, it is not finished. Plain verbs and a named beneficiary beat abstract nouns every time.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 60
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "One preferred mission draft under forty words exists, with two named things it rules out and a competitor test passed."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "8"
              tasks:
                - "Write three mission drafts of under forty words each"
                - "Check whether your nearest competitor could sign each draft unchanged"
                - "List two things each draft would make you turn down"
                - "Pick a preferred draft and record why the others lost"

            - name: Stress test of the draft against past decisions
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                The quickest way to find out whether a draft mission has teeth is to run the last year's biggest decisions through it. If it would have changed none of them, it is too vague; if it would have forbidden the ones everyone is proud of, it is wrong.

                ## Milestones

# Company Vision and Mission

This area is for the founder writing a mission for the first time, the manager asked to make the company's purpose mean something to a team, and the enterprise leader refreshing statements that nobody quotes any more. It starts with an honest audit and the people who own the wording, moves through drafting, testing and approval, then builds the routines that keep the statements steering choices, and ends with situations such as a pivot, a merger, a new chief executive and mission drift.

What repeats is light but steady: a quarterly mission check in the leadership meeting, a monthly look at the decision log and the story bank, a staff understanding pulse and a yearly health review before the planning cycle. The **Meeting notes** template pairs with the interviews and workshop, **Feedback themes** with the staff sessions and **Board meeting** with the approval. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
