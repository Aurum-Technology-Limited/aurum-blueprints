            - name: Personal ethical red lines statement
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most leaders who cross a line they later regret never decided to; they were asked for something small on a busy day and had no answer ready. Writing down, before the pressure arrives, the five to eight things you will not do whatever the target or the instruction gives you a reply you have already rehearsed. It also shows where your grey zone starts, which is where most real dilemmas sit.

                ## Milestones
                1. A one-page statement of five to eight red lines, each phrased as a specific action you will refuse.
                2. Each line tested against a realistic scenario from your own sector.
                3. A short list of grey-zone situations where you will always pause and take advice.
                4. The statement shared with one trusted person who has agreed to hold you to it.

                ## Notes
                Write lines as behaviours, such as signing off a figure you know is wrong, not as virtues such as honesty. A virtue cannot tell you what to do at five o'clock on a Friday.
              priority: high

            - name: A written test for hard ethical calls
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Under pressure, people reach for whatever reasoning justifies the answer they already want. A short set of questions applied the same way every time (who is harmed, could I explain this to the person affected, would it survive the front page, what precedent does it set) slows the decision just enough to catch the obvious mistakes. Six questions on one card beat a philosophy degree at the moment of decision.

                ## Milestones
                1. Five to seven test questions written, covering harm, fairness, honesty, legality, precedent and publicity.
                2. The test applied to two past decisions to see whether it would have changed them.
                3. Questions that changed nothing cut or reworded after that trial.
                4. The finished card kept where you make decisions, on paper and on your phone.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A decision test of five to seven questions exists, has been trialled on two past decisions and is kept where you make decisions."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Draft six questions you would want answered before any hard ethical call"
                - "Apply the questions to two decisions you made in the last year"
                - "Cut or reword any question that did not change your thinking"
                - "Keep the finished test card beside your desk and on your phone"

# Ethical Leadership Dilemmas

This area is for managers, founders and executives who want to make hard ethical calls well rather than discover their standards in the middle of a crisis. It starts with foundations (red lines, your obligations under the code, conflicts declared, a decision test and people to call), moves through the routines that keep integrity in daily use, the skills of reasoning, refusing and listening to dissent, the specific dilemmas leaders most often face, the events worth preparing for and the situations of founders, middle managers and global executives, and ends with the panels and measures larger organisations build.

What repeats is a Friday check on what you bent, a monthly read of your decision log, a promise check, a team case discussion and a follow-up on open concerns, a quarterly ethics risk review, gifts check and look back at past calls, and a yearly refresh of your red lines, conflicts declaration and team refresher.
