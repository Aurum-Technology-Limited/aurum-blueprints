            - name: Deciding whether coaching is the right help
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Coaching works on behaviour you are willing to change; it is a poor fit for a skills gap a course would close, a mentor's industry advice or distress that needs a counsellor. Spending an hour naming the problem and who it affects before anyone books a coach saves months of sessions aimed at the wrong thing.

                ## Milestones
                1. The leadership problem written in two sentences, with one recent example of it happening.
                2. Coaching, mentoring, training and counselling compared against that problem in a short table.
                3. A decision recorded that coaching is, or is not, the right first step.
                4. If not coaching, the alternative chosen and a first step booked.

                ## Notes
                If stress, sleep or mood is the main issue, talk to your doctor or an employee assistance service first. A good coach will refer you there anyway.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A written decision on whether coaching is the right intervention, with the problem statement and the alternatives considered."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "2"
              tasks:
                - "Write the leadership problem in two sentences with one recent example"
                - "List what a coach, a mentor, a course and a counsellor would each offer"
                - "Ask one trusted colleague whether your problem statement matches what they see"
                - "Record your decision and the reason in one paragraph"
            - name: Coaching goals written in your own words
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Engagements drift when the goal is a label such as 'be more strategic' that nobody could observe. Two or three goals phrased as behaviours other people would notice, each with a reason it matters to the business, give the coach, the sponsor and you the same target from the first session.

                ## Milestones
                1. No more than three goals drafted, each describing a behaviour rather than a trait.
                2. For each goal, the business situation where the change would show up named.
                3. Each goal tested against the question 'how would my team know?'
                4. The goals shared with the coach before the first full session.

                ## Notes
                Expect the goals to sharpen after the stakeholder baseline. Write them now anyway; a draft gives the contracting meeting something to work on.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A one-page note with up to three observable behavioural goals and the business reason for each, sent to the coach."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "Write every frustration about your leadership you have heard in the last year"
                - "Group them into no more than three themes"
                - "Rewrite each theme as a behaviour someone could watch you do"
                - "Add the meeting or decision where each change would be visible"

            - name: Coach shortlist and chemistry calls
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Fit with the coach predicts more of the outcome than any single method, and the only way to test it is a short free conversation with each candidate. Meeting three coaches against the same questions, then choosing within a week, avoids both settling on the first name offered and dragging the search out for a quarter.

                4. One coach chosen and the other two thanked by message.

                When the organisation pays, your line manager or board chair is a party to the engagement whether or not anyone says so.             - name: Coaching agreement and confidentiality terms
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A signed agreement settles the practical questions before they become awkward: the number and length of sessions, cancellation terms, fees, how records are kept and who may see them. The confidentiality clause matters most, because a coachee who suspects the sponsor will hear everything stops bringing the real issues.

                ## Milestones
                1. Session count, length, format and term written into the agreement.

                3. The confidentiality clause, and its legal and safety exceptions, read and accepted.

                5. The agreement signed by the coach, you and the paying organisation if it is separate.

                ## Notes

            - name: Telling your team what you are working on
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Telling your direct reports which behaviour you are trying to change, and asking them to point out when they see it, makes them far more likely to notice the improvement. People judge change through what they expect, and a team that knows nothing assumes nothing has changed.

                ## Milestones
                1. One or two behaviours chosen that you are comfortable naming to the team.
                2. A short message written in plain words, without the confidential detail of sessions.
                3. The message delivered at a team meeting with a request for suggestions.
                4. Two team members thanked by name for a suggestion within the following month.

                ## Notes

# Executive Coaching Engagement

This area is for the manager or founder who is about to work with an executive coach, or already is, and wants the money and the hours to show up as different behaviour that other people notice. It starts with the foundations (deciding coaching is the right help, writing goals, funding, choosing and checking a coach, three-way contracting, a stakeholder baseline and a development plan), then the routines that make sessions count, the skills of using feedback and running behaviour experiments, the decisions about goals, coaches and endings, the dated reviews, situations for founders, newly promoted leaders and sponsoring managers, and finally the specialist work of team coaching, internal coaching panels and measuring results.

What repeats is the rhythm around the sessions: a prep note before each one, commitments written up after, a weekly experiment review, a monthly sponsor update and metrics entry, and a quarterly stakeholder pulse. The **Vendor**, **Feedback themes**, **Development plan**, **Meeting notes**, **Metrics log** and **1:1** templates pair with coach selection, the stakeholder baseline, the plan, session capture, progress tracking and the sponsoring manager's check-ins.
