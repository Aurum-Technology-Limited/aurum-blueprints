              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Presence is judged differently in a bank, a hospital trust and a software start-up, and advice that ignores the setting often makes people look as if they are acting. Writing down what the senior people who are respected in your organisation actually do, in plain behaviours rather than adjectives, gives you a target you can observe and practise.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three senior people in your organisation named whose presence colleagues rate highly.
                2. For each, five observable behaviours written down: how they open, how they handle challenge, how they sound, how they use silence, how they close.
                3. A one-page description of presence in your setting, in behaviours rather than words like gravitas.
                4. The page checked with one senior colleague for accuracy.

                ## Notes

              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most people misjudge how they come across, usually in both directions: they worry about things nobody notices and miss the habit everyone mentions. Asking five people who see you at work the same three short questions gives you a baseline in a fortnight without the machinery of a formal survey.

                ## Milestones

# Executive Presence Development

This area is for the manager or specialist who keeps hearing that they need more presence before the next step up, and wants to know what that means in practice and how to build it. It starts with the foundations (what presence looks like where you work, an honest baseline from colleagues and a recording, your manager's expectations and a plan with three behaviours), then the routines that build it week by week, the core skills of speaking, voice, stillness, challenge and listening, the decisions about habits, appearance and outside help, the dated moments such as an executive committee slot or an assessment centre, situations from leading former peers to working in a second language, and finally the specialist work of media interviews, hostile meetings and coaching others.

What repeats is small and steady: a weekly look at next week's high-stakes meetings, a weekly rehearsal slot, a two-minute reset before key moments, a monthly recording review and progress log entry, a quarterly visibility plan and a yearly review against your original baseline. The 1:1, Development plan, Habit tracker, Metrics log, Reading queue and Purchase decision templates pair with the projects that name them.
