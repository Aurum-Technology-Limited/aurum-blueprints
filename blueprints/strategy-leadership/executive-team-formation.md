            - name: Executive team purpose in one paragraph
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most leadership groups cannot say what they do together that their members could not do alone, so their time drifts into functional updates. Writing a single paragraph on the work only this team can do, such as setting enterprise priorities, allocating capital between functions and owning the culture, gives every later decision about membership and goals something to test against.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of the five to eight decisions or outcomes that genuinely need the whole team, not one function.
                2. A paragraph of no more than 120 words describing the team's collective purpose.
                3. Every current member's reaction to the draft gathered in a short conversation.
                4. A final version agreed and placed at the top of the team's shared folder.

                ## Notes
                If the list of collective work is short, that is useful information: the group may be better run as a working group with a smaller core team.

            - name: Executive team size and membership decision
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Research on top teams suggests that groups above eight or nine people struggle to hold real debate, yet removing someone from the leadership table is one of the most sensitive calls a chief executive makes. Deciding the size and the criteria for membership on paper, before any names are attached, makes the final list easier to explain and to defend.

                ## Milestones
                1. Written membership criteria, for example enterprise accountability, scope of decisions and capability needed.
                2. A target size agreed with a reason, typically between five and nine.
                3. Each current member and candidate tested against the criteria, not against seniority.
                4. A final membership list with a plan for how each change will be communicated.

                ## Notes
                People who leave the core team can still join a wider leadership forum. Offering that forum before the conversation, not after it, keeps the change from feeling like a demotion.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A recorded membership decision naming the team's size, criteria and members, with a communication plan for each change."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "8"
              tasks:
                - "Write three to five membership criteria before considering any names"
                - "Choose a target team size and note why"

                Job descriptions written for recruitment rarely say what an executive is accountable for once they sit on the team, which is where turf disputes start. A one-page charter per seat, covering outcomes owned, decisions held, key interfaces and the enterprise contribution expected, removes most of the ambiguity that otherwise surfaces in the first argument over a budget or a hire.

            - name: Executive team charter and working agreements
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Teams that never agree how they will work fall back on the habits of whoever is most senior or most vocal. A short charter covering purpose, membership, collective goals, how decisions are made in the room, how conflict is handled and what stays confidential gives new and existing members the same rules and makes it fair to call out a breach.

            - name: Collective goals the whole team owns
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                If every executive goal belongs to a single function, the team has no reason to work together and every review becomes a series of individual reports. Choosing two to four goals that no executive can hit alone, such as a customer retention figure or a company-wide launch, and holding everyone jointly accountable is the clearest signal that this is a team.

                ## Milestones
                1. A shortlist of outcomes that require at least three functions to deliver.
                2. Two to four collective goals chosen with a measure and a target date each.
                3. Every executive's contribution to each collective goal written in one line.
                4. Collective goal progress reviewed by the team every month.

                ## Notes
                Joint accountability only works if it shows up in performance reviews and incentives. If it never does, people will learn that it does not matter.
              priority: high
              frontmatter:
                mode: operating
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "Two to four collective goals with measures and owners are recorded and reviewed by the full team every month."
                cadence: rolling
              tasks:
                - "List outcomes from the strategy that need three or more functions"
                - "Choose two to four collective goals with a measure each"
                - "Write each executive's contribution to every collective goal"
                - "Review collective goal progress with the full team @recurring(monthly:15)"
            - name: Baseline executive team effectiveness survey
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Without a baseline you cannot tell whether a year of effort has made the team better or simply busier. A confidential survey of 20 to 30 questions covering clarity of purpose, trust, quality of debate, commitment and accountability, completed by every member, shows where the team sees itself today and where perceptions differ most.

                ## Milestones
                1. A survey instrument chosen or written, covering purpose, trust, conflict, commitment and results.
                2. Every member's response collected anonymously.
                3. Results summarised with the three highest and three lowest scoring items and the widest spreads.
                4. The results discussed openly in a team session and kept as the baseline.

                ## Notes
                The spread between members often tells you more than the average. A question where scores range from 2 to 9 is a conversation waiting to happen.
              priority: medium
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:

                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A completed anonymous effectiveness survey from every member, summarised and discussed, saved as the team's baseline."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "6"
              tasks:
                - "Choose a published team effectiveness questionnaire or draft 25 questions"
                - "Send the survey through an anonymous form with a one week window"
                - "Summarise the highest, lowest and most divided items on one page"
                - "Rerun the same survey to compare with the baseline @recurring(yearly)"

            - name: Practising productive conflict at the top table
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Executive teams that never argue usually make worse decisions, because the objections surface later as quiet non-compliance. Practising structured debate on real issues, using techniques like assigning a devil's advocate, asking each member for their view before the chief executive speaks and naming the trade-off explicitly, builds a habit of disagreeing about ideas without damaging relationships.

                ## Milestones
                1. Two or three structured debate techniques chosen for the team to try.
                2. Each technique used on a real decision in at least two meetings.
                3. Members asked afterwards whether they said what they really thought.
                4. The technique that worked best adopted as standard for major decisions.

                ## Notes
                The chief executive speaking last is the single simplest change. Many teams find it moves the quality of debate more than any formal method.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: learning
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "Two structured debate techniques are trialled on real decisions over six weeks and one is adopted as standard for major choices."
                cadence: phased
                effort_hours_estimate: "8"
              tasks:
                - "Choose two structured debate techniques to trial"
                - "Use the first technique on a real decision at the next team meeting"
                - "Ask members privately whether they said what they really thought"
                - "Adopt the technique that produced the most honest debate"

            - name: Assessing executive candidates for team fit
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Executive hires usually fail because of how they work with peers, not because they lack functional skill, yet most interview processes test only the function. Learning to assess candidates against your team charter, through peer interviews, a realistic team exercise and referencing focused on collaboration, makes it far likelier that a new hire strengthens the team rather than splitting it.

                ## Milestones
                1. Team fit criteria written from the charter and working agreements.
                2. A structured peer interview guide with questions tied to each criterion.

# Executive Team Formation

This area is for chief executives, founders and senior leaders who want a leadership group that acts as one team rather than a collection of department heads reporting in turn. It starts with the foundations (why the team exists, who is on it, what each seat owns and what the team is collectively accountable for), then the operating habits that keep it healthy, the skills of trust, conflict and peer feedback, the hard staffing decisions, the dated launches and reviews, the situations founders and new chief executives face, and finally the specialist work of designing a top team for the next stage of scale.

What repeats is a weekly 1:1 with each executive and a Friday commitments check, monthly health pulses, peer pair check-ins and disagreement reviews on different days, quarterly effectiveness and capability reviews, and a yearly refresh of role charters, incentives and team composition. The Skills matrix, 1:1, Reading queue, Vendor, Onboarding 30/60/90 and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them.
