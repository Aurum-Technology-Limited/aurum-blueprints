            - name: Three-circle map of family, ownership and management
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most arguments in a family company happen because one person is speaking as a parent, another as a shareholder and a third as an employee, and nobody has said which. Placing every relative and key manager on the three overlapping circles of family, ownership and management shows who has a legitimate say on which questions, and where the friction is likely to come from.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every family member over sixteen, every shareholder and every senior manager placed in one of the seven segments of the three-circle model.
                2. The segment with the most people and the segment with the most tension each named in a sentence.
                3. Three recurring disagreements traced back to people speaking from different circles.
                4. The map shared with the senior generation and any adult family members working in the business.

                ## Notes
                Draw it by hand first. Spouses who own nothing and work elsewhere still sit in the family circle, and their views reach the business through the dinner table whether or not the map shows it.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A one-page three-circle map naming every relevant person, with the main tension points written beside it, has been shared with the family members in the business."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "3"
              tasks:
                - "List every family member, shareholder and senior manager on one sheet"
                - "Place each name in the family, ownership or management circle, or an overlap"
                - "Write down three recent disagreements and which circle each side spoke from"
                - "Walk the senior generation through the map over a quiet coffee"
            - name: Share register and ownership documents in one place
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Many family companies cannot say with confidence who owns which shares, because transfers between parents and children were agreed over dinner and never filed. Gathering the share register, articles, any shareholder agreement, trust deeds and wills that mention the shares, and checking them against the company registry, is the base every later governance and succession decision rests on.

                ## Milestones
                1. The current share register, articles of association and any shareholder agreement collected in one secure folder.
                2. Every shareholding checked against the filing at the company registry, with any mismatch listed.
                3. Trusts, holding companies and nominee arrangements that hold shares identified with their trustees or directors.
                4. A list of open questions sent to the company lawyer for answers in writing.

                ## Notes
                Treat this as record keeping, not legal advice. Where documents disagree, the company lawyer decides what the true position is and how to correct it.
              priority: high

                ## Purpose
                Families often discover only during a crisis that one branch wants to sell, another wants to grow at any cost and the founder assumed the grandchildren would take over.             - name: Separating household money from company accounts
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Personal bills paid from the company card, family cars on the books and cash taken without a record create tax exposure, resentment from siblings who do not work there, and accounts no bank or buyer can trust. Agreeing with the accountant how family money and company money will be kept apart, and then checking it every month, is one of the quickest ways to make the business look professionally run.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every personal cost currently paid by the company listed with its amount and the family member it benefits.
                2. The accountant's advice on each item recorded: stop, repay, put through payroll or document as a benefit.
                3. Separate cards and bank accounts in place for household and company spending.
                4. A monthly check of the director's loan account or equivalent running for three months.

                ## Notes
                Tax treatment differs by country and by company type. Let the accountant decide how each item is handled; your job is to find them all and stop new ones creeping in.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: habit
                success_criteria: "No personal spending has passed through company accounts for three consecutive months, confirmed by a monthly check with the bookkeeper."
                cadence: phased
                effort_hours_estimate: "6"
              tasks:
                - "Go through the last three months of company card statements for personal items"
                - "Book a meeting with the accountant to agree treatment of each item"

                - "Review the director's loan or family drawings balance with the bookkeeper @recurring(monthly:4)"
            - name: Continuity plan if a senior family owner is suddenly unavailable
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                In a family company the founder often holds the bank mandate, the key supplier relationships, the passwords and the only copy of the will, so a stroke or an accident can freeze the business for weeks. A written continuity plan naming who signs, who runs operations and who speaks for the family keeps wages paid and customers served while the family deals with the person, not the paperwork.

                ## Milestones

                4. A sealed instruction letter stored where two named people can reach it.

                ## Notes
                This covers the first ninety days. Longer-term leadership and ownership succession have their own projects further down the list.

            - name: Family employment policy
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Without a written policy, every nephew who needs a job becomes a negotiation, and capable non-family staff watch less qualified relatives get promoted past them. A family employment policy sets the qualifications, outside experience and application route for family members, how they are managed and paid, and how they leave, so the answer is the same for every relative.

                ## Milestones
                1. Entry rules agreed: minimum education, years of outside work experience and an open vacancy to apply for.
                2. Rules on who manages and reviews family employees, with no relative reporting directly to a parent where avoidable.
                3. Pay and promotion rules tied to the company's normal grades.
                4. An exit route written for a family employee who is not working out.
                5. The policy agreed by the family council or owners and shared with the management team.

                ## Notes
                Many families require two to five years of work outside the company before a relative joins. Whatever you choose, write it before the next request, not during it.
              priority: high

# Family Business Leadership

This area is for owners, family members and non-family managers in a family-owned company, from a husband and wife running a shop to a third-generation group with thirty shareholders. It starts with foundations (who owns what, which hat each person wears, pay, employment rules and continuity cover), moves to the operating machinery of family council, owners' updates and reviews, then the skills of governance, accounts, valuation and hard conversations, the decisions about a constitution, independent directors, outside executives and ownership options, the dated events of retreats, placements and handovers, situations for founders, successors, siblings, cousins, couples and in-laws, and finally specialist work on ownership succession and shareholder exits.

What repeats is a weekly management meeting kept apart from family time, monthly one-to-ones and a monthly look at money flowing between the company and the household, a quarterly family council, owners' update and risk review, and a yearly cycle of family assembly, dividend decision, owners' expectations letter, pay benchmarking and constitution review. The 1:1, Meeting notes, Risk register, Onboarding plan and Development plan templates pair with the projects that name them.
