              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Plenty of strong individual contributors are offered a team because they are good at the work, not because they want to spend their days on people. Before saying yes, or before asking for the role, test what the job really involves: fewer hours on the craft, more on conversations, hiring and other people's problems. A deliberate choice now saves a painful step back eighteen months later.

                ## Milestones
                1. Three working managers interviewed about how their typical week is really spent.
                2. A written list of what you would give up and what you would gain by managing.
                3. Your organisation's senior specialist track checked, including its pay range and promotion criteria.
                4. A yes, no or not yet decision written down with the reason behind it.

                ## Notes
                Not yet is a legitimate answer. Leading a project or covering for an absent manager first is a cheaper test than a permanent change of role.

            - name: Getting-to-know-you conversation with each report
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Before changing anything, a new manager needs to know what each person does, what they are proud of, what frustrates them and how they like to be managed. A forty-five minute conversation with every report in the first three weeks, using the same questions each time, gives you a fair picture of the team and shows each person they are worth your time.

                ## Milestones
                1. A set of six to eight questions written and reused for every report.
                2. Every report met individually within three weeks.
                3. Notes from each conversation saved in one place per person.
                4. The common themes across the team listed, with anything urgent flagged.

                ## Notes
                Useful questions: what should I not change, what slows you down, how do you like to get feedback, what would you like to be doing in two years. Listen far more than you talk; this is not the moment for your plans.

# First-Time Manager Leadership

This area is for anyone leading people for the first time, and for the knowledge worker deciding whether to take that step at all. It starts with the foundations (expectations agreed with your own manager, a first conversation with every report, handing over your old work and learning the people policies you now own), then the weekly machinery of one-to-ones, team meetings, delegation and feedback, the core skills of coaching, goal setting and difficult conversations, a set of practical fixes, the first review and pay rounds, the situations that catch new managers out, such as leading former peers, and finally the harder specialist work of underperformance and supporting someone in crisis.

What repeats is a weekly one-to-one with each report, a Monday priorities post, a Wednesday feedback note, a Thursday update to your own manager and a Friday delegation check, with monthly people notes, workload checks and recognition on different days, quarterly career conversations and meeting audits, and a yearly pay round and manager README refresh. The Skills matrix, Onboarding 30/60/90, 1:1, Person, Development plan and Onboarding plan templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
