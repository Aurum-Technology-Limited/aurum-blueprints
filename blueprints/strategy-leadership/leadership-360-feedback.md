                Inviting only people who like you produces a flattering report, and inviting too few in any group breaks anonymity.             - name: Anonymity and report access rules
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                People will only be candid if they believe their comments cannot be traced back to them, and that belief is easily broken by a group of two or a manager who reads raw comments. Writing down the minimum group size, who sees which parts of the report and how long the data is kept protects raters and your own credibility.

                ## Milestones

                3. A rule that raw comments are never shown to anyone with authority over the raters.
                4. A data retention period agreed with the administrator.

                ## Notes

            - name: Reading a 360 report fairly
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Behind the headline average of a 360 report sit ranges, norm comparisons and rater group breakdowns, and the average often hides the real story. Learning to read the distribution, the spread within each group and the difference between statistically meaningful and trivial gaps stops you overreacting to a 0.2 difference or missing a split team.

                ## Milestones
                1. The report's scale, norm group and minimum rater rules understood.
                2. Spread and range checked for every item, not just the averages.
                3. Gaps smaller than the instrument's meaningful threshold set aside.
                4. Three findings written down that the averages alone would have hidden.

                ## Notes
                A middling average with a wide spread usually means different people see very different leaders. Ask the facilitator how the instrument treats this.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: learning
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "Three written findings from your report that come from ranges, spreads or group differences rather than headline averages."
                cadence: phased

# Leadership 360 Feedback

This area is for a manager at any level who wants to know how their leadership lands with the people around them, through structured feedback from their own manager, peers, direct reports and key partners. It starts with the foundations (a purpose for the 360, the instrument, the raters, the anonymity rules and your self-assessment), then the routines that turn a report into changed behaviour, the skills of reading scores and comments fairly, the choices about themes and what to share, the debrief and share-back events, the situations that change how a 360 should run, and finally the work of sponsoring and designing 360s for others.

What repeats is a weekly behaviour practice log, a monthly progress check and one feedback ask a month, a quarterly pulse on your focus behaviours, and a fresh 360 every year or eighteen months. The Vendor, Feedback themes, Development plan, Metrics log, 1:1 and Meeting notes templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
