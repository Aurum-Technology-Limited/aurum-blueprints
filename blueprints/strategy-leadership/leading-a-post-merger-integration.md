            - name: Integration value case on one page
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Deals are approved on a model, but integrations are run by people who have often never seen it. Translating the board paper into one page, with the three to five sources of value, the figure attached to each and the year it was promised, gives every workstream the same answer to the question of why this deal was done.

                ## Milestones
                1. The board paper, deal model and any lender or regulator submissions gathered in one folder.
                2. Three to five sources of value named, each with its figure and the year it was promised.
                3. The things the integration must not break, such as key customers, licences or a product line, listed beside them.
                4. A one-page value case reviewed by the deal sponsor and the finance lead.
                5. The page shared with every workstream lead before their first planning session.

                ## Notes
                Keep the deal model's numbers and the integration plan's numbers in one place from the start. Two versions of the savings target, one in finance and one in the integration office, is the most common source of later arguments.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A one-page value case naming each source of value, its figure and its promised year is signed off by the deal sponsor and held by every workstream lead."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "8"
              tasks:
                - "Request the final board paper and deal model from the deal team"
                - "List each source of value with its figure and promised year"
                - "Write down the three things the integration must not damage"
                - "Review the draft page with the deal sponsor and finance lead"
            - name: Integration leader mandate and sponsor agreement
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Integration leaders are often handed the title without the authority, then spend months negotiating with functional heads who outrank them. Agreeing in writing what you can decide alone, what goes to the steering group and how much of your time and budget the role carries prevents that stall in the first month.

                ## Milestones
                1. A written mandate listing the decisions the integration leader can take alone.
                2. An escalation route to a named executive sponsor, with a response time agreed.
                3. Your split of time between integration and your existing role settled, with cover for the rest.
                4. The mandate sent by the sponsor, not by you, to both leadership teams.

                ## Notes
                If the sponsor will not put the mandate in writing, treat that as information about how much support you will get later.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 14
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A mandate signed by the executive sponsor sets out the integration leader's decision rights, escalation route and time allocation, and has been sent to both leadership teams."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:

            - name: Integration management office set-up
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Integrations with twenty workstreams and no central office drift into twenty separate projects with clashing deadlines. A small integration management office, often three to five people, owns the plan, the decision log, the reporting pack and the cadence, and lets workstream leads get on with the work.

                ## Milestones
                1. Office roles filled: lead, planner, finance analyst and communications partner at minimum.
                2. Workstreams named, each with a lead from each legacy organisation where both exist.
                3. One integrated planning tool and file structure chosen, with access granted.
                4. A standard status template and reporting calendar issued to every workstream.
                5. A kick-off session held with all workstream leads.

                ## Notes
                Pair a lead from each side on the bigger workstreams. It costs time, but it is the fastest way to find out what the other organisation actually does.
              priority: high

            - name: Degree of integration by function
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Not everything should be combined. For each function, decide whether the acquired business is absorbed, kept standalone, combined as best of both or rebuilt as something new, because that single choice sets the cost, the risk to people and the speed of every workstream that follows.

                ## Milestones
                1. Every function and business unit from both organisations listed in one grid.
                2. One of four approaches chosen for each row: absorb, preserve, best of both or transform.
                3. The reason for each choice tied back to the value case.
                4. Functions left standalone given a date when the choice will be revisited.
                5. The grid approved by the steering group and published to workstream leads.

                ## Notes
                Preserving the acquired company's product or engineering teams is often what protects its value. Absorb the back office first and leave the parts customers feel until you understand them.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A grid showing the agreed integration approach for every function, each with a reason linked to the value case, has been approved by the steering group."
                cadence: one-shot

              tasks:
                - "List every function and business unit from both organisations in one grid"

                ## Purpose
                Before completion the two companies are still separate competitors in law, and sharing prices, customer terms or pay data too early can breach competition rules and delay or sink the deal. Setting up a clean team with written rules lets planning go ahead on sensitive data without exposing either side.

                ## Milestones

                ## Notes

            - name: Critical people retention plan
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                In most acquisitions the people who leave first are the ones with the best options: top sales staff, senior engineers and the managers who hold customer relationships. Naming the 30 to 60 people whose departure would damage the value case, and agreeing what each needs to stay, is one of the few integration actions that cannot wait for Day 1.

                ## Milestones
                1. A list of critical people from both organisations, with the reason each one matters.
                2. Flight risk rated for each person by their current manager.
                3. A retention approach agreed for each: a role conversation, a retention award, a project or a senior sponsor.
                4. Every person on the list contacted by a senior leader within two weeks of announcement.
                5. A monthly review of the list and any departures in place.

                ## Notes
                Money alone rarely keeps people through an integration. Clarity about their future role, delivered early by someone senior, matters as much. Agree any retention award terms with HR and legal.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: deliverable
                success_criteria: "A named list of critical people with flight risk ratings and an agreed retention approach for each exists, and everyone on it has had a senior conversation within two weeks of announcement."
                cadence: phased
                effort_hours_estimate: "12"
              tasks:
                - "Ask each business head to name the people whose loss would hurt the deal most"

# Leading a Post-Merger Integration

This area is for the manager or executive asked to bring two organisations together after a merger or acquisition, and to do it without losing the people, customers and value the deal was bought for. It starts with the foundations (the value case, your mandate, the integration office, the degree of integration by function, clean team rules, the baseline, retention and targets), then the operating machinery, the skills worth learning, the big choices on structure, systems, terms and footprint, the dated events from announcement day to close-out, situations such as a merger of equals, a cross-border deal or a carve-out, and finally the specialist work of cross-selling, regulated operations, earn-outs and a repeatable playbook.

What repeats is the integration rhythm: a weekly steering group, status pack and decision review, a weekly update to staff, monthly checks on savings, costs, customers, retention, risks and obligations, and a quarterly employee pulse. The **Risk register**, **Meeting notes**, **Metrics log**, **Month-end close** and **Onboarding 30/60/90** templates pair with the risk register, the steering group, the savings tracker, the first combined close and the seconded lead's first months.
