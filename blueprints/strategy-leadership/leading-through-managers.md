              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Leaders who inherit or grow into a large organisation often cannot say how many layers sit between them and the front line, or which managers carry twelve reports while others carry two. Drawing the map once, with names, spans and layers, shows where messages are likely to be lost and where a manager is stretched too thin to lead at all.

                ## Milestones
                1. A chart showing every manager in your organisation, their reports and their layer below you.
                2. Span of control and layer count recorded for each team.
                3. The three teams with the widest and narrowest spans marked for attention.
                4. The map shared with your direct managers and corrected where they spot errors.

                ## Notes

# Leading Through Managers

This area is for heads of function, directors and general managers who now lead people they rarely work with directly, because every message, decision and piece of bad news travels through at least one layer of managers first. It starts with the foundations (a map of your layers, written intent, clear expectations of your managers and a baseline of each team's health), then the operating habits that keep you connected, the coaching and diagnosis skills a manager of managers needs, the improvements and hard decisions about individual managers, the dated cycles and announcements, the situations you may be leading in, and finally the specialist work of running a large organisation through its managers on purpose.

What repeats is a weekly 1:1 with each direct manager and a Friday note to all managers, monthly skip-level conversations, front-line visits, a managers' forum and a team health check on different days, quarterly talent and coaching reviews, and a yearly refresh of your intent, calendar and cover plans. The Feedback themes, 1:1, Meeting notes, Metrics log, Development plan and Onboarding 30/60/90 templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
