            - name: Timing and readiness for a new strategic plan
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Charities often start a new plan because the old one has expired, not because they are ready, and a process launched during a chief executive vacancy or a funding crisis usually stalls. A short readiness check, covering leadership stability, trustee appetite, cash for the process and any decision that must come first, tells you whether to start now, start smaller or extend the current plan for a year.

                ## Milestones
                1. A readiness checklist answered by the chair and chief executive, covering leadership, cash, trustee time and pending decisions.
                2. Any blocker that must be settled first, such as a vacancy or a funding cliff, named in writing.
                3. A recommendation to start now, run a lighter refresh or extend the current plan, put to the trustees.
                4. The trustees' decision and the agreed start month recorded in the minutes.

                ## Notes
                Extending a plan by twelve months is a legitimate choice. It is better than a rushed plan nobody believes.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "The trustees have minuted a decision to start a new plan, run a lighter refresh or extend the current one, with a start month."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:
                - "List the end date of the current plan and any major decision due in the next year"
                - "Ask the chair and chief executive to answer the readiness questions separately"
                - "Compare their answers and draft a start, refresh or extend recommendation"
                - "Put the recommendation to the next trustee meeting for a decision"
            - name: Strategy steering group of trustees and staff
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                When the chief executive writes the plan alone, trustees feel handed a finished document; when the whole board drafts it, meetings drown in wording. A small steering group of two or three trustees, the chief executive and one or two senior staff, with written terms of reference, keeps the work moving between board meetings and makes clear what the full board still decides.

                ## Milestones
                1. Terms of reference stating the group's purpose, members, meeting frequency and what it may decide.
                2. Members confirmed, including at least one trustee with finance experience and one staff member close to service delivery.
                3. A clear line written between what the group recommends and what the full board approves.
                4. The terms of reference approved by the board.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "Board-approved terms of reference name the steering group's members, its meeting rhythm and the decisions reserved to the full board."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "4"
              tasks:
                - "Draft one page of terms of reference for the strategy steering group"
                - "Ask the chair which trustees have time for monthly meetings until approval"

                - "Take the terms of reference to the board for approval"
            - name: Planning process timeline and budget
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Most nonprofit strategy processes take six to nine months from first meeting to approval, and they overrun when consultation, drafting and board dates are not lined up in advance. A one-page timeline working back from the approval meeting, with a small budget for venues, facilitation and consultation costs, stops the process drifting into a second year.

                ## Milestones
                1. The board meeting at which the plan will be approved fixed as the end date.
                2. A timeline with dates for evidence gathering, consultation, the away day, drafting and approval.
                3. A budget for the process covering facilitation, venues, travel and thank-you costs for participants.
                4. The timeline shared with staff so nobody is surprised by the consultation weeks.

                ## Notes
                Leave four weeks between the final draft and the approval meeting. Trustees need time to read, and staff need time to see the draft before it is final.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building

                cadence: one-shot

            - name: Charitable objects and governing document check
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Charitable objects limit what a charity may spend its money on, and new ideas such as a trading arm, work in another country or a new beneficiary group can fall outside them. Reading the objects and powers at the start, and taking advice where an idea sits near the edge, avoids a plan the trustees are not allowed to deliver.

                ## Notes

            - name: Theory of change for the core mission
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                A theory of change sets out the long-term change you exist for, the outcomes that lead to it and the activities that cause those outcomes, with the assumptions made explicit. Building one with staff, trustees and some of the people you serve forces the questions a strategic plan otherwise dodges, such as which activities contribute little to the outcomes that matter.

                ## Milestones
                1. A long-term goal agreed in one sentence.
                2. The intermediate outcomes and the activities that lead to them drawn as a single diagram.
                3. The key assumptions behind each link written down and the weakest ones marked.
                4. The diagram tested with at least five service users or frontline staff and revised.
                5. The final version adopted as the basis for the strategic plan's priorities.

                ## Notes
                Keep it to one page. A theory of change with forty boxes describes everything and decides nothing.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 90
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact
                success_criteria: "A one-page theory of change, tested with service users or frontline staff, is adopted by the steering group as the basis for priorities."
                cadence: phased
                effort_hours_estimate: "12"
              tasks:

                - "Run a workshop to map outcomes backwards from that goal"

                - "Test the draft with five service users or frontline staff"
                - "Revisit the theory of change against new evidence @recurring(yearly)"

            - name: Growth or depth decision for core services
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Funders reward reach, so charities are pulled to serve more people more thinly, sometimes losing the intensity that made their results good. # Nonprofit Strategic Planning

This area is for chief executives, senior managers and trustees of charities and nonprofits, from a volunteer-run group to a national network, setting a strategy that fits the mission, the money and the people available. It starts with foundations (readiness, a steering group, the timeline, the last plan's results, the governing document, finances, need, the wider landscape and capacity), moves through the skills of theory of change, full costing, funds, outcomes, participation and facilitation, then the big choices on focus, priorities, programmes, funding, reserves and collaboration, the dated consultation, away day, approval and launch, the operating rhythm that delivers the plan, situations such as losing a major funder or inheriting a plan, and finally specialist work on systems change, mergers and independent evaluation.

What repeats is a monthly round of outcome data entry, management accounts by priority, funding pipeline review and a staff strategy slot on different days, quarterly trustee progress reports, risk register and feedback reviews, and a yearly cycle of operating plan, reserves check, funding mix review, impact report and theory of change refresh. The Skills matrix, Feedback themes, Meeting notes, Board meeting, Metrics log and Risk register templates pair with the projects that name them.
