            - name: Due diligence before accepting a board seat
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Joining a board ties your name and your legal duties to an organisation you do not run, so the time to find problems is before you accept. Reading the last two years of accounts, minutes and risk reports, and asking the chair, chief executive and auditor direct questions, tells you whether this is a board you can contribute to and leave with your reputation intact.

                ## Milestones
                1. The last two annual reports and the latest management accounts read.
                2. Board minutes and the risk register for the past year reviewed.
                3. Private conversations held with the chair, chief executive and at least one other director.
                4. A written list of red flags, each either answered or still open.
                5. A clear yes or no recorded, with conditions if any.

                ## Notes
                Ask why the vacancy exists and why the last person left. A reluctance to share minutes or meet the auditor is itself an answer.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 45
              frontmatter:

                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A due diligence note covers accounts, minutes, risks and conversations, with every red flag marked resolved or open and a final decision recorded."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "12"
              tasks:
                - "Request the last two annual reports and recent board minutes"
                - "Search news, court and regulator records for the organisation's name"

                - "Ask to speak to the external auditor or audit committee chair"
                - "Write your red flag list and mark each item resolved or open"

# Serving as a Non-Executive Director

This area is for anyone who sits, or wants to sit, on a board without running the organisation: a senior manager taking a first outside seat, an executive adding a listed company role, a charity trustee or a school or public body governor. It runs from foundations (whether to do it at all, finding a seat, due diligence, appointment terms, insurance and duties) through the personal routines of reading packs, declaring interests and spending time with the business, then the skills of challenge, finance, risk and committee work, the decisions about conflicts, dissent and portfolio size, the dated moments like a first meeting or signing the accounts, the situations particular to trustees, employed executives and family boards, and finally the specialist work of distress, investigations, investors and chairing.

What repeats is a monthly pack reading block, time budget check and sector reading on different days, quarterly conversations with the chair and site visits, a quarterly look at your declared interests and confidential papers, and a yearly self-review, fee record and portfolio check. The Resume, Onboarding plan, 1:1, Risk register, Board meeting and Development plan templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
