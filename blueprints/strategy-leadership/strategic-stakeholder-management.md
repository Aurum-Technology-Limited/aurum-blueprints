            - name: Strategy dependencies on outside support
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Every strategy rests on a handful of yeses that the organisation cannot give itself: a licence renewed, a funder's second grant, a partner's channel, a union's agreement to new shifts. Going through the strategy choice by choice and writing down whose support each one needs turns stakeholder work from general goodwill into a list tied to what the plan must deliver.

                ## Milestones
                1. Each major strategic choice listed with the outside or internal approvals and support it relies on.
                2. Every dependency marked as a formal approval, a resource, a channel or informal consent.
                3. The five dependencies that would most damage the strategy if withdrawn identified.
                4. The list reviewed by at least two members of the leadership team.

                ## Notes
                Include internal stakeholders who hold a veto in practice, such as a regional director or a works council, even if the organisation chart says they do not.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 21
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: knowledge
                success_criteria: "A written list linking each strategic choice to the named stakeholders it depends on, with the top five dependencies agreed by the leadership team."
                cadence: one-shot
                effort_hours_estimate: "6"
              tasks:
                - "Open the current strategy and list its five to ten main choices"
                - "Write beside each choice who must approve, fund, supply or tolerate it"
                - "Rank the dependencies by damage if that support were withdrawn"
                - "Walk two leadership colleagues through the top five and record their changes"
            - name: Named stakeholder map for the current strategy
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Maps that say "government" or "the community" are useless when it is time to pick up the phone. Placing thirty to sixty named people and groups on a grid of influence over the strategy and interest in it shows where attention is needed, who is being neglected and who is getting far more time than their influence warrants.

                ## Milestones
                1. A long list of named individuals and organisations drawn from the dependency list and colleagues' knowledge.
                2. Each one placed on an influence and interest grid with a one-line reason.
                3. Groups split into the people who actually decide or speak for them.
                4. The map checked by colleagues in at least three functions.

                ## Notes
                Name people, not institutions, wherever you can. A ministry has a permanent secretary, a policy lead and a minister's adviser, and they rarely want the same thing.
              priority: high
              deadlineOffsetDays: 30
              frontmatter:
                mode: building
                output_kind: artifact

                cadence: one-shot

              tasks:

                ## Purpose

# Strategic Stakeholder Management

This area is for the leader whose strategy needs other people to say yes: investors, funders, regulators, ministers, partners, major customers, unions, communities and the senior colleagues who can quietly stall a plan. It starts with the foundations (which parts of the strategy depend on whom, the map, the profiles, the gap between where people stand and where you need them, and an engagement plan), then the operating machinery that keeps relationships warm, the skills of reading interests, listening, negotiating and building coalitions, the decisions about time, disclosure and engagement levels, the dated moments such as announcements and consultations, situations for nonprofits, enterprises and people inheriting relationships, and finally the specialist work of public affairs, materiality and relationship health measures.

What repeats is a weekly interaction log and concerns triage, monthly temperature checks, commitment reviews, risk reviews and a leadership agenda slot, a quarterly map refresh and time audit, and a yearly perception study and report back. The **Person**, **Feedback themes**, **Risk register**, **Meeting notes**, **Purchase decision** and **Metrics log** templates pair with the stakeholder profiles, the concerns log, the stakeholder risk register, pre-meeting briefings, the choice of a relationship tool and the relationship health index.
