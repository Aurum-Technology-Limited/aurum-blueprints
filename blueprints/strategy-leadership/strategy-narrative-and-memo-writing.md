            - name: One-page strategy statement in diagnosis, policy and actions
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Richard Rumelt's kernel of good strategy, a diagnosis of the challenge, a guiding policy and a set of coherent actions, fits on one page and exposes vague goals quickly. Writing your current strategy in that form gives every longer document a spine and tells you whether there is a strategy to write about at all.

                ## Milestones
                1. A diagnosis of the central challenge written in no more than five sentences.
                2. A guiding policy that rules some options out, in two or three sentences.
                3. Three to five coherent actions that follow from the policy, each with an owner.
                4. The page read by two colleagues who can restate it without looking.

                ## Notes
                If the guiding policy rules nothing out, it is a goal, not a policy. Go back to the diagnosis.

# Strategy Narrative and Memo Writing

This area is for executives, founders and managers who have to put a strategy into words that other people will read, argue with and act on without the author in the room. It starts with foundations (a look back at your own documents, the reader and decision for each one, a one-page strategy statement, house formats and a fact base), then the routines that keep writing moving, the specific skills of structure, diagnosis, trade-offs and numbers, the improvements and decisions that change how your organisation uses documents, the dated memos that matter most, versions for founders, enterprise divisions and team managers, and finally the specialist work of assumption tracking, candid post-mortems and a memo culture.

What repeats is a weekly drafting block and a weekly review queue, monthly rhythms for the leadership letter, the fact base, red-team reviews, the decision log, peer feedback, investor updates and coaching, a quarterly refresh of the strategy narrative and its assumptions, and a yearly annual memo, glossary check and archive clear-out.
