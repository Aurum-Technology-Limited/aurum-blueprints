            - name: Deciding between CEng and IEng
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Chartered Engineer and Incorporated Engineer are both professional titles on the Engineering Council register, but CEng asks for evidence of innovation, complex problem solving and technical or commercial leadership, while IEng centres on managing and applying existing technology. Reading both competence descriptions against your actual role tells you whether to go straight for CEng or register as IEng first and transfer later, before you spend a year writing toward the wrong standard.

                ## Milestones
                1. The CEng and IEng competence statements read side by side for all five competence areas.
                2. Your three most demanding pieces of recent work placed against each title's descriptions.
                3. A written decision on which title to apply for now, with the reason in two sentences.
                4. Your mentor or a registered colleague's view on the decision recorded.

                ## Notes
                Engineers often underrate their own work because they describe it as routine. Judge by the decisions you made and the uncertainty you handled, not by your job title.
              priority: medium
              frontmatter:
                mode: research
                output_kind: decision
                success_criteria: "A dated note names the title you are applying for and links it to at least three pieces of your own work matched to that title's competence statements."

                - "List your three most demanding pieces of work from the last three years"
                - "Mark which title each piece of work best evidences and why"
                - "Ask a Chartered colleague to sanity-check your choice over coffee"

                ## Purpose

Picking the one whose discipline matches your work and whose process suits how you write evidence saves an awkward transfer later.

# Chartered Engineer (CEng) Registration

This area is for engineers working toward Chartered Engineer registration through a UK licensed institution, whether you are a graduate on a training scheme, a mid-career engineer who never got round to it, or an engineer abroad who wants the title recognised in the UK. It starts with the foundations (the right title, the current UK-SPEC and institution guidance, your institution, your degree, your mentor, your manager, the costs and a dated plan), then the routines that turn everyday work into evidence, the skills of writing to each competence area, ethics, sustainability and commercial awareness, the decisions about projects, further learning and sponsors, the submission and the professional review interview, situations such as career breaks, self-employment and overseas qualifications, and finally the work of an experienced Chartered Engineer.

What repeats is a monthly evidence-writing session and a weekly paragraph, monthly mentor meetings, a quarterly self-review against UK-SPEC, monthly updates to your development record and evidence file, a weekly read of your sector's news, and, once registered, quarterly CPD entries and the yearly subscription and code of conduct check. The Skills matrix, Development plan, Meeting notes, 1:1 and Remediation plan templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
