                A 30 percent fall sounds survivable until it is shown as the price of a car or two years of savings vanishing from the screen.                 5. Any mismatch between that answer and your current mix noted for your next allocation review.

# Investor Behaviour & Panic Control

This area is for any investor who trusts their plan on a calm day but worries about what they will do when the screen turns red, or when everyone else seems to be getting rich. It starts with foundations (your own record in past falls, loss tolerance in money, ring-fenced spending cash, a cooling-off rule, a decision journal and a crash letter), moves to the routines that keep behaviour steady, then what is worth learning about biases and market history, the decisions that remove temptation, dated moments such as the first week of a big fall, situations from a first-time investor to a grieving heir, and finally specialist work such as pre-mortems and commitment devices.

The rhythm is quiet on purpose: a weekly news window, monthly checks that contributions went in, tips reviewed and the journal reread, a quarterly portfolio check-in, and yearly jobs such as the crash drill and rereading your crash letter. The Operational checklist, Habit tracker, Reading queue, Metrics log, Risk register and Meeting notes templates pair with the pre-trade checklist and crash week plan, the news window, the history reading, the behaviour gap check, the pre-mortem and the group protocol. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
