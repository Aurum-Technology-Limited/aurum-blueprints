                ## Purpose
                Before buying a single REIT, decide what job property is meant to do: income, diversification away from shares, a partial hedge against inflation, or simply matching a global index. Listed property has fallen as hard as shares in most crashes, so a written reason stops you expecting it to behave like bricks and mortar. One paragraph now saves a great many second guesses later.

                ## Milestones
                1. The main job you want property to do written in one sentence.
                2. What listed property did in at least two past downturns, such as 2008 and 2022, noted beside it.
                3. What you are not expecting property to do written down, for example protecting capital in a crash.
                4. The paragraph filed with your investment policy or portfolio notes.

                ## Notes
                Keep it to one main role if you can. Holdings bought for income and for growth at the same time tend to be judged against whichever one is failing that month.

# REIT & Property Fund Investing

This area is for anyone who wants property in their portfolio without owning buildings, through listed REITs, property investment trusts and property funds. It starts with the foundations (what property is for, what you already hold through other funds, which wrapper, which account and how much), moves on to reading REIT accounts and running the routines that keep holdings honest, then covers the decisions worth making once, events such as takeovers and rights issues, situations from renters and former landlords to retirees, and specialist work on valuation, withholding tax and niche trusts.

The rhythms are a monthly distribution log, discount check, purchase check and voting check, a quarterly holdings review, factsheet comparison and risk register, and yearly jobs such as the rebalance, the tax pack and the benchmark comparison.
