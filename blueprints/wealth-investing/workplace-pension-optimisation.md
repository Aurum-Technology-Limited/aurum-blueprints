                ## Purpose
                Plenty of people pay into a workplace pension for years without knowing whether it is a defined contribution pot or a defined benefit promise, who runs it, or what the employer actually pays.             - name: Employer matching formula decoded
              description: |-
                ## Purpose
                Matching formulas vary widely: some employers pay a flat rate whatever you do, some match pound for pound up to a cap, and others match half of what you pay up to a set percentage. Working out exactly how your employer's formula behaves, including waiting periods and vesting, tells you the minimum you must contribute so no employer money is left unclaimed.

                ## Milestones
                1. The written matching rule found in the handbook, contract or scheme booklet.
                2. A small table showing the employer contribution at three or four of your own contribution levels.
                3. Any waiting period, vesting schedule or clawback on leaving noted.
                4. The contribution level that earns the maximum match written on your fact sheet.

                ## Notes
                Matches are sometimes calculated per pay period, so a large one-off contribution late in the year can miss match that steady monthly contributions would have earned. Ask whether the scheme makes a year-end true-up.

# Workplace Pension Optimisation

This area is for anyone paying into a workplace pension, from a first salaried job to a senior role with a complicated pay package, who wants every bit of employer money and a fund they actually understand. It starts with the foundations (the scheme fact sheet, the full-match contribution rate, a beneficiary nomination and the default fund), then the monthly and yearly routines, the knowledge that makes statements readable, the decisions on salary sacrifice, fund choice and contribution types, the events of joining, leaving and redundancy, the situations that change the picture, and finally the specialist work of defined benefit members and experienced savers.

What repeats is a monthly payslip check, a quarterly log of pot value and contributions, the annual statement review, a step-up at each pay rise, and a yearly look at contribution limits, nominations, bonus elections and the benefits enrolment window. The Metrics log and Meeting notes templates pair with the projects that name them. Installing adds all 50 projects as active, so archive the ones that are not for you yet.
